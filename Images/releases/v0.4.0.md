> ## The Cut as a moment, new music in three chapters, and the pad in both hands
>
> **Played an earlier version?** Unzip into a **new, empty folder**: this release removes six recordings, and unzipped over an old install the old files would stay and return to the chapters' rotation. Your save and settings live in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\BS3D`, so they carry over anyway. **The Silhouettes (levels 41–50) and the Quarry (64–70) were rebuilt**, so those have new leaderboards; the Scaled-display note below may change how your mouse feels.

## What's new in v0.4.0

**The Cut**
- **The Cutter round has a look of its own**: a dark polished steel ball with a circular saw through it, its teeth glowing white-hot and cooling to amber. The magazine's disc, the aim beam, the muzzle, the launch smear and the award number carry the same white-hot tint, so the round no longer wears the zap's colour or a hidden one (#766).
- **Using the Cut is a moment in four beats.** Pressing R spins the saw up and the gun's light surges, with a rising charge sound that ends in a bright chime. In flight the lens zooms in a little. At the strike a white-hot slash runs across the storey, with a flash, sparks, a ringing "slice" and a brief freeze of the picture. What the cut frees falls in slow motion, longer the more it freed, and the two cut faces glow and cool. Firing again ends the slow motion. On a pad the trigger pulse rises under both triggers and clicks at the top, and the cut knocks in the hand (#767).

**Music**
- **Three chapters have new recordings**, a first round that later rounds will keep refining: **the Gallery** (chapter 2: the desert-blues and the jive recordings are now a Sahel guitar band and a mbaqanga band, and the mbira no longer clips) (#706), **the Silhouettes** (chapter 5: all ten recordings, mysterious and magical, from a clarinet fairy tale to a shadow-theatre gamelan) (#732), and **the Grid** (chapter 8: eight of its ten recordings were regenerated; the festival "big room" is now a 1990s piano house) (#824). The Eruption's eight recordings were regenerated too (#841).
- Some recordings were renamed to match their new sound, so they show under new names in Extras › Jukebox.
- **The music no longer clips in play.** More than half of the recordings overshot the decoder's rail and were being hard-clipped as they played; the decoder now keeps 1.5 dB of headroom and the voices are raised by the same amount, so nothing is louder or quieter than before, just clean (#919).
- Extras › Jukebox loops the recording you picked until you pick another, instead of moving on after one pass (#899).

**Scenes**
- **The savanna's acacia grows its fork as one surface** on High and Ultra, in a finer wood that the trunk swells into, instead of a bough stuck on (#758).
- **The spruces of the aurora and the forest are boughs** on High and Ultra, with snow lying in lumps and a lighter crown far off, instead of stacked lampshades (#733).
- **The mountains on High and Ultra are a finer range**: the rock is carved in three dimensions, ledges carry the snow, and it is about 3 ms cheaper to draw on a fast desktop at 4K. Low, Medium and Potato (the lowest quality tier) are unchanged (#781).
- **The meadow's hay bales are the size of the fence's metre** and stand clear of the fences (#864).
- The savanna's acacia leaves lie along their twigs (about half of them pointed away from the crown) (#882), and its campfires' flames taper at the sides instead of being cut off sharply (#886).
- The neon city's roofs carry one mast each and share their equipment with the day's roofs (#850); the city intro's rooftop shot keeps the whole mast and its beacon in frame (#840).
- The crate's wooden frame no longer flickers at its corners (#837).
- **A scene's tour from Extras › Scene can be skipped** like a chapter's (click, Space, Enter, Esc, or A/B/RT on a pad), and nothing on the page answers while it flies, so it can no longer be broken by picking another scene mid-flight (#861).

**Help and About**
- **Help opens on Controls**, and its six topics are a row of tabs — Controls, Basics, Scoring, Odd balls, Campaign, The glass — one click, ←/→ or LB/RB each (#857).
- **Help's paragraphs are set across the whole text width** on every tab, and the tabs sit against the top of the frame, so a page scrolls less (#865).
- **The odd balls are shown, not only described**: on the Odd balls tab each kind turns beside its words, drawn by the game itself, so it looks as it does on the map (#856).
- About is four short pages instead of one long one, and the original score's player is the last of them (#807). **MADE WITH says it straight**: the original game is the author's own code, extended with Claude since 2026 (#866).

**Statistics**
- **The game counts how each level is played** (starts, retries, clears, losses out of balls and at the line, quits, skips, first clears, shots and seconds), per level and on your computer, in `Stats.json` in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\BS3D`, next to your save (#709).
- **Only with online scores on, those counts are sent**, per level and nothing else: no nickname, no address kept, no game version, no time of day. They go in the background at least ten minutes apart, never inside a request that names you. Turn online scores off and what is still queued is thrown away (#709).

**Levels**
- **The Silhouettes** (41–50) are solids hung ten different ways — a fish in relief, an umbrella of twelve wedges, a bell with a red heart behind a glass bottom, a cat as a puppet, a teapot, a key on a board, a rocket with windows, a coronet, a guitar with glass strings, an anchor (#736). Glass is first met on Umbrella, and the cards for the kinds come in campaign order.
- **The Quarry** (64–69) adds one idea to each level the gun can see: an arch, an overhang, a stone hung on a single white column, two depths, a sealed crystal, a crystal on a cord (#742). **Colossus** (70), the chapter's closer, is now a giant holding the glass up, its heart sealed in its chest (#849).
- A tall level's feed brings the glass down only as far as you can reach, and the Brake refuses a lift that would take the bottom out of reach (#843). The aim's upward limit on a tall level holds from the start and lifts once the whole cluster hangs in view (#842).
- The Brake works only while the glass stands still; the chips tell "used up" from "not now" (#844).

**Colours you can tell apart by shape**
- **Every colour carries its own pattern**, and each ball style has its own set: figures on wool, porcelain and plasma, emblems on vinyl and metal, lines on marble, ice and gem, spots on bubble (the lava has none). The balls in the gun's queue carry the same pattern (#737).
- The bomb is drawn the size of the balls round it; it used to sit in its cell like a smaller ball (#860).

**Score and feedback**
- **The points a shot gains zoom up past the score, hold to be read, then fly into the corner**; the completion bonus gets its own award (#846).
- **A lost multiplier cracks and shatters** with a falling run of notes, and the Swap chip flashes when a swap would keep the streak (#845).
- The blue ceiling step (a feed, or the Brake's lift) has its own rising marimba sound, climbing step by step through a run (#726).
- **When a level is cleared the HUD steps aside**: the chips, the queue and the side cut fade out as the celebration starts, and the score and its bonus stay (#862).
- The landing preview grows to exactly a whole ball with a rubbery beat (#831), and the camera tilts with the barrel's elevation when you are not looking down the barrel (#720).
- The failure page's "skip" note carries the same dark outline as every other line there, so it no longer vanishes over a bright sky (#918).
- **"STREAK LOST ×N"** is said above the crosshair when a miss ends a multiplier, so you know what the shattering was (#875), and the result page's "balls unused" row is priced at the level's own value of a ball, not a flat 50 (#881).

**Gamepad**
- **The release rumble grows with how much fell**, in its own layer under the kicks (#810). The pad buzzes steadily while the aim is pushed where the gun will not fire (#835); the Brake's lift and each bomb's blast are felt (#836). The tab-turn pulse is weaker (#800).
- **The pad walks the level picker's grid**; LB/RB turn chapters, drawn under the arrows (#848). The level picker's chapter arrows and dots are drawn as shapes centred on their plates, and the dots are smaller (#847). The keys and pad buttons are drawn in the menus beside what they do, for whichever hand last moved (#799). Quit asks first (#826).

**Settings**
- **10-bit colour** on the Display page: where your monitor reports 10 bits per colour the game draws its last step at 10 bits, and the row turns it back to 8 (#859). The FPS counter row moved to the Game page.
- **Scaled displays** (Windows set to 125 % or more): the game now draws at your display's full resolution, so menus and the picture are sharp. Mouse aim is faster than before on such a display; the **Aim sensitivity** row sets it (#825).
- The captions and values line up on one seam (#833); the texts on Help, Settings, the tutorial cards and the boards are set to typesetting rules, with real dashes and quotes, and numbers in columns line up (#806). No line starts with a space any more (#855).
- "Unlock all" is a development row and no longer in a release (#854).

**Raspberry Pi and the lowest quality tier (Potato)**
- **The Potato renderer, the simplest drawing path, which the Raspberry Pi always uses and Windows uses when you pick Potato on the Quality row or start with `potato`, now draws the scenes**: the savanna, desert, meadow, mountains, Moon, volcano, both cities, the cavern, the Grid, the big top, space, the aurora and the dream each have a backdrop of their own instead of a bare sky (#829).
- **The ball styles and the colours' patterns are drawn in Potato**: each material's gloss from its desktop constants, and the figures of #737 in four cheap programs (#828).
- **The special kinds read in Potato**: the bomb beats red, the zap flickers cold, the glass shows through, the ice is pale and the iron matte, the acid's pool, the sick ball's crust and the gravity well's eye are drawn, and the frozen ball is ice, no longer a pink block (#863).
- A scene's backdrop is built once, not on every level start, so a restart no longer hitches; the glass ring keeps its own colour (#897).
- Auto quality says "(resolution)" while the probe may lower the resolution and "(locked)" once one is chosen (#832); the resolution step waits for ten seconds under the floor, not one (#834).

**Smaller things**
- The window icon follows a scale change, and the Resolution row follows a window resize (#825).

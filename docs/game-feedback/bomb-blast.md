# A bomb goes off

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

A detonation (#389) is answered where it happened by six things at once: a flash, a fireball, a spray of sparks, the light they throw on everything round them, a report of its own and a rumble of the camera. The bomb's rules, and the throw of its victims, are under "Bomb, zap, acid" in [Special balls](../game-session/special-balls.md); the report is in "The sound" of this area.

## What makes it possible: `BallLanding.Detonations`

- The landing's `World` is the cell the shot stuck to, which stands beside a bomb and never on one, and `Released.Destroyed` is shared with the zap and the acid, so nothing downstream could say where a blast was or tell a chain of five from one bomb. Each detonation is recorded (`Detonation`: the bomb body's world position, its `Link` down the chain, how much it took) in a list the contact handler owns and hands out. It is valid for the `BallLanded` call alone; `Blasts.SetOff` copies what it needs.
- ⚠ **Every bomb that goes off gets a record, including one nobody armed.** A bomb has a third trigger, losing its last path to the glass, resolved in `BallsConstraintsBuilder.ResolveDisconnected`, the pass a match, a zap, a shaft and a blast all end in. The record is written in that loop at the one line where a bomb is decided to have fired, so an orphaned bomb flashes, reports and jolts like an armed one. The list is therefore filled by all four removals and emptied once per landing, in the handler, never just before the blast (that would discard the bombs a match had already set off). A bomb the walk fires comes one `Link` after everything already gone off in that pass, and at link 0 when a match, zap or shaft cut it loose.

## The effect

- **`Game/Effects/Blasts.cs` and `Blast.fx`**: one static buffer and one draw call for up to `MAX_BLASTS` blasts. Per blast: a flash billboard (white-hot, short), a fireball billboard and `SPARKS_PER_BLAST` streak sparks (the fireworks' screen-velocity stretch, with a far harder drag so they stall inside a couple of radii). Drawn in the launch smear's slot and states: additive, depth-read, before the drain's glass.
- ⚠ **No shock ring.** A thin annulus racing out past the radius read as a clean cream-white halo drawn over the cluster (on a chain, two of them, like an icon), and nothing in an explosion is a perfect circle. The fireball's outline and body are torn by three octaves of value noise and burn through into rags as it cools. The sparks are mostly fire-coloured: mostly white reads as glitter.
- **The flash and the fireball are pulled towards the lens** (the flash by its radius, the fireball by half): both stand in the hole the blast just opened, and a billboard left at the centre is cut along hard flat lines by the balls round the rim.
- **The light is one `SceneLights` slot for one frame** (`SceneLights.SetFlash`, "Scene point lights" in `docs/rendering.md`): a chain's links folded into one light at their strength-weighted centre, capped, lasting 0.45 s. It has to be strong enough for the balls round a blast to take it on their rims.
- **A chain is played as a chain.** The rule takes a whole chain inside one landing and its debris flies in one step, so the effect staggers it: each link goes off `Blasts.CHAIN_STAGGER_SECONDS` behind the bomb that set it off, flash, report and rumble together. It is short because the fragments are already flying, and a flash that trails its debris by more than a few frames reads as a second event.
- **On the simulation's clock, slow motion included.** A blast is the world, not a rule of the level, so it is stepped, not stamped on the wall clock the way the laser net is: the biggest blasts are the ones that engage the drop cinematic, and a flash at full speed over debris crawling at 0.4x is over before the balls have moved. Under the result page it steps with the world (#241) and its rumble is dropped, the lens being the page's; under a pause it stops.
- **The rumble** is `CameraShake.Rumble` ("Recoil and camera shake" in `docs/game-session.md`), `BLAST_RUMBLE` times the blast's size as each one goes off.
- **`detonate=<t1,...>`** sets off the level's lowest bomb a shot could reach at those wall-clock seconds (`shot=`'s clock) and answers it as a real blast is answered, minus the shot: no landing thunk, no award, no streak. A real blast needs a shot landed beside a bomb, and the Game's aim cannot be scripted.
- **Looking at it**: a first blast of 26 or 117 balls engages the drop cinematic, so the play camera's view has to come from a second detonation that cannot beat 1.25x the first (`level=Vent`, `level=Sill`). Seen from the cinematic's climb, a bomb buried inside a cluster shows its fireball mostly hidden behind balls, and what reads is the light through the gaps. Under `balls=lava` and `balls=plasma`, the two materials that glow on their own, the flash is still the brightest thing in the frame.

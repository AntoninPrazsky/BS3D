# The ceiling and the floor alarm

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

The two ends of the descending-ceiling pressure rule: the glass stepping down announces itself (a red flash, a wave through the cluster and a sound), and the floor's laser net warns that the end is near. What happens when the cluster does cross the line is in [Losing on the line](line-loss.md).

## The ceiling announces itself

The glass stepping down is the pressure the `ceilingStep` rule exists to apply, and a translucent plate sliding a little way against a sky is nearly invisible, so it says so on the frame it steps.

- **A step waits for the drop cinematic.** The step comes due on the frame a shot is fired, but the shot lands about a tenth of a second later, so a shot that cut a large group loose would put the red flash and alarm wave on top of the drop cinematic: the game's one punishment animation shown over the player's reward. `CeilingDescent.Update` holds a due step for `CEILING_STEP_HOLD` (0.45 s) and then for the cinematic to be over.
  - **A count, not a flag**: a level with `ceilingStep` 1 steps on every shot and two shots inside the hold must not lose one. The hold is re-armed per release, so queued steps come down one at a time, not as one double-height lurch.
  - It cannot deadlock: the release is gated on a timer and on `_cinematic.Engaged`, never on a shot resolving, so a ball that never comes to rest cannot leave a level unloseable.
  - The `[ceiling]` log line carries `waited`, the one figure that says whether the deferral did anything (the minimum is the hold itself; a step that landed on a cinematic reads over a second more).
- **The plate lights up red.** `EmissiveTint` is a `float3` on `InstancedModelRenderer`, added at the end of `ShadePixel` so every technique shading through it can use it, zero everywhere else. `CeilingDescent.Flash` spikes to 1 on a step and decays linearly (the `CameraShake` rule: it really ends), and the tint is scaled by its square, so the glass is unmistakable on the frame it steps and thin before the slide finishes. It marks the event; it does not colour the plate for the duration.
  - **`EmissiveTint` is not multiplied by the material's alpha**, unlike the specular ambient beside it: alpha is how much of what is behind a surface comes through, and a glowing pane emits rather than transmits. Attenuating a warning by the glass's own transparency would make it invisible.
  - **The number has to be far over 1** (`CEILING_FLASH_COLOR`): the plate is well under half opaque (`CeilingPlate.GLASS_ALPHA`), so most of what is seen there is the sky behind it, and at 1.5 the red merely tinted that sky and the plate came out pink.
  - A feed step (the tall level's reward) flashes cold blue (`CEILING_FEED_COLOR`) because nothing went wrong.
- **A red wave runs down through the cluster**, seeded from every ball on the topmost occupied level at once (`ClusterRipple.StartFromTop`), because a descent is a shock delivered by the ceiling to everything hanging on it. The walk only moves outwards, so it travels down. How the two waves share one channel: [The ripple](ripple.md).
- **The step is heard, from the plate, in its colour's own sound.** `PlayCeilingStep` speaks on the frame the plate lights, placed at the plate (0, `CeilingDescent.Y`, 0) and attenuated with distance to a landing's floor. It says "look up" under the music and never competes with a shot. The `[ceiling]` line ends with what was heard: `heard <cue> at <volume> +<n> st` (the buffer, the volume handed to the voice, how far a pour had climbed). The cue is `<name>.ogg` or `<name> bake`, so a run with a file missing says so, and it is named after the voices that spoke, not after the step asked for, so a blue step routed to the red step's voices would print `ceiling-step.ogg`.
  - **The red step** (pressure) is `Sfx/ceiling-step.ogg` at `CEILING_LEVEL`, two voices: a Stable Audio render, "a heavy elevator platform descending one notch", a drone then a distinct settle at 1.25 s, 86.7 % of its energy under 200 Hz; `BakeCeilingStep` when the file is missing. The settle lags the 0.4 s slide by most of a second (a heavy plate coming to rest), which is a judgement for the ear. The owner hears it.
  - **The blue step** (a feed, and the Brake's lift) is `Sfx/ceiling-feed.ogg` at `CEILING_FEED_LEVEL` (0.25), three voices, with `CeilingFeedSynth` when the file is missing ([its recording](sound-effects.md#the-blue-ceiling-step-726)). It is the red step's opposite in everything the ear can tell: two struck notes **rising** an octave (C4 then C5), tonal, short, and 76.7 % of its energy at 300 Hz-4 kHz. ⚠ **Until #726 it was the red recording at 0.35, and the owner heard nothing**: he listens on his monitor's built-in speakers, which carry almost nothing under 200 Hz. Measured as played (A-weighted, loudest 400 ms, a 250 Hz fourth-order high-pass standing in for the monitor): the old blue step -40.3 dB, the red step -37.2, the new blue step at 0.25 -28.1, a 3-ball release -25.4, a 10-ball release (which a feed usually follows) -22.6, the shot -22.7. So the lower level is 12 dB more audible on those speakers and still under the release that earned it.
  - **The Brake's lift plays the same blue sound, not a rising counterpart**, because the blue sound already rises (the issue asked that the lift be at least not a descent), and because colour is the language: the flash and the ripple are one blue for a feed and a lift, so the ear gets one blue too. A falling version for the feed (the file reversed was the cheap candidate) was not made: every falling figure in this game says "no" (the refused shot, the aim stop, a streak breaking), and the feed is a reward.
  - **A pour climbs.** A band that goes can owe a tall level a run of feed steps, one a `CEILING_STEP_HOLD` apart: eleven in a row in a scripted Totem run. Each blue step within `CeilingFeedPour.WINDOW_SECONDS` (1 s) of the last plays a semitone above it, up to `CeilingFeedPour.TOP_SEMITONES` (a fifth), so a pour rises instead of repeating one chime eleven times; after a longer gap it starts at the bottom again. A red step plays at its own pitch and does not break a pour. ⚠ **The pour counts on the gameplay frame's seconds, not the wall clock**: `GameplayScreen` advances it beside `CeilingDescent.Update`, so the pause page, which stops the screen dead, stops the pour with the steps, and a pour paused halfway goes on from where it was. A wall clock read at the step would start it over after any pause longer than the window.
  - **The distance law does not reach its floor on a tall level.** On Totem the plate hangs 30-37 units from the listener: the voices were handed 0.16-0.18 for the blue step and 0.33-0.36 for the red one, a distance factor of 0.64-0.72 against `VolumeForDistance`'s floor of 0.45 at 60 units.

## The floor alarm: the laser grid

When the cluster's lowest ball is within `LASER_WARN_STEPS` (3) more ceiling steps of the death line, a grid of pulsing red beams appears across the field's footprint, on the plane a ball's surface would touch at the moment the level is lost (`CEILING_DEATH_Y - BALL_RADIUS`; the loss compares ball centres, and only once they have held the line, "A cluster that merely swings has not lost" in `docs/game-session.md`, so the net can be touched for a moment without the level ending). It stands down when the danger passes, and after the level ends either way it keeps pulsing for `LINGER_SECONDS` (2.5 s) after the result page goes up, then goes out.

`Game/Effects/LaserGrid.cs` owns the net, `LaserGrid.fx` (in `Prazsky.Shaders`, loaded only by the Game) draws it, and `GameplayScreen.UpdateLaserWarning` owns the trigger.

- **The plane is half a unit over the island's stone cap**, which fixes the line's clearance at one unit: any less and the net is drawn inside the cap it is meant to hover over.
- **The trigger is geometry, read off the walk the loss is decided on.** `CheckLevelLost`'s scan over the live body poses tracks the minimum Y, and the warning is `lowest <= CEILING_DEATH_Y + LASER_WARN_STEPS x CEILING_DESCENT_PER_STEP`: the loss's own comparison, three descents early (at the set's cadence of 4-9 shots per descent, roughly that many shots of noticing it). It is geometry and not a shot countdown because a level whose ceiling never steps still warns when attaching balls grow the cluster down to the line. `LASER_WARN_HYSTERESIS` is on the way out only, so a shot's shove cannot arm and stand down the net with every swing. An empty field's `MaxValue` stands it down for free.
- **A drop cinematic holds the level's endings, never the warning** (`CheckLevelLost`'s `mayLose`). The release that engages a cinematic is the one that rescues a low cluster, so a warning frozen behind the cinematic gate kept blazing across the player's reward for the whole dive. The walk and the arm/stand-down run on every frame of a built session; only `LoseLevel` and the budget test wait.
- **Everything time-driven is stamped in wall-clock seconds and evaluated in `Draw`, and that is load-bearing.** A lost level pushes the result screen over the session, the session stops running its rules (the covered frame is the world's alone, #241), and `Draw` keeps running (`DrawsUnderlying`). A fade advanced in an update would freeze at full brightness exactly as the player is told what it warned about. So `SetVisible` and `NoticeLevelEnded` stamp transitions, `Draw` evaluates them, and the linger expires in `Draw`. The linger is stamped in `ShowResultScreen`, the one funnel both endings come through, not where the level logically ends: a clear's page arrives `LEVEL_CLEARED_BEAT` later, plus a whole cinematic, and a linger stamped at the clear was spent before anyone saw it. Under a pause the net keeps pulsing, like the ball heartbeat.
- **The beams billboard about their own axes** (ShotTrail's trick), and here it is load-bearing: the play camera stands at Y -7.9, barely below the grid's plane at -6.0, so a flat quad in that plane is seen edge-on and vanishes.
- **The red is the ceiling flash's own** (`CEILING_FLASH_COLOR`, passed to the constructor so the two cannot drift): the plate says "the glass stepped", the net says "and this is where it ends". The channel has to be far over 1 for the glare's sake: the bright pass keys on luminance and red's Rec. 709 weight is 0.2126, so a pure red needs about 2.6 to clear `GLARE_THRESHOLD`.
- ⚠ **The white core bleached the whole net at every peak.** The hot filament lifts the channels red lacks, and since the play camera sees the grid near edge-on the beams stack additively, so at a `CORE_WHITE` of 2.5 the net turned white at its loudest moment. At the shipped 0.9 the peak blooms red-pink and the trough stays pure laser red.
- **The pulse** is a raised cosine squared at `PULSE_HZ` (1.6) between `PULSE_FLOOR` (0.35) and 1: sharp peaks, wide troughs, an alarm's shape rather than a breath; the trough sits just under the glare threshold and the peak far over it, so the pulse breathes the bloom too.
- **The net spans the field footprint plus `FOOTPRINT_MARGIN`**, which keeps it inside the drain's mouth: every authored footprint's half-diagonal is well under `ArenaIsland.FUNNEL_TOP_RADIUS`, so the beams never cross the gold bead at the rim, and the gun (whose orbit clears the footprint) never stands in them. One static buffer per level, rebuilt in `Fit` from `FitFieldToMap`, one draw call.
- ⚠ **Draw order is load-bearing: additive, `DepthRead`, `CullNone`, between the two glasses.** The net writes no depth, so the order has to encode the geometry: after `DrawSettingGlass` (the funnel is entirely below the net, and a drop cinematic looking down the throat has the net in front of the glass; drawn earlier, the cone wrongly dimmed it) and before the ceiling plate (which is above the net, so an overhead shot gets the plate composited over it). The gun's pane is drawn after everything ("The gun's hardware, in one copy" in `docs/testbed.md`).
- **`lasers` on the command line pins the net on** while a level is played, since reaching it honestly means playing to within two steps of losing, which can no more be scripted than clearing a level can.

# The ceiling and the floor alarm

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

The two ends of the descending-ceiling pressure rule: the glass stepping down announces itself (a red flash, a wave through the cluster and a sound), and the floor's laser net warns that the end is near. What happens when the cluster does cross the line is in [Losing on the line](line-loss.md).

## The ceiling announces itself

The glass stepping down is the pressure the `ceilingStep` rule exists to apply, and a translucent plate sliding a little way against a sky is nearly invisible, so it says so on the frame it steps.

- **A step waits for the drop cinematic.** The step comes due on the frame a shot is fired, but the shot lands about a tenth of a second later, so a shot that cut a large group loose would put the red flash and alarm wave on top of the drop cinematic: the game's one punishment animation shown over the player's reward. `CeilingDescent.Update` holds a due step for `CEILING_STEP_HOLD` (0.45 s) and then for the cinematic to be over.
  - **A count, not a flag**: a level with `ceilingStep` 1 steps on every shot and two shots inside the hold must not lose one. The hold is re-armed per release, so queued steps come down one at a time, not as one double-height lurch.
  - It cannot deadlock: the release is gated on a timer and on `_cinematic.Engaged`, never on a shot resolving, so a ball that never comes to rest cannot leave a level unloseable.
  - The `[ceiling]` log line carries `waited`, the one figure that says whether the deferral did anything (the minimum is the hold itself; a step that landed on a cinematic reads over a second more).
- **The plate lights up red.** `EmissiveTint` is a `float3` on `InstancedModelRenderer`, added at the end of `ShadePixel` so every technique shading through it can use it, zero everywhere else. `CeilingDescent.Flash` spikes to 1 on a step and decays linearly (the `CameraShake` rule: it really ends), and the tint is scaled by its square, so the glass is unmistakable on the frame it steps and thin before the slide finishes. It marks the event; it does not colour the plate for the duration.
  - **`EmissiveTint` is not multiplied by the material's alpha**, unlike the specular ambient beside it: alpha is how much of what is behind a surface comes through, and a glowing pane emits rather than transmits. Attenuating a warning by the glass's own transparency would make it invisible.
  - **The number has to be far over 1** (`CEILING_FLASH_COLOR`): the plate is well under half opaque (`CeilingPlate.GLASS_ALPHA`), so most of what is seen there is the sky behind it, and at 1.5 the red merely tinted that sky and the plate came out pink.
  - A feed step (the tall level's reward) flashes cold blue (`CEILING_FEED_COLOR`) because nothing went wrong.
- **A red wave runs down through the cluster**, seeded from every ball on the topmost occupied level at once (`ClusterRipple.StartFromTop`), because a descent is a shock delivered by the ceiling to everything hanging on it. The walk only moves outwards, so it travels down. How the two waves share one channel: [The ripple](ripple.md).
- **The step is heard, from the plate, in its colour's own sound.** `PlayCeilingStep` speaks on the frame the plate lights, placed at the plate (0, `CeilingDescent.Y`, 0) and attenuated with distance to a landing's floor. It says "look up" under the music and never competes with a shot. The `[ceiling]` line ends with what was heard: `heard <cue> at <volume> +<n> st` (the buffer, the volume handed to the voice, how far a pour had climbed). The cue is `<name>.ogg` or `<name> bake`, so a run with a file missing says so, and it is named after the voices that spoke, not after the step asked for, so a blue step routed to the red step's voices would print `ceiling-step.ogg`.
  - **The red step** (pressure) is `Sfx/ceiling-step.ogg` at `CEILING_LEVEL`, two voices: a Stable Audio render, "a heavy elevator platform descending one notch", a drone then a distinct settle at 1.25 s, 86.7 % of its energy under 200 Hz; `BakeCeilingStep` when the file is missing. The settle lags the 0.4 s slide by most of a second (a heavy plate coming to rest), which is a judgement for the ear. The owner hears it.
  - **The blue step** (a feed, and the Brake's lift) is `Sfx/ceiling-feed.ogg` at `CEILING_FEED_LEVEL` (0.25), three voices, with `CeilingFeedSynth` when the file is missing ([its recording](sound-effects.md#the-blue-ceiling-step-726)). It is the red step's opposite in everything the ear can tell: two struck notes **rising** an octave (C4 then C5), tonal, short, and 76.7 % of its energy at 300 Hz-4 kHz. ⚠ **Until #726 it was the red recording at 0.35, and the owner heard nothing**: he listens on his monitor's built-in speakers, which carry almost nothing under 200 Hz. Measured as played (A-weighted, loudest 400 ms, a 250 Hz fourth-order high-pass standing in for the monitor): the old blue step -40.3 dB, the red step -37.2, the new blue step at 0.25 -28.1, a 3-ball release -25.4, a 10-ball release (which a feed usually follows) -22.6, the shot -22.7. So the lower level is 12 dB more audible on those speakers and still under the release that earned it.
  - **The Brake's lift plays the same blue sound, not a rising counterpart**, because the blue sound already rises (the issue asked that the lift be at least not a descent), and because colour is the language: the flash and the ripple are one blue for a feed and a lift, so the ear gets one blue too. A falling version for the feed (the file reversed was the cheap candidate) was not made: every falling figure in this game says "no" (the refused shot, the aim stop, a streak breaking), and the feed is a reward.
  - **A pour climbs.** A band that goes can owe a tall level a run of feed steps, one a `CEILING_STEP_HOLD` apart: eleven in a row in a scripted Totem run. Each blue step within `CeilingFeedPour.WINDOW_SECONDS` (1 s) of the last plays a semitone above it, up to `CeilingFeedPour.TOP_SEMITONES` (a fifth), so a pour rises instead of repeating one chime eleven times; after a longer gap it starts at the bottom again. A red step plays at its own pitch and does not break a pour. ⚠ **The pour counts on the gameplay frame's seconds, not the wall clock**: `GameplayScreen` advances it beside `CeilingDescent.Update`, so the pause page, which stops the screen dead, stops the pour with the steps, and a pour paused halfway goes on from where it was. A wall clock read at the step would start it over after any pause longer than the window.
  - **The distance law does not reach its floor on a tall level.** On Totem the plate hangs 30-37 units from the listener: the voices were handed 0.16-0.18 for the blue step and 0.33-0.36 for the red one, a distance factor of 0.64-0.72 against `VolumeForDistance`'s floor of 0.45 at 60 units.

## The floor alarm: the laser grid

When the cluster's lowest ball is within `LASER_WARN_STEPS` (3) more ceiling steps of the death line, a grid of pulsing red beams appears across the field's footprint, on the plane a ball's surface would touch at the moment the level is lost (`CEILING_DEATH_Y - BALL_RADIUS`; the loss compares ball centres, and only once they have held the line, "A cluster that merely swings has not lost" in `docs/game-session.md`, so the net can be touched for a moment without the level ending). It stands down when the danger passes, and after the level ends either way it keeps pulsing for `LINGER_SECONDS` (2.5 s) after the result page goes up, then goes out.

`Game/Effects/LaserGrid.cs` owns the net, `LaserGrid.fx` (in `Prazsky.Shaders`, loaded only by the Game) draws it, and `GameplayScreen.UpdateLaserWarning` owns the trigger.

- **The plane is half a unit over the island's stone cap**, which fixes the line's clearance at one unit: any less and the net is drawn inside the cap it is meant to hover over.
- **The trigger is geometry, read off the walk the loss is decided on.** `CheckLevelLost`'s scan over the live body poses tracks the minimum Y, and the warning is `lowest <= CEILING_DEATH_Y + LASER_WARN_STEPS x CEILING_DESCENT_PER_STEP`: the loss's own comparison, three descents early (the set's cadence is 4-16 shots per descent, most levels 6-9: roughly that many shots of noticing it). It is geometry and not a shot countdown because a level whose ceiling never steps still warns when attaching balls grow the cluster down to the line. `LASER_WARN_HYSTERESIS` is on the way out only, so a shot's shove cannot arm and stand down the net with every swing. An empty field's `MaxValue` stands it down for free.
- **A drop cinematic holds the level's endings, never the warning** (`CheckLevelLost`'s `mayLose`). The release that engages a cinematic is the one that rescues a low cluster, so a warning frozen behind the cinematic gate kept blazing across the player's reward for the whole dive. The walk and the arm/stand-down run on every frame of a built session; only `LoseLevel` and the budget test wait.
- **Everything time-driven is stamped in wall-clock seconds and evaluated in `Draw`, and that is load-bearing.** A lost level pushes the result screen over the session, the session stops running its rules (the covered frame is the world's alone, #241), and `Draw` keeps running (`DrawsUnderlying`). A fade advanced in an update would freeze at full brightness exactly as the player is told what it warned about. So `SetVisible` and `NoticeLevelEnded` stamp transitions, `Draw` evaluates them, and the linger expires in `Draw`. The linger is stamped in `ShowResultScreen`, the one funnel both endings come through, not where the level logically ends: a clear's page arrives `LEVEL_CLEARED_BEAT` later, plus a whole cinematic, and a linger stamped at the clear was spent before anyone saw it. Under a pause the net keeps pulsing, like the ball heartbeat.
- **The beams billboard about their own paths** (ShotTrail's trick), and here it is load-bearing: the play camera stands at Y -7.4, barely over the grid's plane at -8.0, so a flat ribbon in that plane is seen edge-on and vanishes. Each beam is a ribbon of quads along its path, every row turned about the path's direction at that row (the chord of its two neighbours, so a bend has no crack in it) to face the eye. That keeps the beam's width, 0.11, wherever the camera stands: from a camera exactly in the net's plane the whole net is one scan line, drawn as a bright line about that wide, and it does not vanish.
- **The net ends on a boundary through its four corners, and the boundary is a beam** (#941). `LaserNetShape` (`Prazsky.BS3D`) holds the shape in one copy: the corners stand at the last whole `BEAM_PITCH` within the half-footprint plus `FOOTPRINT_MARGIN` (`CornerFor`: 6 on an 11-wide stage, 7 on a 12, 9 on a 16 and a 17), a beam on every whole step from the centre to them, and each beam is a line of that lattice carried through `Place`, which bows it: each coordinate is stretched by `BOW` (-0.08) of itself times how far the other is from its corner, so the corners stay where they are and each side is drawn in in a parabola, most at its middle (`BOW` of the half-extent: 0.5 on an 11 across, 0.7 on a 17). The outermost beams are the images of the lattice's own edge, so they are the boundary, and every other beam stops exactly where it meets one (a T), with no tip past the edge it ends on. Flat: every vertex is on `PlaneY`, the bend is in plan only. A beam is `BEAM_SEGMENTS` (8) quads, the bow's sag over one of them a fraction of a beam's width; the widest net (17 x 17, 38 beams) is 740 vertices and 328 quads in the net's one draw, about a hundredth of the 16-bit index limit (`Fit` throws if a net ever passes it). The ends fade over `BEAM_FADE_LENGTH` (0.08), barely more than the beam is wide: a longer fade left a gap in every T and in the corner, where the outermost pair meet and the cone lands. `LaserNetShapeTests` hold, over the real path of every beam for every pair of stage sizes, that every end is on the boundary, nothing is past it or past the corner (plus the swell where `BOW` is positive) (seen to fail with the lines run a margin past the corners, as the old net's did), the outermost beams are the boundary and meet at the four points `LaserEmitterRing.NetCorner` names, and no lattice cell folds.
  - **A bow was chosen over a square frame** because the lines then read as thrown from the four corners rather than ruled; 0.08 is gentle enough that from the play camera, which sees the net nearly edge-on, the shape is a slight bend of its far edge, and plain enough from above to read as meant (0.15 as a swell looked like a cushion). **It is drawn in, by the owner's choice** (2026-10-23): the first build swelled out by +0.08, which covers the whole footprint; he preferred the same bow the other way, -0.08, a gentle pincushion, and that is what ships. The price is that the middle of a side stands up to `BOW` of its half-extent inside the straight line between its corners, so it can stand short of the footprint's edge where the corner is no further out than the footprint; computed from `CornerFor`, not measured in a capture. Keep the corners on the emitters' aim; change only `BOW`.
- **The red is the ceiling flash's own** (`CEILING_FLASH_COLOR`, passed to the constructor so the two cannot drift): the plate says "the glass stepped", the net says "and this is where it ends". The channel has to be far over 1 for the glare's sake: the bright pass keys on luminance and red's Rec. 709 weight is 0.2126, so a pure red needs about 2.6 to clear `GLARE_THRESHOLD`.
- ⚠ **The white core bleached the whole net at every peak.** The hot filament lifts the channels red lacks, and since the play camera sees the grid near edge-on the beams stack additively, so at a `CORE_WHITE` of 2.5 the net turned white at its loudest moment. At the shipped 0.9 the peak blooms red-pink and the trough stays pure laser red.
- **The pulse** is a raised cosine squared at `PULSE_HZ` (1.6) between `PULSE_FLOOR` (0.35) and 1: sharp peaks, wide troughs, an alarm's shape rather than a breath; the trough sits just under the glare threshold and the peak far over it, so the pulse breathes the bloom too.
- **The net spans the field footprint** (corners at `LaserNetShape.CornerFor`, which counts `FOOTPRINT_MARGIN`), which keeps it inside the drain's mouth: every authored footprint's half-diagonal is well under `ArenaIsland.FUNNEL_TOP_RADIUS`, so the beams never cross the gold bead at the rim, and the gun (whose orbit clears the footprint) never stands in them. One static buffer per level, rebuilt in `Fit` from `FitFieldToMap`, one draw call.
- ⚠ **Draw order is load-bearing: additive, `DepthRead`, `CullNone`, between the two glasses.** The net writes no depth, so the order has to encode the geometry: after `DrawSettingGlass` (the funnel is entirely below the net, and a drop cinematic looking down the throat has the net in front of the glass; drawn earlier, the cone wrongly dimmed it) and before the ceiling plate (which is above the net, so an overhead shot gets the plate composited over it). The gun's pane is drawn after everything ("The gun's hardware, in one copy" in `docs/testbed.md`).
- **`lasers` on the command line pins the net on** while a level is played, since reaching it honestly means playing to within three steps of losing, which can no more be scripted than clearing a level can.

## The laser emitters (#874)

The net does not appear from nothing: four emitters mounted inside the drain's funnel, one at each corner's bearing, project it ([where and what they are](../scenes/drain.md#the-laser-emitters-in-the-funnel-874)).

- **Standby.** Each lens carries a faint red ember (`LaserEmitters.STANDBY_GLOW`, well under the glare threshold) from the first frame of every level: a lamp that is off, saying that something is to be lit.
- **Armed.** The lens glows with the net's own brightness (`LaserGrid.IntensityAt`, the very figure the beams are drawn at: envelope, pulse and the kill flare), at `ARMED_GLOW` times it over the ember, as the renderer's `EmissiveTint` (which the glass's alpha does not dim). So the glass and the light leaving it are one light and pulse together; at the pulse's crest the lens is over the glare threshold and blooms.
- **Each emitter aims exactly at its corner**, per level (and stands at rest, straight up, until the opening reveal turns it there: [below](#the-net-is-shown-before-it-warns-933)): the housing turns on its pin until its axis runs through the net's corner, where its two outermost beams end and meet (`LaserGrid.CornerX`/`CornerZ` on `PlaneY`; a corner of the boundary, [above](#the-floor-alarm-the-laser-grid)), and `LaserEmitterClearanceTests` checks the axis meets it within a thousandth for every footprint from 5 to 17 a side, oblongs included.
- **The projection cones.** `LaserGrid.Fit` adds one soft cone from the centre of each lens (its crest) through its corner, in the same buffer and the same draw as the grid, so it carries the net's pulse, wave and flare and is lit only while the net is. A cone is `CONE_SECTIONS` quads of one axis, billboarded like the beams: it leaves the lens as thin as a beam and widens by `CONE_SPREAD` per unit (its half-width at the corner held between `CONE_CORNER_MIN_HALF_WIDTH` and `CONE_CORNER_MAX_HALF_WIDTH`), dims as it widens (radiance as the width ratio to `CONE_GAIN_POWER`, so the pencil at the lens is as hot as a beam, white core included, and the wide end a red haze), and lands on the corner half lit, dissolving `CONE_LANDING` of its width beyond it.
  - ⚠ **A cone's quad is a trapezoid, and its two triangles disagree about a bare across coordinate**: interpolated as -1..1 it draws a crease down every cone's diagonal. The vertex carries the across coordinate times the half-width and the half-width, and the pixel divides them; both are affine over the quad, so the quotient is exact (a beam's quotient is its old side, unchanged).
  - **Every vertex carries its own point, direction, half-width, gain and two fade lengths**, so the shader has no per-beam uniform: the beams are the cones' special case (constant width, gain 1, `BEAM_FADE_LENGTH` at both ends) and a bent beam is as much a ribbon as a straight cone. The rows of a strip share their vertices (13 floats each: position, tangent, side/along/half-width/gain, fades and the distance along the path for the wave).
  - **The geometry decides the length.** The lenses are just inside the mouth (12.1-12.8 from the axis, 0.3-0.9 over the rim) and the net's plane is 1.7 over the rim, so on an 11-wide net (corner 8.5 out) a cone runs 3.8 in and up at 21 degrees, on a 13-wide one (9.9) 2.5, on a 15-wide one (11.3) 1.4, and on a 16- or 17-wide one (12.7) the corner stands almost over its lens: the housing points nearly straight up (85 degrees) and the cone is 0.8.
- **The order.** The lenses are drawn after the drain's glass and before the net, translucent and **writing no depth**: the cone starts at the lens's crest, and a lens that wrote depth would hide the first hand's breadth of every cone behind its own dome.
- Potato draws the metal and the lens plainly through the same renderers, and the net and the cones through its own port of `LaserGrid.fx` (`GamePi/Shaders`, compiled and committed with `compile.ps1`; the vertex layout is the desktop's, so the two shaders change together). Neither the Help page nor the cards mention the emitters.

## The net is shown before it warns (#933)

The net is dark until the cluster is near the line, so a player meets the line's card (Saturn, "Keep the cluster above the red line") before ever seeing the line. Two things light the net for the telling, neither of them the alarm. `Game/Effects/NetReveal.cs` holds the clocks (plain floats, no device: `NetRevealTests`); `GameplayScreen.UpdateNetReveal` steps them every frame after `_tutorial.Update`, sets the emitters' turn and tells the grid what to burn at.

- **The reveal, on every level from the line's card on** (`Tutorial.NetIsShownAt`: the level the card is first taught on, counted from its chapter's end like the card, and every level after it in every chapter; none before it, none without a level set). The four housings start at rest (`LaserEmitterRing.RestAxis`, straight up: a lamp at attention, which lies in every bearing's plane and square to the pin) and turn about their pins to this level's aim over `SWING_SECONDS` (0.6, smoothstep; `AxisTurned`/`HousingTurned`, so the pin never moves and `Housing` is the turn's end bit for bit), the lenses charging to `CHARGE` as they go. Then the net rises over `LIGHT_RISE_SECONDS` with a flash (`LEVEL` 1.4 plus `FLASH` 0.8, settling over 0.4 s), burns until 1.55 s and goes out over 0.45: `TOTAL_SECONDS` is 2.0, under what a fast player would grudge. It burns above the alarm's own peak of 1 because a net shown to someone it is not warning is seen nearly edge-on against a bright sky, and the glare keys on the light's strength. Potato has no glare and the beams stacked edge-on whiten as it nears 1, so it is capped at `POTATO_NET_CAP` (0.6).
- **Never waited for.** The reveal runs on the session's own frame seconds: a pause holds it, a hitch advances it once, and it does not begin while the cluster is held for the chapter's tour or a camera takeover has the lens (a chapter's first level reveals after its tour). The first shot that leaves (`Skip`) finishes it at once: the housings land within a tenth of a second (`SKIP_SPEED`), the light goes out in `SKIP_FADE_SECONDS`, and a net not lit yet never is. A decided level skips it too, and a level built or left for the front end puts the housings back at their aim (`SetTurn(1)`), so a kept session never shows them half turned.
- **The blink, while the line's card is up** (`Tutorial.ShowsTheLine`: arriving or shown, not praised, not stepped aside): the net blinks at its real place, lit for `BLINK_ON_SECONDS` (0.75) of every `BLINK_PERIOD` (1.2) and dark between, slower than the alarm's 1.6 Hz pulse so it reads as a blink and not as a warning. It eases in and out over 0.25 s and goes with the card whatever took the card down (read out after 7 s, the Tutorial row off, a decided level), and returns whenever the card does (a replay with the Tutorial on, #715).
- **It is not the alarm.** `LaserGrid.SetPreview` is a second light the beams, the cones and the lenses are drawn at, the larger of it and the alarm's (`IntensityAt`); `Visible`, the trigger and its hysteresis, the contextual `line` card, the alarm sound and the HUD never read it. A preview not renewed within `PREVIEW_HOLD_SECONDS` (0.2) fades over 0.3 s: a result page stops the updates that renew it, and a light asked for as a level ended would otherwise burn under the page for ever.
- **It makes no sound.** The look is the cue; a hum would be a new sound to judge on the owner's monitor speakers.
- **Idle costs nothing.** `NetReveal.Net` is two comparisons when nothing is revealing or blinking; `SetTurn` rewrites the four housings' instance matrices in place (no allocation) and only when the turn changed; no draw call, shader or mesh is added, so GamePi and the Potato path draw it through the net's own effects.
- **Looking at it.** `play level=Smiley revealshot=0,0.3,0.6,0.9,1.3,1.8` saves the frame at those seconds of the reveal's own clock (`SessionTestOptions.RevealShotSeconds`): the clock is held at each for one frame, so a capture shows the stated moment however long the one before it took. Each says `[revealshot] +0.300 s of 2.00: turn 0.500, housing 0 at 68.4 deg, net 0.00, lens charge 0.35`: the housing's axis over the horizontal runs from 90.0 (rest) to 46.8 (a 15-wide net's aim) with the turn, and the net's light is 0 until the turn ends and 1.40 at the hold. Use a level in the middle of a chapter (a chapter's first level holds the reveal through its tour). `[net]` lines say whether a level reveals (`[net] level 12: the net is revealed at its start`), when the reveal begins and ends and when the card's blink goes up and down, on the clock `shot=` counts. Saturn's card needs a save that has taught the cards before it (`aim fire match lean ceiling line traverse walk combine` in `Progress.json`'s `lessons`), or those queue first.

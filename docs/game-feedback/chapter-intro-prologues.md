# Chapter intro prologues

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md). The flight itself is in [The chapter intro](chapter-intro.md); the remaining scenes' shots are in [the other scenes' shots](chapter-intro-scene-shots.md).

A scene that opens on a **prologue** plays cut-together `IntroShot`s (a path and a look) before the tour's last leg. `Effects/IntroPrologues.For(SceneKind, IntroContext)` picks the builder (`IntroContext`: the renderer, the city, the two plantings, the dome's sun, the wall clock now and to read later, and the pose the intro hands back at); `IntroPaths` holds the shared geometry. A declarative shot list was rejected: builders *search* their scene with reject-and-retry loops, which is logic, not data.

## What every prologue shares

- **A prologue is taken with a cut and skipped with one.** The blend cannot make a cut (it would glide the lens through the towers), so `Begin` sets it to 1 with a prologue and `TrySkip` drops it to 0.
- **After a prologue the tour flies only its last leg** (`ARRIVAL_SECONDS`, 4.5 s): the tour's scene stands lie on a circle round the arena that in a city runs through the towers. **Unless the last shot lands** (`IntroShot.Lands`, #972): a shot whose path and look end on the gameplay pose is followed by no tour leg; `ChapterIntro` holds its last frame while the gun's pose blends back in, a blend between two identical poses.
- **A shot may bank** (`IntroShot.RollAt`, #972): `ChapterIntro.Roll` reaches `RecoilCamera.BaseRoll` in play and in the menu's replay, which writes it every frame, level when no tour flies: written only while one flew, it kept the bank a session left on the camera when Pause and Main Menu were taken mid-swing. The tour itself stays level.
- **One concrete object per shot, an establishing view first.** `[intro]` and `[tour]` name the shots, e.g. `[intro] block 'The Eruption' (9/13), cut 'the flank' 3.2s | 'the crater' 5.4s | cut -> map 43 out at 46deg -> game pose, 4.5s; 13.1s in all`. Totals run 11-17 s, the cities about 20-22 (their swings home are 6.6-8.9 s and land; 22 or 22.5 when the old reel stands in), the meadow 22.9 (#893, its two orbits).
- **`IntroShot` moves without corners (#645, #1109)**: `Pose` extrapolates the look-ahead along the last leg (swapping in a direction swung the view in one frame); `At` is a Catmull-Rom (linear interpolation changed velocity at every point), whose missing neighbour past either end is extrapolated along the end leg: with the end point repeated in its place the lens left every cut at 69 % of the speed it had a frame later and slowed into the next the same way (`AShotKeepsItsSpeedThroughTheCut`). A shot that lands still ends exactly on its last point.

## The cities (#488, #780)

`Game/Effects/CityIntroShots.cs` builds four shots off the city being drawn (the streets lie ninety units under the island, out of any spline's reach). **Every path runs down a street's centre line**, which keeps it out of the towers. The two cities are two places (the owner, 2026-10-20) and get two reels, each ending on **the swing home** (below): the day city's street, plaza, rooftop (#972); the neon city's plaza, street, rooftop (#973), its street and plaza higher so its flat paint is not pointed out. Where no swing home is clear, either plays the old reel (street, plaza, swing, rooftop) and the tour's arrival.

- **The street**: 26 units up and 36 degrees down (the cars' and crossings' paint is flat and reads only from above), in the deepest of twelve rolled canyons. **The neon city's is 46 up and 24 down** (#973, the owner: "higher ... so that the details that the street level is actually 2D are hard to notice"): the frame's bottom edge meets the asphalt about 40 units ahead instead of 15.
- **The plaza**: an 80-degree orbit over an open block, rising 72 to 105 in the day city and 78 to 112 in the neon one, whose towers stand taller (#972: "we do not want a close view of the square ... the camera should only go higher"; #973; it was 32 to 54). ⚠ **From 16 units up the trees read as holes** (discs painted on the paving); from 30 up they are a canopy.
- **The swing**: round a tower corner into the cross street.
- **The rooftop** (#780): an 85-degree arc round one dressed roof (`CityRooftops.Showcases.Best`), which must stand over every roof within 26 units (else the lens looks down a canyon at walls). The old roof skim is the fallback.
  - ⚠ **The whole mast stays in frame (#840).** Looking at the mast's lower third from a fixed height, a tall mast's beacon ran off the top edge for most of the arc. `FrameMast` bounds the look by the beacon's top (its radius included), held 6 % of the frame's height under the top edge, and the mast's foot, held 15 % over the bottom one, from every point of the arc, and keeps the lower-third look wherever it fits; where no look fits, the lens climbs in half-unit steps until one does (a higher lens sees the mast foreshortened). The foot's margin is the wider because the skip hint stands centred on the bottom edge for the whole intro, up to 12.2 % of the frame's height, and the mast is centred across: at 6 % the foot stood behind the hint. The projection is Hor+, so the window's shape does not change this.
  - The look stays on the mast's own axis (`RoofShowcase.MastFoot`): a mast stands on the roof's middle **cell**, which on a 2×2 roof is off the roof's middle. A roof carries one mast at most, because the neon extras fill only the cells the day's dressing left (#850): where the day's put none, `Best(neon: true)` reports the extras' mast.
  - The beacon is not phased to the shot: the city's beacons flash in eight clusters on the wall clock, each with its own period (`BeaconPeriod` times 0.75 to 1.02, so at most 1.63 s), and a 5-second shot holds at least three flashes of the mast's own cluster wherever the clock stands (`CityRooftopsTests` counts them; a period over 5/3 s would break it).
- **The swing home** (#972, #973, `CitySwing`): the owner's "Spider-Man-style swinging fly-throughs until we finally arrive at the island and the camera gets into the game camera position". A staircase on the street grid (a street, a banked corner, the cross street, a corner the other way, and so on, then a last street towards the pose), then the release: a curve off the last street's line onto the pose, climbing out of the canyon past the floating island's underside and slowing to rest on the pose, the look easing onto the pose's own over the last 1.6 s. Lands, so no tour leg follows.
  - **Each city's own** (`CitySwing.Style`): the day city two corners, tops 80 over the street, sags up to 20, 28 u/s round a corner; the neon city three corners on 1-2-block legs through its narrower streets, tops 92, sags up to 22, 22 u/s round its tighter corners and a slower settle (`ReleaseTimeScale` 2.2 to 2.0).
  - **A pendulum.** The corners are the tops of the swings and the legs sag between them, the speed being the drop traded for it; the path is laid out by length and walked by that speed, so its points come out evenly spaced in time. The release decelerates on a cubic in time. ⚠ **A sag is held to 0.3 of its leg's length** (`DIP_PER_LENGTH`): the neon city's one-block legs sagging the full depth pitched the look 99 degrees a second.
  - ⚠ **Every sag is level at both ends.** Sine sags meet over a corner at an angle, and the look pitched 200 degrees a second over it. And ⚠ **the look follows the path's own direction, averaged over ±0.3 s, not a point ahead**: a chord to a point 0.35 s on cuts the corner, and short across the turn while the drop was not, it pitched the view 140 degrees a second. Averaged, the view turns into a corner before the lens does.
  - **How it arrives.** Along the pose's facing, halfway, or still along the street with the view swivelling onto the pose as it slows: a pose just inside the clearing's edge on an axis bearing can be reached only the last way (without it, 15 of 128 probed poses had no route). A release tighter than **twice** a corner's fillet is refused: a corner is the slow top of a swing, the release starts at a sag's fast bottom, and one as tight as a corner turned the view 170 degrees a second.
  - ⚠ **A level opens with the gun on an axis (bearing 90), and at least a quarter of the candidates come down a cross street** (`CROSS_STREET_SHARE`). Weighted only by the pose's facing, a pose on the axis was only ever approached along it, and in the neon city no street along it can curve onto a pose 30 out: its streets run 12.5 either side and the clearing ends 41 out. The Arcade's opening fell back to the old reel; the day city, swept on the exact axes, found a swing for 77 of 108 poses.
  - **Banked** in proportion to the heading's rate of turn (0.1 degree per degree a second), up to 14 degrees, smoothed, level by the landing.
  - **Found, not assumed**: 96 rolled candidates, each refused if any point comes within 2 units of a tower's box (cornices included, through `CitySwing.Towers`, the boxes by block) or the island; the most walled-in of the clear ones flies. `CitySwingTests` over three cities of each kind and 36 poses each, the axes exactly: a swing for at least 95 % (107 and 108 of 108 in the probe), landing exactly on the pose with the game's lens and no bank, never within 1 unit of a box at 1500 poses a shot, and the ride inside its limits (65 u/s, the view turning 170 and pitching 80 degrees a second, 14 degrees of bank, 8 u/s in the last half second, 5.5-9 s; measured: the day city 59 u/s, 155 and 51 deg/s, 6.8-7.8 s; the neon 51 u/s, 164 and 56 deg/s, 6.6-8.9 s). Each was seen to fail on a mutation.
- ⚠ **The order alternates what the lens sees** (street, plaza, swing, rooftop; 4.5, 4.0, 4.0, 5.0 s, or the roof skim's 5.5 s where no roof qualifies): two canyon shots in a row from slightly different places read as a glitch. `CityIntroShotsTests` builds the prologue over the real dressed roofs (`CityRooftops.ArrangeShowcases`, no device) and holds a minimum shot length, no equal consecutive names, the swing never next to the street, a minimum turn or travel at every cut, clearance from the real towers' boxes, and the rooftop's whole mast inside the frame, its foot over the skip hint, at 3840×1600, 1920×1080 and 1600×900.

## The volcano (#530) and the aurora (#531)

- **Volcano** (`VolcanoIntroShots`, through `SceneRenderer.VolcanoGroundHeight`, a mirror of `Volcano.fx`), the owner's order (#975): *the volcano* (3.6 s), a push in from 600 to 520 units off the cone's axis, 120 over the plain, 22-45 degrees round from the arena's side and drifting back 8, so the cone, its rivers and steam and the island stand in one frame; *the eruption* (3.8 s), a low push in at the cone's foot (310 to 270 out, 12 over the ground) 85-125 degrees round the other way, the look 40 over the vent; *the crater* (5.4 s), a run **beside the axis, never over it** (straight above a fixed look-at there is no horizontal forward to build an up vector from), lifted over the rim so the bowl opens late. Then the tour's last leg, 17.3 s in all.
  - ⚠ **The old first two shots were one picture.** The flank (an arc 129 out, lens on the vent) and the crater run both stood on the arena's side and looked at the vent: at the cut the lens moved 56 units and the view turned 18 degrees at best. `VolcanoIntroShotsTests` holds every cut to film's thirty-degree rule AND 100 units (the cities' test takes 22 degrees or 40 units); the new cuts turn at least 61 degrees over at least 248 units, and the old flank put back second fails it.
  - **The burst is cued, not hoped for.** A burst is on 4.5 s of every 19, jittered in its period, so a fixed shot met one about a third of the time, and the storm's stretching cannot reach events 9 to 30 s apart. `SceneRenderer.CueVolcanoBurst` puts one at full size 0.9 s into the eruption shot (`VolcanoLava.CueBurst`): the natural bursts that would start between the intro's first frame and the schedule's shortest gap (0.45 of the period, 8.55 s) after the cue give way, one already running is never cut, and the light, the jets, the deck's glow and the boom all read the one schedule (`VolcanoBurstCueTests`). ⚠ **The burst runs on the wall clock and the intro does not**: a pause stops the intro's clock and a hitch holds it to `MAX_INTRO_STEP` a frame, so cued only at the build the burst went off behind the pause page and the eruption shot then showed a quiet cone for the quiet's 8.55 s (found by the review of the first cut). The eruption shot cues it again on its first frame (`IntroShot.Start`, off `IntroContext.Clock`) whenever the intro has fallen more than 0.1 s behind (`ChapterIntroShotStartTests`).
  - The weather fades in from the menu's over `WEATHER_FADE_SECONDS` (8 s, deliberate), so the first two shots play under the previous scene's clouds.
- **Aurora** (`AuroraIntroShots`, off `ForestScatterRenderer.Scatter`'s instance arrays, re-authored in #963): *the wood* (3.4 s) is *found*, the best of 600 rolled runs heading within 35 degrees of the curtains' bearing, measured against every conifer and snag (crown plus 1.4), trunks on both hands weighted (⚠ a bare count chose a grove's edge), inside the far stand's ring (⚠ its spires are not in the planting, and a run among them flew through one: a black frame); *the moon* (3.2 s) glides 1.6 over the open snow beside the island towards the Moon, the lens 7 degrees under it, the wood's shadows running at the lens; *the curtains* (3.4 s) drift 2.2 over the snow behind the island from them, the lens 14 degrees up, on the spot with the fewest trunks round it (clear by seven: ⚠ four put a crown on the lens). Then the tour's last leg, 14.5 s in all. Heights come from `SceneRenderer.AuroraGroundHeight` (the snow as drawn, its drifts included).

## Smoothness of the terrain-hugging shots (#645, #1109, #1112)

A lens over rough ground rides like drone footage: its height and its tilt change over a second, never from one frame to the next. `Tests/BS3D.Tests/IntroShotSmoothnessTests.cs` builds every shot whose height `AridIntroPaths.Hug` lays (the desert's erg, pyramids, crest and flock; the mountains' range, pass and summit; the outback's plain, approach and crown; the volcano's crater) on the shipped configs over twelve rolls, samples it at 60 fps on its own eased clock, and holds **peaks**, since a judder is one bad frame in a hundred good ones:

| limit | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| view pitch rate | 20 °/s | a drone gimbal's tilt |
| view pitch acceleration | 40 °/s² | that tilt reached in no less than half a second |
| vertical acceleration | 20 u/s² | the climb rate changes over a second, not a frame |
| vertical jerk | 150 u/s³ | the acceleration changes by at most 2.5 u/s² a frame |
| yaw rate | 50 °/s | |
| speed (crest, pass, crater) | 28 u/s | "too fast" at 30-59 (#645) |
| clearance | the shot's own, every frame | smooth through a dune is not a fix |
| mean height over the clearance, a shot with no floor | 9 | smooth by flying high is not a fix |

The derivatives are taken from three frames inside each end: a cut is a discontinuity of everything (its speed has its own test, above). The worst now: 13 u/s² (the outback's crown, a crane), 122 u/s³ (the crater), 35 °/s² (the pass), 18.9 °/s (the crown), 8.8 over the clearance (the pass, on its worst roll). The highest frame is not held: the lift over a cut crest is a plateau whose height is the crest's cut however wide it is, so a bound on it fails only where the mean already does. A designed floor is held by its own test: a straight climb and a level height are flown exactly as laid, a crane lands on its foot and its top, and the real crown ends on `CRANE_OVER_CROWN` on every roll.

- **Height** (`AridIntroPaths.Hug`): the clearance over the highest ground at each point and on all four hands of it (or the shot's floor, a designed crane, fixed height or the crater's ramp, where higher), **rounded** by a Gaussian over `ROUNDING_SECONDS` (0.7 s) of travel at the shot's peak speed (`IntroShot.PEAK_PACE`, so a fast run is rounded over as much time as a slow one), then **lifted** where the rounding cut a crest under the ground's clearance: by the deepest cut within three sigmas, averaged by a Gaussian of `LIFT_SECONDS` (0.4 s) over three more, so every point keeps its clearance by construction and the lift is a swell, not a step. A box has a corner at each end, and the lens's vertical acceleration jumped at every one; a Gaussian keeps a straight climb and a level height exactly as they are.
  - **A crane's smoothstep is rounded, not kept**: the lens flies nearer a straight climb, over the smoothstep in its lower half and under it in its upper half by about a fourteenth of the rise on a 3.2 s shot (the erg 1.7 units of its 24, the outback crown 4.5 of its 64), with its foot and its top where they were laid. ⚠ What the rounding takes off a floor's own curve is **not lifted back**: the lift holds a cut over its reach, so lifting a crane's upper half back carries its top over the design (the crown 4.5 units over `CRANE_OVER_CROWN` on every roll, the erg 1.7). Flown exactly as designed instead, the crown's smoothstep on the eased clock peaks at 30 u/s², over the limit. The crater's ramp is rounded the same way and passes at most 0.3 under itself.
  - ⚠ **The lift is a plateau, and on a low run it is most of the shot.** It is full at the crest and tapers to nothing six `LIFT_SECONDS` either side, in travel at peak pace: the lens starts to rise that long before a crest it was cut under. One roll of the outback's approach (clearance 2.4) passes a 3-unit outcrop near its end: the lens rises from a sixth of the way in and ends 5.2 over its clearance, where the other eleven rolls end within 0.6 of it. The pyramids' run (clearance 7) flies 5.8 over it on average on its worst roll and up to 12.1 at single moments (14.5 over the sand at the cut on one roll, twice its clearance).
  - ⚠ **The heights are carried on past both ends** for the Gaussian's reach. A window that stops at the ends kinks where it first fits whole, a quarter to half a second after the cut: it was the worst vertical acceleration of seven of the eleven shots. Open ground is read on along the plan's end directions; over a floor the heights are turned over their end point (2 h(end) - h(end ∓ k)), which keeps a straight climb straight and a crane's foot and top where they were laid (read on, the level plain beside the outback crane's foot rounded the foot four units up).
  - `rise` makes a run climb ahead of the ground: the volcano's crater run rounds the highest ground within 1.2 s of travel, which keeps it as high over the flank as it was approved (#530).
  - The cost of the two constants: a narrower lift (0.25-0.3 s) hardly lowers the lens and puts the jerk back over its limit; a wider rounding flies higher (2 s put the pass 11.8 over its clearance).
- **Pitch**: a lens looking along its travel tips into every slip face; `IntroShot`'s `pitchDamping` (0-1, default 0) levels the look (0.6 crest, 0.4 pass). A longer look-ahead buys little: 40 units for the pass's 24 took its peak tilt rate from 17.7 to 15.7 °/s. The other shots look at a fixed point.
- **Speed**: the pass flies 85 units in 4.2 s, the crest 72 in 4.0, the crater 5.4 s. The pyramids' run (38 u/s) and the outback's approach (34) are faster: their subject is far off or ahead, not under the lens.

Not measured: the `IntroGround` shots (their own paths; `pitchDamping` applies there too).

# A cut is made

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

Using the Cut is staged as an event (#767): the press, the flight, the severing and the fall, each seen, heard and felt, and every beat saying what the cut did. A cut through a storey with nothing hanging on it is still an event (the severing alone); a bigger result adds beats on top. ⚠ **Nothing of it slows the world** (#1046, the owner's ruling: "the game always runs in real time; the slowing makes no sense and players do not like it"): there is no hit-stop and no slow fall, and none may come back. The rules of the Cut itself (the storey, the lower half, the preview) are in [Power-ups](../game-session/powerups.md#cut-the-cutter-round).

The pieces: `Game/Effects/CutStaging.cs` holds the session's state of the beats, `CutBeats` the curves as pure functions of each beat's age, `CutRumble` what the pad is asked, `Blasts`' second style the strike's picture (its slots' ages and light in `BlastSlots`, which has no device), `CutSynth` the two sounds. The design came from references drawn with both models (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\767-zimage`, `767-klein`, prompts in `prompts-767-cut-staging.json`): a long white-hot blade line through the cluster, a grinder's fan of sparks, the line cooling into amber fragments, and the falling part's cut face glowing white-hot.

## The beats

| Beat | Clock | Picture | Sound | Hand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Press** (R) | wall | The saw spins up from rest to `CUTTER_SPIN_FLYING` over `CHARGE_SECONDS` (0.42 s) and eases back to the loaded idle over `CHARGE_SETTLE_SECONDS`; the round's light surges to `1 + CHARGE_GLOW` (1.6) of its accent; the collar flares (`CUT_CHARGE_COLLAR_FLARE`), the rays flare and reach further; the Cut chip flares white-hot into its spent dim | The **charge**, from the muzzle | A hold rising under the charge, mostly the triggers (0.45), a little of the heavy motor (0.22), then a click on both triggers (0.7) at the top |
| **Flight** | wall | The lens pinches by `PINCH` (5 %, in 0.04 s, out 0.38 s); what the round will cut off stays lit while it flies | The shot's own: the flight is a few hundredths of a second, so the slice's zing is the blade arriving | The shot's own |
| **Severing** | world | `Blasts.Cut`: a white-hot flash, the **slash**, a grinder's sparks, a white-hot light on the balls round it; the camera heaves (`CUT_RUMBLE` + `CUT_RUMBLE_GROWTH` × size); the award waits half the slash's life (`AwardZoom.CUT_WAIT`, 0.9 s), so it zooms in over a slash already cooling; a cut that empties the field with no drop cinematic over it is born at once (`PlayHud.BirthWaitingAwards`): its wait, read and flight (0.9 + 1.75 s for the biggest award) would not fit the 2.5 s cleared beat | The **slice**, from the struck point, in place of the landing's note | One knock, raised **to** 0.45 heavy / 0.65 light (never added onto the shot's thump), 0.8 on both triggers |
| **Fall** | world | The freed part falls at the world's full speed; its cut face glows white-hot and cools as it falls (`FALL_GLOW` × `HeatAtDepth`, the storey cut at full, a storey down under half); the storey left above the cut glows too (`FACE_GLOW`) | The release's pops for the count | The release's roll-off along its own curve under its ceiling (#810), for every ball the cut let go, the storey included |

- **One size for every beat**: `CutBeats.Size` is the release rumble's logarithm (`LandingRumble.Size`), so the pad and the effect grow along one curve and cannot drift apart: the effect, the sound and the hand grow with everything freed.
- **A cut that engages the drop cinematic** is followed down by it at full speed, as every release is (#877). The world and the wall clock run together; the beats keep the two names because the strike's picture and glows belong to what the simulation moves, and `RealTimeSourceTests` refuses any world time scale anywhere in the Game's sources.

## The slash

The strike's figure is `Blast.fx`'s **blade style** (`BlastShape.z` 1, `.w` the half-length, `BlastAxis` the line and where its middle is; GamePi's port packs all three into `BlastData`), in the blasts' one buffer and one draw, so the next power-up round's impact is one more style of the same thing. Its figures are `CutBeats`' severing constants, which both `Blast.fx` files repeat and a test reads back. ⚠ It is sized to be seen: at half its thickness, half its life and half its sparks it reads as a faint line and a few glints.

- **Where it runs** (`CutSlash`): the cut takes the struck ball's storey, a horizontal layer, and the blade comes in along the shot, so the line is the storey's plane met by the plane square to the shot's level heading: **level, through the struck ball's centre, square to the shot's XZ direction** (`BallLanding.ShotDirection`: the velocity the striking step began with, the line the sweep landed the cutter along, before that step's contacts turn it towards a ball's flank; the gun's aim if unknown), facing the player who fired along it. It spans the destroyed storey's balls projected onto that line, a ball (`OVERRUN`) past each end, never less than `MIN_HALF_LENGTH` (2 units) either side of the strike, so a storey of one is still a cut; its middle need not be the struck ball. It shoots out from the struck ball to both ends over about a tenth of a second.
- ⚠ **Square to the shot, never to the lens**: the overview's lens does not turn with the barrel, so a slash square to the lens is square to the shot only with the gun straight ahead, and with it yawed runs off the storey to one side.
- ⚠ **Only its depth is pulled towards the lens** (half its reach, at most 4 units, never past half the way): drawn where it is, it stands exactly on the storey on screen, and depth-tested as if in front of the storey's own balls (released, still in place for the first frames), which otherwise hide all but a few glints. Moved along the line to the lens instead, it lies off the storey.
- **Its life and size**: `SLASH_SECONDS` 1.8 s and `SLASH_HALF_THICK` 1.5; it holds white-hot, thins, cools to amber (the cutter's `CutterEmber`) and burns through into torn lengths. A static noise along it decides where it parts, never a pattern sliding along it. No ring and no disc (#389's lesson, #640).
- **The flash** is nearly a bomb's size and lasts `FLASH_SECONDS` 0.3 s, cubed as it fades: under a tenth of its light after 0.16 s, so the one part that can hide the field is gone almost at once.
- **The sparks are a grinder's**: twice as many as a bomb's (part 3 is the blade's extra 96, the buffer's second run, drawn only while a blade burns), each coming off the slash where its baked place puts it (thickest at the struck ball) once the slash has shot out that far, thrown along the line and towards the gun (never back into the cluster, which hid them), flat and lifted a little, white-hot cooling to amber, twice as bright and 2.2 times as big as a blast's, and living `SPARK_SECONDS` 1.25 s jittered from 0.55 to 1.45 of it, holding their light most of the way: seven in ten live a second or more.
- **Its light** is the blasts' one `SceneLights` slot, white-hot, never under half strength however little the cut freed, lasting `LIGHT_SECONDS` 1.0 s (a bomb's 0.45).
- **Skippable**: a shot that leaves while it burns plays the rest of every blade's picture `HURRY_RATE` (6) times as fast (`Blasts.HurryBlades`, `[skip] cut's slash hurried`), so it never stands in the way of the next shot: the slash thins and cools, the sparks run out and the light fades through every age they would have passed, all of it gone within a third of a second (`CutBeats.HurriedSecondsLeft`) and nothing in one frame. ⚠ A hurry that jumped the picture to its last stretch put the light out and turned a full white-hot slash thin amber in one frame. A refused pull hurries nothing: nothing crosses the field.
- Its sound and its jolt are not `Blasts.Update`'s: the session plays the slice and the knock on the strike's own landing.

## Rules it keeps

- **Never a rule.** `Activate` still never calls the scorer or feeds the ceiling's cadence; the strike is scored as it always was. Nothing eats an input, and the press holds nothing back: the round can be fired at any moment of it.
- **The same at any frame rate.** Every beat is a closed-form function of its age, and the saw's angle is the closed-form integral of its speed (`CutBeats.SpinOffset`): ⚠ changing the speed inside `clock × speed` makes the blade leap (#766's note). `CutStagingSessionTests` run the session's own `CutStaging` on a real cut of a hung slab, stepped as the game steps it (the wall clock's beats, then the world's), at 30, 60, 75 and 144 Hz against a 2 kHz reference: the faces' heat, and the blasts' own slots (`BlastSlots`, whose ages the draw reads) putting the blade's light out and freeing its slot at its own life, agree to a frame's rounding, as does a hurried picture's end at 30, 60 and 144 Hz, and a stepping that counts frames instead of seconds is seen to fail the same checks. ⚠ **The charge never spins the saw past `CUTTER_SPIN_FLYING`**: faster, the teeth alias at 30 Hz, the strobe #766 had to fix. A pause stops every clock feeding the beats.
- **The charge is the round's.** Its glow and its spin are drawn on the loaded cutter wherever it sits, so a Swap that moves it back a slot takes them with it while its sound and its hold in the hand go on; ⚠ applied at the muzzle alone, a swapped cutter leapt by the whole spin offset each time it left the muzzle and came back. The rays and the collar are the muzzle's, and flare only while the cutter is there.
- **The preview hands over to the heat.** The balls the preview lit stay lit while the cutter flies (`CutterInFlight`), and at the strike the heat takes over the same balls on the same frame; the face's and the fall's glow ride the preview's channel (`PhysicsBall.CutPreviewGlow`), which the ceiling's alarm wave still outranks.
- **Every tier draws every layer**: one more slot of the blasts' one draw, the light the blasts already use and two glows through the ripple's existing channel, no new pass. The Potato path draws the same through GamePi's `Blast.fx` port; it has no rays, as before.

## Looking at it

- `powerups=cut:1 cut=<t> fire=<t>` with an `aim=` that strikes where wanted: on Teapot, `aim=2:26:0` cuts the bottom storey (16 destroyed, nothing freed) and `aim=2:29:0` frees 51; on Elephant `aim=2:24:0` frees 56 (`level=Elephant aim=2:24:0 powerups=cut:1 cut=4 fire=8`). Neither is the quarter of its level the drop cinematic asks for since #1011, so the gun keeps the lens.
- ⚠ **`shot=` cannot frame a strike**: the flight is a few frames, and a capture at 1920×1080 costs about half a second, which moves every later frame. **`cutshot=<s1,...>`** saves the frames at those seconds after a cutter strikes (`[cutshot] +<s> s after the strike`).
- Each use prints `[cut] press: ...` (the pad's hold and click) and `[cut] strike: ...` (destroyed, orphaned, size, the slash's reach, middle and line, the shot's direction, the knock and the fall's rumble, and whether the drop cinematic has the lens). On Elephant `aim=2:24:14` cuts with the gun yawed, the slash running across the shot and off the strike to one side.

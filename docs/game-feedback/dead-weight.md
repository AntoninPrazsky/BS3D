# Dead weight: a released ball that never fell

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

A group the cluster has let go of usually falls into the drain. On some map shapes it does not: the balls wedge against their neighbours and stop, and from the 3D view they are indistinguishable from live cluster, so a player keeps shooting at colours that can no longer complete anything. The mark below is what says so (#342, remarked #620).

## What it looks like

- **The ball's own colour and material with pixels dithered out of it, and its heartbeat stopped.** A dead ball goes out through its own colour's bucket (the level's vinyl, glass or marble, in the colour the player shot) with the shader's dither dissolve turned up on its instance to `BallRenderSet.DEAD_DISSOLVE`: about half its display pixels are cut away and what is behind shows through. It costs nothing: no extra bucket region, draw or draw-order change. Its shadow thins with it, since the sun's depth caster cuts the same dither (#920), where it used to cast a whole ball. It does not breathe (the released ball's still plane, #473). On the Pi's Potato tier a dithered ball is a draw of its own with a `clip()` (`PotatoBallDither`, "The Potato path" in `docs/rendering.md`), which is why those draws are kept to the few that need them.
- ⚠ **It is the third mark, and each of the first two was rejected in play.** A cool ash grey (#342) reads as stone, because a grey opaque ball among coloured ones is what a rock is. A weakly transparent shell (#412, the glass's two-wall alpha pass) reads as the clear glass special: the owner took dead balls for specials and could not say what they did. Both took away the ball's colour or material, so both collided with a ball defined by lacking one. Taking away pixels collides with nothing: no special is drawn with holes.
- **It is not the landing preview's ghost's look**: the ghost is never still and a whole, undithered ball only at the top of its beat (`GhostBeat`: up to a whole ball and down to `LOW` of one, `HZ` beats a second) in the aim cell in the loaded colour, while a dead ball is full-sized, dithered, steady, and stands where the cluster is. If the two are ever confused in play, `DEAD_DISSOLVE` is the one dial. The dither is the ghost's own earlier language, "a ball that is not really there".
- **The trade is contrast against the same colour**: the cut pixels show what is behind, so a dead red ball in a red pyramid is a mottle. At 0.42 the balls sank into a same-coloured field, at 0.5 they read as coloured balls with grain. How it reads as a few dead balls among live ones from the overview is the owner's to judge in play.
- **It eases in and out**: the instance's dissolve is `DeadWeight x DEAD_DISSOLVE`, one ramp over `DEAD_WEIGHT_FADE_SECONDS` read in both directions, so a ball knocked loose again by a later landing decays on the same ramp backwards.

## What qualifies

- A released ball has to be under `DEAD_WEIGHT_SPEED` for `DEAD_WEIGHT_HOLD_SECONDS`, long enough that a group merely slow at the top of its fall never flashes it, **and** it has to be resting above the field's own floor, which the session passes in (`_clusterWorldOffset.Y`). The second test is what keeps it honest: a normal release drains without a mark because those balls come to rest on the island's stone or in the funnel, below the field, on their way out. A caller that passes no floor (the Testbed, the editor) marks nothing.
- **The state is the ball's** (`PhysicsBall.StillSeconds`, `PhysicsBall.DeadWeight`), advanced by `ClusterCollector`'s single per-frame walk beside the occlusion ease, the arrival glide and the ripple, since each ball is visited exactly once. It reads the body's velocity, not a pose difference, because a resting body the solver still jitters reads as moving in a pose difference and as nearly zero in the velocity the threshold is set against.
- **A shot passes through dead weight** (#410), as through anything a landing has released: the loose mark the contact handler puts on released balls stays on a ball that comes to rest. That is the honest reading of the landing preview, which solves against the hanging structure only and already draws its ghost behind a dead ball; a shot that bounced off one would break the ghost's promise.
## How it is taught

- **The tutorial's card, once per player** (#1023, "The dead weight's card" in [the tutorial](tutorial.md)): the first ordinary ball to wear the mark in full fires *"This ball is already shot down"* / *"It just hasn’t fallen yet"*, with a ball drawn beside it as the map draws this one, in its colour.
- **The Help page's Odd balls tab**, after the two kinds the cannon holds, under a line of its own: *Shot down*, with the same dithered ball in its square (`OddBallCopy.DeadName`/`DeadText`, `HelpPage.CollectBalls`). It is not a kind, so it is not an entry of the kinds' table.

## Making one in a scripted run

The Testbed marks none, and the Game's aim cannot be scripted, but a blast can be: `detonate=` sets off a bomb at a wall-clock second. A level built for it gives a real stuck group on every run. A 9 x 9 field (`l` 12) holds a 7 x 7 plate on its layout's lowest level, held up by four pillars at the plate's corners. Above the plate's middle, a 3 x 3 group two levels up hangs from the glass only through a bomb: glass, anchor, bomb, link, group. Played as `play level=110 levelfile=<it> tutorial detonate=22 powerups=swap:0,brake:0,cut:0 userdata=<dir>`, the blast throws the group and the chain onto the plate and they come to rest there. The card fires about 5 s later. The level's place past the second chapter keeps the ladder's cards out, and `powerups=` zero keeps the tools' cards out, since the Swap's action card would stand up to 22 s in front of it. Seen from the gun, the plate hides most of what lies on it, so the 2D cluster profile shows the stuck balls better.

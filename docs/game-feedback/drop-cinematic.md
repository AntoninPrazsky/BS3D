# The drop cinematic

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

The reward for a good shot: the camera lets go of the gun and follows the wreckage down the fall, into the glass drain and, where the scene allows, out under the island. `Game/Effects/DropCinematic.cs` owns the camera; the trigger's rule and dials are `Prazsky.BS3D.DropTrigger`'s (one copy: game, tests, `LevelGen --drops`); `GameplayScreen` owns the call, the subject, `LevelRun.BiggestDrop` and the gun not answering.

## When it fires

- **The bar is a record, not a count.** A drop fires it when it beats the level's biggest so far by `DropTrigger.MustBeatBestBy` (2x) and is at least `MIN_BALLS` (12) and `MIN_SHARE_OF_LEVEL` (9 %) of the balls the level started with, or when it clears the level. A flat count cannot survive a new level (Pinwheel drops 102, Mosaic's best is 24), and a per-level figure is a dial to get wrong. The first qualifying drop always fires.
- **Measured on the lattice** (`LevelGen --drops`): about one a level before the clearing shot; the old 1.25x margin gave about two, too often (#615).
- **Every release raises the record**, even ones the gates refuse; the `[cinematic]` line prints the bar it beat. **A blast counts** (#326): `BallsReleased.Total` has a third category, balls a bomb destroyed by geometry, and the bar and `Audio.PlayRelease` read the total.
- **The clearing shot always gets it (#424)**: `GetRemovableBallsCount() == 0` (the count `CheckLevelCleared` reads a line later) and something fell; the trigger runs *before* `CheckLevelCleared`. The line says `cleared the level`. Other gates stand.
- **Both level endings wait for it** (`CheckLevelLost`, the cleared countdown).
- **Settings, Drop camera (#290)**, on by default, is read only in `TryBeginDropCinematic` (a running one finishes; `BiggestDrop` moves either way).

## The camera

- **A pose and a blend, not a mode and not a clock.** It holds no camera, simulation, input or time scale; `UpdateCamera` Lerps the gameplay pose into its pose, a **second** Lerp stacked on precise aim's: at a blend of 0 the gameplay pose returns bit for bit, so an ended or skipped cinematic cannot strand the camera (`BLEND_IN_TAU` 0.09, `BLEND_OUT_TAU` 0.16).
- **The arc follows the balls, not a stopwatch**: each framing parameter is a three-key curve over how far the subject has fallen, against three anchors (release height, the island's stone, the drain's throat). **Each leg and the dive and submerge pulls are held at their furthest (#616)**: a subject whose lowest balls are culled first *rises*, and the arc stepped back.
- **The subject is a weighted focus and the drain wins whenever anything is in it (#616, `Prazsky.BS3D.DropFocus`).** The plain mean hopped at every cull and framed a split group over the throat's wall. Each ball's weight fades continuously (over `RIM_FADE` past the arris; for a ball outside the mouth with depth, `OFF_STONE_REACH`/`OFF_STONE_DEPTH`; over the last `KILL_FADE` above the kill plane); a zero-weight ball off the stone is a latched **stray**; the whole group frames the shot only when no funnel weight is left (`FunnelShare`); the drain emptying **ends** it. `DropFocusTests` requires the focus to stay steady, with the plain mean required to *fail*.
- **The shot ends when the drop stops being one**: `Stalled` (under a unit of descent over `STALL_SECONDS`, 0.9 s of game time, a frame counting at most `STALL_FRAME_MAX`, 0.1 s: a hitched frame has simulated far less than it lasted, and a capture's half second ended a shot on its own). `MAX_SECONDS` (6.5 s) is the backstop: a pile can rest near the mouth, and the game culls only at the kill plane.
- **The subject is held by body handle, not list index** (indices shift as the kill plane removes balls). Bepu recycles handles; safe **only here**, since nothing is added to the simulation during a cinematic.
- **Variety is rolled per shot.** A third stay level (a tilt every time reads as a broken horizon); the swing never exceeds a quarter turn from the player's bearing. `RecoilCamera.BaseRoll` carries the tilt, added to the shake's roll.
- **Two `[cinematic]` lines per shot**, ASCII and `InvariantCulture` (an arrow came back as `r` on a Czech console): the roll when it opens, and `ended after <s> s (<why>), world time scale <min> at the slowest` when it lets go. `<why>` is `the subject was gone`, `the drop stalled`, `the backstop` or `skipped`; the scale is the slowest the world ran while the cinematic had the lens, **1.00** by construction.

## The clock: ordinary game time, no slow motion

- **The world runs at 1 while the cinematic has the lens (#877).** The owner: a falling cluster in slow motion under a moved camera reads as a replay, not as the player's own success. `GameplayScreen.WorldTimeScale` is `1` while `Engaged` and the Cut's (`CutStaging.TimeScale`) otherwise; `DropCinematic` has no time scale, no slow-motion roll and no ramp. `DropCinematicClockSourceTests` holds that, and the `ended` line prints the slowest scale the world ran.
- **The Cut keeps its own slow motion** (#767), on the play camera; a cut that engages the cinematic (a big release) gets none, so its fall runs at game time like any other drop's. `[cut] strike` says `slow motion: the drop cinematic's` there, meaning the cinematic's clock, which is 1.
- **What the length is without it** (measured with `detonate=2.5`, `level=Paroxysm` 64 balls and `level=Sill` 117, four seeds): every shot used to end on the 6.5 s backstop, the world having covered only about 2.5 s at 0.34 to 0.43; at game time it ends when the drop does, `the drop stalled` after 4.3 to 5.7 s, and a seed that holds on a pile still ends on the backstop. The dials were left where they were: `STALL_SECONDS` was always measured in game seconds (the old figure was 2 to 3 wall seconds of slow motion), the follow (`Exp(-elapsed / 0.10)`) stays 1.5 to 2 units behind a falling ball at full speed, and the tilt (`ROLL_PERIOD`), the orbit and the skip hint (1 s) all fit inside the shortest shot.
- **Everything else on the clock is unchanged**: the step is the fixed 1/120 s, the blasts and the wormhole step on the world's time, the ceiling's descent rides the steps and is held for the takeover (`CeilingDescent.Update`).
- ⚠ **Render interpolation stays**: a fixed step lands only every few rendered frames whenever the world runs slower than its step (the Cut's slow motion) and, above 120 Hz, even at game time; before #293 half of such frames moved the subject 0. Every drawn ball interpolates between the pose before the last step and the live one (`PhysicsBall.InterpolatedPose`, `_renderAlpha`) and `TryGetDropCentre` feeds the camera the same read. The drawn world runs up to one step behind the simulation, which nothing reads; a *placed* body resets its history so no blend straddles a teleport.

## Where the lens can go

**`SceneRenderer.OpenBelow` is the exact complement of `IsSolidTerrainScene`**, not a second list: the question is what is opaque under the island, and the whole shot is bounded by keeping the balls on screen.

- **Open scenes** (the cities, the sea, space, the storm): the glass is translucent, so balls read through the cone and pour out, the shot looking up past the island ("The drain funnel" in `docs/scenes.md`).
- **The ten solid-terrain scenes** have a near-black pit shaft hugging the glass; no vantage below sees a ball (#75). Their shots climb (elevation +48...72 degrees against -38...-18) and end looking down the throat. A lens above the mouth sees a ball below only through it, so it must lie in the cone from the ball up through the rim; the platform is solid (`PushOutOfIsland`, the last resort). ⚠ A per-scene floor on lens Y alone put the camera inside the stone.
- ⚠ **In open scenes the cone and push-out caused "gets too close, then jumps back" (#407)** (reach 27.7 to 12.7 in eight frames, a 15.9 jump, a 5.5 snap). So the lens goes **round the outside of the drum** and the cone is skipped there.
- **One continuous curve past the rim, clear of it by construction (#878).** A path that is a function of the subject's height with a switch in it hops: the lens was shoved 9 units sideways in two frames as the subject crossed the island's top, then dived 90 units a second and stopped dead (acceleration 4000 units/s^2 at the median roll, 18000 at the worst). Three terms made it, all in the open scenes' path, and each is smooth now:
  - **No switch on the subject.** The **radius** is lifted until the lens stands `RIM_REACH` (34.5: the stone's 26, the clearance and a margin of 7) from the drain's axis whatever its elevation (`SoftMax`, so the corner is a bend; the pivot's own off-axis offset is added, so the worst azimuth is clear). The shot is wider for the smaller rolls (up to 43 units out at the dive's -36 degrees), and the margin keeps the drum's blank wall from filling the frame as the lens goes past it (at 2.5 units off it did). `KeepOutsideRim` stays on, never gated by the subject, as the net (its weight eased with `Smooth`): it has nothing to move, and `PushOutOfIsland` is the last resort behind it. ⚠ A floor above the top face that is replaced by the rim's push below it is the switch; do not bring it back.
  - **The dive is unhurried.** `StoneDivePull` takes the elevation to the OUT leg's angle over `STONE_DIVE_DEPTH` (12 units of the subject's descent, about a second) with a quintic ease (`Smoother`: acceleration continuous at both ends); the lens is under the drum when the subject is 3 to 4 units into the throat. Over 3 units it was the plunge. A group that lingers in the mouth sinks at a third of a falling ball's speed, so the dive is also on a clock: the stronger of the depth's ease and one over `STONE_DIVE_SECONDS` (1.1 s) from the moment the subject went under the top face, or the lens would stand at the wall for as long as the group lingered (`A_lingering_group_...` test).
  - **No corner in the progress.** The three legs summed ran at a different rate on each side of the island's top and the throat's foot. In an open scene `SmoothProgress` is one monotone cubic through the same anchors (the slope at each a harmonic mean of the legs'); the closed scenes keep the sum.
  - `DropCinematicPathTests` flies the real cinematic against a falling subject (40 rolls, seven open scenes, 60 and 144 Hz) and holds the lens's second difference under 800 units/s^2 (the worst roll is about 450, peak speed 94), holds the lens clear of the rim, and holds a closed scene's path to samples taken before the change. The solid scenes' own path (2000 to 3800 units/s^2 at the same rolls: the progress's corners and the cone's) is untouched.
- ⚠ **The sea's underwater murk** (`FinishSceneDraw` ramps it by how far the lens is under `SceneRenderer.SeaLevelY`) **hid the balls from the camera (#193)**: the fall leg looks down, so a subject that sank early left the lens above a surface the fade was closing over the cluster. `DropCinematic.SubmergeElevationPull` pulls the **elevation** (clamping Y would leave X/Z at the wide fall radius) towards the OUT leg's diving angle over `SUBMERGE_PULL_DEPTH` (8 units, the same ease), as the **stronger** of it and the stone's dive, not a second pull on top (the island's top is above the water, so both ran at once and the lens plunged at twice the speed); it gates on the sea (`SceneRenderer.LensSubmergedAmount`), the cities having no surface.

## Skip

- **Skip is LMB / Space / gamepad A or right trigger; Escape stays pause** (one key must not mean two things). The trigger is spent like a shot's pull, so the pull that fired the triggering shot cannot skip it.
- **`SKIP_LOCKOUT` (0.3 s)**: the triggering shot was fired with the very button that skips, so a burst-firing player would never see one.
- Scripted tests: Myra reads input once per frame, so a press must be **held across a frame**.

### The skip hint (#499, #699, #814)

- A HUD line at the bottom centre, the fire glyph + "Click or Space to skip" (the trigger glyph + "to skip" on a pad), in after `SKIP_HINT_DELAY` (1 s) and out when the takeover ends. **Every skippable takeover, every time**, no count or retirement.
- **Not over the shot that cleared the level** (a click still skips): `GameplayScreen` hands the HUD a drop running over a decided level as not running. Neither appears with under `SKIP_HINT_MIN_REMAINING` left (known for the tour, not a drop). The line loss has none.
- **The chapter flythrough shows it too**, `SKIP_HINT_INTRO_DELAY` (1.5 s) in. The tour hides the HUD (#693), so it is drawn alone (`PlayHud.DrawSkipHintAlone`), **only while the gameplay screen is on top (#814)**: drawn onto the frame it skipped a page's blur and its fade (stepped by `Update`) froze, so a pause showed it sharp at full strength.

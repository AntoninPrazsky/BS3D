# The in-play HUD

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

What the scoring rules ("Scoring" in `docs/game-session.md`) look like while a level is played, and how they move. Siblings: [magazine strip, chips and muzzle marks](hud-magazine-and-muzzle.md), [the ceiling-warning side cut](hud-cluster-profile.md), [the tutorial card](tutorial-card.md).

## Structure

- **`Game/Screens/PlayHud.cs`** is its own class so the feel's dials are visible together. It owns no score: it is handed the `ScoreKeeper` each frame.
- Drawn in one `SpriteBatch.Begin/End` **after the tonemap resolve**, in display space (a HUD downsampled with the scene is soft). Under a page's blur it goes into the overlay layer and is softened with the frame (#438, "The end of a level goes out of focus" in `docs/game-feedback.md`).
- **Not Myra** (no animation, consumes input). **No shader over the text**: each glyph is its own quad, so a blur is clipped at its edge. Use `FontSystemEffect.Blurry` (rasterized into the atlas) and repeated draws for smear.
- **Corners** are the only space free in both camera poses: score top right (the FPS line owns top left), balls left bottom left, magazine strip bottom right, cluster profile down the left edge. Scaled by `Viewport.Height / 2160` through `Scaled()`. `HUD_MARGIN` must clear the **halo**, or a glow against the frame's edge is cut in half.

## Authored loud

A game HUD is not a data readout (an overlay-sized first pass read as barely there): the score is 140 design units and its labels 76, and an award zooms well past the score (below). The halo is drawn `HUD_GLOW_PASSES` times because **one blur pass is always fainter than its glyph**, and **centred by its own measurement** (`MeasureString` with the effect and amount), since a blurred glyph is a larger bitmap with its own render offset.

## `Pulse`: "this number just changed"

A scalar resting at 1, **displaced** by a kick and sprung back (a second kick mid-settle adds to the first). Snapped to rest under an epsilon, **sub-stepped at 1/120 s** (an explicit spring this stiff goes unstable near 2/ω ≈ 0.11 s, and weak hardware runs at ten frames a second) and saturating at `MAX_OFFSET` (`HUD_FONT_SCORE` is chosen so a fully kicked score clears the frame). **The direction of the kick is the information**: score and streak grow, the ball count shrinks.

## Awards and the score

- **An award zooms, is read, shrinks and is thrown** (#846, `Game/Screens/AwardZoom.cs`, the curves pinned by `AwardZoomTests`). Born on its cell (reprojected every frame, rising over it), it zooms from near nothing to **1.5 times the score's type for a bare match and up to 3 times for a big release** (`AwardZoom.Peak`, logarithmic in the points, 2 000 and up being big) and is **read for 0.55 s up to 1.2 s**, zoom included, swelling a little. The read (`Hold`) grows with the *square* of that weight, because it is the time a number stands over the cluster being aimed at: a match of three on the capped streak (150) is read 0.65 s, a 1 260 release 1.07 s. It then **shrinks where it stands** to half the score while the throw's wind-up (`HudEase.InBack`) is still behind its source, and is thrown into the corner over `AwardZoom.FLIGHT` (0.55 s), turning amber, with a smear. **Landing is what pays** and kicks the score. `_scoreShown` chases `score.Score` *less the points still in the air*, recomputed from the list, so a level torn down mid-flight still agrees.
  - ⚠ **One big number at a time, in turn: handed on, not merged.** A landing is one award (a bomb's chain is scored with the landing that set it off), so two awards together are two shots landing close behind each other. The newer one takes the read and every award still being read leaves for the corner now, once it has been read for `MIN_READ` (`AwardZoom.HandOff`), so each is seen whole, on its own cell, in its own colour with its own multiplier, and the corner is hit once per shot. ⚠ **Leaving is not vanishing**: the older one shrinks where it stands over the throw's wind-up and is still larger than the score for 0.16 s (a bare match) to 0.23 s (a big release) after it leaves, under a quarter second (pinned by `AwardZoomTests`), while the newer one is larger than the score within 0.03 s of its birth. So two numbers larger than the score do stand together at every hand-off: for about 0.1–0.2 s when the landings are `MIN_READ` or more apart, and for what was left of `MIN_READ` longer when they are closer (0.24–0.31 s for shots landing 0.3 s apart). It reads as a hand-off, the newer one drawn over the older and bursting in as the older shrinks off. A merged total was rejected: it would have to show one colour and one multiplier for two shots born on two cells, and the player would lose what each shot was worth.
  - **The completion bonus has an award too**: white (no ball earned it), captioned **"unused"**, the word the result page and the Help page pay it under ("shots unused"; "bonus" pointed at the page's "streak bonus" row, another figure), born in the balls-left count and rising out of it once the clearing shot's award has been read (`AwardZoom.BonusWait`, at most `MIN_READ`), sending that award on its way exactly as a newer one would. It is often the biggest figure of the level and the reward for playing well, and without an award it counted up in the corner out of nowhere while the collapse played. ⚠ **It must land with time to be counted in**, not just before the page: the page hides the HUD, and the corner closes only a share of the gap a second (`SCORE_CHASE_PER_SECOND`), so a bonus landing 0.15 s before the page is cut off at 59 % of its count. Wait, longest read and flight come to 2.15 s, leaving `AwardZoom.BONUS_COUNT_IN` (0.35 s, nearly nine tenths of the count) of the 2.5 s cleared beat (`LevelPhases.ClearedBeatSeconds`, which the tests hold it to). Under a drop cinematic it lands long before the page, which waits for the cinematic while the HUD keeps ageing.
  - **A cut's award waits for its severing** (#767, `PlayHud.CUT_AWARD_DELAY`, 0.7 s): born at once it stood, zoomed to its largest, exactly over the slash running across the storey it paid for. It is born on the struck cell once the slash has been seen, its points withheld meanwhile, and its birth hands on any award still being read as the bonus's does. Every other award is born on its landing.
  - **Its type is a ladder of faces** (`PlayHud.AwardFace`): the label's 76, 112, the tutorial card's 200, 320 and `HUD_FONT_POPUP_PEAK` at the top of the zoom's overshoot, the smallest at least as big as the size drawn, no two rungs more than 1.8 times apart. One face for every size would be shrunk six times on landing (the atlas has no mips, so it shimmers) or stretched and soft at the peak. The backing's blur and the shadow's offset follow the size drawn, not the face's scale, so nothing steps between faces.
  - Held `POPUP_EDGE` inside the frame at the size it has, so a zoomed number born near the top is not cut off, and through the throw's wind-up too, which gathers the number behind its source: the completion bonus, born against the left edge, would otherwise be carried half off the frame by it.
- **Every score-like figure goes through `ScoreText`** (`Game/Screens/ScoreText.cs`): thousands grouped with a **space** (`12 340`), locale-independent (`"N0"` once printed one number two ways in a frame), rebuilt only when the number changes.

## Colour: one accent, meaning gain

Amber (`HUD_ACCENT`) survives every scene palette; it goes on the score's arrival flash, the streak and the last two balls. The only other colour is an award's at birth: the balls' colour **as the lit ball shows it** (`TypeColor`, #153), turning amber in flight (the completion bonus is born white).

**Balls left escalates in three steps, only the last a colour:** at `HUD_LOW_BALLS` it grows (`HUD_LOW_EMPHASIS`, a *size*: Anton has one weight) and breathes (motion alarms better than hue); at `HUD_CRITICAL_BALLS` it takes the accent. Breathing, not blinking, off the **play** clock.

## Legibility: a blurred black backing under every readout

⚠ **An offset shadow darkens only ONE side of a letter.** The count sits on the island's concrete deck in every scene, where `MENU_TEXT_DIM` (for asides on a dark plate) is darker than the ground, so the caption has its own `HUD_CAPTION`. The backing is `Blurry` (two passes, in `DrawString`), **tighter than the flash** (`HUD_BACKING_BLUR` against `HUD_GLOW_BLUR`): it must hug the letterform, or it becomes the plate the corners refuse to have.

## The streak

- Warms ×2 to ×5 (`MENU_TEXT` to `HUD_ACCENT`) over `ScoreKeeper.MaxMultiplier`; held back at ×1.
- **At the cap** the badge changes face (the loud label), takes a travelling colour sweep and flares once. The sweep is a **hot arc, not a rainbow** (a full wheel's cyan vanished against the meadow's sky); stagger a sixth of the ramp per glyph (a third read as two colours), so it is drawn glyph by glyph, dropping kerning. The glow keeps a floor, or a held ×5 looks ordinary.
- **A lost streak breaks, and is heard breaking** (#845), so the player notices the loss and wants to chain shots or reach for the Swap: the badge **cracks** (`STREAK_BREAK_CRACK`, 0.16 s: it swells about its right edge, jolts sideways, flashes white-hot, dark cracks run across it) and **shatters**: its glyphs are thrown apart (the × to the left, the digit dropping) and tumble down with shards of it, keeping the colours they wore for a beat, then cooling to `STREAK_LOST` grey and going out, while **"×5 lost"** rises where it stood in the loud face. `ProceduralAudio.PlayStreakBreak` plays the falling run ([sound effects](sound-effects.md#a-streak-breaking-845)). **Everything scales with what was lost** (`StreakCues.Severity`, ½ for ×3, 1 for the cap): a lost ×3 swells to 1.6 times, throws 11 shards and lasts 1.1 s; a lost ×5 swells to 1.9 times, throws 16 and lasts 1.4 s, and its sound is two notes longer and louder.
  - ⚠ **Size is what makes it seen.** The first cut squeezed the badge as it cracked and threw label-sized pieces that cooled to grey at once: at 1600×900 the whole break was a smudge twenty pixels high, pale on a pale sky. The badge is the HUD's smallest figure, so it grows as it breaks, the pieces keep its size and colour for a while, and every shard has a dark copy of itself under it.
  - **The session decides whether it is told** (`GameplayScreen.AnswerLostStreak`, at the very end of both doors a shot resolves through without scoring, `OnShotSpent` and `OnBallLanded`): never once the level is decided or its last shot spent (`StreakCues.BreakIsTold`), where it would play over the result; there the badge simply goes.
  - Every piece is a closed-form throw from a hash of the break's seed, drawn from the break's age alone: nothing listed, nothing allocated per frame.
- **The loss is also said where the eye is** (#875). The owner's report of 2026-10-15: he heard the falling run repeatedly without knowing what it was, and never saw the shatter at the top right of a 3840×1600 monitor, because **while aiming the eye rests on the middle of the frame**. So the call that starts the sound (`BreakStreak`) also puts **"STREAK LOST" and the lost multiplier** over the aim point (`PlayHud.DrawStreakLost`, the rules in `StreakCues`, pinned by `StreakCuesTests`). The corner's shatter stays as the record.
  - **It is an award's own type and layout** (`LayOutAward`, `DrawAwardAt`): the words in the display face with the award's blurred backing, the multiplier in the accent beside them as an award carries its own, here **at the words' size** (an award's is two thirds of its figure's, and at that size the × read as a bullet: "STREAK LOST •3", photographed). It **settles at 1.325 scores for a lost ×3 and 1.45 for a lost ×5** (`StreakCues.LostCaptionSize`; the multiplier climbs by `ScoreKeeper.MultiplierStep`, so a real break is a lost ×3 or a lost ×5, never a ×2 or ×4, which only a pinned `streak=` run shows, at 1.2625 and 1.3875; the constant `LOST_CAPTION_LOW_SIZE`, 1.2, is the line's anchor at a lost ×1, which is never told), under the 1.5 a bare match zooms to: the news must not be louder than a reward. Everything scales with the HUD (`Scaled`); measured off captures, the words' ink (settled) is **41 to 47 px tall and 271 to 314 px wide at 1600×900** and **76 to 84 px by 502 to 550 at 3840×1600** (pinned `streak=` runs from a lost ×2 to a lost ×5; a real lost ×3 stands inside that range).
  - **In full on its first frame**, the frame the sound is called on, and only fading over its last 0.25 s (`LostCaptionPresence`): a fade in would put it behind the sound it explains. It slams in swollen (1.3 times, settling over 0.12 s through `HudEase.OutBack`), is white-hot and cools to the badge's grey over 0.3 s, and drifts up 70 units, away from the aim, as it fades. **0.8 s for a lost ×3 to 0.9 s for a lost ×5** (`LostCaptionSeconds`; a pinned `streak=2` run stands 0.75 s, and `LOST_CAPTION_LOW_SECONDS`, 0.7, is the anchor at a lost ×1, never told), never blurred.
  - **Off the aim point.** Its bottom edge is fixed `LOST_CAPTION_CLEARANCE` (170 design units) above the middle of the frame, which is clear of the crosshair at its most swollen (`Crosshair.FULL_STRAIN_REACH`, 132) by at least `LOST_CAPTION_GAP` (30 units, 12 pixels at 900p; the test holds the one above the other), and the pop grows upwards from it. It stands centred, over the aim, not beside it: the eye is there. On the overview it stands over the cluster's lower half, which a few hundred milliseconds of dark-backed type does not hide.
  - **An award being read gives way to the caption, and the caption to a new award.** A landing that scores cannot break the streak (`AnswerLostStreak` runs only for a shot that scored nothing), so the two are never born together; but an award still being read from an earlier shot can be up when a later one misses. `BreakStreak` sends any award still being read on its way to the corner (`HandOffReads`, the rule #846 gave a newer award: only one big number at a time), and a new award born while the caption stands starts its fade (`AddAward`), so the newer number takes the read either way. The caption is drawn last: an award already thrown towards the corner (shrunk to the score's size or less) can still cross it, under it.
  - **Never over the result:** `EndStreakQuietly` (the level decided, its last shot spent) takes the caption away with the badge, and a new level's `Reset` does too. The tutorial's streak card ("A miss resets the streak") stands along the top of the frame, far above it, and its words and the caption say the same thing.
  - It runs on the play clock, so a pause holds it. Nothing is allocated per frame: the two strings are built when the break begins.
- `streak=<n>` pins what the readout **shows**, never the scoring, **until a shot breaks it**: the first shot that resolves without scoring breaks the pinned streak as it would a real one and lifts the pin, so a script can photograph the break of a ×5 nobody earned.

## When it stops

A **won** level lets go of the HUD in two steps (`PlayHud.Draw`'s `HudParts`):

- **The moment the field empties** (`LevelPhase.ClearedBeat`, #862) the readouts of *playing* (the side cut, the queue, the power-up chips, balls left, the streak, the crosshair; the tutorial card has already gone, since a decided level ends it, #700) fade out through the overlay layer over `CLEARED_HUD_FADE_SECONDS` (0.5 s, on a clock that runs through a drop cinematic), while the **account** (the score, the awards flying into it, the clear's bonus among them, and a drop's skip hint) stays sharp over the celebration. The owner's rule: once the level is done the rest means nothing and the celebration has the frame. Under a page's blur the beat's layer carries the account alone; the Potato path has no layer and drops the rest at once.
- **With the result page** (`!LevelOver`, #582) the account goes too: the page states the score, and an award in flight would hang frozen (it ages on the play clock, which the covered frame does not step, #241).

A **lost** level keeps the side cut and the magazine strip (#639): `HudParts.Field`, drawn **sharp**, never in #438's blurred layer.

## Testing it

- An award lives 1.1 s (a bare match) to 1.75 s (a big release): capture in a burst. A scratch level loaded with `levelfile=` (a rock lid with one blob of one colour under it) makes a one-shot big release, and with nothing else removable the same shot clears the field and shows the bonus. Shots `fire=`d 0.3 s apart at three cells photograph the hand-on.
- ⚠ **State the aim** (`aim=4.5:33.8:0` is the rest pose): a run with `fire=` and no `aim=` has fired from a gun turned off its rest pose and missed, where the same command landed on another day. Something live on the desk (the cursor, a pad's stick) still steers a gun no `aim=` holds.
- **The break** (#845): `level=23 seed=1 streak=5 aim=5:-5:0 fire=7 logshots` fires into the drain, and `[streak] x5 lost at 7.29 s, told` says when the break began on `shot=`'s clock; a 1600×900 burst at 7.33, 7.42, 7.6, 7.8 and 8.1 catches the crack, the shatter and the words. At 3840×1600 take one shot a run: a PNG that size blocks the frame long enough to move the next. **The caption** (#875) is on the same frame as that line, for 0.8 s (a lost ×3) to 0.9 s (a lost ×5) of the play clock; `rmb=4:9.5` holds precise aim over the loss so the crosshair stands under the words. A capture a fifth of a second before the shot is fired (`shot=6.4`) keeps clear of the scripted `fire=`: a PNG taken at 6.95 s stalled the frame past the 7 s press, and no shot was fired.
- **Drive menus with Enter alone, never Down** (a held Down passes `NAV_REPEAT_DELAY` and can open the Scene page), and confirm focus first (`UpdateMenuChrome`'s `IsActive` block).
- A scripted `rmbdown` never released stays held at OS level after the process is killed; the next run's menu clicks vanish.

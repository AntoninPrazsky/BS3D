# Making the recordings

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

What a future music round needs: where the recordings come from, how a loop is cut, the checks, and the exact `MusicBake` commands. Playback is [The game's music](music.md).

## Where they come from

- **ACE-Step 1.5 through acestep.cpp v0.0.5** on the desktop's RX 6900 XT via Vulkan, each prompted from the description of the composition in its slot (the `MusicTheme` docs). Scripts in `Tools/Generation` (README there): `generate-music.ps1` makes one render (`-Loop` cuts a whole-bar loop with `loop_crossfade.py`, which keeps the untouched render as `<name>.raw.wav`; `-NoCotCaption` stops the language model rewriting the brief; `BS3D_AI_ROOT` points at the engine and models).
- The float masters of the first eleven are in `Research/AI-Music/`, each with a `.json` sidecar holding **the exact prompt, what the language model rewrote it into, the loop cut and its check**: that is where a brief that worked is kept, and the next family's brief starts from the nearest one. Later families' masters (#486) live **outside the repository** (`MusicBake --tracks --masters <dir>`); the `.ogg` is the artefact.
- **A brief that worked, for the pause loop (#668):** nine renders over three briefs (bossa nova, vibraphone and air, chillhop; `generate-music.ps1 -Loop -NoCotCaption`, 120 s, three seeds each), picked by seam: context match 0.949, crossfade rhythm r = 0.99, spectrum cosine 1.00 (`pause-vibes-12`, still awaiting the owner's ear).

## Naming a family's offenders

**Which recordings of a family offend is decided by measurement against the desert's `ember` family** (the owner, 2026-10-11): tunelessness, the low-mid share, energy above 6 kHz. `Tools/Generation/measure-music.py <files> --reference ember` measures loops or candidate masters and bolds every figure outside ember's range (band shares, the tonal share and flatness of 300 Hz-2 kHz, held-note changes, tempo wander, clipped runs, pitch wobble and detune). A second table holds the arrangement columns: short gaps, how long the opening repeats and held notes out of key (below). Measure ember in the same run: the tune columns are only comparable within one. The tables it gave are on #841 (magma), #824 (pulse), #706 and #905 (mural) and #906 (summit).

- ⚠ **"Drunk" was not in the pitch or the timing**: no mural recording wobbles or drifts more than an ember one, while a synthetic ±25-cent wow moves wobble and detune three to seven times. What the owner hears there is timbre or playing, which only the ear names; a note sent from the pause names the recording (#824).
- **How loud the drums stand against the mix is its own column** (#905, #906): `Tools/Generation/drum-share.py` compares a recording's separated drums stem with its mix in 300 Hz-4 kHz, the band the monitor speakers play (level, the loud 250 ms, the stem's crest, the share of windows within 6 dB of the mix). For a shipped recording it reads the layer in `Music/Drums`; for a candidate it takes the loop and the stem `separate_drums.py` makes of it (`--pairs`; Demucs on the CPU, about a minute a loop, no GPU lock). It fired on `summit-glacier`, the owner's "blows and pounding": its stem stood level with the mix (-2.3 dB, -1.1 dB in its loud 250 ms, crest 38 dB) where the rest of its family read -6.1 to -21.2 and only the house track `summit-deephouse` (-1.3) stood as high. It did **not** single out `mural-sunset` ("the drums override the melody"): its -8.9 dB sat among its family's -7.9 to -18.3, so what the owner heard there is not an outlier by this number and the column is a target to get under (the candidates read -12 to -35). The stem is Demucs' guess, an upper bound: a marimba's attack and a plucked bass can land in it.
- **How much of the pitched energy sits on the key's scale** (#905): `Tools/Generation/key-share.py` counts every note in 100 Hz-2.5 kHz, where the held-notes-out-of-key column follows the dominant pitch alone and cannot see a second voice a semitone off. A 15 s stretch of `mural-sunset` shifted by +1 semitone moved it from 90.7 % to 70.4 %. It does not rank the old Sunset ("a bit disharmonic") at the bottom of its family either (90.7 % against 84.8-99.8 %), so it is a target, not a finding: the candidates read 90-97 % and the one that shipped is the highest.

## Choosing a candidate

- Render **three candidates of a brief** and measure them in the same `measure-music.py` run as ember. The band shares do not choose by themselves. Two faults they miss:
  - **the step across the wrap**: `magma-doom` c3 sat inside ember's range on every band and jumped +15.4 dB at its seam (p99 of its own steps);
  - **a hole**: a long stretch 10 dB or more under the loop's loud level. `magma-electro` c4 spent 24 s of its 43 s at −9 to −16 dB, the fault the old `doom` was replaced for.

  The loop cut's own check must also say "in time". Two more faults ruled candidates out in #732:
  - **a dropout**: two seconds more than 20 dB under the loop's loud level (its p95 1 s RMS). No shipped `ember`, `magma` or first-generation `puppet` loop dips more than 15.8 dB; `puppet-waltz` c6 dipped 45 dB with only 12 % of its loop under −10 dB, so the hole share alone passed it;
  - **a seam whose two sides disagree**: the cut's spectrum cosine under 0.3 (`puppet-fantasy` c1 printed −0.03). The cut's log prints negative r and cosine values, so a parser must keep the sign.

  A large wrap step over 250 ms can be one drum hit, so measure it over 1 s and 2 s too: `mural-desertblues` ships with −9.0 dB (p94) over 250 ms, +0.9 dB over 1 s and −1.0 dB over 2 s (#706).
- **Three faults the band shares and the tune columns cannot see**, each a column of `measure-music.py`'s second table, read against ember in the same run (the owner's Jukebox notes of 2026-10-16, #913, #910, #914; the tool's docstring defines them and says how each was shown to fire):
  - **Short gaps**: runs of 30–500 ms more than 20 dB under the loudness around them (RMS of 20 ms frames, the median of 2 s as the loudness). The hole check, the dropout check and MusicBake's 6 s refusal all miss a gap of a fraction of a second, and a silence of 0.5–2 s as well. Ember reads 0.0–27.0 gaps a minute at 20 dB: three of its ten (`ember-garage`, `ember-rockabilly`, `ember-surf`) give 25–27, up to 350 ms long and 43 dB deep, so the rate is what a candidate is held to, not the existence of a gap. The figure depends on `--gap-db` (ember's highest is 89.6 a minute at 10 dB and 11.7 at 30 dB): compare rows only within one run.
  - **Opening repeat**: the bars from the start over which one pattern of 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars keeps coming round, each bar a vector of pitch-class energy in eighths of the bar (harmonic part), near-identical at a cosine of 0.90. Ember reads 1–3 bars, 1.4–7.5 s: its opening hardly repeats. "Longest repeat anywhere" does not separate anything: `ember-stoner` holds one pattern for 48 s. A generated take never repeats exactly, so a repeat the ear hears as the same can sit at 0.90–0.93 and vanish at 0.95: read the opening at `--similar 0.90` and `0.93` before calling a loop repetitive. The bar comes from `--bpm` when given (it overrides the sidecar's loop cut for every file in the run), else the sidecar's loop cut, else the recording's own beat folded into 90–180 bpm (the same bar as the cut's log on 102 of the 129 shipped loops that have one, half or double on 18 of the rest, mostly slow tracks); a waltz is not 4/4.
  - **Notes out of key**: the share of held notes whose pitch class lies outside the sidecar's `keyscale` (natural minor or major), with the count, and outside the best-fitting scale of the twelve. Ember reads 0.0–8.6 % of its held notes (0–30 notes outside E minor), so a handful of wrong notes in a 45 s loop reads inside it; what it fires on is a stretch off the scale (a 15 s section a semitone or a quarter-tone off read 15 % and 12 % of a 47 s loop). It sees only the dominant pitch, never two voices that clash. Where each out-of-key note starts is in the `--json` row (`out_of_key_at`).
- **Ask the groove to play throughout.** "A hushed breakdown", "a secretive opening" and "a slow build from a hushed spell" put 19–38 % of the loop under −10 dB in 13 of 30 first-round renders of a mysterious brief (#732). "Playing throughout", "the full ensemble from the first bar" and "quiet breakdown, near silence, long soft passages, fade in" in the negative left aurora, theatre, oud and trip-hop at least two loops of three without a hole in the next round.
- Brief the drums as **"kick, snare and toms with a dark ride cymbal"**, with bright crash cymbals, open hi-hats, tambourine and shakers in the negative: it brought two of three drum and bass renders to 2.0 % above 6 kHz or under, where the first three sat at 2.5–3.5 % (#841).
- ⚠ **Some styles keep their treble whatever the brief says**: breakbeat rock stayed at 2.1–5.1 % above 6 kHz over nine renders and electro at 2.5–4.6 %, the synth lead made "warm" instead of "bright" included. Township jive stayed at 2.3–4.5 % in seven of eight renders (#706), a mellow round guitar tone, soft highs, low hand drums and "jangly guitar, crisp hi-hats, bells" in the negative included. When three rounds do not clear ember's line, ship the best and name the figure still outside it in the sidecar's `chosen.why`, as `theme-magma-breakrock.json` and `theme-magma-electro.json` do.
- ⚠ **And some keep their dynamics or their tune figure** (#732): an orchestral waltz left a hole or a dropout in six renders of six and a cinematic orchestra in seven of nine, "playing throughout" and a pop beat under it included; the one orchestra that held its level kept loose time (tempo wander 2.66 %, ember at most 0.29 %) and ships with that named in `theme-puppet-fantasy.json`. A waltz carried by a gypsy-jazz rhythm guitar and walking bass held its level. Trip-hop stayed under ember's 2.74 held-note changes a second in all nine renders (0.9–2.73), a long flowing melody asked included.
- ⚠ **Four-on-the-floor styles carry their treble in the drums, and it moves more between seeds than between briefs** (#824). In every dance render over ember's 2.0 % above 6 kHz, 70–90 % of that energy sat in the percussive part (hats, ride, claps; median-filter HPSS), and one brief gave italo 0.3–5.0 % and electro-funk 1.2–11.3 % across its seeds. Naming no cymbal at all ("a deep round snare and warm toms … soft dark percussion"), with ride, crash and hi-hats in the negative and "soft, warm, rolled-off highs" in the production line, brought five of nine acid, italo and synth-pop renders to 2.0 % or under, against one of nine on the brief before. In synth-pop it also thinned 200 Hz–2 kHz to 6–9 %, and no synth-pop render of seventeen held both; `theme-pulse-synthpop.json` names its 6.8 %.
- **A bright house piano is treble too**: its hammer attacks land in the percussive part, and piano house stayed at 2.2–5.0 % above 6 kHz in eight of nine renders whatever the drums were. "A warm, mellow grand piano", with "bright house piano, M1 piano" in the negative, gave 1.8 % and 0.8 %; a Rhodes gave 5.0 % and 2.9 %.
- ⚠ **Ska keeps its full-band rests whatever the brief says, and the one render inside the line lost its tune figures** (#904). Eight renders of three briefs (offbeat guitar skanks, a trombone and a baritone saxophone in the mids) read 26.7–101.3 short gaps a minute against ember's 27.0 at most. In the gaps the bass and the mids fall 24–36 dB together, so the whole band is resting. The plain brief gave 40.0, 47.5 and 29.3; the two renders of the brief that added a warm organ holding the chords between the skanks, 66.0 and 101.3; the echo-and-reverb brief, 82.5, 61.0 and 26.7. That last one had 2.03 held-note changes a second (ember at least 2.74) and a pitch wobble of 4.3 cents (ember at most 3.8). Cutting the 29.3 render again from other stretches of its raw render gave 29.3–30.7, and stretches short enough to leave its busier parts out gave loops of 15–23 s. `bloom-ska` ships the 29.3 render (c3, a 45 s loop: the only column outside ember's range) on the owner's ruling of 2026-10-18 that it is better than the #486 recording it replaced (99.2 gaps a minute, 21 clipped runs); `theme-bloom-ska.json` names the figure beside the owner's words.
- **Measure the wrap over 1 s and 2 s as well as 250 ms** (#905): `mural-sunset` c4 read -6.6 dB over 250 ms (p67 of its own steps) and **-9.3 dB over 1 s, the largest of its loop's own 1 s steps**, a loop that ends loud and restarts soft. Cut again from the render's seconds 40-120 (`loop_crossfade.py` on that stretch of the `.raw.wav`; eight stretches take ten seconds) it read +2.9 / +1.9 / +0.4 dB (250 ms, 1 s, 2 s) at the same cut quality (context match 0.745, cosine 0.93) and shipped, the sidecar's `recut` saying how. `summit-glacier` ships a cut of +5.6 dB over 1 s (p86; its loop's largest 1 s step is 10.9 dB), and no other stretch of its render did better.
- **Soft drums are a seed's luck as much as a wording** (#905, #906; 24 renders). Sunset's "only a quiet shaker and a soft low hand drum" or "only a very quiet shaker and fingertip conga far behind everything", with "kick drum, snare, toms, log drum, heavy drums, loud drums, pounding drums, drum fills, clatter" in the negative, put five of six stems 12-35 dB under the mix in 300 Hz-4 kHz (the old log drum brief: 8.9). Glacier's eighteen stems stood 4-36 dB under whatever the wording, and one brief ("a deep soft round bass playing long notes", "only a faint distant brushed shaker, soft and sparse, no kick") gave -7.2, -8.4 and -18.5 dB: measure each candidate's stem and do not trust the caption. Demucs files a bass with a hard attack under drums ("a deep soft round synth bass" with a muted frame drum left -4 to -8 dB). Asking a slow downtempo for "a flowing melody of steady eighth notes" did not raise its held-note changes (2.0-2.7 a second, ember at least 2.74); the replaced brief's own "crystalline plucked synths repeating a simple melody" did (2.8-3.8), and it was the only wording of the three rounds to give a render inside ember on every column with soft drums (one of eighteen). Sunset was easier: three of six renders were inside ember on every column.
- The sidecar of a regenerated recording keeps the old one under `replaces` (its brief, its loop cut and why it went) and the losing candidates' seeds under `alternatives`, so a listener who prefers another candidate can name it and it can be cut from its master again.

## Cutting a loop

- **Cut a loop out of the render's body, never from its own ends.** A generated piece *ends* (a fade or a final hit into silence), so folding the tail onto the head was sample-seamless and audibly an ending followed by a beginning: the level fell from 1.0 to 0.04 of the median across one wrap.
- Loops are **whole bars**, aligned to the millisecond on the transient envelope and crossfaded over about two beats. Every render came back within 1 % of the asked tempo but one (asked 180, rendered ~96 felt at ~192, cut on the half-time grid), so measure the tempo.
- **A band that stops dead mid-render leaves a dropout in a long cut.** Cut again from the part of the render before the stop: run `loop_crossfade.py` on that stretch of the `.raw.wav`. `mural-jive` c4 stopped for two seconds 74 s in (−20 and −28 dB), so its 108 s cut failed the dropout check. Recut from its first 73.5 s, it gave a 40 s loop with context match 0.93 and shipped (#706; the sidecar's `recut` says how).
- **The check** is the rhythm correlation of the two streams the crossfade mixes against the track's own passages a bar apart. Every loop keeps time by it except **Bohemia** (0.43 against 0.53), whose render changes rhythm near 76 s and so yields only 42 s. The `menu` loop predates the method and dips to a tenth of its median at the wrap; the owner heard it and kept it.
- **A hole is not cut around.** Trimming a render to its longest stretch at level and cutting again, to step around a breakdown, left loops of 15–32 s (#824): render another seed instead.

## `MusicBake`

The usage line (`Tools/MusicBake/Program.cs`; every argument is read before anything runs):

```
MusicBake [--out <dir>] [--theme <name>] [--no-write]
        | --tracks [--masters <dir>] [--only <prefix>] [--quality <-0.1..0.59>] [--no-write]
        | --shipped [--only <prefix>]
        | --sfx <wav> <name> [--music [--root <midi>] [--bpm <n>]] [--no-write]
        | --drums <wav> <recording> [--no-write]
```

- No mode renders the procedural compositions to `.wav`. `--tracks` encodes masters to `Game/Music` Ogg and checks each through `OggTrack`; `--shipped` measures shipped tracks through the game's decoder; `--sfx` writes a sound effect ([The sound](sound-effects.md)); `--drums` writes a drums layer.
- Exit codes: 2 usage; 3 a stored `LIMITER_DRIVE` is stale; 4 a streamed render differs from the one-shot by more than a step; 5 the loudness meter is off its standard. See "The music bakery" in `docs/formats-and-tools.md`.

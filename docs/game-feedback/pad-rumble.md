# The pad: rumble and haptics

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

The pad answers through `Game/GamepadRumble.cs` (#378), a mixer over four motors: the two body motors (left the heavy low-frequency one, right the light buzz) and the two impulse motors in the triggers. **The strengths are the owner's verdicts, not design reasoning**: each was brought down on his hand (Xbox One S, USB, from 2026-10-05). Do not raise a figure without his hand on the pad.

## The mixer

- Each channel (`PadChannel`) is **accumulated and clamped to 1** on `Kick(left, right, seconds)` and **decayed linearly to zero over the latest kick's `seconds`** (`CameraShake`'s shape). One `SetVibration` call a frame, so two kicks on one frame blend instead of the second overwriting the first.
- **A landing's release rings in a second layer of the channel** (#810, `PadChannel.KickUnder`), with **its own fall**, and is blended with the ordinary kicks (`PadChannel.Blend`): it fills its share of what is left under its ceiling above everything else ringing, `free + bounded × (1 − free / ceiling)`. So it never lifts a motor past its ceiling, nor past what the ordinary kicks ask for on their own, and it reaches the ceiling only when it is at the ceiling itself. Two releases compose the same way and keep the later of their two ends. **The traps it closes:** with one fall per channel reset by every kick, the gun's rattle (0.035 s) or the next shot cut a release's 0.9 s tail to their own length; with a plain sum, the next shot's 0.55 landed on a 0.33 tail at 0.88.
- `KickTriggers` does the same as `Kick` for the trigger motors. `Hold(left, right)` is a level asked **every frame** and gone the first frame it is not. `Play` steps a pattern of timed pulses. `Silence()` zeroes everything. `Feel`/`FeelTriggers` bypass the hand gate.
- Every trigger is a `Game.Rumble.Kick` (a landing, `Game.Rumble.Landing`) **beside a sound or flash that already answers the same moment**. `RumbleStrength` in `GameSettings` sits beside the volumes.

## What triggers it (`GameplayScreen`)

- **Shot** (`Shoot`, `SHOT_RUMBLE_*`), **a star landing** (`ResultPage.AnnounceLandedStars`).
- **A landing and the group it releases, under one ceiling** (`OnBallLanded` → `Game.Rumble.Landing`, `Game/LandingRumble.cs`, #810). A plain attach is a light tap (`TAP_*`), an ordinary kick, the one the owner felt and kept. A release asks for a **share of the ceiling** (`Share`) that grows along a **logarithm of the count** (`KNEE` 10 balls, from `RELEASE_FLOOR` 0.14): three to five balls are a light tap, and it keeps growing past 20, more slowly, up to the whole ceiling at `FULL_COUNT` (250). It gets **longer rather than harder** (0.2 s to 0.9 s), and **the light motor leads at every size**, its ceiling (`CEILING_RIGHT` 0.65) above the heavy motor's (`CEILING_LEFT` 0.5): the wrist-shaking is taken to be the heavy motor's mass. The release rings in the channel's ceilinged layer (above), so a burst of releases, the next shot and the rattle never lift it past the ceiling or cut it short. The trap it replaced: a straight line (0.22 + 0.04 a ball) that hit the motors' full at 20 balls, while in `LevelGen --drops`' modelled plays the median release is 19 to 27 balls, so about half of all releases were the same full-strength pulse. 250 is about the 99th percentile of those releases. **The trap: the shot's own thump is usually still ringing when its ball lands** (measured in a scripted Pennant run, a flight of about 0.08 s: a plain landing stood at 0.29/0.38 against the tap's 0.12/0.30). A release summed onto it reached the heavy ceiling from about 32 balls; filling the room left above it instead, the mixer's arithmetic over that thump puts the heavy motor at 0.34 for 3 balls, 0.39 for 20, 0.44 for 80 and 0.5 only at 250 (`LandingRumbleTests` pins that the ceiling is the full count's alone, whatever is ringing).
- **Ceiling step** (`AnnounceCeilingStep`): halved on a feed step; lowered three times on the owner's feel to a light-motor buzz (`CEILING_RUMBLE_*`) over `CeilingDescent.SLIDE_SECONDS`, just above the carriage's rattle. **The Brake lifting the glass** (#836, `BRAKE_RUMBLE_*`): heavy-led and firmer, so the lift reads differently from the pressure.
- **Each blast** (`BLAST_PAD_*`): per unit of its size, so a chain reads bigger than one bomb, which stays under full (#810's rule). Its kick is an ordinary one: the first bomb goes off with the landing, and the matched group's release fills only what is left under the ceiling above it, so a release never makes a blast harder.
- **The loss at the line** (`BeginLineLoss`): the heaviest pulse, as two kicks (heavy long, buzz short) because one kick decays both motors over one length (`LINE_LOSS_RUMBLE_*`).
- **The elevation stop**: one buzz-led knock (`AIM_STOP_RUMBLE_*`) as `Cannon.ElevationStrain` goes full, re-armed only once the strain lets go, **plus a steady `Hold` while the hand keeps pushing** (#835, `AIM_REFUSED_HOLD_*` times the strain). It holds full while a cutter is aimed into the storeys the Cut may not strike (#692), since no clamp stops the aim there.

## The triggers (#188)

- **Only `Windows.Gaming.Input` reaches the trigger motors.** XInput has the two body speeds only, and MonoGame's four-argument `SetVibration` drops the trigger pair on WindowsDX **and still returns `true`**.
- `Game/Platform/PadMotors.cs` keeps the first pad it lists (from its added/removed events; `Gamepad.Gamepads` builds a new vector view per call). All four motors go through it whenever a pad answers there; the body's two go through XInput only when none does, **never both on one frame** (two APIs overwrite each other). The log says the route once: `[pad] rumble through Windows.Gaming.Input: body and trigger motors`.
- It needs the Game's TFM to carry the Windows SDK version (`net10.0-windows10.0.19041.0`). Tried over USB; Bluetooth not (impulse triggers historically fail on a PC).
- **RT is the gun's trigger**: a short full break on the shot (`SHOT_TRIGGER_RUMBLE`, shorter than the body's thump, so they read trigger then recoil) and a weak dry click on a refused one.
- **LT is the aim's.** While the lens is leaned in past `AIM_TICK_MIN_BLEND` **by the pad's own trigger** (the mouse button leans in as far, and a pad beside a mouse player must not tick) the aim is a ratchet: one faint tick per `AIM_TICK_STEP` of `Cannon.ElevationAim` and `Traverse`, at most one per `AIM_TICK_MIN_SECONDS`, so a fast swing never buzzes (#800: the first cuts "buzzed terribly"). It counts the **input's** motion, not the barrel's pose, which carries the rubber's spring and ticked with no hand on the aim. A detent marks a full lean (`PreciseAim.TRIGGER_FULL`).

## What gates the output

- **The window must be active**, read fresh each frame in `BS3DGame.Update` and passed to `AudioDirector.UpdateRumble`; menus answer the pad too, so gating on gameplay would mute them. **Play's rumble stops where play stops**: the frame a pause goes up or the level is left, `Silence()` zeroes it rather than letting a step decay under the pause menu. The result page still counts as play.
- **Vibration is a device state, not a frame state.** `UnloadContent` sends a last `SetVibration(0, 0)` and `PadMotors.TrySet(0, 0, 0, 0)`; on the frame the condition returns true the state is written once more, zero included, because Windows.Gaming.Input stops listening as the window leaves the foreground and a dropped zero could resume a motor.
- **Events are felt only while the hand is on the pad** (#817, the owner's rule). `GamepadRumble.HandOnPad` gates `Kick`, `Landing`, `KickTriggers` and `Hold`; it is set where play decides which hand is in use for the tutorial glyphs (`GameplayScreen.NoteDevice`: a key or mouse input is the keyboard's; a stick past its dead zone, a trigger or A is the pad's), so prompts and motors cannot disagree. **The `pad` launch argument pins only the prompts**: the hand reaches the motors with the first input in play, because `GameplayScreen` is built before the mixer exists, so a scripted run with no hands feels no game event (#852).

## The rest of the vocabulary (#800)

- **Menus**: a focus step is the lightest thing (`UI_STEP_*`; 0.04 s went unnoticed, since a motor spun from rest needs tens of milliseconds); a page turn is one pulse, the same whichever way it turns (`UI_PAGE_*`; two unlike motors cannot make the grips feel alike); confirm firmer (`UI_ACCEPT_*`), back softer (`UI_BACK_*`). Only for the pad's own presses.
- **The greeting**: the first time a pad is there within `SIGNATURE_WINDOW_SECONDS`, a signature plays; a pad plugged in later gets a short "ba-dum". Only while the window is active.
- **The carriage's rattle**: while the gun rolls (never a placed stance), one light-motor bump per `CARRIAGE_RATTLE_STEP` of `Cannon.RollTravel`/`SlideTravel`, at most one per `CARRIAGE_RATTLE_MIN_SECONDS`, strength between `CARRIAGE_RATTLE_LOW` and `CARRIAGE_RATTLE_HIGH` off a spare xorshift, **never the session's generator, which deals the balls**. The first cut ("extremely strong") used the heavy motor. Only for a player on the pad (`Tutorial.OnGamepad`).

## Felt and not felt

The owner's hand has judged: the shot and landing (kept); the ceiling step, page turn, aim tick and rattle (each too strong, lowered); the line loss and elevation stop (added; the knock alone was not enough, hence the hold); the release (pleasant but too strong, "it shakes my joints", 2026-10-05; rebuilt as the curve above in #810, which is unfelt). **Everything else is reasoning about the event's shape**, unfelt: star, brake, blast, trigger detent, greeting.

# The procedural score and the About page's player

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

Since #443 these compositions are not what a level plays ([The game's music](music.md)). They play on the About page's last page, a small easter egg, and `Game/Audio/ProceduralMusic.cs` still bakes the fanfares. Five authored themes (`MusicTheme.Pulse`, `Bohemia`, `Nocturne`, `Mural`, `Ember`) and the `menu` loop.

## The About page's player (#443, #464)

- `ProceduralJukebox` plays the six in slot order, looped and **whole**; its visualizer clock is pinned to the start of the chunk the voice is playing at each chunk boundary, as the Jukebox's `RecordingPlayer`'s is (#899, `ChunkQueue`; [Scene, Extras and the Jukebox](../game-shell/front-end-pages.md#scene-extras-and-the-jukebox)). A piece is **rendered when asked and let go when the player moves on** (0.5-4.7 s of synthesis, ~26 MB as 16-bit PCM). Launch it with `about=play` (`docs/game-shell.md`).
- **It plays while still being composed.** Every step loop calls `PublishProgress` once a bar (`RenderProgress`), publishing everything behind a **four-bar lookback** (`LOOKBACK_STEPS`) through **one volatile write after the buffer**; the player reads the count first, so a count above zero guarantees every frame under it is final. It feeds a `DynamicSoundEffectInstance` in half-second chunks and loops the piece itself once it all exists. **Next cancels the running render** (`RenderProgress.Cancel`). The `menu` loop cannot stream (`BakeMenu` folds its tail onto its head) and lands whole.
- ⚠ **The limiter was the obstacle**: `Limit` derived its drive from the RMS of the *whole* piece, which a stream cannot know. It is split into `ComputeDrive`/`ApplyDrive`, and a streamed render uses **`LIMITER_DRIVE`**, valid only because every piece renders bit-for-bit identically (#229). `MusicBake` asserts it every run (exit 3 if stale) and compares streamed with one-shot as 16-bit (exit 4 if more than a step out).
- The **visualizer** (24 log bands, 40 Hz-16 kHz, `Spectrum.Fft` shared with `MusicBake`) is **tilted 6 dB an octave about 1 kHz, measured**: untilted, the bass bars sat at the top and the eight highest were dead.

## Rules of the scores

- **Authored, nothing rolled (#229).** Passes once rolled tempo, key and ornaments; the owner ended it ("we should always pick what sounds best", which a roll cannot do). Each roll became a decision: one tempo (Pulse 128, Bohemia 116, Nocturne 96, Mural 120, Ember 134 notated / 67 felt, `menu` 100), one key, one progression, and a **placement** per ornament instead of a probability. Renders are **bit-for-bit identical**. All themes sit at -14.9 to -15.2 dBFS RMS (`menu` -19.5), so a switch is no level step.
- **Melodies are chord tones**, so a motif re-keys by one number and is consonant by construction (Mural's alone index a scale). **PolyBLEP** on every saw and square: a naive one folds inharmonic tones past Nyquist into wrong notes. **`SamplesPerStep` is the one place a step's length is rounded.**
- **A section's loudness is what is in it; the `Level` dial only trims.** A breakdown is emptier, not quieter. Outros end in silence so a join lands in silence.
- **Stereo (#119) is a seating plan.** *Low stays centre* (a panned low end loses half its power in a mono downmix). *Width comes from what is already detuned* (the lead's two saws twelve cents apart, the strings). **Seat by voice index, never pitch** (`note % 12` rotates with the key). **A part that can ever play alone stays centre** (the defeat melody, seated as one of a pair, leaned 1.41 dB). The limiter drives the interleaved buffer with **one linked number**, or the image pumps.
- **A low-band share is comparable only against the same filter** (here: one pole at 200 Hz over the mono sum). ⚠ **RMS, peak, DC, balance and correlation all look healthy on a piece missing a voice**; take a low-band figure.
- The `menu` loop's line on a bare square read as a **touch-tone phone**; `Keys` (a fundamental-heavy body, a bright **inharmonic** tine fading faster) fixed it. Its seam folds a bar past the loop point onto the head.

## The five compositions

- **Pulse**: A minor eurodance, ten eight-bar sections (prelude, intro, verse, chorus, breeze, verse, chorus, build, chorus, outro), the hook **one motif transposed across the chords**. Bass on the **off-beat eighths** (on the beat it doubles the kick and both go to mud). **`SubBass` is a separate sustained voice** (re-enveloping `Bass` would have re-voiced Bohemia, the lobby loop and the victory), held **past the bar line**, **ducked 55 % on each beat**, with upper harmonics because its 37-82 Hz fundamental is silent on laptop and phone speakers (the owner's monitor speakers likewise: a sound's identity sits in 300 Hz-4 kHz, see [The sound](sound-effects.md)). The **breeze** sections carry a second tune and no kit. **The shock** is one event: the build's last bar cuts to silence and an **E major brass chord** lands off the beat (the harmonic-minor dominant, resolving onto the Am every progression opens on); brass is heard nowhere else.
- **Bohemia** (#120): D Dorian, twelve sections, strings and a tuned drum over Pulse's floor. **A second subject** (stepwise, on the keys, where a dance track puts a breakdown) is a better reason to take the drums away than a gap.
- **Nocturne** (#162): a jazz trio, C major, four-note sevenths on ii-V. **Only the off-beat eighths swing** (`SWING` 0.62; a 2:1 triplet reads as a shuffle). The walking bass is an algorithm: root, two chord tones, then a chromatic approach under the next root. ⚠ **`UprightBass` and `Piano` were silent from #162/#210 until #218**: each guarded its render loop with `if (env <= ...) break;` while the envelope begins at zero with an attack ramp, so the loop ended on the first sample. **A `break` is safe only on a value already past its peak; use `continue`.** A returning voice needs re-levelling, because the limiter normalizes the whole pass (a piece gaining a bass leaves every bassless section far down).
- **Mural** (#264, recomposed #346): a bass-led groove, G major. #346 is the owner hearing it and rejecting it ("primitive to the point of reading as a joke, sad, sometimes outright off-key, slow, and with poor rhythm") **after every measurement had passed**. Fixes: melodies index a **scale** (`MURAL_SCALE`) over moving harmony instead of being respelled onto each chord; **I-IV-vi-V**; pad and stab up an octave (the tune was inaudible); tempo 108 to 120. ⚠ **The off-key was real**: the closing push glided from a fixed whole tone below, so F natural slid into G under a chord holding F-sharp (`MURAL_APPROACH` gives the diatonic step). ⚠ **The kick contradicted the 3+3+2 tresillo grid** (it played step 10); it plays 0, 6 and 11. Kept: the log drum with its **thirdless** riff (two bass notes a third apart under 100 Hz are mud). **Whether it is worth humming is the owner's call.** `ThemeFor` parses `dechovka` (the replaced polka) as an alias.
- **Ember** (#163): a rock ballad, E minor, ten sections. **The chords have no third**: distortion multiplies notes, and root-fifth-octave (2:3:4) puts every product on notes already in the chord where a third's do not, so `Guitar` renders the whole chord itself. **The build is the gain, not the fader**: each section's `Drive` goes through a waveshaper normalised at its output, so it changes spectrum, not level. The two double-tracked takes hard either side lean through the **cross-term** with the centred bass, not through either's level, so which take sits where is swapped twice a bar **by energy, not by count**.

## The rotation

`GameMusic.ThemeFor(named, index)`: with nothing named the position alternates Pulse, Bohemia, Nocturne, Mural, Ember; an **unknown name falls back to the rotation rather than throwing**. For recordings see [Families rotate](music.md#families-rotate-a-level-can-pin-a-recording-486).

## The fanfares

`PlayVictory`/`PlayDefeat` play on their **own** `SoundEffectInstance`, so `Stop()` on the theme does not cut the announcement. The victory is a recording since #482 ([The sound](sound-effects.md)); the bake below is the fallback for it and (#446) the defeat when the file is missing.

- **Victory is a eurodance drop**, not a herald's call (a lone trombone read as old and cheap): an optional snare-roll build, three drop bars of **I-vi-IV** (dropping bar three of I-V-vi-IV would lose the IV the vi sets up), a supersaw hook in the **3-3-2 tresillo**. It uses the theme's own `Lead`: **register belongs to the instrument**, and a trombone could not carry that register. It scales with the score (`FANFARE_FULL_SCORE`).
- **Defeat is the inverse**: minor, falling, slow, low and **no percussion** (a beat gives momentum). A good score that still lost gets a harmony and a resolution; a poor one **does not play its last note**. It is a **trombone** (`Brass`): its filter envelope opens with the note (a fixed filter is a synth pad), with breath on the attack, a sub an octave down and two filter poles. Below `DEFEAT_FULLER` it is three bars (**i-VI-i**: keep the VI the piece sags on and the i it lands on; #190), above it four.
- ⚠ **The closing chord needs room to ring past the last bar, and the two fanfares need different amounts** (`VICTORY_TAIL_STEPS`, `DEFEAT_TAIL_STEPS`): one shared constant clicked on the defeat's pad.
- **The fireworks duck under the fanfare** (`ProceduralAudio.FireworkDuck`, to `FIREWORK_DUCKED`), as a gain on the *next* play, not a per-frame walk over sounding voices (`BestPractices.md`).

# The trophy cup

Part of [The game: feedback](../game-feedback.md).

The result screen presents a trophy close to the lens, turning. It says *how well* the player did, as an object, where the fanfare says *that* they won and the fireworks answer it. Bronze and Silver are plain; Gold has handles (#232); the top tier, Diamond, is **crystal** rather than a fourth metal (#228). Since #429 every tier is a tall, jewelled chalice (stepped plinth with a drum of stones, fluted stem between two collars, gadrooned knop, a bowl with a band of stones under its lip) and the jewellery climbs with the tier.

- `Game/Effects/TrophyPodium.cs` owns the presentation and the ornament's layout.
- `Prazsky.Core/Render/TrophyMesh.cs` builds the cup's geometry and names the surfaces the ornament sits on; `TrophyOrnaments.cs` builds the stone, its setting and the bead.

## Placement and motion

- **It is placed against the frame, not the world.** The result page releases the camera onto a slow orbit of the island, so anything left standing in the arena swings behind the score panel within seconds; anchored to the lens it holds its place while the arena turns behind it, which reads as *presented*. The anchor is stated in normalised device coordinates (`NDC_X`, `NDC_Y`) and turned into a world offset through the camera's own projection (half-height `d / M22`, half-width `d / M11`), so it holds at any field of view and aspect; vertically it is aspect-independent (an ultrawide only adds width).
- **It is deliberately tall enough to reach into the page's own UI** (#226): a reward that kept its distance from the page read as exhibited in a case beside it.
- **`NDC_Y` is -0.15 because that is the highest that survives the whole dolly period.** At 0 the bowl is cut by the top edge at the dolly's near end (`SIZE` against the frame's half-height at `DISTANCE - DOLLY_DEPTH`, and `LEAN_ANGLE` swings the rim higher still). The earlier -0.30 was protecting the bowl by cutting the plinth, which is the right trade: a cut bowl is worse. Centring it would cost a smaller cup or a shallower dolly, the owner's to spend. #429's slimmer cup left placement and `SIZE` alone.
- **The dance is a turn, a bob, a lean that precesses, and a dolly** (`SPIN_RATE`, `BOB_RATE`/`BOB_DEPTH`, `LEAN_RATE`/`LEAN_ANGLE`, `OVERSHOOT`). The lean is about the cup's own Z and the turn about its Y afterwards, so the tilt travels round the axis rather than rocking like a metronome. The dolly is a real change of distance (the scale is not fudged) and, since the NDC anchor derives from the live distance, the cup holds its place in the composition while its perspective changes. #226 turned everything up on the owner's brief that the reward may be extreme.
- **Cost.** The whole presentation at #226's size is ~0.24 ms (meadow, `quality=high`, 1600x900 at ssaa 2x: 1.60 ms with `stars=0`, 1.84 with a cup); Gold draws the identical handled mesh opaque, so it needs no new number. #429's jewelled cup and the refracting crystal could not be separated from noise: the page orbits the camera and nothing pins it, so a figure finer than ~0.3 ms needs a pinned camera this page lacks (the crystal reads roughly +0.3 to +1 ms). The owner ruled that the result page is where frame time may be spent.

## The sharp foreground layer (#225, #242)

- The cup draws into `PostProcessPipeline.ForegroundTarget` and `CompositeForeground` tonemaps it onto the resolved frame **after** the defocus, so the arena, fireworks and confetti melt into bokeh behind a cup that stays crisp. The layer is linear radiance and the composite runs the same exposure, ACES curve and grain as the resolve, and the layer's bright pass is added to the bloom pyramid's head, so the metal keeps the glare its finishes were tuned against. The defocus is built from a scene the cup is not in, so no blurred copy stands behind the sharp one.
- ⚠ **The layer is filled in `BeginSceneDraw`, before the scene target is first bound.** MonoGame clears a `DiscardContents` target on binding, so a mid-frame swap of targets wiped the drawn scene and showed a **black result page on the MSAA path**. The placement is a fix, not a preference (see the defocus notes in `docs/rendering.md`). A change here must be captured at both `High` and `Medium` (ssaa 1 + MSAA 8x), not `High` alone.
- **Since #242 the composite is the last picture the frame draws, past the Myra desktop**, so the cup is over the UI (the owner is fine with it covering a panel). The weather and the celebrations are behind the cup; nothing 3D is drawn after the composite.

## Materials

### The traps that every material edit meets

- ⚠ **A metal's diffuse is dark.** Authored at a painted surface's diffuse (0.66 for gold) with the sky reflected at full strength on top, every cup came out of the tonemap as flat pale plastic with no highlight, and it **hid the Diamond's handles completely** (the shape information was there and saturation destroyed it: remember this when geometry appears to be missing). Metals have almost no diffuse; their reflectance colours them.
- **The tiers differ by finish; the top two by shape; the top one by material.** Specular power climbs with the tier (bronze a cast thing with the broadest highlight, gold polished). Bronze and Silver differ only in hue, which is the first thing a dark scene takes away, so Gold and Diamond take the handled mesh, told apart by silhouette. Diamond carries a small emissive term; at three times the shipped value the cup came out of the glare as a white blob.
- **The metals are stated once** (`BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` in `TrophyPodium`), and the body and every setting and bead in that metal read the same figures, so a setting cannot drift from its cup. The one exception is `GOLD_ON_CRYSTAL`, the Diamond's settings and beads: the old gold on the plain material, because the owner ruled the crystal stays exactly as it was.

### The reflection is the level's own dome (#232)

- `InstancedModelRenderer.SkyColor`/`GroundColor` default to flat `Vector3.One`; reflecting a uniform white sky off a uniform white ground is reflecting nothing, so every tier's Fresnel term was a flat wash (Silver read "flat white and matte"). The cup is enrolled through `TrophyPodium.Renderers` in `BS3DGame.SkyLitRenderers`, like the cannon's, because the cup stands over the exact level it was won on. **Every ornament renderer is enrolled too**, so a gold setting takes the same dome and floor as the gold cup it sits in (left out it opened as a flat white reflection).
- ⚠ **`SkyLightRig.ApplyToPresented` floors the rig and LIFTS, never SCALES.** The Moon's and cavern's rigs are near-black by design, and with the body dark Silver came back a near-silhouette. `ApplyToPresented` floors `SkyColor`/`GroundColor` to `PRESENTED_MIN_SKY_LUMINANCE` and `PRESENTED_MIN_GROUND_LUMINANCE` by *adding* a flat grey equal to the shortfall: scaling a near-black up to the floor is a ~350x multiplier that amplifies any colour impurity into a tint and is undefined at exact zero, while the lift costs nothing once a dome already clears the floor (every scene but the darkest).

### The three metals: polished metal (#602)

The plain material cannot mirror anything sharp, and the metals read matte. Measured on all scenes, 80-100 % of a bowl patch's pixels sat within 0.1 of its median luminance. Causes: the reflection was a linear ramp (a smooth cup mirroring a smooth gradient has no horizon and no sun); the dark domes' floor is a flat grey; and much of what was drawn was diffuse (gold's painted body outweighed its mirror, and on the volcano the ramp's lower half was brighter than its upper, so every cup was milky).

- **The technique is `InstancedPolishedMetal`** (`InstancedModel/PolishedMetal.fxh`, selected by `InstancedModelRenderer.PolishedMetal` on the three metal tiers and their metal jewellery): no diffuse; the tier's `SpecularColor` as F0 through Schlick; the rig's highlight at full strength; and a **sharp** environment: a horizon that is an edge, a sky 1.9x the dome's ambient at the horizon falling to 0.55x overhead, a ground part bounce colour and part sky light with a dark band just under the horizon, half the dome's saturation, and the sun as a glint. No cast or cloud shadow on the key (the cup is placed against the frame). Reflection strength is 0.90-1.00 (there is no diffuse under it to wash out).
- ⚠ **Each figure's note in the file records the cut it answers.** Taking the ground down as `MetalSky` does drew a **black bowl** on every dark dome (most of the bowl mirrors the ground); lifting it evenly gave **white paint** (the ground's shape is what the third cut added); and mirroring the meadow's cyan sky at full saturation turned gold **olive** (gold's F0 has almost no blue).
- **The outline (#640):** below `dot(N, V)` 0.22 the whole result fades to 12 % of the alloy's F0. The grazing mirror showed the sky behind the cup and dissolved the rim into pale wedges; the owner chose a drawn dark edge over keeping the alloy's hue or a dark ground in the mirror.
- **The crystal is untouched, provably**: it draws `MainPS`, its jewellery keeps `GOLD_ON_CRYSTAL`, and the new technique declares no uniform and is included last, so all 38 programs the effect compiled to before came out byte-identical. The cannon's and the drain's gold rims differed only where two runs of one build differ.

### The crystal (#228, #231, #271, #426)

Transparent crystal that is only transparent is a ghost; these are what make it glass.

- **Alpha is per surface and the depth write is off.** Just under a third each, so a look through the bowl crosses near wall and far inside and lands near half, while the one-wall stem stays at a third: glass that gets denser where there is more of it falls out of the geometry. With the depth write on, only the nearest surface of each pixel survives and the cup is one flat film. This tier only exists because the foreground layer is composited by premultiplied alpha.
- **It is a dielectric**: `Metalness` 0.06 lifts normal-incidence reflectance from the shader's 4 % to about 0.10. The face is nearly clear and the edges mirror, which is Fresnel and the entire shape language of glass (a metal reflects the same at every angle: a mirror with a hole in it).
- **`SpecularAlphaWeight`** (`InstancedModel.fx`, 1 everywhere else) keeps the reflection out of the alpha attenuation: alpha says how much of what is *behind* a surface shows, and a reflection is light off the *front*. Attenuated, a 38 %-transparent cup keeps 38 % of its sparkle and reads as a coloured film.
- ⚠ **The blue goes in `EmissiveTint`, not the diffuse.** The diffuse is premultiplied by alpha and then sRGB-decoded; 0.34 at the shipped alpha reaches the light as ~0.015 of linear radiance: black. The first crystal was a white ghost for that reason. The diffuse is authored high (a colour at a third of itself) and the small `EmissiveTint`, added in linear radiance, carries the rest.
- **The environment goes up, not down: 0.85.** #228 turned it down on the metals' argument; at `Metalness` 0.06 the material reflects 0.088 head-on and rises to 1 only at grazing, so the strength paints the **edges** (silhouette, lip, every ornament edge) and not the face. A ghost is bright and even; glass is mostly the background with bright edges, so the body came down (diffuse ~40 % of #228's, emissive a third).
- ⚠ **No facets, ever (#271).** A 24-gon rim was refused, and so was keeping the cuts in the normals alone on a round silhouette: **visible edges are edges whether geometric or shading.** Every tier draws one smooth revolve. (Visible cuts are the point on `OmniWheelMesh`'s rollers, never here.)
- **It refracts** (#426): the cup used to be composited by coverage and never saw the frame behind it, so what showed through was dimmed and tinted, never bent, and read as fake. How it bends is under "One layer is exempt from it" in `docs/rendering.md`. `REFRACTION_DEPTH` (0.55 at the cup's rest size, scaled with the cup through the reveal and dolly) is strong enough to flip the background inside the bowl and stem; a nudge read as a wobbly pane. Dispersion is 0.025 (0.06 drew rainbow noise down the fluted stem).
- ⚠ **The ornament draws first, opaque, writing depth**, because the crystal body blends without writing depth: drawn after the stones it lays glass over the far-side ones, which then show *through* the cup. On the metal tiers the order is free.

## The ornament (#429)

- **What each rung carries**: Bronze, eight amber stones round the plinth's drum. Silver, ten sapphires on the drum and beaded mouldings along both edges of the drum and the band. Gold, the beads, alternating sapphires and rubies on the drum, twelve big sapphires and rubies round the band with small emeralds between (`BAND_COUNT`), eight emeralds round the bowl's calyx (`CALYX_COUNT`). Diamond, Gold's whole jewellery **in gold** on the crystal (stones floating in glass read as bubbles; set in metal mounts they read as jewellery).
- **Stones are separate instanced meshes**, because a stone is a different material and one renderer is one material (built into the cup, every sapphire would be gold). Each is a unit mesh on its own +Y; `TrophyMesh.Ornament` sizes it onto a surface the cup names: `DRUM_RADIUS` and `BAND_RADIUS` (straight walls, so a stone sits on exactly that radius) and `CALYX_Y`, whose curved flare `TrophyMesh.OuterSurface` reads off the densified profile, not the chord between two authored rings. Gold is six draws (body, three stone colours, settings, beads); instance arrays are allocated once.
- **Stones are cabochons**, smooth domes: the Crown of Saint Wenceslas the owner named is set with polished cabochons, and facets would bring back the visible edges of #271. They are dielectrics with their colour in a small `EmissiveTint` (a dark dome loses a diffuse to sRGB decoding; a sapphire black under the Moon is not a sapphire), and Fresnel makes a dome read as polished stone.
- **A beaded row breaks only where a handle is, and that is the band** (#450, #724). A bead half inside the handle's tube reads as a burr on the casting. `TrophyMesh.HandleHalfAngle(radius, y, clearance)` measures, from the handle's own centreline and tube radius, how far either side of the handle's plane a ring comes within `clearance` of the tube; `BeadRow` asks it with a bead's radius plus `HANDLE_BEAD_MARGIN`. The lower band row needs no gap and runs all the way round; the upper stops short either side of azimuth 0 and pi. A handle retune moves the gap with it (the old hand-picked angle could not).
  - ⚠ **The drum's rows take no gap**: the drum is at `y` 0.028-0.110 and the handle's lowest point is 0.580. `BeadRow` once applied the clearance to all four rows, so Gold and Diamond lost ten of the drum's ninety beads in two arcs for nothing; Silver, built without handles, always closed all the way round, which proved the skip was the whole cause. The clearance is an argument to `BeadRow`: true for the band rows, false for the drum's.
  - The small emerald at azimuth 0 and pi stays skipped (`EMERALD_CLEARANCE`; the handle sits on it).

## Geometry traps

- **The silhouette was refused three times.** (1) One point at the knop and a flat disc for a foot read as an eggcup on a washer: the knop is four points (five since #429) and the plinth two courses; no material change fixes a silhouette, which is read before a surface. (2) Once the cup reached into the page's UI (#226), straight runs between creases read as **chords** (a low-poly silhouette at presentation size). No number of axis facets fixes that, because the side-on silhouette *is* the profile; the smooth runs go through a centripetal Catmull-Rom (`TrophyMesh.DensifyProfile`, `PROFILE_SUBDIVISIONS` samples a span, authored rings untouched, every crease crisp). (3) #429's refusal was *plain*: a wide, low bowl on a short stem is a sports-day cup. The profile was redrawn taller (lip 0.64 across against 0.88) and every change of section is now a moulding, because an unbroken run reads as turned in one pass.
- **Flutes and gadroons** (12 on the stem, 16 on the knop) are waves around the axis on **smooth normals**, eased in and out under the collars, so there is no edge in them (#271 stands). The normal is the cross product of the reeded surface's own two tangents, window slope included, because where a band fades in the surface tilts along the axis too. They are why the axis is 96 facets (a wave wants several vertices across it: eight per flute, six per gadroon); the handle's tube is 20 sides.
- **The vertex budget is not a ceiling any more (#429).** `MeshBuilder.Build` picks the index width as `LatheMesh` does: 16 bits up to 65 535 vertices, 32 past that (every executable runs HiDef). The plain cup is 76 608 vertices, the handled one 86 448.
- **The handle is a cubic Bézier.** It must meet the bowl at both ends whatever the profile does between them, and a circular arc did not (one end buried, the other hanging in the air). A Bézier is stated by its ends and its control points then say how it bows; the cubic lets it rise towards the lip and come down into the upper root like a trophy's ear. It is thin (0.025), reaches 0.46 from the axis and tops out at 0.97.
- ⚠ **"Buried by construction" was false (#228).** Measured against the *densified* profile (the surface actually drawn): there is **no anchor on this profile that buries a full-thickness tube**. The bowl's wall is ~0.05 thick where a handle can reach it and the solid base ~0.04 deep, against a tube 0.084 across. So the tube **tapers** (0.025 to 0.015 over the last 3.5 % of the sweep; 12 % left it surfacing into the band at 57 % thickness, reading as a stalk), the upper root is planted in the **raised band** (the one place the thin upper wall has depth; the inside wall is drawn thinner behind it), and the lower root in the solid base under the bowl's floor, which #429 raised to give it metal. The roots are **capped**: an open end reads through the crystal as a pipe with the far side of the room down its bore. Margins to keep when the profile or handle changes: both end rings at least 0.012 inside the body (shallowest points 0.0174 and 0.0134) and no tube point within 0.0135 of the hollow.
- **The underside is a vault (#502)** because the glance up (#430) shows it. The profile starts 0.108 up at the axis and springs off a flat standing ring (the outer 0.034 of the foot, at `y` 0) whose inner edge is a crease, so from below the foot reads as a ring with a dome inside. Depth is bounded by the metal above it (thinnest place 0.028, where the dome springs under the foot's step onto the drum), measured on the densified profile. From the glance's angle it is a grazing view and reads as a dark recessed edge, since there is no light under a cup; a deeper recess is one number.

## Result-page test levers

`stars=<0..4>` rates the `result` page (the cup is a function of the rating; three is the only tier a scripted run reaches otherwise). `lost` makes it a FAILED page (#238; it implies `result`) and uses the real failure text `GameplayScreen.Rules` produces, because a stand-in of a different length is a different layout. `nextlocked=<stars|sequence>` (#397; implies `result`) shuts the next level by one lock or the other, with real figures (the longest name ahead, a total two short of the gate), so the widest the sentence gets is what is photographed.

⚠ **The fail page has neither a plate nor a scrim**, and the palette's rule for `MENU_TEXT_DIM` is "asides, always on a dark plate". The failure line was `FontBody` at that grey and came out the least legible thing on the screen; it is heading size at full white now. Check any new line on this page over a bright lit arena, not a dark one.

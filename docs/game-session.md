# The game: a session

What a level actually is while it is being played: the simulation, the cluster, the gun, the level files and the score.

Part of the BS3D documentation. CLAUDE.md holds the project overview, the build commands, the ball grid and the repo-wide conventions, and says which of these documents covers what.

## The game (Game)

The shipping product, founded as a thin executable over the shared libraries rather than as a fork of the Testbed monolith. It is split along the line between what outlives a session and what *is* one (#65): `Game/BS3DGame.cs` is the **host** — the frame, the device and content, the shared setting every screen stands in, the post-processing and the screen stack — and `Game/Screens/GameplayScreen.cs` is the **session**, which owns the gun, the shot, the simulation and the level. Both borrow the instanced ball shader, the procedural `City`, the shared `SceneRenderer`'s self-lit backdrops, the `SkyDome`, the `CloudField` and the linear-radiance pipeline from `Prazsky.Core` exactly as the other two executables do. It builds `InstancedModel.fx`, `Sky.fx`, `Glare.fx`, `Tonemap.fx`, `ShotTrail.fx`, `Fireworks.fx`, the twelve scene shaders (`Sea`/`Savanna`/`Desert`/`Acacia`/`Flame`/`Birds`/`Mountain`/`Snow`/`Spray`/`Meadow`/`Forest`/`Space`), **all eighteen** `Skyes/SkyDome*.dae` and the overlay's `Fonts/segoeui.spritefont` out of the Testbed content directory (the island's marble, `GameObjects/Ground_8.png`, was built here long after both islands had moved to procedural `SurfaceTexture`s — dead content, removed). The only files it owns are the menu's three TTFs — **Anton** for the loud type and **Inter** for the small print — which are `EmbeddedResource`s rather than content (see "The front end" in `docs/game-shell.md`).

Since #72 the session is **partial across nine files**, all in `Game/Screens/`. `GameplayScreen.cs` holds every field, the constructor, `CoveredChanged`, `Update` and `Draw`; beside it are `.Rules.cs` (what a landing does, when a level is cleared or lost, the ball census, the result), `.Session.cs` (a session's lifetime — install, the field's derived figures, tear-down), `.Ripple.cs` (the wave a landing sends through the cluster), `.Input.cs`, `.Ceiling.cs` (the descending plate's state machine), `.Camera.cs` (the pose, precise aim, the fit solve, the recoil stroke), `.Physics.cs` (the Bepu world and its fixed step) and `.Draw.cs` (the balls in the frame). **No state moved** and `Update` did not move, both deliberately — see "Both halves are partial across several files now" in `docs/game-shell.md` for what that split is and is not, and read the partial's own doc before working in it: each names the extraction it is staging for and the invariant that survives there.

What it is: the round stone island the gun stands on with the Testbed's **glass drain funnel** in its middle, standing in **any of the eleven settings** — the same `SceneKind` set the Testbed and the map editor draw, picked at random each launch and switchable from the menu — under a sky of **drifting procedural clouds**, with a **hanging cluster** (a stepped square pyramid, apex down, in four colours) hanging from the Testbed's own **translucent glass ceiling**, the procedural cannon with its magazine showing through the barrel's slot, the shot at `SHOOT_SPEED` (200 u/s) with its launch smear, each scene's own point lights, and the recoil below — which moves the gun as well as the camera. Post-processing is the Testbed's own: supersampled HDR target → glare → ACES tonemap, with the same constants, so the two agree — including the sea's **underwater murk**, which arrived with the drop cinematic (the one thing here that takes a lens below the waterline).

**The game runs a real Bepu simulation** — the founding slice's biggest deliberate omission, and it is gone. The cluster is bodies held to each other and to a kinematic glass ceiling by `BallSocket` constraints, so it sways and a shot shoves it; a shot is a dynamic body thrown down the bore; the island's drain is a collision mesh balls run down and fall through; and a group of three or more touching balls of one colour lets go and falls, taking anything it was the last anchor for with it. See "Physics in the game" below.

**The cluster is a level off disk**, read through the shared `Level`/`BallsMap` loaders from the files a `LevelSet` orders — see "Levels in the game" below.

**Clearing the field wins the level and advances through the set**, gated on the level's minimum score — see "Levels in the game" below.

What it still deliberately does **not** have: no aim feedback beyond precise aim's crosshair (#47). It is no longer silent — the shot, the landing and the fireworks are synthesized from raw PCM at startup (#46, "The sound" in `docs/game-feedback.md`), and a level plays over a synthesized eurodance loop ("The music" in `docs/game-feedback.md`). The score, the streak and the balls left are on screen while a level runs (#60, "The in-play HUD" in `docs/game-feedback.md`), the outcome is a result screen over the stopped frame (#59), and a level can be lost as well as won (#58) — the budget running out, or the descending glass pushing the cluster past the death line.

The first pass over it after it was founded closed four gaps against the Testbed — the cluster hung from nothing, the island was a flat grey band, the sky was a flat purple wash, and shot balls stuck where they touched and chained into an icicle pointing at the camera. All four are above; the icicle is below, along with the traps each one turned out to carry.

- **Recoil and camera shake.** `Prazsky.Core.Camera.CameraShake` is the game-agnostic half: `Kick(strength)` on firing, and it produces **offsets only** — a rotation triple (pitch/yaw/roll), a lens displacement, a set-back along the view and an FOV punch — so a kick can never accumulate into the pose, and at rest every output is exactly zero. It models *two* things because a gun does two things: a **directional recoil** (thrown back along the view, muzzle rising — not random, which is what separates recoil from a generic wobble) that recovers quickly, and a **random rattle** that decays a little slower. Both decays are **linear in time, not exponential**, so the shake genuinely ends instead of leaving a permanent sub-pixel jitter; the amplitude is the trauma *squared*, because a linear decay spends most of its life in the middle amplitudes and reads as a lingering wobble rather than a jolt. The rattle's phase runs off accumulated seconds (two sines at incommensurable frequencies), never off a frame counter — a per-frame random would shake faster on a faster machine.
- **`RecoilCamera` is its own `ICamera`, and the reason is roll.** It lives beside `CameraShake` in `Prazsky.Core.Camera` (hoisted from the Game in #77 — nothing in it was game-specific). `BasicCamera3D` rebuilds its view with a fixed world up, which is exactly right for a level horizon and exactly wrong for a camera being knocked sideways; `RecoilCamera` keeps the base pose (which the game rewrites every frame from where the gun stands) separate from the shaken one, rotates the view basis by the shake and **rolls the up vector about the view axis**, so the whole frame tips. Rolling is what makes the eye read "the camera was hit" rather than "the aim moved a little", so `MaxRoll` is the largest of the three angles. Measured at full strength: 40 ms after a shot the mean frame difference is ~9 %, and by 200 ms it is back under 1 % — a hard jolt that is gone before the next shot. `BaseRoll` is the *deliberate* roll on the same axis (the drop cinematic's tilt), added to the shake's rather than applied separately, so a gun fired mid-tilt still reads as a kick on top of the tilt.
- **The camera's ceiling was lowered, and the gun took over the difference.** At full strength the shake read as the *lens* being hit rather than as a gun going off, so every dial of `CameraShake` is scaled by one number in `Initialize` (`CAMERA_SHAKE_SCALE`, 0.45) — the shape of the response is kept, only its ceiling comes down, and that is the one number to turn. It is the **ceiling** that is scaled and not `RECOIL_KICK`, which stays 1: `Kick` accumulates and saturates at 1, so a fractional kick would let two shots in quick succession climb straight back to the response that was too strong. What replaces it is the gun's own recoil (`CANNON_RECOIL_BACK`/`_DECAY`): the barrel is thrown straight back along its bore and slides home over ~0.24 s, carrying the balls loaded in it, because the barrel's draw matrix (`Cannon.BarrelWorld`) and the queue's placement (`Magazine.Pose`) are both handed the same `CannonRecoilBack()` — a **positive** scalar, since both shared helpers subtract it along the bore, and a scalar because neither owns the stroke's shape or its decay (only this executable animates one at all). Squared in the stroke, so the throw is instant and the return eases — the same reasoning as the shake's amplitude. It is **drawing only**: a shot leaves along the true aim on the frame it is fired, before the barrel moves, so nothing about where a ball goes depends on it (the trail stays anchored at the un-recoiled muzzle, ~1 unit from the drawn one at the peak, which is invisible against a 7-unit smear).
- **The camera pose** is the Testbed's game camera, and since #76 literally the same expression: `GameCameraFit.CameraPosition`, back from the field centre along the gun's own bearing (`Cannon.StandBearing`) and `CAMERA_HEIGHT` below its trunnions, looking at the cluster. The bearing is **flattened to the horizontal** for the same reason as in the Testbed — taken straight from the gun's offset it tilts down by however far the gun stands below the cluster and eats the camera's height. The stand-off and the aim height are solved per level and per display, not tuned; see "Levels in the game (Game)".
- **The shot is a swept sphere test, not a point test.** At 200 u/s a ball crosses several diameters per frame, so a point test at the end of the frame tunnels clean through the cluster; `TryHitCluster` solves for the **moment of contact** instead — the quadratic in the step parameter whose smaller root is where the two spheres first touch. It first ranked candidates by their *clamped closest approach*, which is wrong in exactly the case that matters: on the frame a ball first comes within reach it is still approaching, so every candidate's closest approach lies past the end of the step and clamps to 1. The whole field ties, and "first touched" then falls out of the array order rather than the geometry. Cheap either way — a few hundred quadratics per flying ball.
- **A landed ball snaps into the lattice; sticking it where it touched is what made the game read as broken.** `BallsMap.PutBallAtClosestEmptyPositionNextTo` puts it in the free cell touching the ball it hit that lies nearest the contact point, so it lands flush in the packing. Stuck at its raw contact point it stands off the surface — and since `_clusterBalls` is also what the next shot is tested against, each ball then sticks to the one before it and they chain into an **icicle pointing back down the line of fire**. Three things follow from doing it properly:
  - The `BallsMap` is now kept alive as the truth about the cluster (it was a local in `BuildCluster`), and `_clusterBalls` is a flattened copy of it — each ball carrying its cell — rebuilt by `RebuildClusterBalls` whenever a ball lands. Rebuilding the lot rather than appending is what keeps the **neighbours'** occlusion right: a ball that arrives also boxes in everything it arrived next to.
  - **The layout hangs at the top of a taller field**, exactly as a map file's does, so the empty levels underneath are room to grow downwards. Because a level's height is its index over √2, the layout would ride that many levels higher, so the cluster offset (`_clusterWorldOffset`, solved per level by `FitFieldToMap` — see "Levels in the game") hangs the field's top where the frame wants it: the grid is the truth, and the offset is applied where the lattice frame meets the world and nowhere else (the general-purpose `MapToWorld`/`WorldToMap` pair this used to go through is gone — see "Physics in the game" for the two crossings that replaced it). What constrains `CLUSTER_EXTRA_LEVELS` is that **`FIELD_LEVELS` must come out even**, i.e. the field's topmost level must be one of the shifted (odd-index) ones. `BallsMap.Center()` centres the map on that top level and offsets by its bounding box's *half-extent* less a ball radius — on a shifted level, whose cells run from 0.5 to N−0.5, that lands exactly on the origin; on an unshifted one it lands half a unit off the axis the gun orbits and the camera looks down. (It was 12 + 6; it is 9 + 7 now that the layout is a nine-level pyramid.) The old rule of thumb that `CLUSTER_EXTRA_LEVELS` itself had to be even belonged to a layout whose shape was written in raw cell indices; `BuildCluster` measures each level's width against the *shifted* position of its cells instead, so the pyramid comes out the same at either parity. **Extra room in X/Z is *not* free the same way** — the same half-extent offset only lands on the origin when the top level's occupied cells start at index 0, so insetting the layout laterally would silently push the whole cluster off centre by the inset.
  - **The first level is a stepped square pyramid, apex down, in four colours.** Widest at the top level (the full `CLUSTER_X` × `CLUSTER_Z` base) and half a unit narrower on every side per level down, so the side count steps 9, 8, 7 … 1 — half a unit, not a whole cell, because consecutive levels are offset by +0.5 in X and Z and shrinking by a cell would put every second level off the axis and zig-zag the flank. `LEVEL_BALL_TYPES` holds four of the eight types and **the magazine draws from the same list**, since loading a colour that is nowhere in the cluster is a shot that cannot be spent; a fixed `LEVEL_SEED` makes it the same level every run, which until map files arrive is the whole of its authoring.
  - **The first ring is not always enough.** The ball a shot reaches first is on the cluster's outer face, and where that face is the field's own wall there is no cell beyond it — so the pocket around an edge ball fills after a handful of shots and every ball after that was silently eaten (measured: 12 of 21 shots on one fixed aim). `TryFindCellInSecondRing` widens the search to the free cells touching the balls that touch the one it hit, nearest the contact first: local by construction, so the ball never lands somewhere it could not have rolled to. That took the same test from 9 of 25 attaching to 25 of 25.
- **The landing preview, and why the field's edge needed one (#70).** The field is a **box**, and the levels shipped with it have **no lateral margin at all** — `One.json` is `sx`/`sz` 10 against a layout occupying x 0…9, z 0…9, and `Two.json` is 12 against 12 — so every ball on the cluster's side face *is* on the field's wall and the room a map gives is only downwards. Aim into a pocket in that wall once both rings are full and the shot sticks nowhere: the ball bounces off, falls through the drain, and costs a ball from the budget **and** the streak (`RemoveFallenBalls` scores it a miss), for a refusal the player had no way to see coming. Three things were considered and rejected before the preview. **Widening the search** to a third ring is the defect #70 was opened for, arriving from the other side — the locality is what keeps a ball from attaching visibly behind what was aimed at. **Refunding the shot** is silent, reads as a bug, and the shot count is what drives the ceiling's cadence. **Giving the field margin** moves the edge without removing it, and it is not free: the glass plate is fitted to `StageSizeX`/`StageSizeZ`, so it would hang visibly wider than the cluster, and the camera fit would stand further out and shrink every level. So the edge stays where it is and becomes **legible** instead.
  - **One function decides, twice.** `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics.ShotPlacement` owns the rule; the contact handler asks it when a ball really touches and attaches with what it gets back, and the preview asks the identical call every frame from the barrel's own line. This is a correctness requirement, not tidiness — #70 records that a line drawn over behaviour it does not share would disagree with the outcome and make the frustration *worse* than showing nothing. `BallsMap` grew query counterparts (`TryFindEmptyCellNextTo`, `TryFindEmptyCellInSecondRing`, `TryFindEmptyCeilingCell`) so the question can be asked without writing, and the `PutBallAt*` methods are thin wrappers over them. That retired a real trap on the way: the ceiling placement used to fill its `out` cell in from the rounded contact *before* testing bounds and occupancy, so a refusal handed back a plausible-looking cell — and a caller testing the cell rather than the returned position indexed the structure array out of bounds or overwrote a live ball.
  - **A swept sphere, not a ray.** The shot has a radius, so it touches the first ball whose surface its own surface reaches; a thin line down the middle of the bore misses a ball just off to the side that the ball itself would clip. Testing the aim against each structure ball grown by the sum of both radii is exactly that, in closed form. It walks the live `PhysicsBall` array rather than the simulation, for two reasons: it is the same array the attach path consults, so the two cannot disagree about which balls exist, and a Bepu sweep would want a shape, a pool and a hit handler per frame where this wants a few hundred dot products and **allocates nothing**. What it cannot promise is the stepped simulation's own answer — the shot travels 1.667 units a step and attaches on the first step that overlaps, and the cluster sways through the ~0.1 s of flight — which is why it is drawn as a ghost rather than a promise.
  - **The ghost is the signal; the crosshair only confirms it.** A mostly-dissolved ball in the cell it would land in, in the colour actually loaded at the muzzle — the useful question is not only "does this stick" but "does it stick *next to two more of its own*", which a neutral grey would hide. It rides the shader's existing **dissolve**, a noise cut over 7³ cells of the ball's surface rather than a transparency, so it needs no blend state, no sorting and no shader change: opaque geometry with half of itself missing, which reads as "not there yet". Drawn at the cell's **ideal** lattice position and not offset by the local sway the solve took out, because the cell is where the ball comes to rest and chasing the sway would jitter it. The ghost appears exactly when a shot sticks, so its absence covers every way one does not — a full pocket, open sky, the glass — with no case where it says the wrong thing. The crosshair reddens only when a refusal is **certain** (the aim reaches a ball and neither ring has room), and only in precise aim, because that is the one mode where the screen's centre is where the gun points: in the overview the lens looks *at* the cluster rather than along the bore, so a mark in the middle of the screen would name a spot the barrel is not aimed at. `PREVIEW_DISSOLVE` is tuned against the **overview**, where the ghost is a few dozen pixels — 0.62 was easy to miss there, and much lower reads as a ball that is already present, which would send the player aiming elsewhere.
  - **The beam is what the overview gets instead of a crosshair** (`Prazsky.Core.Render.AimBeam`). A dashed line of light out of the muzzle, crawling towards its far end, ending where a shot fired now would arrive. It exists because the overview lens looks *at* the cluster rather than along the bore, so before this the only clue to where the gun pointed was the barrel's foreshortened angle — and because it is what carries the **refusal** to that mode: a red beam stopping on the ball it cannot stick to says there what the reddened crosshair says in precise aim. Its far end carries the meaning, so it ends at the **touch** and not at the cell — that is where the ball actually stops, and it puts the tip on the surface it strikes instead of pushing it through into the cluster. Aiming at nothing it is drawn `openEnded`, fading out over its tail so the line dies away rather than stopping at a phantom.
    - **The beam and the crosshair hand over to each other**, rather than both being up at once: the beam's opacity is `1 - PreciseAim.Blend`, the inverse of the crosshair's own. That is a measured need and not symmetry for its own sake. Leaning in over the barrel the lens looks *along* the bore, so the beam foreshortens into a stack of dashes piled over the very cell it is pointing at — it was obscuring the ghost in the one mode where precision is the point, and it had nothing to add there either, because a crosshair on a lens aimed down the shot ray already *is* the trajectory. Each mode ends up with exactly one signal.
    - **Its width and brightness were set by looking, and the first guesses were wrong by a lot.** 0.085 half-width and 1.35 radiance were reasoned from the ball's radius and from the smear's boost, and the beam came out **invisible** at the overview stand-off — the mode it exists for. It was then bracketed from the other side (0.45 and 8 are unmistakable and far too heavy) and settled at 0.15 and 3.5, which is *above* `LaunchSmears`' boost even though a permanent guide ought to be quieter than a shot going off: the smear is briefly enormous where this is permanently thin, and a thin additive line over a lit cluster needs the radiance to read at all. Anything derived from a ball's size rather than from what a few dozen pixels of additive line look like over that cluster should be expected to be wrong the same way.
  - **It borrows `ShotTrail.fx` from `LaunchSmears`, and that had a consequence worth knowing.** The shader is already a camera-facing billboard between two world points that fades each segment in over its first quarter and out over its last — so a *short* segment of it is a dash with soft ends, and the beam needed no shader of its own and no new blend state. But `LaunchSmears` set the trail's two widths **once in its constructor**, correctly, on the reasoning that a compile-time constant need not be re-sent (`BestPractices.md` §1 names those two parameters as its own example). That stopped being true the moment a second component shared the effect: parameter values belong to the `Effect`, the two want different widths — the smear tapers, the beam is parallel-sided — and whichever constructor ran last would have decided how both look. Both now push them once per `Draw`, which is per frame per component and still not per primitive. **A shared effect turns any "set it once" into a race**, and this is the second component to share one.
  - **Shown exactly when a shot would leave the barrel**, which is what keeps it from promising: silent under a drop cinematic, and silent once `_score.OutOfShots` — the *same* test `Shoot` refuses on, so the two cannot drift. A level being cleared or lost is deliberately not on that list, because firing through the collapse is allowed.
- **The ambient-occlusion direction is a sum of unit vectors and must be divided by `BallRenderSet.MAX_OCCLUDERS` before the shader sees it.** Handed over raw it is up to twelve times too long, the shader's dot against it saturates over most of the ball, and every surface ball wears a hard-edged black crescent instead of the soft inward shading that makes the cluster read as one body rather than a heap of spheres. **That is history now rather than a rule to hold to, and it is worth keeping because of what it cost:** the Testbed divides — as all three executables do since #76, through the shared `BallRenderSet.OcclusionTarget`, which is the only code path that can build the vector at all and divides inside it; the game did not, and it cost the cluster its whole look, the crescents going from every surface ball the moment that one path became the only one. Nothing can differ about it again, because there is nowhere left for it to differ (see "The rest of the hardware, in one copy" in `docs/testbed.md`).
- **The stone ring is a dish now, and that is a big-map fix before it is a look.** The walkable top between the drain's mouth and `ArenaIsland.FLOOR_RADIUS` falls `ArenaIsland.DISH_DEPTH` (1.2, ~6.4° over the 10.7-unit run) from its outer arris to the rim, drawn and collided from the same figures (`IslandMesh` takes the depth, `FunnelPhysics.Build` takes the same constant), and the drain's rim, its gold bead and the pit's mouth all sit that far below `TOP_Y` with it. It exists because the flat ring only ever worked for small maps: a ball that lands on flat stone stays where it stops, and the wider the field the larger the share of released balls that came down outside the mouth's radius 14 and simply parked there. On the dish they roll in — Bepu spheres carry no rolling resistance (friction resists sliding, not rolling), so the shallow grade is plenty, and the drain finally swallows what a big map drops. Verified with the Testbed's `End` release of `Full.json`: the ring stays clean while the funnel floods and drains, where balls used to rest across the stone. The gun floats above the ring (it has no drawn carriage and its trunnions are a fixed −6.4), the play camera and every camera floor key off `TOP_Y` and only gain clearance, and a ball below the anchor saturates the ground-darkening term to 1, so nothing else moved.
- **The island is a washer again, because the drain arrived to fill it (#48).** The platform's bore came over from the Testbed's rim radius (`ArenaIsland.FUNNEL_TOP_RADIUS`, 14) before the funnel did, which left a 28-unit hole dead centre under the hanging cluster, open onto the city below; it was pinned to 0 until the funnel could fill it, and it is back at the rim radius now that it does. The glass cone, its two gold beads and every figure of them were the Testbed's unchanged, and since #75 they are **literally the same object**: `ArenaIsland` builds the whole assembly and both executables draw it, in the same order — the opaque rims with the opaque scene and the one-sided glass after them, inside one `CullNone` sandwich, before the glass ceiling. The two things this file used to say had **not** come over have both since arrived. There is a real Bepu simulation here, and `FunnelPhysics.Build` hands it the drain's own surface as the island's whole collision floor, so a ball landing on the stone rolls down its dish into the cone exactly as it does in the Testbed (see the dish bullet above). And the dark **pit shaft** is drawn too, in the five `SceneRenderer.IsSolidTerrainScene`s — in the city, which is the scene this was first written against, the drain simply looks through onto the canyon below and no shaft is wanted. Note the play camera sits 0.6 above the island, so the drain reads from it as a gold ring low across the bottom of the frame with the dark dish behind it — it opens up as the camera moves, the same as in the Testbed's game mode. The platform is not merely like the Testbed's, it is the same `ArenaIsland` — one `IslandMesh` profile, two materials over `SurfaceTexture`'s procedural stone and concrete, one set of figures (see "The setting, in one copy" and "The city and the glass arena" in `docs/scenes.md`). The trap CLAUDE.md flags elsewhere is real and was exactly what left it a flat grey band at one point — **without a `DetailTexture` the renderer falls through to the plain technique and every `SlabSize`/`CavityStrength`/relief setting is silently dead** — and the texture it was then given, `Ground_8.png`, turned out to be black over half its canvas, which is why nothing is loaded here now.
- **Precise aim (ADS) is the Testbed's — since #76 the same `PreciseAim`, not a port of it — and so is the reason for it.** Held on the **right mouse button** (or the gamepad's left trigger), the lens leans in over the barrel — `BACK` behind the muzzle along `-aim`, `RISE` above the bore — and looks at a point *on the shot ray*, so the shot goes where the screen-centre crosshair points; `PreciseAim.FOV` (36°) is a modest 1.19× lean-in on `GAME_FOV`. It exists because how much angle onto the map the overview can give is fixed by where the camera stands, and a lens looking *along* the aim sees whatever the barrel points at, head-on. One reversible scalar `PreciseAim.Blend` eases 0→1 with the `Magazine.Slide` idiom and `BlendedPose` returns `Lerp(overview, leaned, Blend)`, so at 0 the overview pose comes back bit for bit and an interrupted hold never snaps. One real difference from the Testbed, and it is why the component hands a pose **back as a value** rather than writing a camera: `RecoilCamera` takes a base pose rather than `BasicCamera3D`'s ordered setters, so the FOV → position → target ordering that file insists on has no counterpart here — the drop cinematic goes on lerping over the returned pose, and the shake is applied on top of whatever came out. The focus gate is the *same* rule by a different route, not a difference: the Testbed rebuilds its held flag with an `IsActive` term every frame, while here `UpdateAim` simply does not run while the window is inactive, so `_adsHeld` is cleared in the inactive branch instead — `PreciseAim.Step` still runs, which is how the lean eases out rather than dropping. Either way a gamepad trigger — which XInput reports to an unfocused window — cannot keep an alt-tabbed game leaned in. `PreciseAim.MIN_Y` floors the lens a unit above the island: aiming steeply up, `-aim` points down and the set-back would otherwise drop it through the stone and show the island from underneath.
- **The FPS line and the crosshair.** `Prazsky.Core.Render.InfoRenderer` draws the FPS as a `DrawableGameComponent` (so `base.Draw` puts it out last, in display space, with its own `SpriteBatch`), off the Testbed's own `segoeui` font built out of the Testbed content directory; **F12** hides it, the key that hides the Testbed's text. It is authored for 2160p and scaled by `Viewport.Height / 2160`, so `RecomputeScale` has to be re-run on a resize *and* on a fullscreen switch. The crosshair needs no bitmap — four bars struck from a 1×1 white texel, scaled the same way — and since #76 it is not the game's own either: `Prazsky.Core.Render.Crosshair` makes its own texel and the Testbed draws the same bars, `Bitmaps/Aimer.png` having been retired with the hoist (and the host's white pixel with it, the crosshair having been its only consumer). It is drawn **only while precise aim is leaning in**, fading up with the blend, because that is the only pose whose lens looks along the shot; in the overview a screen-centre mark would point at nothing in particular. That is the caller's decision and not the component's — it takes an opacity, and the Testbed passes a plain 1 in the free mode where the lens *is* the shot.
- **The clouds are the Testbed's weather, minus its ambient half.** One `Sky.fx` entry, `_sky.Effect`, and `_clouds.ApplyTo` on *both* the sky and the instanced effect each frame off the wall clock — which is what keeps the cloud the player looks at and the shadow it throws the same cloud. The ten cloud look values it used to keep of its own are `CloudField`'s since #75, pushed once by `ApplyStaticParameters`. The cloud shadow on the balls, city and island then costs nothing extra: `CloudSunlight` is already called unconditionally in `InstancedModel.fx` and returns a flat 1 while `CloudCoverageGain` is 0. `CloudField.ApplyPalette` is **not** optional despite the fixed dome — the two cloud colours have no defaults, and unset means a black deck. The overcast ambient flattening (`SkyLightRig.StepOvercast`) is deliberately left out: its palette is authored for daylight and is brighter than this dusk dome's own, so lerping towards it would *lighten* a night city as the weather thickened.
- **Controls.** Mouse aims (always — game mode is the only mode), LMB / Space / gamepad right trigger fire, **RMB / gamepad left trigger hold precise aim**, A/D traverse the carriage, F11 toggles fullscreen, F12 hides the FPS line, **Escape pauses**, and while a drop cinematic is running the gun does not answer at all — LMB / Space / gamepad A skip it instead (see "The drop cinematic" in `docs/game-feedback.md`) (quitting is a menu item now — a game that closes the instant Escape is tapped is one that loses a game). `quality=<low|medium|high>`, `ssaa=<n>`, `exposure=<f>`, `nocap`, `fullscreen`, `scene=<city|sea|savanna|desert|mountain|meadow|neon|forest|space|dream|cavern>`, `sky=<1..18>`, `logfps`, `celebrate` (fire the victory display on the front end — see "The victory fireworks" in `docs/game-feedback.md`) and `lasers` (pin the floor alarm's laser net on while a level is played — see "The floor alarm" there too) on the command line — the first three seed the settings screen, which then owns them; the scene names are the Testbed's — literally, since #75: `SceneRenderer.TryParseScene` is the one parse for all three, so one script drives any of them unchanged. `scene=`/`sky=`/`logfps` exist for measurement: without pinning, the scene is a different one of the eleven (`SceneRenderer.SceneCount`) every launch and nothing is comparable (see "The quality tier"). **`play`** (testing only) drops straight into the first level, skipping the title card and the menu, through the very pop-and-push a player's Play click takes — the session's `[field]`/`[camera]` figures and its endings only reach stdout once a level is built, and building one honestly needs a mouse on a Myra button, which a scripted run does not have. Every edge-driven input — Space, LMB **and** the gamepad trigger, which XInput reports whether the window has focus or not — is skipped for one frame after focus returns, or the click that refocuses the game arrives against a stale "released" and fires a shot the player did not ask for. `ssaa=1` falls back to 8× MSAA on the scene target the way the Testbed does (multisampling antialiases geometry edges but not shading, so it only earns its memory with supersampling off) — that is the setting a weak machine reaches for first, and it had no antialiasing of any kind. `SetGraphics` reads `GraphicsAdapter.DefaultAdapter.CurrentDisplayMode` rather than `GraphicsDevice`: it also runs from the constructor, before the device exists, and reading the device there made `fullscreen` mode-switch the display down to 1600×900 instead of filling it.
- **A resize re-solves the framing, and it must also drop the aim's baseline (#52).** `OnClientSizeChanged` and `SetGraphics` both go through `UpdateCameraAspect`, which re-runs `FitCannonAndGameCameraToLevel` — the fit is checked on *both* frustum axes and only the vertical one is aspect-independent, so a resize has to re-solve the stand-off rather than just the projection (measured on `One.json`: 4:3, 21:9 and 16:9 all settle at 28.0 out, the vertical axis binding at each, which is what "Resolution and aspect" in `docs/rendering.md` says should happen). What that alone still got wrong is the **aim**: it is a delta measured against the viewport centre, and a fullscreen switch moves the centre while the cursor is still parked on the old one — so the first frame after F11 read a delta of half the screen and slammed the barrel into its elevation clamp, leaving the gun pointing at the sky. That is what the bug report's "camera position and other relevant constants fail to recompute" actually looked like on screen. So the resize does not only re-solve the fit: `UpdateCameraAspect` hands the change to the session (`GameplayScreen.OnViewportChanged`), which calls `MouseAim.Invalidate()` **before** re-solving, so the next frame recentres and applies nothing — the same thing `CoveredChanged` does on the way into play, and `MouseAim.Initialized` gates the shot test **explicitly** (it used to sit inside that block by nesting rather than by intent), so no phantom shot goes off either: on the frame the baseline is dropped there is no aim to fire along. It is reached through `_gameplayScreen?.`, which is exactly right: with no session there is no aim baseline to invalidate.
- **Two traps worth remembering when testing it.** The cursor is captured and recentred every frame, so **any external `SetCursorPos` reads as a huge aim delta** — the `screenshot.ps1` title-bar click yanks the aim to the elevation and traverse clamps, which is a test artefact, not a bug (a shot then flies 45° wide of the cluster). Compensate by setting the cursor a known offset from the viewport centre afterwards; the aim moves ~0.002 rad per pixel, and there is no auto-recentre to fight. And the keyboard is polled **once per frame**, so a `keybd_event` down+up sent back to back is never seen as a press and the shot silently does not happen — the key has to be *held* across a frame (~40 ms is plenty). Both cost a wasted round of screenshots before they were spotted.

## Levels in the game (Game)

The game builds its cluster from a **level file**, not from the procedural pyramid it shipped with. What orders those files is a **`LevelSet`** (`Prazsky.BS3D/Levels/LevelSet.cs`, beside `Level`): a JSON object marked `"format": "bs3d-levels"` listing entries **in play order**, the first entry being the first level. `Game/Levels/Levels.json` is the set; `Game/Levels/One.json` and `Two.json` are the levels — **One** a 10×10×15 field of 315 balls in eight colours, **Two** a 12×12×18 field of 364 in six, a stepped square ziggurat standing on its point (plates of 144/100/64/36/16/4 stepping in one cell a side per level down) whose colours are laid out in 3×3 blocks *rolled per level*, so every level has findable groups and no single colour stacks into a vertical column that would go all at once and read as a bug. Two is authored tighter than One rather than merely bigger: 45 shots against a ceiling that steps every 4. They are plain `Content` copied beside the exe (`CopyToOutputDirectory`), **not** MGCB assets — they are data read with System.Text.Json at runtime, and being editable next to the binary without a rebuild is the point.

- **The order lives in the set, never in the directory listing.** A filesystem has no order worth relying on, and reading the directory would silently promote whatever was dropped into it. Entries are objects rather than bare strings so a level can carry a display name now and par, unlock rules or a preview later without breaking a file that predates them; paths are relative to the set's own directory (captured in `LevelSet.Directory` at load), so a set and its levels move together and nothing depends on the working directory. `LevelSet.Load` validates and throws, mirroring `Level.Load`, and `IsLevelSetFile` is the same cheap marker probe `Level.IsLevelFile` is.
- **A level file or a plain map file, both `.json`, so the loader probes** — `InstallLevel` runs `Level.IsLevelFile` exactly as the Testbed's `DeserializeMapFromFile` does. A full level's scene and sky are applied; a plain map leaves whatever the player picked from the menu standing. `One.json` is a plain map (`sx`/`sz`/`l`/`b`), so it plays in whichever scene is up.
- **Clearing the field wins the level, and winning advances through the set.** The condition is `GetBallsCount() == 0`, tested **only on a landing** (`CheckLevelCleared`) — that is the one thing which can empty the field, and polling it per frame would declare a level authored with no balls won before a shot was fired, and keep declaring it. The end is then held for `LEVEL_CLEARED_BEAT`: balls leave the map at *release* time while their bodies are still falling, so ending the level the instant the last group is cut takes the collapse the player earned off the screen before they see it. The completion bonus is awarded at the moment of clearing, not at the end of that beat, so a shot fired into the empty field meanwhile cannot eat the balls they finished with.
- **`BuildLevel(index)` is the one path** a first Play, a New Game, a retry and an advance all take. `FinishLevel` picks between three outcomes: below the finished level's `minScore` the level is replayed (cleared but not passed); past the last entry the campaign is complete, said plainly and returned to the front end — the session is torn down so the main menu offers a new game rather than "Continue" into a level that is already over — and otherwise the next entry is built. It runs **last** in `Update`, because it may tear the session down and build another in its place, and anything above it that frame would then be working against a simulation that no longer exists.
- Every branch of `FinishLevel` is a placeholder for the result screen (#59), which is where the player will actually be *told* which of the three happened and choose what to do. Until then a missed gate simply replays, which without a screen is an unbroken loop — Escape → Main Menu is the way out, so it traps nobody, but it is the strongest argument for doing #59 next.
- **A level's rules ride on its set entry** — `shots` (the ball budget), `minScore` (what unlocks the *next* level) and `ceilingStep` (shots between descents of the glass), all optional. `LoadLevelSet` logs every entry's rules at startup, spelling each absent one out as what it means ("unlimited shots, no score gate, ceiling holds"), so an inconsistently authored set is obvious in one place before a level is played. All three are consumed now (#55/#56/#58): the budget is the HUD's "balls left" and, unspent, the completion bonus; the gate is what `FinishLevel` weighs the score against before advancing; and `ceilingStep` steps the glass down every so many shots towards a fixed death line, which is one of the two ways a level is lost (`[ceiling] Step to …` and `[level] Lost … ClusterReachedLine` are its log lines). `shots` and `ceilingStep` are **not independent**: the descent is driven by shots against a fixed death line, so the geometry implies a budget of its own, and `LevelSet` deliberately does not try to cross-check them (the implied limit is the game's geometry, not the set's data).
- **Nothing about a missing or broken level is fatal.** `LoadLevelSet` runs once in `LoadContent`, so a bad set is reported at startup rather than at the moment Play is pressed; the set staying null, or a level failing to parse, falls back to `BuildFallbackMap` — the procedural pyramid, kept for exactly this. Measured: with the set present, `One.json` gives 315 balls / 1088 constraints; with the `Levels` directory gone, the fallback gives 285 / 1377, which are the figures the pre-level build had.
- **The field's size and depth are no longer constants.** `FitFieldToMap` derives them per level: the field's topmost level hangs at `FIELD_TOP_Y` — **or higher, when the field is deep enough that its bottom level would otherwise start past the death line**: the top is `max(FIELD_TOP_Y, CEILING_DEATH_Y + FIELD_FLOOR_MARGIN + topLevel/√2)`, with the margin a ball's radius, so a ball hung in the field's lowest cell rests its surface exactly on the line, alive and one descent from loss. The whole field is playable by construction however deep it is, and the empty levels an author leaves under a layout are the level's *starting clearance*, not dead space past the line — pinning the top unconditionally meant any field 17 or more levels deep (its bottom more than ~15.8 level-steps below its top) started with cells below the line, and a 30×30×30 map with only its top levels occupied **lost the level on the frame it was built** (`ClusterReachedLine`, a ball at −6.36 against the −5.5 line, before a shot was fired). Measured after the rule: the same 30-level map hangs at top Y 15.51 with its lowest ball 8.99 above the line and plays; `One.json`, `Two.json` and the fallback are in the figures below. A `[field]` line logs each level's top, floor and lowest-ball clearance, which is the figure an author sizing a deep map actually wants. `_ceilingY`, `_clusterCentreY` and the glass plate's footprint follow from the solved top. `FitCeilingToMap` rebuilds the plate's mesh and renderer per level (through the host's `RebuildCeilingRenderer`, since the glass is lit with the rest of the scene), which is why `SkyLitRenderers` tolerates a null ceiling renderer and `BuildLevel` runs `ApplySkyLighting` again afterwards — the same dance the Testbed does around its own `FitCeilingToMap`.
- **`_clusterWorldOffset` gained an X and a Z, and that is the parity trap finally handled rather than avoided.** `BallsMap.Center` offsets by the top level's bounding-box *half-extent* less a ball radius, which lands on the origin only when that level is a shifted (odd-index) one whose cells start at index 0. An **odd** field tops out on an unshifted level, whose cells run 0…N−1 rather than 0.5…N−0.5, and the whole cluster then hangs half a unit off the axis the gun orbits and the camera looks down. That used to be a rule the hard-coded field satisfied by hand; a level is authored elsewhere and cannot be held to it — `One.json` is fifteen levels deep — so the residual is measured off the centred top level through `GetRealCenteredPosition` and folded into the offset the physics builder and the contact handler already take. No new frame crossing, and it is exactly zero for the old even-field pyramid.
- **The magazine's colours come off the loaded map** (`CollectLevelBallTypes`), not from a fixed list: loading a colour that is nowhere in the cluster is a shot that cannot be spent, and an authored level picks its own palette (`One.json` uses eight). `RandomBallType` stopped being static for it. An empty map falls back to the default four.
- **The camera and the gun are solved to the field, not tuned to it** — `FitCannonAndGameCameraToLevel`, run on every level load and every aspect change, over the shared `GameCameraFit.Solve` (since #76 the Testbed's copy and this one are the same solver; see "The gun's hardware, in one copy" in `docs/testbed.md`). `_gameCameraDistance` and `_gameCameraTargetY` replaced the two constants: the stand-off is **bisected** for the smallest distance at which the field's eight corners, the top of the glass and a box around the gun all land inside *both* frustum half-angles, and the aim height comes from bisecting the resulting elevation range, which is what centres the subject. The gun is then placed `GameCameraFit.CANNON_CAMERA_STANDOFF` in front of the lens — off the *lens*, not the field, because the magazine showing through its slot is really a HUD element and has to keep its size on screen — held off by two lower bounds (clear the field's footprint; keep the resting aim inside the elevation clamp) whose binding one changes with the level. Each solve depends on the other, so they alternate three rounds, and it very nearly settles on the first — but not exactly, and both copies used to claim it did: a sub-0.1 residual survives to the third, in both directions. What this file assigns is three values the solve *returns*; the gun is moved **once**, after it, and no longer parked at each intermediate guess.
  - **The one thing that differs between the two callers is the Y frame**, which is exactly why the field's vertical extent goes into `Solve` as two arguments and never as an assumption. In the Testbed the lattice frame *is* the world frame, so the field's floor is `y = 0`; here level 0 sits at `_clusterWorldOffset.Y`. The empty growth levels are deliberately inside the fitted volume — the cluster grows down into them, so they have to be in frame before the first ball lands there.
  - Measured, all at the default window and 16:9 unless noted, and re-measured after the field-floor rule above: 9×9×16 (the fallback) → 27.6 out, aim Y −0.2, gun orbit 13.5 and 10×10×15 (`One.json`) → 28.0, aim Y 0.0, gun orbit 13.5 — **both bit-identical to before the rule**, whose two branches meet just past a 16-level field on purpose; 12×12×18 (`Two.json`) → 30.9, aim Y 0.9, gun orbit 16.0 (its field was reaching 0.86 past the line and now hangs 1.36 higher — the one shipped level the rule moves, and its ceiling pressure was nominal either way); 20×20×20 → 36.8, aim Y 2.6, gun orbit 21.7 (raised 2.78; it was 32.9 / 1.5 / 18.0 under the old pinning). `One.json` at 21:9 gives **the same 28.0**, which is the vertical axis binding at every targeted aspect exactly as "Resolution and aspect" in `docs/rendering.md` says it should — the extra width goes to scenery.

## Scoring (Game)

The rules for what a shot is worth live in **`Prazsky.BS3D/Scoring/ScoreKeeper.cs`** — no MonoGame, no Bepu, no map, no frame. It is fed three events and answers what a HUD and a result screen need. That placement is the point: the rules are the part of this game most likely to be argued about and retuned, and they have no business being another few hundred lines inside a `Game` subclass.

- **Two kinds of released ball, and the difference is the skill.** `BallsConstraintsBuilder.ReleaseSameTypeCluster` used to return `cluster.Count + disconnected.Count`, one number; it now returns a **`BallsReleased`** pair. *Matched* is the group the shot completed (10 points); *orphaned* is everything that fell afterwards because that group was its last anchor (**20** — double, because cutting a support and collapsing half the cluster is the shot worth learning). Orphans cannot happen without a match: the disconnected walk only runs once a group has actually been taken out.
- **The streak.** A scoring shot is awarded at the multiplier standing when it lands and *then* raises it, capped at ×5 — so the shot that starts a streak scores at ×1 and it is continuing that pays. `Multiplier` therefore reads **forward** (what the next scoring shot will get), which is why `Landed` returns a `ScoreAward` carrying the multiplier actually applied rather than leaving a popup to read the property after the fact. Any shot that drops nothing resets it to ×1.
- **The three events do not arrive together, and wiring any two to one place would be wrong.** A ball is spent the instant it leaves the barrel (`Shot()`, in `Shoot`); what it *did* takes a physics step or more to resolve (`Landed`); a shot that misses everything resolves later still (`Missed`).
- **Every shot resolves exactly once, and `ContactEvents.IsListener` is that guarantee for free.** A shot ball is a listener from `Shoot` until its shot is over — on attaching, on touching something it cannot attach to, or on being culled. So `BallContactEventHandler` reads *whether it was still listening* before the unregister that its own static-contact branch performs, and the kill-plane cull in `RemoveFallenBalls` reports a miss only for a ball that is still a listener. No per-ball state anywhere. **A miss is resolved at the moment the ball strikes the island, not when it is finally culled** — the player knows they missed instantly, so the streak has to break then; the roll down the stone dish and out through the drain that follows is scenery, not scoring. It also closes the rare case of a shot that comes to rest on the island anyway (a pile propping itself up near the mouth) and is therefore never culled at all.
- **The handler reports; it does not score.** `BallLanded` and `ShotSpent` are events on it, and the game routes them through methods that read the `_score` field rather than binding the instance — the scorer is replaced per level, and a handler holding a stale one would score into a keeper nothing reads.
- The budget comes from the level's set entry (`shots`, null = unlimited). `Shot()` clamps at zero and **refuses nothing**: ending a level when the budget is spent is the lose condition's job (#58), not the scorer's.
- `BaseScore` is what the score would have been at ×1 throughout, so `StreakBonus` is exactly the difference — the line a result screen wants. `CompletionBonus`/`AwardCompletionBonus` (unused shots × 50) exist for #56/#59 and are called by nothing yet; a failed level must never get them.
- Nothing displays any of this. A temporary `[score]` line logs each scoring shot until the HUD (#60) exists — a score that is counted but never seen is indistinguishable from one that is not counted.

**The magazine may only load a colour that is still hanging.** `RecountBallTypes` refills `_ballsOfType` off the map every time a shot lands, and `RandomBallType` draws evenly among the types whose count is above zero. That is the **next-colour policy handed to the shared `Magazine` at construction**, and it is the whole of the difference between this magazine and the Testbed's, which draws uniformly over all eight because its cluster is whatever map was dropped on it: answering "what is still alive?" needs a map, a level and its rules, so the shared type takes the answer as a delegate rather than learning any of the three. Without it the queue goes on offering a colour the player has eliminated, and a ball of a colour that exists nowhere can never match anything — it can only be parked, which grows the cluster the player is shrinking, wastes a budgeted shot and in the limit makes a level **unwinnable**. Recounted rather than maintained incrementally on purpose: the walk is the field's cell count (1500 for `One.json`) and runs per landing, not per frame, while a running total would be three places to get wrong (release, attach, second-ring fallback) for microseconds nobody can measure. A colour with fewer than `MINIMUM_CLUSTER_SIZE` left is arguably dead weight already and is deliberately *not* dropped — that changes which levels are solvable, so it is a difficulty decision rather than this fix.

**A ball already in the barrel whose colour has just died is re-coloured where it sits** (`Transmute`, over the shared `Magazine.Recolour`), not left to be fired at nothing. Letting a stale queue play out costs the player up to `Magazine.SIZE` shots on colours that cannot match, through no fault of their own; this is a game and not a simulation, so re-colouring a loaded ball is allowed. The colour changes **immediately** and the animation is cosmetic — firing mid-transition gives the new colour, never the dead one. The replacement is drawn at random from what survives; picking whichever colour would help most would quietly make the game easier, which is a difficulty decision and not a fix.

- **The transition is a dither cross-fade, and that is a technical choice before it is a stylistic one.** A ball's colour is a **per-draw uniform** (`GetDiffuseTintByType` selects the bucket), so there is nothing per-instance to lerp: blending two colours means drawing the ball **twice**, once in each bucket. Two coincident *translucent* spheres would need depth sorting and would come out muddy — a dither cut needs neither, and both draws stay in the opaque path.
- `ModelInstance` therefore grew a **`Dissolve`** float (TEXCOORD6, a `Single` rather than another `Vector4` — one scalar is all it is, and this rides on every instance in the scene, the city alone being over a thousand). **Zero draws the whole thing**, so nothing has to know it exists to opt out. Positive *goes* (keeps pixels whose noise is above it), negative *arrives* (keeps those below its magnitude); the two are exact complements, so `+t` and `-t` together cover every pixel exactly once. Only the ball pattern technique reads it; an unconsumed element in the vertex layout costs the other techniques nothing.
- **The noise is object-space, not screen-space, and this is the trap worth remembering.** A screen-space dither is laid down at the *supersampled* resolution and the box filter then averages it straight back into a smooth cross-fade — the pixelation the effect exists for would vanish at exactly the settings that make everything else look better. Cells in the ball's own space survive any resolution and turn with it. The hash has no `sin` in it, for the same reason the cloud field's has none.
- The cut is branchless (a select, not an `if`: `Dissolve` varies per instance and a branch would diverge inside one draw call) and sits at the top of `PatternPS`. At the settled zero it reduces to `clip(noise)`, and the hash is never negative.
- `_magazineTransmute` counts **down** from 1 to 0, so the dissolve's progress is its *complement*. Feeding the countdown straight in runs the effect backwards — the new colour arrives complete on the frame of the swap and the old one is never seen. That is exactly what the first build did, and only a zoomed screenshot showed it.
- **The shift is the shared `Magazine.Advance`'s, and the cross-fade state rides along on a hook.** `_magazineFrom` and `_magazineTransmute` are drawn in step with the colours, and shifting only the colours would leave a slot dissolving out of the ball behind it — so `Advance` owns the queue's shift and reports every `(destination, source)` pair to the `slotCarried` delegate wired once in the constructor, and this file carries its two arrays along the same path in the same order. The colour state stays here because *which* colours have died is a question about this cluster; only the knowledge that a queue moved is shared, and there is now exactly one place holding it. `Refill` fires the companion `slotLoaded` hook for every slot, which is what stops a level loaded over a mid-transmute session inheriting its half-finished dissolves; `Recolour` deliberately fires neither, because the old colour is the one thing this cross-fade must keep.

## Physics in the game (Game)

The Testbed's simulation, ported into `Game` (its own decision, taken after the founding slice shipped without it). What made the port cheap is that `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` was already complete: `BallsConstraintsBuilder` builds the constrained structure, attaches a newly landed ball, and — in `ReleaseSameTypeCluster` — **already implements the match rule** (`MINIMUM_CLUSTER_SIZE` 3) including the follow-up release of everything left unanchored, via `BallsMap.GetCellsDisconnectedFromCeiling`. Wiring Bepu therefore delivered "balls fall on a match" for free; it was never a separate job.

- **Three files moved out of the Testbed into `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` rather than being copied**: `Callbacks.cs` (the narrow-phase and pose-integrator callback structs, in class `Simu`), `ContactEvents.cs` (adapted from Bepu's demo) and `KinematicBody.cs`, plus `IContactEventHandler`, which was a loose interface at the bottom of `Testbed.cs`. All four were pure Bepu infrastructure with **zero** coupling to Testbed types — they only happened to live in its namespace. Two divergent copies of the contact material (`FrictionCoefficient` 1, `MaximumRecoveryVelocity` 2, contact `SpringSettings(30, 1)`) would have been exactly the kind of silent drift this repository keeps one copy of shaders and scenes to avoid. `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` needed `AllowUnsafeBlocks` for them.
- **The one place the game deliberately does not do what the Testbed does is the timestep.** The Testbed takes one `Timestep(min(frameTime, 1/60))` per rendered frame, which runs the simulation in **slow motion below 60 FPS** (at 30 FPS everything moves at half speed) and with a dt that varies with the display and the load above it — and Bepu's own guidance is to keep the step constant. The game accumulates the frame time and spends it in whole steps of `PHYSICS_TIMESTEP` (1/120 s), capped at `PHYSICS_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` so a frame that hitched drops the remainder instead of spiralling. It runs `IsFixedTimeStep = false` and offers `nocap`, i.e. precisely the configuration the Testbed's approach breaks under.
- **The frame question is the one that would have silently wrecked the port.** `Game` has a lattice frame and a world frame, offset in Y by the cluster offset `FitFieldToMap` solves per level (−4.9497 for the fallback field — the offset hangs the field's top at the frame's height, raised for a deep field so its bottom clears the death line); the Testbed has no such offset. The simulation lives in **world**, because everything else it touches does — the funnel floor, the ceiling, the muzzle a shot leaves from, the kill plane — and only *one* thing is lattice-framed: the positions `BuildBallsStructure` reads out of the `BallsMap`. So `BuildBallsStructure` took an optional `worldOffset` (the Testbed passes nothing and is unchanged), and it is applied to the body positions **and to nothing else**: `ConnectBalls`'s anchors are *differences* of two grid positions, so a uniform translation cancels out of them, and `ConnectBallToCeiling` reads only X and Z from the grid (its Y is the ceiling body's own). Applying it twice tears the structure apart on the first step; forgetting it on the ceiling body yanks all 285 balls five units up into the glass. `MapToWorld`/`WorldToMap` were **deleted** rather than kept: the crossings are now countable — the builder's offset, and the offset the contact handler carries to take a world contact down into the grid frame.
- **The cluster does not settle on its lattice.** The ceiling `BallSocket` anchors a ball's top (local +0.5) to the plate's bottom face (local −0.5), so the top level comes to rest one unit below the plate's centre and the whole rigid structure with it — half the clearance `CEILING_Y`'s "+2" looks like it buys. It is the Testbed's own behaviour and is kept for parity, but it means the contact point a landing ball is ranked against sits a unit above the lattice cells it is compared with, which biases attachment towards same-level cells over cells below. `CEILING_Y` is the figure to change if the cluster should ever hang exactly on its grid.
- **`BallContactEventHandler` is its own file**, and the split inside it is the whole point: Bepu runs contact callbacks on worker threads from inside `Timestep`, so `OnTouching` only enqueues, and everything that touches the simulation, the map or the listener set happens in `ProcessQueuedContacts` on the main thread. The order `Timestep` → `Flush()` → `ProcessQueuedContacts()` is mandatory — unregistering a listener is only safe once the per-worker adds collected during the step have been applied — and it runs **per step**, not per frame; since #76 it is `PhysicsWorld.Step`'s own order, with the per-step work handed in as a parameter, so a caller cannot take a step without it. The Testbed's version was ported with three fixes: no LINQ and no `Console.WriteLine` on the contact path, and the game's own second-ring fallback (`TryFindCellInSecondRing`) kept, since the first ring runs out where the cluster's face is the field's own wall.
- **A manifold offset is relative to the pair's *first* collidable**, not to either body's own, so the world contact is `pair.A`'s position plus the offset whichever of the two the shot ball happens to be. Both velocities are zeroed on attach, angular included: residual spin would drag the constraint anchors that are about to be created around with it.
- **The shot's collidable is built from the bare `TypedIndex`**, not from a `CollidableDescription` with a speculative margin, and that is load-bearing rather than sloppy: it is what stops the shot passing clean through the cluster, which at `SHOOT_SPEED` it crosses several diameters of in one step. **It is `ContinuousDetection.Passive` and not a sweep**, though — an *unbounded speculative margin* with the bounding box expanded by velocity, so contacts are generated at any distance rather than at a solved time of impact. That distinction is not pedantry: it is why a shot must be attached on `OnTouching` and never on `OnContactAdded`, and why the contact has to be anchored at the ball it was made against. Both are in `BallContactEventHandler`, with the measurements that forced them.
- **What is drawn is the simulation.** `ClusterCollector.Collect` — the shared walk since #76, one copy for both playing executables — goes over the `PhysicsBall[,,]` plus the shot and falling lists and takes each ball's world matrix from its body pose, **orientation included**, which is what makes the beach-ball pattern read as rotation. Two smoothings ride along: the occlusion is *eased* towards its target (a ball joins or leaves the lattice in one step while it has not moved, so taking it straight pops the shading of everything around a hole a released group left), and a landed ball is drawn *gliding* in from where it actually hit, since it is snapped to the nearest free cell and the constraints then drag its body several diameters. Each ball must be visited exactly once a frame — both advance state on the ball itself, as does the ripple — and that is now enforced rather than remembered: `BallRenderSet.BeginFrame` is the only way to open a collection and refuses to open a second one before the first has been drawn.
- **Falling below `KILL_PLANE_Y` is the only thing that removes a ball, and that is a deliberate parting from the Testbed.** The Testbed also culls any ball whose body has gone to sleep — cheap, and it keeps the scene tidy, but it means a ball that settles on the island's stone winks out in front of the player, which reads as a bug whatever it saves. Here a ball that does come to rest stays there and stays visible — a rare case now rather than the routine one, since the stone ring is a dish (`ArenaIsland.DISH_DEPTH`) that rolls a landed ball into the drain and out through the hole; what can still rest is a pile propping itself up near the mouth, and those fall asleep, leave Bepu's active set and are drawn but barely simulated. `RemoveFallenBalls` is only ever handed the shot and falling lists; handing it the structure array would delete the cluster. **The consequence is a trap, and it cost a level that could not be lost (#66): `_shotBalls` holds shot *bodies*, not shots in flight.** A ball leaves that list only by attaching or by being culled, so one that settles on the stone stays in it for the rest of the session — and the out-of-balls loss, gated on `_shotBalls.Count == 0`, could then never fire again: 0 balls left on the HUD and no result screen, ever. Anything asking "has every shot resolved?" must ask the resolution state and not the list's length — `AnyShotUndecided` reads the **contact-listener flag** (a ball stops listening at exactly the moment its shot resolves, and `Shoot` is the only place anything is registered, so every listener is a shot in the air) plus `Awake`, which covers the one ball the flag alone misses: one resting on another *loose* shot ball has touched nothing static, so nothing ever unregistered it. It bit on the authored 30-shot level and not on a 3-shot test set, which is the shape of every bug of this kind — the longer the budget, the likelier a shot has come to rest by the time it runs out.
- Measured: 285 balls and 1377 constraints at startup, and the frame rate is unchanged from the pre-physics build (vsync-capped) — this cluster is two orders of magnitude smaller than the Testbed's 3000-ball stress map.

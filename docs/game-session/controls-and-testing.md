# Controls, launch arguments and driving the Game

Part of [The game: a session](../game-session.md).

## Controls

- **Mouse aims; LMB / Space / pad right trigger fire; RMB / pad left trigger hold precise aim** ([Recoil, precise aim and the arena](recoil-and-presentation.md)). **A/D traverse** (the camera trails the turn), **W/S walk** towards the field and back ([The field, the camera fit and tall levels](field-and-camera.md)). **The pad's left stick does both** (#189) at a rate that follows its push (the push past MonoGame's own dead zone raised to `STICK_SPEED_EXPONENT` 1.5, #802), a full push being a key's full speed; a key wins on its axis. `PAD_WALK_DEADZONE` only decides which device the tutorial draws for.
- **E / pad X** swap, **Q / pad Y** brake, **R / pad right bumper** cutter ([Power-ups](powerups.md)).
- **F10** hides the FPS line, **F11** fullscreen, **F12** saves a PNG. **Escape pauses**; quitting is a menu item. In a drop cinematic the gun does not answer and LMB / Space / pad A skip it.
- Every edge-driven input (the pad trigger too: XInput reports it to an unfocused window) is skipped for one frame after focus returns, or the refocusing click fires a shot.

## Launch arguments

Full list: `Game/LaunchOptions.cs`.

- **Display:** `quality=<low|medium|high|ultra|potato>` (`potato` is the tier's preset row, not the Potato render path, which is the `potato` flag, #808), `ssaa=<n>`, `exposure=<f>`, `nocap`, `fpscap=`, `fullscreen`, `windowed` (a run's instruction, never written back, #455), `tenbit` / `tenbit=off` (the 10-bit back buffer forced on or off for the run whatever the panel reports) and `ramp` (a grey ramp over the lower third of the frame that shows the back buffer's depth to the eye) — both #859, "10-bit colour" in `docs/game-shell/window-and-dpi.md`. The first three seed Settings, which then owns them.
- **Scene:** `scene=<name>` (`SceneRenderer.TryParseScene`, one parse for all executables), `sky=<1..21>`, `sceneseed=`, `logfps`. Unpinned, the scene differs every launch.
- **Into a level:** `play` (the first level, through the pop-and-push a Play click takes: `[field]`/`[camera]` only print once a level is built, and building one needs a mouse on a Myra button), `level=<n or name>`, `levelfile=<path>` (a level outside the set; pins the run), `result` (a cleared level's result page, held back until the title card is gone; `lost`, `blockdone` and `stars=` stage its other endings, and `campaigndone` the campaign's, with [the finale](../game-feedback/milestone-endings.md#the-campaigns-finale-a-headline-and-one-message-880) in front of it, `finalemessage=<n>` pinning its message), `celebrate`, `lasers`, `tutorial` / `tutorial=demo`, `settings`, `board=<n>[:<page>]`, `highscores[=<chapter>[:all]]`.
- **Pinning play:** `seed=<n>`, `streak=` (until a shot breaks it, #845), `wildcard=<n>` (overrides every level's rule; the only test door to a wildcard), `powerups=swap:n,brake:n,cut:n`, `lineloss=`, `detonate=`, `balls=`, `wind=<scale>` (0 is still air), `logshots`, `logtilt` (the overview's tilt with the aim, a line a frame, #720).
- **Scripted input** (`ScriptedPlay`): `fire=`, `aim=`, `walk=`, `swap=`, `brake=`, `cut=`, `rmb=`, `shot=`, each `<t1,...>` wall-clock seconds, through the very call the key makes. **`cutshot=<s1,...>`** saves the frames at those seconds of the world's clock after a cutter strikes (#767): a strike cannot be framed on `shot=`'s clock (["Looking at it"](../game-feedback/cut-staging.md#looking-at-it)). **`swapshot=<s1,...>`** saves the frames at those seconds of a Swap's own picture (#871): the picture's age is held at each for a frame, so a swap's third of a second can be framed however long a capture takes (["Looking at it"](../game-feedback/swap-effect.md#looking-at-it)). **`revealshot=<s1,...>`** saves the frames at those seconds (0 to 2) of the floor net's opening reveal (#933): its clock is held at each for a frame, and each says the housing's turn and the net's light in numbers (["Looking at it"](../game-feedback/ceiling-and-floor-alarms.md#the-net-is-shown-before-it-warns-933)).
- **A level's buttons on the clock** (#709): `actions=<seconds>:<verb>,...`, each through the call its button makes and logged as `[actions] Testing: <verb> over <page>`: `retry` (the result page's Retry), `restart` (the pause page's Restart, made over the pause), `skip`, `next`, `menu` (the result page's Main Menu with one up, else the pause page's, which keeps the session) and `level<n>` (the picker's tile). A verb whose button is not up does nothing and says so. With `quitconfirm=<s>:quit` and `lineloss=` it walks every event the [play counts](play-statistics.md) count.
- **Filming** (#946): `capture=<folder>` with `capturefps=`, `capturefrom=`, `captureseconds=`, `campos=`/`camtarget=`/`fov=` or `campath=<file>`, `captureshake`, `captureaim` and `capturedemo`: see ["Capture: filming the game"](#capture-filming-the-game-946).
- **`userdata=<dir>`** keeps a run's save, settings, online identity and outbox out of the player's profile (`UserData.UseForTesting`). **Pass it to every scripted run**, or it writes the owner's real `Settings.json` and `Progress.json`. `Program` applies it before anything asks for the directory; it throws otherwise.

## Saving frames: `F12`, `shot=<t1,t2,...>`

`Prazsky.Core.Render.ScreenshotWriter` (shared; "Saving a frame" in `docs/testbed.md`) writes the presented frame to `Screenshots/` beside the exe and prints `[shot] <path>`. **The schedule is the whole point**: every external capture route can silently return the wrong picture (the lock screen, a covering window, a blank `PrintWindow`), and on a locked desktop no keystroke arrives. `F10` hides the overlay first. A capture is a ~0.1 s edge, never per-frame.

## Capture: filming the game (#946)

**`capture=<folder>` turns a run into a film of itself**: the frames of a window of game time written to the folder as `frame-000001.png`, `frame-000002.png`..., with a `capture.json` (rate, frame count, size, scene) beside them, and the run ends itself after the last. It is what the website's pictures and looping clips are made of (`Tools/web-capture.ps1` runs it and encodes the frames, [the website](../website.md#filming-the-game-946)). The code is `Game/Capture/` and `BS3DGame.Capture.cs`; a run without `capture=` holds a null where it would be and asks it nothing.

- **`capturefps=<15..240>`** (60): frames of game time a second. **`capturefrom=<s>`** (0) is where the window starts and **`captureseconds=<s>`** (5) how long it is; **0 is one frame**, a still. Frames before the window are drawn and not written, so the world is where it would be.
- **A stated camera** replaces the play camera's pose for the whole run, level, held still, with no kick of the gun's shake: **`campos=x,y,z camtarget=x,y,z`** with optional **`fov=<degrees>`** (the Testbed's spellings, world coordinates), or **`campath=<file>`**, a keyframed path ([the file](../website.md#the-camera-path-file)). Not both. `captureshake` lets the gun's kick shake a stated camera. The run's first captured frame prints `[capture] play lens: campos=… camtarget=… fov=…; gun at …; cluster centre …`, the play camera's own pose in the spelling the argument takes (the Game has no other way to learn the world's coordinates; the Testbed's `C` key is that for the Testbed).
- **`capturedemo`** is the benchmark's action (#1018, [The benchmark](../game-shell/benchmark.md): `GameplayScreen.StepBenchmarkDemo`, the gun firing by itself at balls whose group will fall, two cuts among the shots) and, unless a camera is stated, its lens circling the gun. It starts when the level's opening tour has handed the gun over, stepped on the capture's clock like the rest, and plays `BenchmarkDemo.SECONDS` (24 s).
- **`captureaim`** draws the aim beam and the ghost ball, which a capture hides as the benchmark does (nobody holds the aim of a clip); the aim logic itself runs either way, so a scripted cutter at a storey it may not strike is still refused.
- **What a capture implies** (`LaunchOptions.ImplyCapture`, each only where not given): `windowed`, `mute`, `nofps`, `nosplash`, `nofocuspause`, `quality=high`, 8-bit back buffer, `sceneseed=0`, `seed=946`, **`scene=space` and `preview=1`** (the front end, below), `play` when no level is named, and **`userdata=` a scratch folder in the temp directory** when none is given, so a level cleared on camera writes no save and sends no score. The tutorial's cards are off, and the session draws no HUD: no score, chips, strip, crosshair, skip hint or lost-level readouts.
- **No input reaches a capture.** The session takes the unfocused branch of its frame whether or not the window has the focus (a launch that does not take the keyboard is the norm, `Tools/quiet-capture.ps1`), so a hand on the mouse moves nothing, and **the scripted arguments are the only hands**: `StepScriptedHands` runs `fire=`, `aim=`, `sweep=`, `rmb=`, `swap=`, `brake=` and `cut=` in that branch, on the capture's clock, held off by a camera takeover as the focused frame's aim is. (`walk=` and `turn=` always sat outside the focus gate.) A script's seconds are **game time since launch**: `fire=10` fires in the frame whose clock reads 10 s, and each `[capture] frame n/N at game time t s` line, once a second, says where the clock is.

**The clock is a fixed step a frame, whatever the frame costs.** `CaptureRun.FixStep` sets `ElapsedGameTime` on the `GameTime` every screen is about to read (a settable property, so nothing is allocated) and the `Draw` after reads the same object: frame *n* is the world at *n* / `capturefps` seconds, `_wallClock` included, so everything on that clock (the scripted arguments, the scene's weather, the gun's recoil, the particles) is where it would be at that second. `CapturePlan.StepTicks` is rounded **up** to the clock's 100 ns, because the session spends its frame in whole physics steps of 1/120 s and a step a hair short would be paid out as a frame of one step and a frame of three. The frame is not paced either: no wait for the compositor, no limiter, no GPU flush, so a frame is as fast as it can be drawn and read back.

- **Physics in a capture** ([Physics in the game](physics-in-the-game.md)): the step cap rises from 4 to `CAPTURE_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` (16), or a rate under 30 would drop the remainder of every frame (24 frames a second is five steps, and the world ran at four fifths); and **the simulation runs on one worker thread** (`new PhysicsWorld(workerThreads: 1)`). With a worker per logical processor (24 here) two identical runs of a level split inside the first forty steps, measured as a checksum of every ball's position after each frame's steps; with one, the checksums of two runs matched on all 94 frames of the first 3.2 s, and so did the pictures of the clips below.
- **The frames are written off the render thread** (`CaptureWriter`): the render thread reads the back buffer into a buffer it rents (`BackBufferPixels.Read`, a stall of the pipeline: the run is paced by that) and hands it over; two to four threads below normal priority encode each to a PNG (`PngEncoder`: truecolour, Sub-filtered, fastest deflate, no graphics device in it, **4.5 MB a 1920x1080 frame** with the film grain on). Each writer thread has two buffers (four to eight in all), so a writer that falls behind holds the render thread (`Rent`) and memory does not grow with the clip. **The first update of a run is never drawn** (measured: a window from game time 0 begins with the picture after update 2, `[capture] writing … the first drawn after update 2`), so the files are numbered by the frames written and the window is the next `captureseconds` of frames *drawn*; an update inside the window that is not drawn is counted and said at the end (`N update(s) inside the window drew no frame`, and `undrawnUpdates` in `capture.json`).
- **Measured, the Meander level on the desktop** (RX 6900 XT, 3840x1600 display, 1920x1080 window, High): the Game ran 360 frames of lead-in and 480 written ones in **15 s** (`capturedemo`, 60 a second, the other session's game and a music render sharing the GPU), the script's whole run with the encode 61 s.

**Repeatable, and where it is not.** Two runs of the same arguments, measured (`compare.py`-style pixel comparison of the PNGs, 1920x1080):

| What | Identical frames |
|---|---|
| `capturedemo` on Meander, 8 s at 60 a second (shots, falling groups, two cuts, particles) | **480 of 480** |
| the same, 14 s at 30 a second | **420 of 420** |
| the meadow, the city and the savanna, 1 s with the demo firing | **30 of 30** each |
| space, 1 s with the demo | **30 of 30** |
| the cavern (`Chest`), 1 s | 10 of 30: **about 50 to 170 pixels differ** (at most 11 of 255) from the eleventh frame of the window |

- **Bepu on several threads is what made the first two rows impossible**: before the single-thread rule 0 of 420 frames matched, the differences in the cluster's balls alone (the sky, the gun and the scenery were pixel-identical).
- **The front end's random scene leaked into the level's frames**: the front end is on screen for the first update, before the level replaces it, and its scene and its preview map are rolled at random each launch. With two different front ends the meadow, the city and the savanna differed from the eleventh frame of the window (up to 33 of 255, in the sky and the far scenery); with one, none did. A capture therefore pins both (`scene=space`, `preview=1`) unless told otherwise. Why the leak shows from that frame and not at the start was not found.
- **The cavern's residue is not understood** (single pixels, ±1 to ±11, scattered over the whole frame, in any window, from the ninth to the eleventh frame): the build of its hall is off the frame (`CavernBackdrop.Prepare`) and `RoofAbove` and `ArenaLights` answer without waiting for it, but the differences are there at 20 s too, long after it is built. Film a cavern level twice if a single frame has to be exact.

**Traps.**

- A chapter's first level flies its opening tour first (the HUD is off in a capture, and a stated camera replaces the lens, but it takes game time and the gun is held): start `capturefrom=` after it or film another level. The result page, if the level ends in the window, is a page and is in the picture: the capture says `[capture] a page is up over the frame` once. It does not stop a level that ends.
- The window must not be resized or minimized during a run (the frames are one size; the run stops and says so). One folder holds one run: a folder that already holds `frame-*.png` is refused before a window opens, as is an argument that cannot be filmed (`[capture] refused: …`, exit code 2), and a run that could not write every frame exits 1.
- `campos=` and its kin without `capture=` are ignored with a line saying so.

## A resize re-solves the framing and keeps the walk

`UpdateCameraAspect` re-runs `FitCannonAndGameCameraToLevel`.

- ⚠ **It must also drop the aim's baseline**: the aim is a delta against the viewport centre, a fullscreen switch moves the centre under a parked cursor, and the first frame slammed the barrel into its clamp. `OnViewportChanged` calls `MouseAim.Invalidate()` **before** re-solving, and `MouseAim.Initialized` gates the shot test.
- **The walk is kept (#560)**: `Cannon.Refit(rest, min, max)` keeps it as the same share of its half of the new range (a level load's `OrbitRadius` + `SetAdvanceRange` parks the gun at rest).

## The cursor

The capture is a **state the session enters**, never something a frame does: a frame that warps the pointer cannot be dragged, resized or left (#99, #154).

- **Arrival** (Play, a tile, Resume, Retry, Next Level) captures at once, but **only for a pointer already inside the picture** (the entry may have come from the pad with the mouse parked elsewhere, and warping in a pointer nobody offered is the hostage-taking #99 exists to prevent). Unfocused arrival stays free. The pressing click cannot fire a shot or yank the aim (`MouseAim.Invalidate()` drops the baseline).
- **After a focus loss** the cursor is the desktop's until a **left press edge inside the viewport** re-takes it: gated on `EdgeInputAllowed` (the click that brings the window forward must not also capture), bounds-tested explicitly (title-bar coordinates are reported), unable to fire.

## Traps when testing

- ⚠ **Any external `SetCursorPos` reads as a huge aim delta** once captured (recentred every frame): the `screenshot.ps1` title-bar click yanks the aim to the clamps and a shot flies 45 degrees wide. A test artefact: send focus clicks before the level is entered, or set the cursor a known offset from the centre (~0.002 rad per pixel).
- ⚠ **Input is polled once per frame**: hold a key ~40 ms and a mouse button ~400 ms, or a down+up pair is never seen.
- ⚠ **A harness that types into the game posts `WM_CHAR` through `PostMessageW`**, never the ANSI entry point: that one pushes the text through the code page and loses letters such as "ž" (the nickname plate and the note page read typed characters).

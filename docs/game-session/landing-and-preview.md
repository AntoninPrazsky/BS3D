# The shot, the landing and the landing preview

Part of [The game: a session](../game-session.md).

## Where a shot lands

- **The shot is a swept sphere test**: at `SHOOT_SPEED` a ball crosses several diameters a frame, so `ShotPlacement.TryFindFirstHit` (`TryFindFirstHitOnSegment` for a bounded run) solves the **moment of contact** (smaller root of the quadratic in the step parameter); ranking by clamped closest approach ties the whole field on the first approaching frame.
- **A landed ball snaps into the lattice**: `ShotPlacement.TrySolveAgainstBall` returns the free cell touching the ball hit, nearest the contact (stuck at the raw contact point, balls chain into an icicle). Deciding without writing lets the preview ask the identical question.
- **The first ring is not always enough**: on the field's wall an edge ball has no cell beyond it and later balls were silently eaten. `BallsMap.TryFindEmptyCellInSecondRing` searches free cells touching the balls that touch the one hit, nearest the contact first. **A third ring is rejected**: locality keeps a ball from attaching visibly behind what was aimed at.
- **A shot attaches to balls and never to the glass (#432, the owner's ruling).** A glass landing hung a ball alone off its ceiling socket. A glass contact is no longer an event: the ball bounces, keeps listening, and is otherwise a miss at the stone or kill plane. The level's top course is hung by `BuildBallsStructure`, and a landing in a top-level cell **beside a ball** still takes a ceiling socket (`GetCellsDisconnectedFromCeiling` counts the top level as anchored; the physics must agree).
- **A refused shot bounces, a state of its own** (`ContactEvents.MarkBounced`): put back where its surfaces first met the ball and no longer swept (else it was refused every step). Cleared in `ContactEvents.Unregister`, so a recycled handle cannot inherit it.

## The landing is the preview's own sweep (#696)

`ShotPlacement` owns the rule and **one function decides, twice**: the handler when a ball touches, the preview every frame. A line over behaviour it does not share would disagree with the outcome and be worse than nothing. The shot sticks in the ghost's cell **97.5 %** of the time (`ShotAgreementTests`, 232 of 238 aims over its six rig levels, Colossus's figure among them since #849; 63.6 % before). What made it so:

- `TryFindFirstHitOnSegment` tests the **touch** (`along - radiusSum >= nearest`), not closest approach, or a ball met inside a step is skipped.
- `BallContactEventHandler.LandSweptShots` sweeps every shot in the air every step with the preview's function (`ContactEvents.TryGetStepStart`), because Bepu's contact comes a step late and from the wrong ball. The contact path remains for stone, glass and overlaps the sweep does not explain.
- The Game's preview passes `worldGravity: true`.
- `NarrowPhaseCallbacks.ConfigureContactManifold` makes **no constraint for a contact the shot has not reached** (a speculative contact beside the line bent the shot off the swept line).
- **A shot in flight passes through loose balls** (`ContactEvents.MarkLoose`; `PhysicsWorld.RetireBall` clears the mark before Bepu recycles the handle).

No ghost promises the cluster's sway, wind, the **mouse path one frame stale** (`MouseAim.ApplyCursor` applies the delta before the click edge; Space is right), or two shots in the air at once.

## The ghost and the beam

- **The ghost is the signal; the crosshair only confirms it.** A ball in the landing cell in the colour at the muzzle (the useful question is "does it stick next to two more of its own"). Its size rides the shader's dissolve channel **below -1** (`ModelInstance.GhostDissolve`): opaque, no blend or sorting, and the Potato rim follows it (#804). **It casts no shadow** (#894): it is collected after the sun's shadow pass, because the depth shader does not apply the ghost's size (it applies the dither in the same channel, #920) and the beat would not reach its shadow; it still receives light and the map (`docs/rendering/sun-shadows.md`). It appears exactly when a shot sticks, so its absence covers every refusal, and only when a shot would leave the barrel (not in a drop cinematic, at `Score.OutOfShots`, or once `LevelDecided`).
- **It beats, up to exactly a whole ball (#831, `GhostBeat`).** A fast rise (`LAND_SECONDS`) lands on a whole ball, holds against it and sags once under it, then a slower elastic settle falls past its rest to `LOW` and springs part of the way back: `HZ` beats a second, a pure function of the wall clock, so every frame rate sees the same beat and even 24 frames a second see both ends of each one (`GhostBeatTests`). ⚠ `HZ` is kept off the muzzle collar's `MUZZLE_MARK_HZ`: both pulse the loaded colour on the same clock and gate, so at one rate they lock into a pair. The top is a whole ball because a smaller sphere in the same place reads as **farther away**, not as "not yet" (the owner, at 0.28-0.72 of a ball); the motion is what says "not yet", and the size never drops so far that the colour cannot be read. ⚠ **Never above 1**: at the lattice's spacing a bigger ghost pushes into its neighbours (the owner withdrew the overshoot he first allowed). The curve's one `MathF.Min` turns the rise's spring overshoot into the hold, and `GhostDissolve` clamps to 1 as well. The beat's first reason (3 of 10 landings in the ghost's cell) is gone since #696; the owner chose a ghost that moves.
- **Where it is drawn differs from which cell.** The balls hang stretched under the anchor and the glass **descends** (`CEILING_DESCENT_PER_STEP` a step) while the lattice stays put, so a lattice-drawn ghost climbed out through the top. It is drawn in the cluster's **live** frame (`ShotPlacement.CellWorldPosition`, off the drift `TrySolveAgainstBall` measured), whole not just Y, so it sways with its pocket; `PhysicsBall.StartRenderGlide` and `BallLanding.World` needed the same. The *decision* was never wrong (`worldOffset` cancels out of the anchored contact).
- **`AimBeam` is the overview's substitute for a crosshair** (that lens looks at the cluster, not along the bore): a dashed line crawling towards the **touch**, red when a refusal is **certain** (the aim reaches a ball and neither ring has room); where the shot snaps into a cell its far dashes widen a little and fade out where the lens sees the beam reach the ball it touches (next bullet), and a cutter's line stops there at full strength. **Beam and crosshair hand over** (`1 - PreciseAim.Blend`): leaning in, the beam foreshortens into dashes piled over the ghost. It borrows `ShotTrail.fx` from `LaunchSmears`: ⚠ **a shared effect turns "set it once" into a race** (parameters belong to the `Effect`; the smear tapers, the beam's dashes have widths of their own), so every user pushes the widths per draw (the beam its first piece's, then each that differs from the last); `TrailHeadFade`, which only the beam sets, the other two draw at the 0 an effect starts with, and the beam puts 0 back after its last dash. With gravity wells it follows the flight ([Special balls: shot and mass](special-balls-shot-and-mass.md)): `AimBeam.Draw` takes the whole path and lays the crawl and the far part along all of it, so neither restarts at a knot.
- **Where the shot snaps into a cell, the far dashes widen a little, lengthen and fade out at the ball (#1144 asked for the landing region; #1196: one thin, quiet dashed line whose dashes "widen slightly and smoothly towards the end, turn transparent and stop at the ball").** The Game tells `AimBeam.Draw` what the far end is (`AimBeam.FarEnd`) and hands it the centre of the ball the shot touches: a `Landing` whenever the beam reaches the cluster, a refusal included; a `Strike` for a cutter, which strikes the very ball the beam ends on and has no cell to show, so its line stays the plain one at full strength; `OpenAir` when it reaches nothing. On a landing the beam is the plain line up to `FAR_FROM` (half) of its length, and over the rest its dashes follow one smoothstep (`AimBeam.Ease`): they widen from `WIDTH` (0.12) to `FAR_WIDTH` (0.26), their period grows to `FAR_STRETCH` (3.2) times `SPACING` with `FAR_DASH_FRACTION` (0.6) of it lit, and their light falls as `1 - Ease` cut by the widening (`AimBeam.FarAlpha`), so the light across a dash's whole width never rises along the beam and is gone where it reaches the ball: the far end is fainter where it is wider. The landing region it points at is measured over 478 aims of `ShotAgreementTests`' six levels: the ghost stands **0.45 off the line through the touch at the median, 0.64 at the 99th percentile, 0.67 at the most** (`LANDING_SPREAD` 0.65), from 0.68 short of the touch to 0.38 past it; `FAR_WIDTH` reaches the ghost's centre in 19 % of those aims, so the far end points at the region and does not outline it, and it never covers or passes the ball it hits. `AimBeamTests` holds the near end to the plain line, the width to one curve with no corner however the flight is cut into legs or bent by a well, no dash bolder than the line, the last dash faded at every phase of the crawl, the far dashes still under a moving lens, and an open beam and a cutter's to the plain line.
  - ⚠ **A landing and a strike end where the lens sees the beam reach the ball, not at the touch** (`AimBeam.VisibleEnd`). The touch lies on the ball's near surface, and the overview sees the beam 6 to 20 degrees off end on (13 at the median over those aims), so the beam's last stretch lies in front of the ball hit on screen: 1.7 units of flight at the median, up to 5.3. Run to the touch, the plain line lays three quarters of a dash's core over that ball's disc at the median, and the far part faded to the touch still 0.06 at the 95th percentile; ended where its sight line first grazes the ball, under a two-thousandth at the 95th percentile, a cutter's full-strength line included, from the overview and from a lens leaning in half way. So `AimBeam.Lay` takes the camera's position, and from the side, where the touch is not over the disc, the two ends are one.
  - ⚠ **A far dash fades along itself, not dash by dash.** Every piece carries the fade's alpha at both its ends and `ShotTrail.fx` lerps between them (`TrailHeadFade`). The far dashes are a few units long and the fade has half a beam, so with one alpha a dash the fade came in steps and the last dash drawn kept 0.2 of the line's light at the median through the overview and up to nearly half, a gap or more short of the ball. A clipped sliver is skipped as much shorter as it is fainter (`MIN_DASH_FRACTION` of the dash itself, times its light), so the dash crawling into the ball is kept until it is gone: a fraction of the far period, as the sliver was measured before, skipped it while it was still most of a ball long. Over the crawl's whole cycle the last dash drawn ends on at most a tenth of the line's light where its own fade-out begins (through the overview 0.003 at the median, 0.05 at the 95th percentile). What it costs is reach: through the overview at a 1080-line frame the line's core falls under a tenth of the light 34 px short of the disc at the median (95th 58), and the ghost stands 54 px from there.
  - ⚠ **The far dashes are laid along the flight and faded by the lens.** Where they widen and lengthen is measured from the muzzle to the touch; only the fade and where they stop come from `VisibleEnd`, so leaning in or the shake of a shot changes their light and not their place. A change in the flight does move them: the far part is laid from the touch, so when the aim slides the touch over a ball, and most of all when it moves to another ball, every far dash moves, not only the tip. The plain line's dashes, laid from the muzzle, never do.
  - ⚠ **Widened dashes read as rungs from the overview unless they lengthen more than they widen.** A dash's length shows there at a fifth of itself or less while its width (the billboard faces the lens) shows whole, and #1144's widened dashes of the line's length squashed into bars level with each other across the beam. With the period and its lit share growing, a beam's squattest far dash with a tenth of the light shows a half-light length over width of 1.20 at the median of the measured aims, and for 95 % of them at least 1.24 times the plain line's own dashes over the same stretch (seen within six degrees of end on, even the plain line's dashes are dots).
  - **Thin and quiet** (the owner: "the aim beam must not disturb: thin, not as conspicuous and bold"): `WIDTH` 0.12 and `BRIGHTNESS` 2.4. Measured on the moon's black sky from the overview at a 1080-line frame (the full width at half maximum of the light it adds, bloom included): about 10 px by the muzzle and 9 px half way; 0.15 and 3.5 made 14.5 and 12.5 px and bloomed into a glowing bead a dash against a dark sky, which is what a thicker or brighter line must be compared against. In daylight over white balls it is a faint line. A beam is 11 to 19 draws (14 at the median), one a dash piece.

## The elevation clamp: rubber, strain, a shot along the clamp (#431, #1008)

- **`Cannon.ElevationStrain`** is raised by `Cannon.Aim` alone (cursor, pad) when input asks past the **upper** clamp in the direction it moved (never at `MinElevation` (#872), never by a walk, the eased reset, `aim=`, or a traverse along the cap). It holds `ELEVATION_STRAIN_HOLD`, falls over `ELEVATION_STRAIN_RELEASE`: **a timed flag, not a magnitude** (one call's overshoot shrinks as frame rate rises).
- **The barrel is rubber (the owner's ruling)**: a push stretches the pose past the clamp towards `Cannon.ELEVATION_OVERSHOOT`, then a ~3 Hz damped spring pulls it back (at the upper end a little past the clamp, then onto it; **at `MinElevation` the spring is critically damped: the barrel runs out under the clamp and eases back onto it, no swing past it**, #872), in steps of at most 1/240 s so every frame rate draws the same swing. **One pose** for the barrel, `AimDirection` and the ADS lens; the ghost, the beam and the shot take `Cannon.FireDirection`, which is that pose except that **a stretch past either clamp is left out**: a shot fired with the barrel stretched past a clamp leaves at the clamp, the stretch being drawn only (#872 at the floor, #1008 at the top). The upper spring's rebound under its clamp fires as drawn.
- **The gun never refuses a shot for the clamp** (#1008, the owner's ruling: "when aiming too high, allow the shot at the limit"). A shot from the top of the stretch once left ~6 degrees past the limit into the band a tall level closes, and #431 answered it by refusing the shot while the strain lasted; an outside player took that refusal for the proof the shot is faked ("that is the only moment you cannot fire the cannon"). Firing along the clamp closes the same band. **The upper end keeps its alarm**: the strain still blinks the crosshair and the beam, the barrel's arrival is still knocked and heard (`PlayAimStop`, #811) and the pad still buzzes while the push lasts (#835), because the gun will go no higher. **The lower end has none** (#872): the player looks down at the floor on purpose, so a push under `MinElevation` raises no strain (no red crosshair or beam, no `PlayAimStop`, no knock or buzz). The ghost shows where the shot will land at either clamp.

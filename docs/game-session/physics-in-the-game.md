# Physics in the game

Part of [The game: a session](../game-session.md).

The simulation is the Testbed's, ported: `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` already implements the match rule (`ReleaseSameTypeCluster`, `MINIMUM_CLUSTER_SIZE`) and the release of everything left unanchored, with the contact material in one copy.

## The timestep and the frame

- **The Game does not step like the Testbed.** The Testbed takes one `Timestep(min(frameTime, 1/60))` a frame (slow motion under 60 FPS, a varying dt). The Game accumulates frame time and spends whole steps of `PHYSICS_TIMESTEP` (`PhysicsWorld.FIXED_TIMESTEP`, 1/120 s, also what the preview's curved solver integrates at), capped at `PHYSICS_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` so a hitch drops the remainder. `IsFixedTimeStep = false`; the rate is held by `Platform/FrameLimiter.cs` (not vsync, #270), which the fixed step makes safe.
- **A capture run steps differently on purpose** (#946, [Capture](controls-and-testing.md#capture-filming-the-game-946)): its frame is a fixed time (`capturefps=`) whatever it costs, its cap of steps a frame is `CAPTURE_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` (16, not 4: nothing hitches, and the cap would only run a slow rate slow) and its simulation has **one worker thread** (`PhysicsWorld(workerThreads: 1)`), because with a worker per processor two identical runs came out differently. A player's game is none of this.
- **The solver is compiled before the first level needs it (#1086).** Bepu's solver, generic over the session's own callback types in another assembly, is compiled the first time a world is stepped, and ReadyToRun cannot precompile it. That was `ReleaseCluster`'s first step at the first level of a launch: 120 ms on the desktop, 57-80 ms on the vivo X300 Ultra, about 0.3 s on the Pi. Where the quick JIT is on (Windows, Android) about 1.5 s of unoptimised steps followed on the desktop, at 4.5 ms against 1.2. So `PhysicsWarmup`, started at the end of `LoadContent`, hangs a 196-ball cluster in a throwaway `PhysicsBench` and steps it on a background thread for `SECONDS` (4 s) while the menus are up, in 2 ms slices with 6 ms rests and on one worker fewer than the machine has. Compiled code belongs to the callback types, so the level's world inherits it. The first release then took about 20 ms on the desktop and 16-26 ms on the phone, and the desktop's steps ran at their steady pace from the first second. `ReleaseCluster` and the start of a benchmark stop the warm-up, so it never shares the cores with a played or measured frame, and the front end's quality probe measures no window while it runs. Its one `[warmup]` line says how many steps it took and what the last cost. ⚠ The warm-up's cluster must stay awake (a breeze, `BREEZE`): a sleeping body is skipped by the solver, which then compiles nothing (`PhysicsWarmupTests`).
- **The drawn frame interpolates between steps** (#293, `_renderAlpha`); nothing reads drawn positions for decisions, and a placed body resets its history so no blend straddles a teleport.
- **The frame waits for the compositor at its END, with its work flushed to the GPU (#634)**: `BS3DGame.PaceFrame` flushes (`GpuFlush`, through MonoGame's private `_d3dContext`; D3D11 otherwise holds commands until Present) and waits at the end of `Draw`. With the wait at the top of `Update`, DWM discarded finished frames. **Judder is motion and no capture shows it**: use the `[pace]` line `logfps` writes beside `[fps]`, and PresentMon.
- **The simulation does not stop when the level does (#241).** `ResultPage` and `CampaignFinalePage` answer `UpdatesUnderlying` true, the only pages over a session that do (an ending is the arena carrying on; a pause is a game put down). `UpdateUnderResult` runs the simulation, the gun's settling (`StepGunHardware`), the line's hum, the blasts, the cut's staging and the wormhole, and **nothing else**: no input, aim, preview, descent, ending or **camera** (two writers of one pose is one losing). ⚠ **Leaving the rules out of that method does not hold them**: contacts are processed inside the step, so a shot in the air still lands into `OnBallLanded`; `LevelOver` holds each door (silent stick, no score, no miss).

## Frames and the hanging cluster

- **The frame question.** The lattice and world frames differ by `_clusterWorldOffset`; the simulation lives in **world**, and only the positions `BuildBallsStructure` reads from the `BallsMap` are lattice-framed. So its `worldOffset` applies to the body positions **and nothing else** (`ConnectBalls`' anchors are differences, `ConnectBallToCeiling` reads only X and Z). ⚠ Applied twice, the structure tears apart on the first step; forgotten on the ceiling body, every ball is yanked five units into the glass.
- **The cluster does not settle on its lattice**: the ceiling `BallSocket` anchors a ball's top to the plate's bottom face, so the top level rests a unit below the plate's centre, stretched, decaying down (`CeilingPlate.CLEARANCE`, `BallsConstraintsBuilder.CeilingRestY`), and **the plate walks down over a level while the lattice stays put**. ⚠ **Nothing may derive a world position from `cell + _clusterWorldOffset`**: use `ShotPlacement.CellWorldPosition` with the measured drift ([The shot, the landing and the landing preview](landing-and-preview.md)); anything asking where balls are reads the bodies (`ClusterCollector`, the death-line walk).
- **`BallContactEventHandler` is one copy** in `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` (#68). Bepu runs contact callbacks on worker threads inside `Timestep`, so `OnTouching` only enqueues and everything touching the simulation, map or listener set happens in `ProcessQueuedContacts` on the main thread. ⚠ **`Timestep` -> `Flush()` -> `ProcessQueuedContacts()` is mandatory, per step** (a listener is only safely unregistered once the per-worker adds are applied); it is `PhysicsWorld.Step`'s own order. It listens on `OnTouching`, not every `OnContactAdded`, and every field on it is `readonly` (both callers rebuild one per field). A manifold offset is relative to the pair's **first** collidable.
- **A landed ball's body is PUT in its cell** (#265): the ceiling socket anchors the ball's own **north pole**, a pair with **two solutions** (hanging under the anchor, or inverted on top of it), and a ball dragged in from above settled into the wrong one when it had few neighbours, was stable, and **went to sleep there** 1.16 units above its cell. The surface-anchored socket also turns the correction into a **torque** nothing damps. Placing the body first makes the constraint error zero; velocities are zeroed on attach, and what the shot carried goes to the cluster as a push (below).
- **The shot's collidable is swept** (`SPECULATIVE_MARGIN` plus `ContinuousDetection.Continuous`). A bare `TypedIndex` is `Passive`: an unbounded speculative margin that stops tunnelling but not landing (the early contact's normal is wrong and the manifold never reaches `depth >= 0`, the gate `OnTouching` fires on); on Static (a Quarry level until #742) it gave **0 attaches**. It is geometry-specific (One attached either way), so a rig that plays only the opening level sees nothing.
- **What is drawn is the simulation** (`ClusterCollector.Collect` takes each ball's matrix from its body pose, orientation included); each ball is visited exactly once a frame ("The hardware shared with the Game" in `docs/testbed.md`).

## A shot pushes what it hits (#1192)

The owner's decision on #1007 ("there is no sign of the impact's energy on the other balls ... even when the ball does not attach, the map should be affected by the impact"). A landing zeroes the shot's velocity, so until this the momentum it flew in with was deleted and the cluster did not answer a hit at all; the ripple ([The ripple](../game-feedback/ripple.md)) is light, not motion.

- **`ShotPush` hands the cluster the shot's momentum** as an impulse along its flight: the shot's mass times the velocity its striking step began with, times `MOMENTUM_SHARE` (1: a ball that sticks carries all of its momentum into what it sticks to). Given on a landing, on a shot's first refusal (the ball it met takes it) and on a cutter's strike. `ShotPush.ShareFor(kind)` is where a kind built to hit harder (#1033's Fire Ball) states its own share.
- **Once, after the landing**: an impulse between two steps, so the same at any frame rate and under the Testbed's varying step, given after the body is put in its cell, its sockets built with no error and the match and the three removals have taken what they take. The cell was decided before anything moved; a group that falls falls as it did.
- **Spread through the sockets**: a breadth-first walk over balls that touch, to `REACH` (5) hops, each hop weighing `HOP_FALLOFF` (0.7) of the one before; no ball's velocity changes by more than `MAX_KICK` (8 units a second). ⚠ **Handed to the struck ball alone, the same momentum tore the lattice**: its sockets stretched 0.2 to 0.8 of a diameter past their length (a lattice visibly torn open stands at 0.4, `HEAVY_MASS_RATIO`'s sweep). Spread, a push adds at most 0.02 to what the landing stretches. The cap bounds a strand's tip, where the walk finds a few balls to share what a block shares among a hundred, and a harder kind: at four times the push the strand Pendant stretched 0.02 past the landing's own.
- **What it does** (`ShotPushTests`' report: a level hung twice on one worker thread, the same shot into both, the farthest ball's distance from its twin without the push): a dense block barely moves (One 0.06 to 0.29); a sheet leans and sways for seconds (Pennant 0.4 to 0.6, Sail 0.7 to 1.4, the peak about a second after the hit); a strand swings most (Pendant 1.3 to 1.7). The lowest ball dipped at most 0.37 under where it would hang (Rope), against the line's `SWING_ALLOWANCE` of 1.
- ⚠ **It moves what the sag gate reads at the edge** (`LevelGen --sag`, which repeats it). On twelve levels chosen for their sag, two passes each: Orrery sagged in 4 and 5 runs of 5 (3 and 3 without the push), so the gate now reports it; Pylon in 2 of 5 (0), its lowest ball swinging 1.04 under the line, past `SWING_ALLOWANCE`, where it had reached 0.75; Rope in 0 (1); Saturn, Amphora, Pendant, Spyglass, Sail, Tightrope, Hammock, Causeway and Kiln kept their counts, the line coming closer on several (Tightrope 0.36 above it to 0.00).
- ⚠ **A pushed cluster stays awake.** A sleeping island is not stepped, and a sheet set swaying by a push was still over the sleep threshold 12 s after the hit (Zigzag, Pennant), where after the same landing without the push it slept in 2.9 to 5.1 s (the dense One: 1.0 s against 0.3). An awake 975-ball cluster costs about twelve times a sleeping one ([Wind](#wind-95)), and the tiers that give the wind up are the ones that count on it sleeping between shots.
- ⚠ **The next shot flies at a cluster that still swings**, and without help one shot in eight then landed beside its ghost: the preview leads the ball it meets and a shot keeps its ghost's promise ([The shot, the landing and the landing preview](landing-and-preview.md)).
- **One copy**, in the contact handler the Game and the Testbed share, **repeated by the sag gate** ([LevelGen's probes](../formats-and-tools/levelgen-probes.md)). `shotpush=<scale>` multiplies it in the Game (0 is none, up to 4; the benchmark keeps 1), and `logshots` prints each landing's push.

## Culling

**Falling below `KILL_PLANE_Y` is the only thing that removes a ball** (the Testbed also culls sleeping balls). ⚠ **`_shotBalls` holds shot *bodies*, not shots in flight** (#66): a ball resting on the stone stays in it for the session, so a loss gated on `_shotBalls.Count == 0` could never fire. Ask `AnyShotUndecided`: the **contact-listener flag** (a ball stops listening exactly when its shot resolves) plus `Awake` (a ball resting on another loose shot touched nothing static).

## Wind (#95)

The air moves the cluster **at High and up, and small**: `QualityPreset.ClusterWind` is a tier's to give up because keeping the cluster awake costs CPU (975 balls: 10.1 ms against 0.83 asleep).

- **`WindField`**: a fixed heading and a gusting strength. **It has to vary**: a constant push is absorbed by the sockets, which lean and stay leant. `PhysicsWorld.Wind` joins gravity in the integrator's per-step broadcast. **`SceneWind`** (`Game/SceneWind.cs`) gives each scene a strength and heading, **none** in space, Moon, Mars, cavern, dream, Grid and the Big Top (`Circus`); `wind=<scale>` multiplies it.
- ⚠ **A sleeping island is not integrated**, so `PhysicsWorld.KeepClusterAwake` sets every ball's sleep threshold negative at the build (waking one ball wakes only its own island).
- The strengths are small (sway is the *level's* answer more than the wind's) and the sag gate is undisturbed. Shots do not integrate wind.

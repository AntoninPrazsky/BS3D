# Physics in the game

Part of [The game: a session](../game-session.md).

The simulation is the Testbed's, ported: `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` already implements the match rule (`ReleaseSameTypeCluster`, `MINIMUM_CLUSTER_SIZE`) and the release of everything left unanchored, with the contact material in one copy.

## The timestep and the frame

- **The Game does not step like the Testbed.** The Testbed takes one `Timestep(min(frameTime, 1/60))` a frame (slow motion under 60 FPS, a varying dt). The Game accumulates frame time and spends whole steps of `PHYSICS_TIMESTEP` (`PhysicsWorld.FIXED_TIMESTEP`, 1/120 s, also what the preview's curved solver integrates at), capped at `PHYSICS_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` so a hitch drops the remainder. `IsFixedTimeStep = false`; the rate is held by `Platform/FrameLimiter.cs` (not vsync, #270), which the fixed step makes safe.
- **A capture run steps differently on purpose** (#946, [Capture](controls-and-testing.md#capture-filming-the-game-946)): its frame is a fixed time (`capturefps=`) whatever it costs, its cap of steps a frame is `CAPTURE_MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` (16, not 4: nothing hitches, and the cap would only run a slow rate slow) and its simulation has **one worker thread** (`PhysicsWorld(workerThreads: 1)`), because with a worker per processor two identical runs came out differently. A player's game is none of this.
- **The solver is compiled before the first level needs it (#1086).** Bepu's solver, generic over the session's own callback types in another assembly, is compiled the first time a world is stepped, and ReadyToRun cannot precompile it. That was `ReleaseCluster`'s first step at the first level of a launch: 120 ms on the desktop, 57-80 ms on the vivo X300 Ultra, about 0.3 s on the Pi. Where the quick JIT is on (Windows, Android) about 1.5 s of unoptimised steps followed on the desktop, at 4.5 ms against 1.2. So `PhysicsWarmup`, started at the end of `LoadContent`, hangs a 196-ball cluster in a throwaway `PhysicsBench` and steps it on a background thread for `SECONDS` (4 s) while the menus are up, in 2 ms slices with 6 ms rests and on one worker fewer than the machine has. Compiled code belongs to the callback types, so the level's world inherits it. The first release then took about 20 ms on the desktop and 16-26 ms on the phone, and the desktop's steps ran at their steady pace from the first second. `ReleaseCluster` and the start of a benchmark stop the warm-up, so it never shares the cores with a played or measured frame, and the front end's quality probe measures no window while it runs. Its one `[warmup]` line says how many steps and sleeps it took and what the last steps cost. ⚠ The warm-up's cluster must stay awake (a breeze, `BREEZE`): a sleeping body is skipped by the solver, which then compiles nothing (`PhysicsWarmupTests`).
- **The cluster's contacts are made at the build, not by the first step (#1240).** A hung cluster touches its neighbours everywhere, and the world's first step created every one of those contacts at once: on a Ryzen 5 3600, 15 ms of a 19.8 ms first step for Eleven's 959 balls (4996 sockets, about 5000 contacts more). On a level a chapter's tour opens, that step is the frame the tour hands the gun over. `PhysicsWorld.PrimeContacts`, called as the level's world is built, runs the step's bounding boxes and collision detection alone: no solve, no integration, so the cluster hangs exactly as built, and no shot is registered yet, so the contact stream reports nothing. With it, and the HUD drawn once unseen on the level's first frame ("The HUD's first draw" in `docs/game-feedback/hud-magazine-and-muzzle.md`), that second's longest frame on the GTX 1060 at `Medium` went from 53.0 ms to 11-12.
- **The cluster's sleep and wake are compiled by the warm-up too (#1241).** A few seconds after the cluster settles, Bepu's sleep pass takes the whole island out of the active set in one step, and a shot's contact puts it back. Stepping never reaches either pass while the warm-up's cluster is kept awake, so the first level compiled both: Eleven's first sleep made a step of 21.4 ms on a Ryzen 5 3600 and 18.9-19.2 ms on a Ryzen 9 5900X, and its first wake took 8.0 ms in a test. So every fourth slice of the warm-up puts its cluster to sleep and wakes it (`PhysicsWorld.SleepAndWake`, through the world's own threads, as the step runs them): after its 57-64 cycles the 5900X's sleep step is 3.3-3.5 ms, beside the 2.0-2.3 ms of the steps around it, and a test's wake 3.3-4.4 ms. The remainder is the sleep pass itself, which moves Eleven's 9 976 constraints out of the active set. Keeping the cluster awake instead would cost every step (975 balls: 10.1 ms of physics a frame on the weak machine, against 0.83 asleep, [Wind](#wind-95)). ⚠ A level started within a fraction of a second of launch (`play level=`) stops the warm-up before its first cycle and compiles them in play as before.
- **The drawn frame interpolates between steps** (#293, `_renderAlpha`); nothing reads drawn positions for decisions, and a placed body resets its history so no blend straddles a teleport.
- **The frame waits for the compositor at its END, with its work flushed to the GPU (#634)**: `BS3DGame.PaceFrame` flushes (`GpuFlush`, through MonoGame's private `_d3dContext`; D3D11 otherwise holds commands until Present) and waits at the end of `Draw`. With the wait at the top of `Update`, DWM discarded finished frames. **Judder is motion and no capture shows it**: use the `[pace]` line `logfps` writes beside `[fps]`, and PresentMon.
- **The simulation does not stop when the level does (#241).** `ResultPage` and `CampaignFinalePage` answer `UpdatesUnderlying` true, the only pages over a session that do (an ending is the arena carrying on; a pause is a game put down). `UpdateUnderResult` runs the simulation, the gun's settling (`StepGunHardware`), the line's hum, the blasts, the cut's staging and the wormhole, and **nothing else**: no input, aim, preview, descent, ending or **camera** (two writers of one pose is one losing). ⚠ **Leaving the rules out of that method does not hold them**: contacts are processed inside the step, so a shot in the air still lands into `OnBallLanded`; `LevelOver` holds each door (silent stick, no score, no miss).

## Frames and the hanging cluster

- **The frame question.** The lattice and world frames differ by `_clusterWorldOffset`; the simulation lives in **world**, and only the positions `BuildBallsStructure` reads from the `BallsMap` are lattice-framed. So its `worldOffset` applies to the body positions **and nothing else** (`ConnectBalls`' anchors are differences, `ConnectBallToCeiling` reads only X and Z). ⚠ Applied twice, the structure tears apart on the first step; forgotten on the ceiling body, every ball is yanked five units into the glass.
- **The cluster does not settle on its lattice**: the ceiling `BallSocket` anchors a ball's top to the plate's bottom face, so the top level rests a unit below the plate's centre, stretched, decaying down (`CeilingPlate.CLEARANCE`, `BallsConstraintsBuilder.CeilingRestY`), and **the plate walks down over a level while the lattice stays put**. ⚠ **Nothing may derive a world position from `cell + _clusterWorldOffset`**: use `ShotPlacement.CellWorldPosition` with the measured drift ([The shot, the landing and the landing preview](landing-and-preview.md)); anything asking where balls are reads the bodies (`ClusterCollector`, the death-line walk).
- **`BallContactEventHandler` is one copy** in `Prazsky.BS3D.Physics` (#68). Bepu runs contact callbacks on worker threads inside `Timestep`, so `OnTouching` only enqueues and everything touching the simulation, map or listener set happens in `ProcessQueuedContacts` on the main thread. ⚠ **`Timestep` -> `Flush()` -> `ProcessQueuedContacts()` is mandatory, per step** (a listener is only safely unregistered once the per-worker adds are applied); it is `PhysicsWorld.Step`'s own order. It listens on `OnTouching`, not every `OnContactAdded`, and every field on it is `readonly` (both callers rebuild one per field). A manifold offset is relative to the pair's **first** collidable.
- **A landed ball's body is PUT in its cell** (#265): the ceiling socket anchors the ball's own **north pole**, a pair with **two solutions** (hanging under the anchor, or inverted on top of it), and a ball dragged in from above settled into the wrong one when it had few neighbours, was stable, and **went to sleep there** 1.16 units above its cell. The surface-anchored socket also turns the correction into a **torque** nothing damps. Placing the body first makes the constraint error zero; velocities are zeroed on attach, and what the shot carried goes to the cluster as a push (below).
- **The shot's collidable is swept** (`SPECULATIVE_MARGIN` plus `ContinuousDetection.Continuous`). A bare `TypedIndex` is `Passive`: an unbounded speculative margin that stops tunnelling but not landing (the early contact's normal is wrong and the manifold never reaches `depth >= 0`, the gate `OnTouching` fires on); on Static (a Quarry level until #742) it gave **0 attaches**. It is geometry-specific (One attached either way), so a rig that plays only the opening level sees nothing.
- **What is drawn is the simulation** (`ClusterCollector.Collect` takes each ball's matrix from its body pose, orientation included); each ball is visited exactly once a frame ("The hardware shared with the Game" in `docs/testbed.md`).

## A shot pushes what it hits (#1192)

The owner's decision on #1007 ("there is no sign of the impact's energy on the other balls ... even when the ball does not attach, the map should be affected by the impact"). A landing zeroes the shot's velocity, so until this the momentum it flew in with was deleted and the cluster did not answer a hit at all; the ripple ([The ripple](../game-feedback/ripple.md)) is light, not motion.

- **`ShotPush` hands the cluster the shot's momentum** as an impulse along its flight: the shot's mass times the velocity its striking step began with, times `MOMENTUM_SHARE` (1: a ball that sticks carries all of its momentum into what it sticks to). Given on a landing, on a shot's first refusal and on a cutter's strike. `ShotPush.ShareFor(kind)` is where a kind built to hit harder (#1033's Fire Ball) states its own share.
- **A refused shot hands over what the touch would** (`BallContactEventHandler.PushRefused`): the part of its velocity along the line between the two centres goes to the balls round the ball it met, and is taken off the shot, which keeps only what runs across that line and slides off. Left on it, the solver bounced the shot off the same ball and the cluster took the blow twice. Once a shot, marked on the shot (`PhysicsBall.Refused`), not by its body's handle, which Bepu hands to a later shot.
- **Walked before the match, given after it**: an impulse between two steps, so the same at any frame rate and under the Testbed's varying step. The walk (`ShotPush.Prepare`) is taken once the body is in its cell and its sockets built with no error, before the match and the three removals take anything; the impulse (`Deliver`) goes after them to every ball the walk found. **What the shot knocks out leaves with its share (#1249)**: a ball the match, the zap, the acid, the blast or a cut has let go of since the walk is pushed like one still hanging, by its hop's share along the flight, so a released group leaves the way the shot flew, fastest where it struck, before gravity takes it; the balls still hanging take exactly the push they took before. Until then the released balls' shares were discarded and a group dropped as if nothing had hit it (the owner: "throwing the impact energy away makes it seem there is no physics in the game"). A cutter's strike is walked before its cut too. `ShotPush.Result` counts the two apart (`Balls`/`Impulse` and `Released`/`ReleasedImpulse`); only what still hangs is settled. The sag gate read the same with it on Pendant (0 of 5), Bridge (1), Sail (0) and Orrery (4, against 5 without: its threaded noise). ⚠ **Walked after the match, a shot that completed a group made of every ball beside its cell pushed nothing**: the walk started from the emptied cell and found no ball, and what the group hung from did not answer the blow (`ShotPushTests.AShotThatTakesEveryNeighbourStillPushesWhatTheyHungFrom`).
- **Spread through the sockets**: a breadth-first walk over balls that touch, to `REACH` (5) hops, each hop weighing `HOP_FALLOFF` (0.7) of the one before; no ball's velocity changes by more than `MAX_KICK` (8 units a second). ⚠ **Handed to the struck ball alone, the same momentum tore the lattice**: its sockets stretched 0.2 to 0.8 of a diameter past their length (a lattice visibly torn open stands at 0.4, `HEAVY_MASS_RATIO`'s sweep). Spread, a push adds at most 0.02 to what the landing stretches. The cap bounds a strand's tip, where the walk finds a few balls to share what a block shares among a hundred, and a harder kind: at four times the push the strand Pendant stretched 0.02 past the landing's own.
- **What it does** (`ShotPushTests`' report: a level hung twice on one worker thread, the same shot into both, the farthest ball's distance from its twin without the push): a dense block barely moves (One 0.04 to 0.29); a sheet leans and sways (Pennant 0.3 to 0.6, Sail 0.5 to 1.3, the peak within the first second); a strand swings most (Pendant 1.3 to 1.6). **Sideways far more than down**: the lowest ball went at most 0.17 deeper than its twin's ever did (Pylon; Chest 0.03), against the line's `SWING_ALLOWANCE` of 1 (its doc in `ClusterHang` says how the two fit). ⚠ Compare the two twins' deepest, not the two at the same moment: a strand swinging on its own after the landing reads a whole unit "lower" at the instant its twin is at the top of its swing.
- ⚠ **It moves what the sag gate reads at the edge** (`LevelGen --sag`, which repeats it, refusals included), **not the gate as a whole**: over the whole set, one pass each on one build, 70 of 705 runs sagged with it and 74 without. Levels at 3 of 5 trade places across `SAG_RUNS_TO_REPORT` either way (the threaded solve is not repeatable), except Orrery: 3 of 5 in four passes without the push, 3 to 5 in six with it and reported in five, most of them with the glass at rest, a layout at the edge the push tips over. Whether to re-hang it or soften the push is open on #1192.
- **And the swing is settled** (`ShotPush.SETTLE_HOLD`, `SETTLE_RATE`, `SETTLE_SECONDS`): nothing in the lattice damps a hanging cluster's swing, so from a second after a push (the hit is seen whole) for four more, every awake ball of the structure is dragged, on the simulation's own step (its timestepper), so every caller settles alike. ⚠ **Without it a pushed cluster stayed awake**: nine of ten pushed landings on five levels were still over the sleep threshold 14 s after the hit, where the same landings without the push slept in 0.3 to 5.1 s (Pendant's and Sail's not within 14 s either), and an awake 975-ball cluster costs about twelve times a sleeping one ([Wind](#wind-95)); the tiers that give the wind up are the ones that count on it sleeping between shots. Settled, the ten sleep 1.0 to 5.5 s after the hit (`ShotPushTests.APushedClusterFallsAsleepAgainWithinSeconds`). ⚠ On a tier with wind the drag calms the wind's sway for those seconds too.
- ⚠ **The next shot flies at a cluster that still swings**, and without help one shot in six fired 0.3 s after the last then landed beside its ghost: the preview leads the ball it meets and a shot keeps its ghost's promise ([The shot, the landing and the landing preview](landing-and-preview.md)).
- **How hard a hit reads is a `ShotPush.Feel`** (#1248): the share of the momentum (over the kind's own), the most one ball's velocity changes by, and how long the swing runs free before the settle and how fast it is settled. The owner accepted `Shipped` (1, 8, 1 s, 1.5/s) on 2026-11-24 and asked to try more energy, so two are offered beside it, chosen with `pushfeel=` until he picks one: `Stronger` (2: 1.75, 12, 1.5 s, 1.5/s) and `Strongest` (3: 2.5, 12, 1.5 s, 1.8/s). A share over 1 is more than the ball carried, a game's exaggeration. Measured (`ShotPushTests`' report, five levels, two aims each, 8 s; shipped / stronger / strongest): the farthest ball's peak 0.06-1.32 / 0.10-2.33 / 0.13-3.46; the stretch past the landing's own at most 0.02 / 0.04 / 0.058 (Pendant's strand, over the 0.05 the test holds the other two to); asleep again 1.0-5.5 / 1.1-6.9 / 1.2-7.1 s; shots 0.3 s apart in the ghost's cell 97.8 / 97.2 / 96.6 %. ⚠ The sag gate repeats the shipped feel only: re-run it over the whole set before another becomes the default.
- **One copy**, in the contact handler the Game and the Testbed share, **repeated by the sag gate** ([LevelGen's probes](../formats-and-tools/levelgen-probes.md)). `shotpush=<scale>` multiplies it in the Game (0 is none, up to 4; the benchmark's measured run has none and a capture the shipped one, [The benchmark](../game-shell/benchmark.md)), and `logshots` prints each landing's push.

## Culling

**Falling below `KILL_PLANE_Y` removes a ball, and coming to rest never does** (the Testbed also culls sleeping balls): a ball winking out in shot reads as a bug. Two exceptions, both in `RemoveFallenBalls`: a spent cutter goes on the step it struck (#823), and **a released ball still above the field's floor `LooseBallTimeout.FIELD_SECONDS` (5 s) after its release, and not falling out of it (`FALLING_SPEED`), dissolves away over `VANISH_SECONDS` and is retired** (#1191, aged on the world's clock, `_simulatedThisFrame`); below the floor nothing is timed, since a big drop drains through the funnel's hole for up to 13 s ([Dead weight](../game-feedback/dead-weight.md#it-goes-after-its-time-1191)). ⚠ **`_shotBalls` holds shot *bodies*, not shots in flight** (#66): a ball resting on the stone stays in it for the session, so a loss gated on `_shotBalls.Count == 0` could never fire. Ask `AnyShotUndecided`: the **contact-listener flag** (a ball stops listening exactly when its shot resolves) plus `Awake` (a ball resting on another loose shot touched nothing static).

## Wind (#95)

The air moves the cluster **at High and up, and small**: `QualityPreset.ClusterWind` is a tier's to give up because keeping the cluster awake costs CPU (975 balls: 10.1 ms against 0.83 asleep).

- **`WindField`**: a fixed heading and a gusting strength. **It has to vary**: a constant push is absorbed by the sockets, which lean and stay leant. `PhysicsWorld.Wind` joins gravity in the integrator's per-step broadcast. **`SceneWind`** (`Game/SceneWind.cs`) gives each scene a strength and heading, **none** in space, Moon, Mars, cavern, dream, Grid and the Big Top (`Circus`); `wind=<scale>` multiplies it.
- ⚠ **A sleeping island is not integrated**, so `PhysicsWorld.KeepClusterAwake` sets every ball's sleep threshold negative at the build (waking one ball wakes only its own island).
- The strengths are small (sway is the *level's* answer more than the wind's) and the sag gate is undisturbed. Shots do not integrate wind.

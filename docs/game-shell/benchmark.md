# The benchmark (Extras > Benchmark, #988)

Part of [The game: shell](../game-shell.md).

The owner: a benchmark in Extras "that both measures valuable data and is visually attractive", because nobody knows what a player runs the game on (Intel, Qualcomm, another AMD driver, the Pi and whatever follows it), and the 125 % desktop (#825) was found by luck on a second machine. It may have little to do with the game.

## What it does

- **The show** (`BenchmarkPage`): it flies the chapter tour of each of the four tours in `SEGMENTS` (Cavern, Neon City, Forest, Meadow: the dearest scenes on the desktop's High) against the front end's backdrop, the page standing aside while it flies (the Scene picker's mechanism), and then **plays the city's biggest level** (below): its chapter's opening into the gun, and the gun firing at the cluster by itself. Then **the swarm** (below) grows a storm of balls until the frame drops under 60 FPS, and the physics is timed. Each tour is played on a **fixed seed** (`BackdropScreen.PlayTour(Random)`, `SEED` + the segment), and the play on its own, so two runs of one machine fly one path. About two minutes and a half. The skip key stops it, keeping what was measured.
- **At the player's own tier, with the frame free** (`BS3DGame.BeginBenchmark`): `FrameLimitHz` is 0 and `PaceFrame` skips the compositor's wait (either alone holds the frame at the refresh: ⚠ the first run read 13.34 ms on every frame of every scene with the limit off and the wait still on), and `TuneQualityToFrameRate` returns at once, so the adaptive probe does not step the tier under the run. The scenery is shown whatever the Scenery row says (`SceneryShown`: with it off the tours draw no scene, and the run measured nothing and offered a higher tier), and the front end's preview cluster is pinned to `PhysicsBench.LEVEL` (`PinnedPreviewLevel`), so every run tours past the same 1204 balls instead of a random level's 225 to 959. **The front end's own gun (#1040) is not drawn while it runs** (`BackdropScreen.MenuGunInFrame`): the tours fly over its island, it cost the Pi's tours about 1.1 ms a frame, and results from before it stood would no longer compare. `EndBenchmark` puts back the player's scene and its dome (a toured scene that brings a dome of its own, the forest's, would otherwise stay over it) and rolls the player's own preview again.
- **Every frame measured** (`FrameStats`, pure, `FrameStatsTests`): the wall time between the page's updates. Per segment: mean, median, 95th and 99th percentiles, the **1 % low** (the frame rate of the slowest hundredth, averaged), the worst frame and the **share over the display's budget** (one frame of its refresh, 60 Hz where it reports none; over is past it by 2 %, `OVER_SLACK`, since a frame held to the refresh lands on it give or take the timer's jitter). A segment's first `SETTLE_SECONDS` (1.5) are the scene's first-use cost (shaders, the cavern's hall built off the frame) and are reported apart: the cavern's first frames reached 1.26 s on the desktop. The frame list is reserved once (half a minute at a thousand frames a second), so a running segment allocates nothing.
- **The live panel** (`BenchmarkPage.DrawPanel`, `FrameTrace`, pure, `FrameTraceTests`): while the run flies, a plate in the top right corner (the FPS line owns the top left, on by default) shows the part (the scene, then `PHYSICS`) and its place in the run (`1 / 5`), a readout of the mean frame over the last `READOUT_SECONDS` (0.25) with its FPS (`warming up` during the first seconds), and the last `FrameTrace.CAPACITY` (240) frames as bars, the newest on the right. The graph's top is two budgets, so the budget's amber line (captioned with the refresh) runs across its middle; first-use frames are dim, frames over the budget (by `OVER_SLACK`, as the figures count them) are in the alert colour, the rest in the note colour, and the frame after a probe, left out of the figures, is left out of the graph too. Under the physics each bar is one frame's slice of steps (`PhysicsBench.SliceMean`) against the step's budget, captioned `real time`, with the balls in the readout and a bar of how far through the steps it is. Its strings are made once a part and the readout is formatted into reused `StringBuilder`s, so a frame allocates nothing; the panel is part of every measured frame, on every machine alike.
- **The machine** (`BS3DGame.BenchmarkSystem`): the build, the runtime identifier and OS, the OS and process architectures (x64, ARM64, x64 on ARM), the CPU's name (the registry on Windows, `/proc/cpuinfo` on Linux) and threads, the memory (`memoryGb`, what the runtime can use), the size the 3D was drawn at (`renderSize`, the Resolution row), on Windows the display driver's version and date and the card's whole memory (`driver`, `driverDate`, `gpuMemoryGb`, from the adapter's own registry key, the one whose `DriverDesc` is the adapter's name: WMI's `AdapterRAM` stops at 4 GB), on a Raspberry Pi the board's model (`machine`, `/proc/device-tree/model`), the adapter's description and its vendor and device ids (where the platform's adapter has them: DirectX's does, DesktopGL's has neither, so they are read by name), the back buffer, the refresh, windowed or full screen, the tier, its supersample, the Potato path, and the display's scale and DPI awareness (#825). Nothing that names the player: no paths, no user name, as a note's context.
- **Where the result goes**: the page (each scene's median FPS, 1 % low and share over the budget, and the machine in a line), `[bench]` lines in the run log, and `Benchmark.json` in the player's profile (`AtomicFile`, no backup: a benchmark is retaken), and **the author** (below). Never `Progress.json`, never the settings. **A run stopped with the skip key** is shown with how far it got, is neither saved nor sent (a run of one scene never overwrites a whole one) and offers nothing. A skip press the tour refuses in its opening moments (its lockout) is ignored rather than stopping the run under a tour that flies on.
- **`benchmark`** (a launch argument) opens Extras > Benchmark at boot and starts it: one command for a machine nobody is sitting at, or one we do not own. With `userdata=<dir>` the JSON lands there. **It is held to online scores as Start is** (below): a scratch profile's `Settings.json` needs `"online": true` beside its `server`, or the run is refused with `[bench] not started: online scores are off…` and the page waits at its Settings door.

## The play (#1018)

The owner: *"in the benchmark I do not see the cannon... the shot from the city where the camera flies all the way to the cannon should be part of the benchmark"*, and *"scenes where the cannon fires at the map and balls fall, with a camera other than the game's, since the player is not playing"*.
- **Two segments inside a real level**: `PhysicsBench.LEVEL` (Girandole, the city's 1204 balls) built as a **benchmark run** (`GameplayScreen.BuildBenchmarkLevel`, `LevelRun.Benchmark`) and put on the stack as any level is (`BS3DGame.StartBenchmarkPlay`). **City intro** is its chapter's whole opening (the city's prologue shots, then the tour that lands at the gun), its first `SETTLE_SECONDS` reported apart (the level's build and first draws). **City in action** starts when the intro hands the gun back, for 24 s. `BenchmarkDemo`'s lens circles the gun itself, close and low (0.27 of the play camera's distance out, 3.2 over it, looking half way to the cluster's centre), from its side round behind it (over its shoulder, along the shot) to its other side. **The gun is aimed as a player's hand aims it** (the owner: *"it should stay there and aim smoothly, as if a player controlled it"*): the barrel glides to its mark on a critically damped spring (7 rad/s, at most 1.4 rad/s), fires once it has held still on the mark for 0.18 s, and stays on the shot's line for 0.3 s before it moves on. **The mark is chosen by the aim's own solver** (`ShotPlacement.TryFindFirstHitCurved`, as the preview): of 28 random balls the gun can reach, the aim whose first hit is a ball of the loaded colour with the most neighbours of that colour, so the shot completes a group and it falls; the mark is that ball, followed as the cluster sways. A cut is given and loaded at 7 s and 15.5 s, aimed a little under its ball, with 2.6 s after it. A drop's cinematic films a big fall over the lens, as in play, and holds the gun. The HUD and the aim's preview are hidden. On the desktop's run: 8 shots, 123 balls matched, 78 fell loose, 9 cut away. `[bench] action:` says what it did.
- **It writes nothing a player's attempt writes**: no play count (started, ended, abandoned), no record in the save, nothing to the online boards, no lesson (no card is queued), no chapter's opening marked seen, no result page (an ending decides the level and the segment ends there). Dealt off `GameplayScreen.BENCHMARK_SEED`.
- **Measured from the host's frame** (`BenchmarkPage.TickPlay`, called above the stack while the page is off it), into the same figures, probe, graph and panel as a tour (the panel is drawn over the play by the host). When the action has had its time or the level is decided, the session is torn down as the result page's Main Menu tears it down and the page comes back over the main menu for the physics (`BS3DGame.EndBenchmarkPlay`).
- **The player's keys end the benchmark** during the play (Escape, Space, Enter, the pad's A, B, Back and Start: `GameplayScreen.StepBenchmarkInput`), keeping what was measured; **not a click**, which is how a window is brought forward and ended a run twice that way; a lost focus pauses nothing. **`benchmarkplay`** (testing only) starts at the play, without the tours: the play in about a minute, shown and never saved or sent. **A session kept for Continue is closed by the play's build**, as a level picked from the list closes it, and the page says so before it starts (the tree is built again when a level has been kept or let go since, `BenchmarkPage.Refresh`). The play's clock starts after the level's build, so its stall is no measured frame. After the play the page comes back over Extras.
- **The tier offered is read off the tours alone**: the play's frames carry the physics, the shots and the falls, which no tier changes, so a machine short of processor is not told to lower a graphics tier that would not help it; the physics' own line says when the processor is the limit.
- The result's segments carry a `part` (`tour`, `intro`, `action`) beside the scene. `BenchmarkDemoTests` hold the lens: the blend, the circle's distance, height and look, its start and end bearings, no step between frames.

## The swarm

The tours say whether a machine holds the player's tier. The swarm (`BenchmarkSwarm`, after the play and before the physics) says **how far past it the machine reaches, in one figure that grows with the graphics card**: a machine nobody here owns (a stronger Pi, a new integrated GPU) is told apart without its name.
- **A storm of the game's own balls**: a funnel round the island, `INNER_FOOT` 13 to `INNER_TOP` 19 units out and `THICKNESS` 8 deep, rising `HEIGHT` 22 from just over the stone in six arms of six of the game's colours, wound `TURNS` 0.6 of the way round, turning slowly as a whole. A ball's place is a hash of its index (`Place`), so a bigger count is the smaller one filled in, the same on every machine and run. The balls go into the front end's frame beside the preview (`BS3DGame.BenchmarkSwarm`, `BackdropScreen.Draw`), so the LODs, the instancing and the shadow pass are the game's, on the player's tier and resolution.
- **In `SWARM_SCENE` (Space)**, a scene whose own cost is small and fixed. **The front end's flight is put back on its wide turn and held there** (`BackdropScreen.HoldOnWideTurn`, a cut that the change of scene covers): a pass at the map already under way flew the lens through the storm. ⚠ Reach the backdrop through `Game.Backdrop`, not the page's `Manager`: the page has only just been opened again over the menu when the swarm begins, and the hold through `Manager` never took.
- **The ramp** (`Tick`, pure, `BenchmarkSwarmTests`): `START_BALLS` 1024, then `GROWTH` 1.3 times more every `STEP_SECONDS` (1.2) while the step's median frame stays within `BUDGET_MILLISECONDS`. The first `STEP_SETTLE_SECONDS` (0.3) of each step is left out (the buffers grow, the new balls' first draws). A count past the budget is measured once more, so a hitch of the machine's own does not end the run; past it twice, the run ends on the last count that held. At most `MAX_BALLS` (262 144).
- **A machine that cannot hold 1024 is followed down**: while nothing has held, a count broken twice is halved (`DESCENT`), to `MIN_BALLS` (32) at the least, and from the first count that holds the storm grows by `GROWTH` again until the next count would be one that already broke (`BrokeAt`). So a weak machine's figure is as fine as a strong one's, and a strong one's run is no longer. ⚠ The Pi 5 broke 1024 in all four of its runs (24.5 to 24.7 ms on the Potato path at native 1080p), so before the way down its figure was "fewer than 1024" and could not tell the next Pi from it. **The budget is 60 FPS on every machine**, not the display's refresh the tours are judged by, so two machines compare by it. The panel shows the count and a bar of how far it has grown on the ramp's own scale.
- **What it says**: "Swarm: N balls at 60 FPS" on the result page ("fewer than 32" when not even `MIN_BALLS` held); `[bench] swarm:` in the log; `swarm` in the JSON (the scene, the budget, the start, `minBalls`, growth and step, `held` with its median, `brokeAt` (the smallest count that broke) with its median, `steps`, `capped`). It plays no part in the recommended tier.
- **On the desktop** (RX 6900 XT, 1920x1080 windowed, `benchmarkplay`, two runs each): **88 728 balls** at High (median 13.0 ms; 115 347 broke at 26 ms), **52 501** on the Potato path. A ramp step is 30 %, so two machines a step apart differ by about that much at least.

## Sent to the author

The owner (2026-10-25): *"as soon as the benchmark finishes, the result, including the specification of the player's hardware it ran on, must be sent to the server"*, for measurements from graphics cards nobody here owns.
- **Online scores on are the consent** (#1041, the owner 2026-10-29): the Online switch is the one place a player says the game may talk to the server, so **a run starts only with them on** (`BenchmarkConsent.MayRun`, `BenchmarkConsentTests`; `null`, never answered, is off). Start with them off does not begin the run: the page says the benchmark sends its result to the author and runs only with online scores on (`BenchmarkConsent.OFFLINE_NOTE`), and offers **Settings** (which opens on ONLINE) and Back; Back from Settings with them on finds Start (`BenchmarkPage.Refresh`). No nickname is asked by the benchmark: the switch is enough, though the Settings row asks one when there is none. `benchmarkplay` sends nothing and is not held to it. Before a run the page says what goes and that nothing names the player (`BenchmarkConsent.SENT_NOTE`). The privacy list on Settings and About says it too (its Benchmark line).
- **What goes**: the object `Benchmark.json` holds (its `swarm` included), under a random `benchmarkId` and the game's version, to `POST /v1/benchmarks` ([BS3D-API](https://github.com/AntoninPrazsky/BS3D-API)). No player id, no token, no nickname, no `Authorization` header.
- **`OnlineBenchmarks`** (`Game/Online`) keeps the notes' rules: written to `Benchmarks/` in the user data before a byte leaves, gone only when the service's answer names the result, kept on no answer, a 408, a 429, a 5xx or a 4xx without the service's reason, dropped on a 4xx with one; drained at a start with online scores on (`DrainsAtStart`: a result kept while they were on waits, with them off since, so turning them off stops everything the benchmark sends) and after each new result, at most `OutboxCapacity` (10) kept; the server is the one `OnlineScores.TryResolveServer` resolves, so a local build sends only to a server `Settings.json` names. `[bench]` lines say where results go and how each fared.
- **The result page says how it went**, in place, whenever the answer comes: "Sending the result to the author…", then sent, not sent yet (it goes at the next start with online scores on), refused, or that this build sends nowhere.
- **The service** keeps each result as it came with its id, when it arrived and the version, and no player or address; the owner reads them on its admin page's **Benchmarks** tab and with `admin benchmarks` on the box.
- `OnlineBenchmarksTests` run the real sender against the loopback stand-in (`OnlineStandIn.cs`, the play counts' too): sent with no token and gone once named; kept and sent at the next start with online scores on (and kept at one with them off) on a 503, a 429, a bare 404, a 2xx naming another id and a reasonless 422; dropped on a refusal; kept, at most ten, by a build that reaches no server.

## The correctness probe

A driver that draws the wrong picture is a surprise no frame time shows. `PROBE_AT` (3) seconds into each tour the finished frame is read back at the end of Draw (`BS3DGame.ServiceBenchmarkProbe`, through `BackBufferPixels` as a note's picture is, into one buffer kept for the back buffer's size) and judged by `FrameProbe` on a sparse grid: **black** when its mean luma is under `BLACK` (0.01), **flat** when the spread of its luma is under `FLAT` (0.004: one colour from edge to edge), else ok. The read stalls the GPU, so the frame after it is left out of the figures. A failed frame is said plainly on the result page, with where to tell the author, and goes into the log and the JSON (`frame`). A frame that **cannot be read back** at all is `Unread`: in the log and the JSON, never said to the player as a driver fault, since a failed readback says nothing about the picture. On the desktop every scene passed with room: mean luma 0.125 (Neon City) to 0.349 (Meadow), spread 0.17 to 0.20. `FrameProbeTests` (a picture passes, a black and a flat frame are each caught, a NaN and an infinity are counted).

**And the HDR scene behind it.** A NaN or an infinity in the scene target (a shader's division by zero, a driver's broken half floats) can pass the finished frame: the tonemap clamps it to black or white in its own pixel. On the probe's frame, just before the resolve, `BS3DGame.CopyBenchmarkHdr` draws five 64-texel patches of the scene target (the middle and the four quarters' middles, at the supersampled size) texel for texel into a small `HalfVector4` target (point sampled, opaque, so a non-finite value arrives as it is), and the end of Draw reads it back. `FrameProbe.CountNonFinite` counts the texels with a non-finite channel from the packed bits; any one makes the segment `NonFinite`, said on the page as "broken values in its picture". The brightest finite channel is recorded beside it (`hdrBrightest`). On the desktop, at High and at Medium (whose scene target is multisampled), every scene read 20 480 texels, none non-finite, the brightest up to 1.69. None on the Potato path, which has no HDR target.
- ⚠ **`Texture2D.GetData` cannot read an `HdrBlendable` target**: MonoGame's size check (`GraphicsExtensions.GetSize`) does not know the format and throws `ArgumentException` ("Value does not fall within the expected range"). Hence the copy into `HalfVector4`, the same texels in a format it knows.
- ⚠ **The copy is made before the resolve, never at the end of Draw**: binding a target and then the back buffer clears the back buffer (its usage is MonoGame's default discard), and the resolve draws all of it anyway.
- ⚠ **MonoGame's `HalfVector4.ToVector4` does not keep a NaN or an infinity** (`FrameProbeTests` saw one come back finite), so the count reads the exponent bits.

## The physics

After the tours the CPU's physics is timed on its own (`PhysicsBench`), because the tours' only cluster is the front end's preview, which runs no simulation, and a frame time cannot tell the simulation from the GPU. `LEVEL` (Girandole, 1204 balls, the largest cluster that ships) is hung in a `PhysicsWorld` as `GameplayScreen` hangs one: the glass sized off `CeilingPlate`, the funnel floor, the start swing, and its scene's air keeping it awake (`SceneWind.For` with no renderer, so the shared heading and the same gusts every run). Nothing is drawn. `WARMUP_STEPS` (120, a second of steps: the JIT and the pool's first buffers) are reported apart, then `STEPS` (1200) are each timed on the stopwatch, stepped in slices of `PHYSICS_SLICE_MILLISECONDS` (25) a frame so the window keeps answering on a slow machine. It is the game's steady load, not its worst: no shot is fired and nothing attaches or falls.

**A step's budget is the time it simulates** (`STEP_BUDGET_MILLISECONDS`, 8.33 ms), at any refresh: the game takes 120 steps a second, so the physics' share of every frame is a step's time over that (`TimeShare`). Past `PHYSICS_HEAVY_SHARE` (half of every frame) the page says the processor is the limit, which no tier changes; the tier is still recommended from the tours alone. The page shows the median step, its 99th percentile and the share; the log and the JSON (`physics`) carry the rest and the thread count. `PhysicsBenchTests` (the cluster hung whole, every step measured, nothing taken without time). On the desktop (Ryzen 9 5900X, 24 threads): median 1.29 ms a step, p99 4.14, worst 4.66, **15.5 % of every frame**, nothing over the budget; the warm-up's worst step 122 ms.

## What it recommends

`BenchmarkVerdict.Recommend`, read from the numbers alone, never from the device's name, so a machine nobody has seen needs no code. **One step at a time**, as the adaptive probe steps, because the run measured one tier and a tier's cost is not a scale factor of another's: a tier that drops frames in any scene (more than `DROPS`, 5 %, of its frames over the refresh's budget) recommends the one under it, naming the scene; a tier whose every scene's 99th percentile stays under `ROOM` (0.6) of the budget recommends the one over it; anything between keeps the tier. The verdict is the tier the run **measured**, kept with the result: the page is kept between visits, and a tier changed in Settings since is said beside it ("Measured at High; your quality is Medium now") instead of judging the old figures as the new tier's. The result page says so in a sentence and **offers** the tier as an entry ("Use Medium", "Try Ultra"), which puts it on as the Quality row's press does (`BS3DGame.ChooseQuality`: pinned, stored, Auto quality off) and leaves the page ready to run again on it. Nothing is applied unasked; a locked or Potato run is offered nothing (its renderer cannot change while it runs). `BenchmarkVerdictTests`. On the desktop at 1920 x 1080 Medium recommended High (every scene over 480 FPS).

## What it read on the desktop

RX 6900 XT, Ryzen 9 5900X, 1920 x 1080 windowed, High (ssaa 2), two runs: Cavern 346 and 361 FPS median, Neon City 196 and 196, Forest 173 and 175, Meadow 245 and 247; 0 % over the 75 Hz budget. A run's own spread is a few per cent.

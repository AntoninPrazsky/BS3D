# Help, About, the splash and the build tag

Part of [The game: shell](../game-shell.md).

`Game/Screens/` holds a `MenuPage` base, one class per page (`SplashPage`, `MainMenuPage`, `LevelSelectPage`, `PausePage`, `QuitConfirmPage`, `SettingsPage`, `ScenePage`, `ExtrasPage`, `JukeboxPage`, `HelpPage`, `AboutPage`, `ResultPage`, the boards' and the nickname's), the two screens that are not pages (`BackdropScreen`, `GameplayScreen`) and `PlayHud` ("The in-play HUD" in `docs/game-feedback.md`). Picker and result: [Level picker and result page](level-picker-and-result.md).

## Help: six topics, as tabs (#427, #857)

Six topics in one row of tabs on the Settings page's pattern (`MenuPage.TabRow`, shared with Settings): **CONTROLS · BASICS · SCORING · ODD BALLS · CAMPAIGN · THE GLASS**, every one visible at once and one click away, the chosen one inverted. **Help opens on Controls every time** — which key does what is what a player opens Help for; until #857 it was the last of six pages walked with Previous and Next, five presses away. The tabs are turned, wrapping, by ←/→, the D-pad, the left stick, LB/RB and the wheel over the row (`HelpPage.PageSideways`, `OnScrollWheel`); they are not in the pad's walk, so Back is the one entry and the cursor arrives on it. `help` / `help=<n>` open it at boot, `n` the tab's number in the row's order (1 = Controls). The odd balls come from a table keyed by `BallKind` (`OddBallCopy.Entries`, one file with no device in it, which `OddBallCopyTests` reads).

- **Short tab names, because six tabs cut to the widest name's width must fit a row**: measured with the section face at 1080p the row is 1036 px against the text frame's 930, and "HOW TO PLAY" in place of BASICS took it to 1210. The frame is never narrower than the row (#770's rule), so the body's frame follows the tabs and the text stands against its left edge.
- **A paragraph opens on a lead-in** (#996, `MenuPage.Paragraph(lead, text, width)`): a few words in the display face's capitals and the loud white, run into its first line, so the leads read down a tab are its point in a few phrases (BASICS: THE GOAL, MATCH & DROP, FIND THE ANCHOR, AMMO & QUEUE, PATTERNS). Run in rather than a heading of its own, so a tab is no taller for it ([Typesetting](menu-look.md#typesetting-806)).
- **BASICS says once that every colour wears a pattern of its own** (#737), the same on every ball of the colour and on its disc in the queue, so that nobody reads a striped or spotted ball as a special one. Only where the balls carry it (`BallRenderSet.DrawsColourCue`: not on the Potato path).
- **SCORING is the price list and nothing of the arithmetic** (#998): four leads with the figure in them (matched, detached, streak, unused balls) and a tip, and no formula, worked example or narration of what play already shows (the caption over the screen, the shattering multiplier). The worked example it carried until then read as a maths textbook to a bubble shooter's player. ⚠ **Every figure is `ScoreKeeper`'s own constant**, interpolated, never typed: a hand-written one is wrong the first time the scoring is tuned and nothing says so.
- **The balls a release brings down are *detached***, on SCORING and on the result page's breakdown row alike (#998); the code's `Orphaned` names stay. "Orphaned" was a programmer's word, and "dropped" was not taken because the tutorial already says "drop a group" for the matched group's own fall.
- ⚠ **The controls tab draws keycaps through `BS3DGame.MenuFontPrompt`, not `HudFontPrompt`**: the HUD's fonts are built by `EnsureHudFonts`, which only the gameplay screen calls, so on a menu page it is null, and **a Myra label with a null font draws nothing, silently**. Columns of their own width (#728, #997): keycaps (as wide as the widest pair, `MenuFontPrompt.MeasureString`), the pad's buttons (#799), the action's name in the display face, and what the key does in the small face, one line each; both hands shown. **A line says what the key does and nothing more** (#997): the Cut's mechanics are the Cutter's entry on ODD BALLS, the brake's are THE GLASS's, and when the three power-ups arrive and their price is one line under the table, read off `LevelSet.SWAP_FROM_BLOCK`, `BRAKE_FROM_BLOCK` and `CUT_FROM_BLOCK`. With the F keys on one more line the tab fits its frame and does not scroll; it scrolled while the price stood on every line and the words were in the display face, which closes up into a block over two and three lines.
- **Every tab stands in the same frame (#728, #729)**: every body is built into a scroller of its own, all six in one page area and one shown (`HelpPage.ShowTab`, from the top), each measured (`Widget.Measure`) and all given the biggest width and height as `MinWidth`/`MinHeight` (minimums, so `MenuScroll`'s `MaxHeight` still scrolls a short window). A tab turn swaps visibility; nothing is rebuilt. The tree is rebuilt on every entry (`Enter`), which is what puts Controls, or the tab `help=` asked for, up.
- **The odd balls are shown, not only described (#856)**: each entry has a square at its left (`BALL_SQUARE`, 176 units, two and a half lines of the prose beside it) and the kind's own ball turning in it, drawn live by the game's ball path — the kind card's device (#735): one more ball in an aside of the ball set's (`BallRenderSet.DrawAside`, after the backdrop's or a paused session's own frame, whose motion record it keeps), hung `BALL_DEPTH` in front of the lens on the ray through the square, sized to fill it, in the style the cluster behind hangs in — into the sharp foreground layer the host composites over the UI (`HelpPage.DrawForeground`, "The sharp foreground" in `docs/rendering/post-processing.md`), so the plate does not dim it. The squares are read where the desktop laid them out (`ToGlobal`; `ActualBounds` is in the widget's own space), so the balls follow the scroll; a square scrolled past the frame's edge is cut there by the host's clip, and one wholly past it is not drawn. The colours are the entry's own (`OddBallCopy.Entries`), varied down the column for the kinds that keep theirs. On Potato the balls are drawn straight over the frame after the desktop, uncut, so only squares whole inside the frame are drawn there. **Cost, measured with the ten cluster kinds** (`logfps`, the Moon pinned, High, 1920×1080 at 2× supersampling, the ThinkPad's integrated GPU): the tab with the balls 40.2 ms a frame against 38.4 on the campaign tab — about 1.8 ms, the full-frame layer's clear and composite more than the ten balls. The two the cannon holds (next) were not measured.
- **The two kinds the cannon holds have a square too (#895)**: the rainbow ball (the wildcard) and the Cutter (the Cut's round), after the cluster's ten and under a line of their own (`OddBallCopy.GunLead`), with the sentences the page carries for them elsewhere (the rainbow ball's paragraph, the Controls tab's line for the Cut). Which group a kind is in is `BallKinds.Shootable` (`OddBallCopy.Entry.InGun`), not a flag typed beside it, so a shot-only kind added later joins the second group and fails the test below until it has its entry. **Both are drawn as they sit loaded in the cannon** (`still: true` into `BallRenderSet.Add`): the rainbow ball does not breathe and the Cutter's blade idles at its loaded rate (`CUTTER_SPIN_LOADED`); a ball added without `still` is drawn in its flying pose (the Cutter's blade at `CUTTER_SPIN_FLYING`), which is not what these squares show. ⚠ **The rainbow ball cycles on a `WildcardCycle` of the page's own** (`HelpPage._cycle`), stepped by the wall clock's elapsed time in `DrawForeground` and **stated to the ball set (`SetWildcardCrossing`) before `DrawAside` opens its frame**: the set holds one crossing for the whole program and only a session states it, so on a Help opened from the main menu, where there is none, an unstated set draws the ball as a plain one of the first colour and it never cycles. Under a Help opened from the pause the session has drawn its wildcards before the page and states its own crossing again before every collection, so the page's statement reaches none of theirs. The cycle walks all thirteen colours (the page has no cluster to keep to) at the game's own pace (`WildcardCycle.CROSSING_SECONDS`). On Potato both are whole squares like the rest: the rainbow ball a flat sphere that changes colour, the Cutter steel with its blade ring.
- **`OddBallCopyTests` holds the table to every kind**: each `BallKind` but Normal has exactly one entry, the cannon's entries are `BallKinds.ShootableKinds` and come last (the page puts its line between the two groups), and every sentence passes `CopyRules`. The table is a file of its own with no device in it, compiled into the test project, because `HelpPage` builds Myra widgets no test can construct.
- **The paragraphs are set across the whole text width** (`TEXT_WIDTH`, 83 characters, not the prose measure: the owner's ruling on #865, less scrolling; [Typesetting](menu-look.md#typesetting-806)), against the frame's left edge and its top, so a tab that fits starts under the tabs where one that scrolls does; an odd ball's words take that less the square and its gap. The body's blocks carry their own gaps (`KEY_LINE_GAP` under a control's line).
- **Back is one row under the text with the Scroll prompt at the row's right** (#738, #857): Back under the text's first letter, a third of the row wide; the prompt (right stick or wheel) shows only while the tab's body is longer than its frame.

## About: four pages, the player last (#807)

The owner's "nobody reads that much text": one idea a page, each short enough to read without deciding to, and **the player last**: it is a small easter egg (the owner, 2026-10-12), waiting for whoever turns that far, so About opens on what the game is. **1 The game**: four sentences, and the online scores' privacy sentence under them as an aside (`OnlineScores.PrivacySentence`, the Settings page's own). **2 Built on**: one line on the procedural look, then the libraries and typefaces with their licence as a list. **3 Made with**: that the original game is the author’s own code, written without AI, and that what it has gained since 2026 was made with Claude through Claude Code (#866), the locally generated rest as a list, the repository (a real `MenuButton`) and Write to the Author ([Send a note](send-a-note.md)). **4 The original score**: one line, the piece's name, the spectrum at the prose measure and Play/Next ("The About page's player" in `docs/game-feedback.md`). The words and the blocks each page holds, in order, are `AboutCopy.Pages` (`Game/Screens/AboutCopy.cs`); whatever does not fit a page belongs in the repository.

- **`AboutCopyTests` holds every credit the two-column page of #463 named**, so a shorter page cannot drop a library, a version, a licence or a tool unseen; every string to `CopyRules`; and the player on the last page with what the game is on the first, the privacy line under what the game is and the repository's block, once each. **The page draws `AboutCopy.Pages` and nothing else** (`AboutPage.BuildBody` sets each block it is handed and throws on a kind it cannot set), so what the test reads is what is drawn. A credit list's role that would wrap is shortened (a wrapped role broke the rows into a ragged block, photographed).
- **The pages turn as the picker's chapters and High Scores' do**: ◀ and ▶ tiles either side of the page's name (the section role), "1 of 4" under it, the pad's LB/RB under the arrows (`MenuPage.ArrowWithShoulder`, nothing for the keyboard, #848); the arrow keys, D-pad and stick (`PageSideways`). Clamped, not wrapped; an arrow at an end keeps its slab and dims.
- **Across the player's row the arrows walk it, and past its ends they turn** (the issue's "decide it once"): `AboutPage.PageSideways` declines from Play rightwards and from Next leftwards, so the host's row rule (#805) takes the press, and turns the page otherwise: left from Play is the page before, right from Next is nothing (the player's page is the last). ⚠ The keyboard has no shoulders: a row that left the turn to LB/RB alone would leave the arrow keys nothing to turn back with from Play, and nothing on screen says to go up to ◀. `ShoulderTurn` turns from anywhere.
- **Every page stands in the biggest page's frame** (`AboutPage.BuildFrame`, Help's pattern since #729): each page's body is built and measured and every one is given the biggest width and height, the shown one built last so the player's widgets the page keeps are the shown tree's. No scroller: the tallest page with the heading, the pager and Back fits the design height with room.
- **The cursor arrives on ▶ on page one**, where About opens, as Help's arrives on Next, and on Play when About is opened on the player's page. A turn leaves it on the arrow that way, or on the other one at an end, so turning onto the player's page leaves it on ◀ (▶ is off there) with Play one press down. `OpenAbout` always opens page one; `about=<n>` any page, `about=play` the player's (`AboutPage.PlayerPage`).
- **`Leave`, raised on the pop, stops the piece**, which brings the game's music back (`GameMusic.Yielding`); turning between About's pages is not leaving, so a piece plays on under the text.

**`MusicVisualizer`** is the first widget that draws itself (Myra's `InternalRender`, `FlatBrush.Fill`, #712). **Its colour (#730) is the one deliberate exception to the menu's "brightness, never hue"**: an instrument, not chrome. 18 LED segments lit by height (`VisualizerPalette.Led` or `Spectrum`); **peak caps** (`Prazsky.Core.Tools.PeakHold`) fall under gravity integrated exactly in seconds (identical at 60 and 240 Hz), stepped from the wall clock inside its draw (the widget has no `Update`).

## The build's name tag

`BS3DGame.VersionTag`/`MenuPage.VersionTag`, bottom right of the front end and About: `v0.2.1` out of a release, `dev-<short sha>` otherwise, from `Game/BuildVersion.cs` (`Release` is the tag `release.yml` stamped; `Name` is the score submission's `gameVersion`). It shows `DisplayName`, `Name` plus ` ARM64` in an ARM64 process (GamePi; display only). It is not in the pad's walk. ⚠ **A dev build's tag changes every commit**, so a pixel diff of front-end captures from two commits always differs in that corner; a `-p:BS3DReleaseVersion=` build (`Tools/release-screenshot.ps1`) shows a fixed name.

## The splash (#454)

`SplashPage` is the intro and the first page that **owns a piece of time**: pushed at boot, it **replaces** itself with the front end (a pop would leave the scene without a menu). Five legs on the page's own clock (frame-rate independent): black; the 2D logo fades up and holds (`FADE_UP_SECONDS`, `HOLD_SECONDS`); the black fades over the orbiting scene (`BLACK_OUT_SECONDS`); a beat (`BEAT_SECONDS`); the picture fades out as the 3D wordmark grows in (`LOGO_OUT_SECONDS`, #621). **No fly or morph into the wordmark** (ruled out by the owner).

- **The titles cross over, they do not overlap**: `SplashPage.WordmarkPresence` takes the wordmark 0 → 1 over the logo's fade-out, into `TitleWordmark.Draw`'s `presence`, which scales the block's **size** about its corner (the letters are opaque; the scene menu's fly-in, #261, does the same and the two multiply).
- **The page stays on top through all five legs**, so Myra and the menu's keys see no input until it replaces itself, and `IsSplashUp` stays true: every startup-argument page waits on it.
- **The picture is drawn in pixels** (two sprites in the host's overlay batch, not a Myra widget: the black must cover the *frame*, and Myra's paint stops short of the bottom rows, #114): scale `min(width / 3840, height / 1600)`, 1:1 from 3840×1600 to 3840×2160, whole pixels.
- **The skip**: input is ignored for `SKIP_AFTER` (else the click that focused the window skips it). The first press finishes the fade up and runs the rest at `SKIP_RATE`; a second cuts to the menu. `play` skips the splash.
- **Off**: the Settings row Intro logo and **`nosplash`** make `BuildMenu` push the main menu where the splash would stand; `nosplash` is applied, never written back.
- **Testing**: the harness's title-bar click dismisses the intro, so use `shot=`; shots under a second apart trip the quality probe (photograph and time in separate runs).

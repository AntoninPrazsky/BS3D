# Menu navigation: pad, keyboard, mouse and the prompts drawn for them

Part of [The game: shell](../game-shell.md).

The menus are fully drivable from a gamepad (#61): Xbox is a target, and `Buttons.Back` already opens the pause menu. The pad also feels what it does here ("The pad" in `docs/game-feedback.md`, #800).

## Who drives

- The D-pad, left stick and arrow keys move a focus cursor; **A** or Enter presses the focused entry; **B** is Escape, both through the one `MenuBack` that owns which screens have a back. The A that completes the Konami code on the main menu (#230) presses nothing.
- **A grid page is walked by the pad in the direction pressed** (`MenuPage.PadWalksGrid`, the level picker, #848): the D-pad and the left stick move the cursor to the entry in line ahead (`NavWalk.Toward`: in line first, then the nearest; out of line only within about 27° edge to edge (the gap across at most half the way ahead), so the grid's ends stop), and the shoulders turn the page. The arrow keys there keep the list's order on up and down and turn the page on left and right, so a keyboard player still reaches every entry. The owner, pad in hand, expected down to light the level under the lit one and found the D-pad turning chapters the prompts gave to LB and RB.
- **Entries are found by walking the shown widget tree** (`CollectNavEntries`: on a screen change, a layout rebuild and a level-picker chapter turn, never per frame), not from a registered list that an entry added later silently falls out of. `Visible = false` keeps the cursor off Continue and Next Level for free.
- ⚠ **Add children in the order the pad should step through them**: the walk follows insertion order.
- ⚠ **Exactly one device drives at a time.** The focus cursor reuses the hover brush, but the pointer need not *move* to sit over an entry (a pause recentres it), which lit a second entry beside the focused one. So while the cursor is up the hover is **off** (`ApplyNavHighlight`); moving the pointer puts the cursor down and restores it.
- **Which entry is focused does not survive a screen change; whether the cursor is up does** (a pad player backing out of a sub-screen would otherwise press a direction every time). `CollectNavEntries` re-baselines the pointer, or the frame after a pause reads its recentring as the mouse moving. **A page turn is the one re-read where the index must survive**: `RefreshNavEntries` names the entry to keep.
- **Accept with the cursor down only *raises* it**: firing the top entry would make the first press mean "New Game" over a session in progress, on the screen that exists to offer Continue.
- **A held direction** steps once, waits `NAV_REPEAT_DELAY`, then walks at `NAV_REPEAT_INTERVAL`, in wall-clock seconds, not frames. One `HeldDirectionFires` serves **two axes** (up/down walk the entries, left/right go to the active page, `MenuPage.PageSideways`: picker, Help, Settings, High Scores), each with its own held-direction fields so a diagonal push holds both.

## A cut list stands clear of its neighbours (#1002)

**A scroller whose content is taller than its bound has a clear band of `MENU_SCROLL_EDGE_GAP` (36 units) at both ends of its clip** (`BS3DGame.MenuScroll`), so a line or an entry cut by the scroll is cut a gap away from the tabs or heading over it and from Back under it. Before, the cut was the neighbours' own edge, and a sliced line of Help sitting on Back read as the page overlapping itself rather than as more to scroll.

- **The band is a margin inside the bound**: Myra counts a widget's margin inside its `MaxHeight`, so the page is no taller (Back stood on the same pixel row before and after, photographed at 1600x900) and the list's window is two bands shorter.
- ⚠ **Myra clips a scroller's content at its margin's outer edge**, not its own box: with the margin alone the cut line was drawn into the band, a margin past the end of the scroll bar. `MenuScrollViewer.InternalRender` narrows the scissor to its own `ActualBounds` (in the back buffer's pixels, through `ToGlobal`), the rectangle Help's odd balls are cut to as well (`HelpPage.ForegroundClip`), so a ball and its words are cut on one line.
- **Only where the content is cut**: `MenuScroll` measures the content unbounded once, when the tree is built. The level picker's two rows of tiles fit at every window size (everything scales with the height), and the band would only have grown its plate over the foot of the cluster the camera frames above it. Help passes `clearEdges` to keep it on all six tabs, the ones that fit too: they stand in one frame (#729), and a tab without the band would start its text a band higher than one that scrolls. So does the Jukebox's list of recordings, which is refilled in place for every chapter and would keep the band its first chapter's count decided.

## The mouse wheel over a list (#952)

Every scrolling menu stands in a `MenuScrollViewer` (`BS3DGame.MenuScroll`: Help's six bodies, the Jukebox's chapters and recordings, the Scene list, the level picker's grid), which replaces Myra's wheel step and nothing else. **A slow notch moves a fifth of the list's own height** (`WheelScroll.VIEWPORT_SHARE`), so the same part of the picture at every resolution and for a short list as for a long one, and **a fast spin accelerates**: each notch already turned in a run of events no more than 0.1 s apart and in one direction adds half a step to the next event's, up to four times a slow notch (`WheelScroll.Momentum`, `BOOST_PER_NOTCH`, `MAX_BOOST`). Four times a fifth is 0.8 of the viewport, the share the right stick and Page Down on Help move (`HelpPage.PAGE_KEY_SHARE`), so even the fastest spin leaves a fifth of what was on screen and no line passes unseen between two events; a pause or a turn the other way starts the run again, so a correction is never thrown at the full rate.

The step is `delta / 120` notches (`WheelScroll.NOTCH`, Windows' `WHEEL_DELTA`), so a free-spinning wheel that lands three detents in one frame moves three steps (paid at the boost before the event, so a burst is not squared), and a high-resolution wheel's eighth-notch deltas add up through a carried fraction. It writes `ScrollPosition`, clamped to `[0, ScrollMaximum.Y]`, which is what the pad's walk, the right stick and Page Up/Down write too, so `KeepNavFocusInView` follows it and a wheel that arrives with the cursor up takes the focus to the first entry wholly in view. The scrollbar's thumb and a drag on it are Myra's.

- ⚠ **Myra 1.6.3's `ScrollViewer.OnMouseWheel` is not a step in pixels you can tune with `ScrollMultiplier`**: it moves `ScrollMultiplier × ScrollMaximum.Y / thumb travel` pixels for any non-zero delta, whatever its size. That is about ten times content height over viewport height, so the same 12 to 23 pixels at 720p and at 3840x1600 (a share of the screen that falls as the display grows), longer lists moved further than short ones, and a spin of several detents in one frame moved one. Raising the multiplier would have kept all three.
- **Measured in the running game** (a probe in `MenuScrollViewer.InternalArrange` that called Myra's own `OnMouseWheel(-120)` and this class's on the laid-out list, 2026-10-20 and again 2026-10-22; windowed, native 3D). Px are the list's pixels; a "notch" is a delta of 120; "range" is `ScrollMaximum.Y`. The first turn of the wheel in a run prints one `[menu] wheel:` line with the slow notch in pixels and as a share of the viewport, so a "the wheel is too slow" report can be checked against the step:

- **Which lists scroll at all depends on the window.** Of Help's tabs only Controls, Scoring and Odd balls overflow the frame at 1280x720 to 3840x1600 (the other three fit), the chaptered level picker's grid fits at every size tried down to 960x540 (and its wheel turns the chapter, `LevelSelectPage.OnScrollWheel`), and a list that fits takes no wheel from its scroller, because Myra hands a delta only to a widget under the pointer that `AcceptsMouseWheel`, which a scroller does only while its content is taller than it. A range shorter than a fifth of the viewport is crossed in one notch.
- A step is a fifth and not a third because a long body (Odd balls, 1267 px at 3840x1600) is then still read in screenfuls, with a spin as the way to cover it fast: a notch every 40 ms crosses the Scene list in four events instead of five, Odd balls in four instead of seven. The acceleration needs a run of events, so a single-detent scroller never meets it.
- **The arithmetic is pure** (`WheelScroll.Pixels`, `Move`, `Momentum.Take`, the clock handed in) in `WheelScrollTests`, which holds the share across viewports, the independence of the content's length, the delta's scaling, the carry, the two ends, the boost's ramp, cap, pause and reversal, and the Scene list's slow and fast crossing at 1920x1080 and 3840x1600.

## The keys are drawn in the UI (#799)

The binding is part of the UI, always visible beside what it does, never a legend elsewhere.

- **What is drawn.** B or Escape on every Back and on the entries that do what B does (the pause's Resume, the Quit confirm's Cancel); LB/RB or ←/→ at the ends of the Settings and Help tab rows (`MenuPage.TabRow`, #857); **the pad's LB/RB under the picker's and High Scores' chapter arrows and About's page arrows (#807), and nothing for the keyboard there** (`MenuPage.ArrowWithShoulder`, #848: an arrow key beside an arrow only repeated it, the owner's call); LB/RB or ←/→ under the level boards' Previous/Next (`LevelBoardPage.PageSideways`); A or Enter on the focused entry; the right stick or wheel beside "Scroll" on a long Help page.
- **One hand at a time** (`BS3DGame.PromptsForPad`): the last device to give a menu any input decides (`NotePromptHand`). The pad means a button, or a stick or trigger past `NAV_STICK_DEADZONE`; keyboard and mouse mean a key held, a button, or the pointer past `NAV_MOUSE_WAKE_PIXELS` (reset on a screen change, so a pause's recentring does not count). A pad beside a keyboard player changes nothing until touched (#817). A pause starts from the session's hand (`Rumble.HandOnPad`). **`pad`** pins the menus as it pins the session.
- **`PromptGlyph` draws whichever glyph is current as it renders**, as wide as the wider of the two, so changing hands changes no layout. Inside a button (`MenuButton`'s prompted form) the word stays centred and the glyph stands at the slab's inner edge. Too-narrow entries get their prompt outside, dimmed with the entry (`StandsFor`): under the level boards' Previous/Next (`MenuPage.PageTurnUnder`, #848; Help's walking buttons had theirs beside them until the tabs of #857).
- **A or Enter is the host's** (`DrawFocusPrompt`, after the desktop), so a walk moves one glyph and relays no page. Drawn only while the cursor is up, never on an entry with its own prompt, and only on one at least `FOCUS_PROMPT_MIN_SHARE` of a column wide.
- **Glyphs.** The shoulders are PromptFont's full-height pills U+2B98/U+2B99 (the HUD's Cut chip's U+2199 is half height and read as small print). PromptFont has no single-arrow keycap, so ← and → are its bold arrows.
- **The pad's Menu button pauses a level and resumes it** (before #799 only View did). Re-check with `pad` and `menucursor=` ([Launch arguments](front-end-launch-arguments.md)).

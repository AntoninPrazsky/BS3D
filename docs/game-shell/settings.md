# The Settings page: tabs, layout and the rows' rules

Part of [The game: shell](../game-shell.md).

`SettingsPage` is reached from the front end and from a pause (pushed over whatever was up, so Back is a `Pop`). The ONLINE rows are in [Online rows and the nickname plate](online-and-nickname.md), the window in [The window](window-and-dpi.md).

## Pages by category (#686)

- Six tabs (`TAB_NAMES`): **ONLINE · SCREEN · PICTURE · AUDIO · CONTROLS · GAME**. The page **opens on ONLINE every time**, because the first online test could not find the rows among the display toggles.
- **SCREEN:** Fullscreen, Resolution (#801), Quality, Auto quality, Scenery (#808, beside Quality, which it turns Custom), FPS limit: how big and how fast the game draws. **PICTURE:** Brightness, Sky, 10-bit colour (#859), Aberration, Film grain, Motion blur: how the picture looks. They were one DISPLAY page of twelve rows until #869, the whole vertical budget (Back about 24 px clear at 1600×900, 15 at 1280×720); split along the seam the owner's UI review drew, six rows each. **Two tabs and not two columns**, because left and right already turn the tabs (`PageSideways`) and a grid would have needed them for its columns; the pad's walk stays one line down a page. `settings=tab:display` still puts up SCREEN for an older script. **AUDIO:** the four volumes. **CONTROLS:** Sensitivity, Aim sensitivity, Rumble, Keyboard lights (#987, a box: a backlit keyboard shows the game, "On (none found)" when Windows lists none, [The backlit keyboard](../game-feedback/keyboard-lighting.md)), Precise aim, and where a touch screen answers Touch sensitivity and Aim on the right (first on a phone, below). **GAME:** Tutorial, Intro logo, Drop camera, FPS counter (moved from the then DISPLAY by #859: a readout the game puts in front of the player, and DISPLAY's rows were its whole budget), then the CAMPAIGN pair under its own heading. The destructive rows are each last on their page.
- **Unlock all is a dev-build row (#349, #854).** It is drawn only when `BuildVersion.Release` is null, so a release (a tag build) ends the GAME page at Reset progress, and `ToggleUnlockAll` refuses there too, so nothing can open a shipped campaign through a row the player cannot see. The gate is the build's own word, not the Debug/Release configuration: the owner plays Release-configuration builds of main, and those keep the row. Where it is drawn it is off at every launch and never written (`GameSettings` excludes it).

## The tab row

- **It is not in the pad's walk** (five tabs ahead of the first row would cost five presses). The tabs are turned, wrapping, by left/right (arrows, D-pad, left stick: `PageSideways`), LB/RB (one press, one page), the mouse wheel over the row, and a click.
- A tab is a `MenuButton` whose `Tag` is cleared: `CollectNavEntries` walks only buttons with an `Action` on their `Tag`, so the mouse keeps hover, press and click sound and the pad never lands on one. The LB/RB prompts stand outside the tabs ([The keys are drawn in the UI](menu-navigation.md#the-keys-are-drawn-in-the-ui-799)).
- **The chosen tab is inverted** (`MenuPage.TabRow.SELECTED_BRUSH`, with `SELECTED_TEXT`), the opposite of a row's focus highlight. **ONLINE stays red from any other tab while the boards still want something done** (Off, or No server, #687). A tab turn is "moving on": a typed name is kept if it is one, and an armed "Sure?" stands down.

## The phone layout (#1151)

On the enlarged interface ([The phone layout](menu-look.md#the-phone-layout-tabs-down-a-rail-the-reading-across-the-width-1151)) the page is `SettingsPage.BuildRailTree`'s, not the row's: the monitor's page, only enlarged, leaves a phone's rows and tabs under 6 mm, a third of its width empty and ONLINE's privacy list in a scroller of a few lines.

- **The tabs down a rail at the left, Back at its foot**, the heading centred on the rows' seam beside it.
- **Every row is a touch target tall** (`PHONE_TARGET`, 9 mm on the X300), `PHONE_ROW_SPACING` apart. That is what the height holds: six rows and the heading, so the page fits at the front end's size. A seventh row on a tab, or a group heading between six, would bring every tab down with it, since every tab stands in the tallest one's frame. No test holds this (the page is a Myra tree on the device); a page brought down says so in the run log, `[ui] SettingsPage is … px tall … laid out at …x`, which a run with `inches=2.8` in a 3168×1440 window and `settings=` must not print.
- **One seam on every tab and two value widths.** ONLINE's and GAME's values are a box, a name or a word (`PHONE_VALUE_WIDTH`), and the width beside their rows is taken by **ONLINE's note**, from the top of the page beside the heading and the rows, and **GAME's campaign pair** under its heading, beside the switches: the heading's row is a row's height there, so the heading stands level with the first switch and the pair's rows with the next two. The other tabs' values reach across the rest of the width, never narrower than the widest value (the Resolution row's native size) between a − and a +.
- **ONLINE's narrow value column is what leaves the note its width**: beside a value column as wide as the other tabs', the note stands 1449 px tall in the emulation against the 1298 the height leaves, and the whole page is brought down from 1.33× to 1.00×.
- **The rows a phone does not have are not drawn in the emulation either** (`SettingsPage.PhoneOnly`): Fullscreen and Keyboard lights, and 10-bit colour, which on a phone only ever said "(panel)" (the game cannot ask a phone's panel its depth).
- **CONTROLS opens on the touch screen's rows on a phone** (#1183, as Help's CONTROLS puts Touch first): Touch sensitivity and Aim on the right, then the mouse's and the pad's (Sensitivity, Aim sensitivity, Rumble, Precise aim) for a pad plugged in, six rows, in the pad's walk in that order. Elsewhere the touch rows stay last, where a touch screen answers beside a mouse and keys.

## Every page stands in the same frame

- The page area is a `Panel` holding all five grids, one shown, fixed at the tallest page's measured height and the tab row's width (#770), so a tab never moves Back.
- **Two columns of one width, the seam on the centre line (#833).** Captions end at the seam, right-aligned; values start from it, left-aligned; a group heading (CAMPAIGN) is centred over it.
- ⚠ Myra lays out only what is visible, so the height is **measured with every page in the tree at build**, and **value labels read "Off" until `Refresh` writes them**: a button round an empty label measures shorter than it stands.
- ⚠ **The page area carries no margin; the gap above Back is Back's own.** Myra counts a `Margin` *inside* an explicit `Height`, so a gap under the area cut the tallest page's last row (the then DISPLAY's).
- **The vertical budget is the trap.** Sizes are 2160p design figures scaled by the viewport's height, so a page that overruns overruns at every resolution and aspect, and rows that run off the bottom take Back, the only way out, with them (#138, #548). **A page past six rows means checking Back is whole at 1600×900 and 1280×720**: twelve on one page left it about 24 px and 15 px clear, and took the rows' spacing and padding down to 12 (`ROW_SPACING`, `ROW_PADDING_Y`, back at 20 and 18 since #869). The tallest page is ONLINE now, its three rows and the privacy list (#868), its plate about 26 px inside a 1600×900 window with the list in two columns (#1034) and its labels in the text's face (#1154).

## Two kinds of control (#816)

- **A checkbox for a row with two states that would read On and Off; a button that cycles its value for every other row.** Never a slider or a drop-down: one click, nothing left half-dragged, and each change takes effect where it is made, so the scene behind the panel is the preview. A row whose caption already says what is on reads faster as a tick than as the word "On".
- **The checkbox is a member of the menu's button family** (`MenuPage.CheckBox` round a `CheckGlyph`): the same slab and brushes, the pad's focus, the click sound and #800's haptics. The box and the tick are drawn as shapes (`CheckTick`, held to a brute-force cover by `CheckTickTests`), because Anton has no ☐ or ✓ and FontStashSharp drops a glyph a face lacks. On Settings the whole row toggles: a press on the caption is a press on the box.
- **The checkboxes:** Online scores on ONLINE (#1034), Fullscreen, Auto quality and Scenery on SCREEN, 10-bit colour, Aberration, Film grain and Motion blur on PICTURE; Tutorial, Intro logo, Drop camera, FPS counter and Unlock all on GAME; and the note page's "Send the picture". A row the tier, the build or the panel holds is dimmed with its reason beside the box ("(tier)", "(locked)", "(Potato)", "(8-bit panel)", "(driver)"): unticked when held off, ticked when held on (Auto quality's "(resolution)" on the Pi, #832). Auto quality and Scenery take their state from the game's own words (`AdaptiveQualityLabel`, `SceneryLabel`), so each reason is written once.
- **Online scores is the one box that is a call to action** (`CheckRow.ShowCallToAction`, [Online rows](online-and-nickname.md#what-is-left-to-do-is-red)): off, it is empty and **red**, never dimmed as a held row is, because ticking it is what the page asks for; on with nowhere to send, it is ticked with "(no server)" beside it in the same red. Turning it on the first time asks for the nickname first, and the box stays empty until a name is kept; a press on the box keeps it, as Enter does.
- **A cycling row has a − and a + at its value's ends where a touch screen answers** (#1151, `BS3DGame.TouchScreenPresent`): a press on the row walks its ladder one way and wraps (Volume 100, 75, 50, 25, Off, 100), which on a touch screen is the only way to move it, so back to 100 % was four taps. The ends step one rung less or more of what the row sets and **hold at the ladder's ends** (`RowStep`, `QualityLevels.StepOnLadder` for the tiers in the order of what they draw, Potato under Low; the Resolution row's sizes, never Auto), their slab kept and their sign dimmed there. Their buttons have nothing on their `Tag`, so the pad's walk keeps to the row itself and the pad and the keys step it as before; each end keeps or drops a name being typed as any row's press does. Brightness, Sky, Resolution, Quality, the four volumes, Rumble and the three sensitivities have them; a row with two states (the FPS limit, Precise aim) or an action (Reset progress) does not. The wheel over a row still presses it.
- **The rows that keep their words:** Resolution, Quality, Brightness, Sky, the FPS limit (two states, but Monitor and Unlimited; on a phone the display's rate in place of Monitor, "60 Hz (phone setting)" while the phone holds the app under a display that offers more, #1070 - and **60 Hz in place of Unlimited** (#1074, the owner's choice): Android presents with vsync, so no limit would change nothing there, and the second state instead asks the display for its 60 Hz mode through the window's preferred display mode (`DisplayRefresh.AskRefresh`, `GameSettings.PhoneSixtyHz`), a cooler phone and a clean frame where a tier cannot hold 120; the display follows within a second and the refresh watch re-derives the floors and the limiter as it does when the phone switches the rate itself), the volumes, the sensitivities and Rumble. Online scores kept its words for its third state, "No server", until #1034 made that the reason beside the box.

## Sensitivity and aim sensitivity (#384, #497)

- `SensitivityLadder.Rungs` (a file of its own, so `RowStepTests` walks the game's own rungs) is 50/75/100/125/150/200/300 % of the shipped feel, 100 % the default; **ratios, not an even step** (about √2 apart). `MouseAim.SENSITIVITY` is an angle per *pixel*, so a 4K panel and a 1600×900 one differ 2.4× in sweep.
- **Read per frame** off `BS3DGame.MouseSensitivity`, not pushed into the session: the page opens from the pause, so the row can move under a running level. From disk a value is snapped **by ratio** (`NearestSensitivityRung`, boundary the geometric mean); anything unusable (zero, negative, NaN) becomes 100 %, since a mouse that cannot turn the gun looks like a broken game.
- **Aim sensitivity** is a second rung off the same ladder (`aimSensitivity`), multiplied in **as the lean blends in** (`Lerp(1, AimSensitivity, Blend)`) on top of the general rung and the FOV ratio.
- ⚠ **1.25 is the rung that was missing.** `PreciseAim.CursorRateScale` slows the leaned cursor by 0.828 (the ratio of the half-angle tangents, 42.86° overview against 36° leaned), compensated by 1.208. With rungs 1 → 1.5 a player answering "too slow" could only leave 17 % of the slowdown in or overshoot to 24 % *faster* than the overview; 0.828 × 1.25 = 1.035 is parity. `GameSettings.AimSensitivity` defaults to 1.5 since #644, a little faster than parity. **A new default does not touch a save that already has the key**, on purpose: a settings file is the player's.

## Tutorial and Intro logo rows

- **Tutorial (#189)** is the opt-out for the tutorial's cards (the first chapter's, and the score's two since #666), on by default. `GameSettings.Tutorial` is **read per frame** (`BS3DGame.IsTutorialEnabled`), so off takes a card down at once and back on resumes the lesson that was up. What was *taught* is `PlayerProgress.Lessons`, cleared by **Reset progress** ("The tutorial" in `docs/game-feedback.md`).
- **Intro logo (#621)** is whether the game opens on the splash: `GameSettings.IntroLogo` (`"introLogo"`), absent reads as on. ⚠ **Read once**, when `BuildMenu` builds the stack (`BS3DGame.ShowsSplash`), so a click takes effect at the next launch. `nosplash` skips the splash for one run.

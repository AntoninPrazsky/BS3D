# The libraries, one by one

Part of the BS3D documentation. `CLAUDE.md` names the three layered libraries and the executables in a few lines each; this document holds the full inventory of what lives where and why, **word for word as it stood in `CLAUDE.md` when it was moved (2026-10-03)**. Read the entry of the library you are about to touch: the "one copy" notes say which code an executable must not keep a second copy of.

## Prazsky.Core

BS3D's shared 3D, rendering and scene library (it was described as game-agnostic infrastructure, and most of its lines are this game's twenty-one backdrops, #594 — since #580 one `Backdrop` class per scene in `Render/Scenes`, with `SceneRenderer` the host that holds them, the services they share and the sun's shadow map): `BasicCamera3D`/`ICamera`, `CameraShake` (a camera's recoil-and-rattle response to a violent event, and since #389 the heavier rumble of a blast out in the scene, offsets only — see "Recoil and camera shake" in `docs/game-session.md`), `RecoilCamera` (the `ICamera` that composes a per-frame base pose with `CameraShake`'s offsets and can roll — `BasicCamera3D` structurally cannot), model/bitmap/info renderers, `InstancedModelRenderer` (GPU instancing), `PostProcessPipeline` (the shared HDR target + bloom pyramid + tonemap resolve all three executables run, and the defocus only the Game asks for — the end-of-level wash and precise aim's centre-sharp lens alike, see docs/rendering.md — plus, since #402, the Game's `MotionBlur`: a velocity target the session draws the gun and the moving balls into, reconstructed over the HDR scene at the head of the resolve, its shutter a fixed length of time read out of a `ShutterHistory` of poses rather than one frame's motion — "Motion blur" in docs/rendering.md), `ScreenshotWriter` (a program saving its own frame — the back buffer to a PNG on a key, on `shot=`'s seconds or on `shotframe=`'s frame indices, in one copy for the Game and the Testbed since #371, because every capture that goes through the *screen* can hand back a sharp picture of the wrong thing), `SkyDome` (the sky: one procedurally built dome mesh and, per sky, a stored palette plus — since #220 — the direction of its own sun, so a dusk dome lights the world from the horizon and a noon one from overhead; it was eighteen `.dae` models built by three content pipelines until #113), the shared setting no executable keeps a copy of any more (#75) — `ArenaIsland` (the drawn stone island, the glass drain, its gold beads, the dark pit shaft and every figure of them), `CeilingPlate` (the glass plate a cluster hangs from — cut as crystal since #541, and in the Game bending a copy of the frame behind it, `PostProcessPipeline.GrabScene`, which is why the scene target alone is `PreserveContents`), `SkyLightRig` (the dome-derived light rig), `SceneLights` (a scene's own point lights, and one frame's blast flash over them, #389; since #795 also with no renderer, for the Raspberry Pi's Potato path, from the neon ring's config and a `VolcanoLava` - the volcano's flows as figures, moved out of its backdrop), `ForestScatterRenderer` (the forest's trees, boulders and stumps — every mesh, material and encoded tint of them) and `SavannaScatter` (everything standing on the savanna since #451 — the acacias in four kinds, baobabs, doum palms, scrub, grass tufts, termite mounds, kopjes, fallen trees and the treeline, planted once into static instance buckets), all under "The setting, in one copy" in `docs/scenes.md`; `SunShadowMap` (#469 — the sun's orthographic shadow map fitted round the camera, its window snapped to its own texels; **twelve of the twenty-one backdrops cast into it and read it** (eleven since #471, the Moon since #680), eleven of them through `ShadowConfig` on the base `SceneConfig` and the twelfth — the city — through `SceneRenderer.SetHostShadowScene`, because its towers, its config and its street shader are the host's and not this renderer's; `ShadowReceiver` is the one copy of the five uniforms every receiver takes, and the towers read the map through the shared `InstancedModel.fx` tap with no registration of their own needed (#470). The street shader carried a receiver of its own for one session and lost it again: paired `shadow=0`/`shadow=1` captures showed the towers' facades changing dramatically and the street pavement barely at all, because the existing occupancy-driven ambient term it shades a canyon street by already reads it as shadowed at every sun height. And **every executable calls `SceneRenderer.DrawShadowMaps` before binding its scene target**, because a mid-frame switch away from that target cleared the sky while it was DiscardContents — it is preserved since #541, whose ceiling glass has to leave it once and come back, and the order stands anyway, since the switch would cost a resolve of a multisampled target for nothing — "Sun shadows" in `docs/rendering.md`) — what a shot puts on the screen, one copy each since #76: `LaunchSmears` (the muzzle streak), `AimBeam` (the dashed line of light out of the muzzle — the overview's only word on where the gun points, and the one that carries a refusal there; it borrows `ShotTrail.fx` from `LaunchSmears`, which is why both push the trail widths per draw) and `Crosshair` (four procedural bars, no bitmap) — the procedural meshes (`SphereMesh`, `BoxMesh`, `FunnelMesh`, `LatheMesh` and the `IslandMesh` built on it, plus `CannonMesh`, the `CannonGlassMesh` that glazes its loading window with the firing round notched out of the pane, `GunCarriageMesh` and the omnidirectional wheel's `OmniWheelMesh`/`OmniRollerMesh` on the small `MeshBuilder` that carries the winding correction as a service, and `LetterMesh` with the `LetterShapes` alphabet beside it — the game's name swept as round tubes for the 3D wordmark, #248), `SurfaceTexture` (procedural, exactly tiling stone and concrete), the screen stack (`Screens/`), `Tools/Constants.cs` (named float constants like `HALF`, `SQRT_TWO` used throughout), and `Tools/AtomicFile.cs` (writing a small file so a machine lost mid-write cannot destroy what was there — temp beside the target, `File.Replace`, one generation kept as `.bak`; every player file goes through it, and since #571 it keeps a copy of one that would not read (`KeepUnreadable`) before anything writes over it, see #353), and `Tools/BuildStamp.cs` (the three `[build]` lines all three executables open with — the managed assembly's write time and hash, then the count, set hash and newest of **this repository's own libraries** beside it, then the same for the compiled shaders, so a capture or a measurement filed with the log carries its own proof of which build produced it; the libraries line exists because the exe's own hash cannot see a change in `Prazsky.*`, where most of this code lives, see #372).

## Prazsky.BS3D

game logic without physics: the ball grid (`GameStructure/BallsMap.cs`), `StaticBall`, `BallType`, `CutReach` (where a Cut may strike and what it would take, asked of the map alone for the gun's preview, #692), JSON map (de)serialization (System.Text.Json), the `Levels/Level` file format with the `LevelSet` order over it and the persisted `PlayerProgress` beside it (`Levels/PlayerProgress.cs` — best score and stars per level, the star total that unlocks entries), the scoring rules (`Scoring/ScoreKeeper.cs`) and the star rating over them (`Scoring/StarRating.cs`), input helpers, and the gameplay hardware no executable keeps a copy of any more (#76) — `Cannon` (the gun's pose, the barrel's and the carriage's draw matrices, its muzzle, the W/S advance walk with its rubber-ended range, and since #115 the recoil stroke — the tube's slide and the undercarriage's smaller, later shove, off one clock the executables only tick), `CannonRig` (the procedural barrel with its domed breech, its **glazed** loading window — a pane of dark blue glass with the round that fires next notched out of it — and carriage: every figure the tube is cut to, plus the cheeks, their trunnion pins, the axle, the split trail and the omnidirectional wheels the walk rolls), `Magazine` (the loaded queue, its post-shot glide and its place in the bore) and `WildcardCycle` beside it (what colour a wildcard is showing this instant — one clock per session and not one per ball, so the queue, the muzzle, the halo, the aim ghost and the ball in flight cannot disagree, #330), `GameCameraFit` (the camera's stand-off, its aim height, the gun's orbit radius and the walk's range, solved together), `DropFocus` (where the drop cinematic looks — the drain first, strays past the rim only when nothing is in it, #616), `PreciseAim` (the lens leaning in over the barrel) and `AimReachability` (whether every cell of a field can be shot at), all under "The gun's hardware, in one copy" in `docs/testbed.md` — plus `BallRenderSet` (the sphere LODs, every figure of the balls' look and the instance buckets, with the once-per-frame ball visit made structural, and since #258 the `BallStyle` a map names — the moulded vinyl beach ball or the transparent glass bubble, a shading choice the level file carries and nothing in the simulation reads) and `MouseAim` (the aim off a captured cursor), under "The rest of the hardware, in one copy" there. (See `docs/formats-and-tools.md`.)

## Prazsky.BS3D.Physics

BepuPhysics representation: `PhysicsBall` (body reference + constraint handle slots + array position), `BallsConstraintsBuilder` (builds the constrained ball structure, and implements the match rule that releases a group), `FunnelPhysics` (the drain's double-sided triangle mesh, which is the island's whole collision floor — one copy since #75), `PhysicsWorld` (the dispatcher, the buffer pool, the simulation, the contact stream and the mandatory order inside one step — the stepping *policy* deliberately stays with each executable, #76), `ClusterCollector` (the one walk that turns a simulated cluster into ball instances, #76), `ShotPlacement` (**where a shot lands** — the one answer, asked by the contact handler when a ball touches and by the aim preview every frame from the barrel's line, so the ghost and the attach cannot disagree; see "The landing preview" in `docs/game-session.md`), `BallContactEventHandler` with the `BallLanding` it reports through (**what happens when a shot touches something** — the snap into the lattice, the constraints and the match rule, in one copy since #68; it stood in the Game and again inside `Testbed.cs`, and merging them changed the Testbed's contact path rather than moving it, so read the handler's own class doc before touching it), and the Bepu callback/contact infrastructure.

## Testbed

the original playable game loop: simulation setup, shooting, contact handling, backdrops, HUD. Test maps live in `Testbed\Maps\*.json`. It is also the project's instrument, and the arguments that make it one are a **contract** the skills in `.claude/skills` drive it by: `campos`/`camtarget`/`fov` and `C`, `arena=`/`capprobe=`, `alt=`, `at=`/`hold=`/`aim=`/`rmb=`, `shot=`/`shotframe=`, `logfps`/`fpscap=`, `mirrorcheck` (see `docs/testbed.md`).

## Game

the shipping product (`Game/`, assembly `BS3D.exe`). Founded small and deliberately **not** a copy of `Testbed.cs`: a hanging cluster over the island in any of the twenty-one shared scenes, the gun with its visible magazine, the fast shot with its launch smear, a real Bepu simulation, and a camera that is hit every time the gun fires — behind a Myra front end (main menu, pause, settings, scene picker) over a slowly orbiting camera, with the game's own name standing in that scene as 3D lettering rather than sitting over it as a widget (#248). It opens on the 2D logo bitmap over black, pixel for pixel at the owner's resolutions; the black then fades away to the menu scene, and the logo fades out as that 3D lettering grows in in its corner — no cross-fade into the letters and no fly since #601, and a Settings row and `nosplash` skip the logo altogether since #621 (#454 — "The splash" in `docs/game-shell.md`). It plays levels off disk, ordered by a level set. Opted in — by a nickname typed into the question the first launch asks for the leaderboards (#763), or in Settings — it submits every cleared level to the online score service (#542) off the frame, through an outbox that survives the service being away (#546); `userdata=<dir>` keeps a scripted run's save, settings and identity out of the player's profile. See `docs/game-session.md` and `docs/game-shell.md`.

## MapEditor

visual editor for those map JSON files (supports drag-and-drop of a map file onto the window). It draws the balls and the sky the same way the game does, so a map looks in the editor the way it will play: the same instanced procedural spheres, the same shader, the same sky-derived lighting and the same full render pipeline — linear radiance into an HDR target, glare, then the ACES tonemap. It loads the same compiled shaders as the other two, out of `Prazsky.Shaders`, so there is one copy of each (the sky domes stopped being content in #113 — `SkyDome` builds the one dome and its twenty palettes procedurally); B cycles the domes and **V cycles all twenty-one scenes** (#380 — it walked a seven-long prefix until then, which meant fifty of the campaign's levels stood in a backdrop the editor could not cycle to, and a level in one of them could not be returned to its own scene without reloading the file), so a map really can be previewed against every backdrop it might play in. It loads and saves whole **levels** (map + scene name + sky; the scenes' parameters are fixed in code since the format-2 refactor). It has no GUI of its own since #522: the live scene-config panel that once tuned those fixed looks is gone, because a scene's look is programmed, not configured. See `docs/formats-and-tools.md`.

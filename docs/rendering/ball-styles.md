# Ball shading styles

Part of [Rendering](../rendering.md).

A map chooses what its balls are made of (#258). A style decides the shading and nothing else: lattice, physics, match rule and score are about `BallType`.

## The style system

- **`BallStyle`** is what a level file names (`"balls": "bubble"`, absent = vinyl; "Levels and serialization" in `docs/formats-and-tools.md`); **`Prazsky.Core.Render.BallShading`** is which compiled program draws. `BallRenderSet.ShadingOf` maps between them; `InstancedModelRenderer` indexes a technique table (`BallTechniqueNames`, checked against the enum at load, so a shading without a technique throws).
- ⚠ **`ShadingOf` is exhaustive and throws** (#307): it once ended in `_ => Vinyl`, a new style never added to it **drew as a beach ball**, nothing failed, and two rounds of tuning went into a shader that was never selected. (Symptom: changing a style's figures changes nothing on screen.) `BallStyles.IsTransparent(_style)`, not `== Bubble`, decides the two-wall draw; `ApplyStyle` has one case per style.
- `BallRenderSet.Style` is the one switch and **every caller states it**. Game `balls=<style>` overrides every level; the editor cycles on **L** and `F4` writes it into the level; the Testbed cycles on L and takes `balls=<name>` (#318), opening a level in its own material.
- **The campaign hangs a material per chapter** (`LevelGen`'s `BALLS_*` carry the reasoning): Meadow bubble, Gallery wool, Coil marble, Tower ice, Reveal vinyl, Quarry metal, Nebula plasma, Eruption lava, Spectrum porcelain, Arcade gem, Mirage porcelain again (chosen against the two ball *kinds* there). Metal needs something to mirror; plasma a dark dome. Vinyl is what everything unauthored draws.

## What every ball technique owes (header in `InstancedModel.fx`)

A technique that drops a line of this looks right in a still and is wrong in play: **(1)** the dissolve clip on **both** signs of `Dissolve`, over `DissolvePixelSize` cells; **(2)** the heartbeat with its `dot(worldPosition, PulseDirection) / PulseWavelength` phase (a wave, not a strobe); **(3)** the ripple in **both** meanings (the sign picks landing wave or ceiling alarm: implement only the positive and that style silently loses the alarm); **(4)** `SurfaceOcclusion`; **(5)** `ApplySeaSubmerge` then `ApplyKillPlaneFade`; **(6)** a cue **in object space** that the ball is rolling (a world- or view-space figure, e.g. a mirror, draws a spinning ball as a still one).

## Lessons for any style

- **A figure built from COLOUR competes with the shading and can lose; one built from the NORMAL is shading and cannot** (marble's veins vanished on six of thirteen tints, wool's strands read on all). Photograph a colour-space figure against the full palette.
- **Cost is dominated by what a style does not do** (plasma never calls `ShadePixel`): estimate against the vinyl's whole lighting model. Against the same vinyl control (cavern, 959 balls, ssaa 2): bubble **+8-10 %** (a doubled pass; the only dearer one), lava -0.5 %, wool -0.9 %, porcelain -1.2 %, gem -2.0 %, marble -2.5 %, metal -4.9 %, plasma -7.4 %; **ice about +19 %** since the #337 fracture (2.04 to 2.44 ms at ssaa 2, `Full.json` 1000 balls, 6900 XT). Run drift is 0.3-2.6 %: trust ratios.
- **Regular line families read as the wrong material** (three `SeamLine` families: quilted leather; three great circles: wire cage): warp the coordinate or use Voronoi. A band limit on a thin line must start late (against its width), on a wave it can start at once; a strand or brush finer than a few pixels at play distance is present and invisible.

## The styles

- **Vinyl (`Beach`)**: [Ball rendering](ball-rendering.md); the baseline. Under the colour cue, the default, the figure replaces its gores and polar discs (below).
- **Bubble** (`InstancedModelBubble`, its own technique: a runtime branch costs the union of both register allocations): a dyed film, `DielectricF0` rising to a rim mirror, **thin-film interference computed** (phase `thickness / cos(theta)`), draining down under the *world* normal, marbled in object space (rolling cue). A **tinted edge glow** beside the Fresnel mirror (under space there is no sky to mirror: flat circles). **A dye must not depend on the backdrop**: transmitted light takes the backdrop as brightness (a red film over a blue sky passed nothing), and `BUBBLE_BODY_OPACITY` is 0.90 (0.26 left 64 % of a bright sky behind each ball) at no cost: re-check before lowering. **A shell fades by how much of the pile is in front** (`BubbleScreenFade`, per ball, short of 1 so holes still read); emission is occluded, squared (films sum into a pastel wash). `BallRenderSet.Draw` owns the states: far wall first (opposite cull, `DepthRead`), near wall **writing depth** (the aim beam, muzzle halo, ceiling glass and laser net composite against it); balls cannot be sorted.
- **Marble** (`InstancedModelMarble`): cut stone with **no relief or seams** (hence cheaper than vinyl), a polish (`MarbleBroadHighlight` 0.3, `MarbleEnvironment` 1.6, one tight lobe `MarbleGloss` 190 off the key only: three pinpoints read as three suns), warped object-space veins. **A figure competes with the shading, not the body colour**: a fixed white vein makes a white ball, and a tint carried towards white vanished on cyan/red/magenta/green/yellow/orange. What works is a second mineral desaturating along the one axis shading does not use (`MarbleVeinPale`, `MarbleVeinFloor`, `MARBLE_VEIN_CONTRAST` 0.6; above it pale types run together) plus more veins and **a dark margin beside each pale core** that takes back the value (#335).
- **Wool** (`InstancedModelWool`, the only soft one): three winding axes blended by a soft maximum (`WoolWindSelect`: too low is a crosshatched net, one axis a spool), a wrapped diffuse added only as the difference from Lambert, a fuzz halo in the **ball's own colour** (a wool ball that mirrors the dome is plastic), almost no specular.
- **Metal** (`InstancedModelMetal`): the tint is **F0** with **no diffuse term**; environment as brightness, not colour (red metal under blue sky is black); Fresnel brings the real sky back at the rim; `MetalF0Floor` 0.16 keeps Type8 a gunmetal. `MetalSky` is `SkyRadiance` with **the horizon edge and a sun disc put back** (a polished surface shows its environment; local to this style, since sharpening `SkyRadiance` would draw a horizon across everything else). **The brush is the rolling cue**: four collinear octaves along `MetalBrushA` (near-parallel waves of two frequencies beat into bumps), `MetalBrushB` perpendicular only for depth variation. Loosest palette of any style.
- **Ice** (`InstancedModelIce`): **opaque** (frosted is scattering, not clear; a transparent one costs the two-pass machinery). `ICE_TRANSLUCENCY` 1.1 is the read: backlit it glows in its own colour (invisible from the sun's side). A **Voronoi crack net** (`VoronoiEdgeCell3`; width wandering per stretch around `ICE_CRACK_WIDTH`; each plate's own value from the cell id, darkening *and* lightening, `ICE_PLATE_CONTRAST`), four-wave frost grain. ⚠ The crack test uses a raw sine, not `ReliefOctave` (which fades amplitude: the whole ball reads as one crack), with a late-starting limit.
- **Gem** (`InstancedModelGem`): facets **shaded, never built** (#271: a polygonal outline is a defect in a cut). ⚠ **A pixel shader here cannot rotate a vector from object to world space** (no tangents, no rotation in that stage), so "snap the normal to the nearest facet" cannot be written: the facet must be a **height field** (distance to its plane) whose screen-space gradient `PerturbNormalFromHeight` takes. This rules out a family of obvious constructions. `GemBodyFloor` keeps Types 8/10/12/13 off one near-black.
- **Plasma** (`InstancedModelPlasma`): dark globe, filaments crawling from a bright core; its read is motion (judge by two frames five seconds apart). A **domain warp** (`PlasmaWave`), heartbeat riding on the filaments (`PLASMA_EMISSION` 0), colour the tint normalised to its peak (Type8 draws **white** arcs). Never calls `ShadePixel`. Hue-strong, value-weak; needs a dark dome.
- **Lava** (`InstancedModelLava`): black basalt plates, molten seams breathing on the beat routed *into* the seams (`LAVA_EMISSION` 0); keeps `SurfaceRelief`. ⚠ **Seams must wander** (the plasma's domain warp, weaker, unanimated): three sine fields cut great circles, a wire cage. **Heat halo (#338):** colour comes from crust within a plate-width of a seam, not from lightening the crust (throws the style away) or widening the seam (a coloured ball with black spots); the halo is the **max** of the three seam fields (a sum saturates), shaped by a power (a plateau is a neon cage), and its width is a *multiple* of the seam's (`LavaHeatWidth` 1.55 with seam 0.46: widening the seam alone floods the plates). **#395: judge the seam cores, not the disc** (`palette.ps1 -Cores`): the carry to near-white was uncapped, so eight of thirteen cores were neutral; `LavaCoreCarry` 0.30, `LavaCoreLift` 1.4, `LavaHuePower` 1.7 (⚠ not `SaturateTint`, whose first step `primary/peak` this style already had), `LavaValuePower` 1.0 raised core saturation 0.283 to 0.510. **A palette here has as many colours as hue families** (brown arrives as dark orange; black, silver, white as a pale net; brightness is what a hanging cluster loses): the Eruption block is inked red and yellow hot, black, cyan and navy cold, a sixth only where needed, never more than six a level (`Tools/LevelGen/Designs/Block08_Eruption.cs`, `ERUPTION_*`): tightest pair 25.2 dE against 6.1.
- **Porcelain** (`InstancedModelPorcelain`): glaze over a **white clay body** (it had been tint-straight: "painted plastic", #339); thin coat shows clay, a craze is glaze parted to stained clay, the clay transmits (`PORCELAIN_TRANSLUCENCY`, a third of the ice's). The highlight uses the **smooth** normal (a broken one says chipped). **The ornament names the style**, where the colour cue is off (`colourcue=none`; by default the colour's figure takes its place, as on the vinyl, below): a **Hilbert meander in white enamel** round the equator, evaluated per pixel from its own cell (no texture, no UVs; it tiles and closes), a colour step plus a raised bead, antialiased off the footprint (not `fwidth`: the distance jumps and `atan2` would leave a bad column). Traps: a craze band-limited on its wavelength was white sparkle (it is a close-range figure); a pure white band lifts every ball to white (black/brown 9.3 to 6.0), so a fifth of the glaze shows through (7.1); crack tone follows the glaze's luminance and **crosses over**.

## The colour cue (#737)

A second channel beside the hue: every colour carries a painted **figure** of its own, so two colours that are close under one material (the wool's black, brown and silver, its beige white and its yellow) also differ in shape. The figure is painted, not carved (a carve needs the finest sphere mesh to hold its outline). There are four **figure sets**, and **each ball style draws one of them** (the owner's ruling: all four, one per style, chosen to fit the material). The cue is **on by default** everywhere: the Game, the Testbed, the map editor.

- **The switch.** `BallRenderSet.ColourCue` is `ColourCues.BY_STYLE` by default (each style its set, `ColourCues.SetOf`), `ColourCues.NONE` for no figure, or a set's number to put that set on every style. `colourcue=<none|motif|motif:N>` sets it in the Testbed (also an `alt=` dial) and the Game (`motif`, `a`, `on` and `style` are `BY_STYLE`; the sets' own names work too). The set's paints (`ColourCues.Paint`: the figure, the frame it is read in, its two inks and the same as scales) are worked out once whenever the switch or the style changes; `DrawPlane` states a colour's paint per bucket like its tint and puts the empty paint back afterwards, and the renderer then picks the style's `InstancedModel<Style>Motif` and sets its seven uniforms. The special kinds draw as shipped. The loaded rounds and the ball in flight carry the figure (they are `DrawPlane` too), and so do the HUD queue's discs ([the magazine strip](../game-feedback/hud-magazine-and-muzzle.md)); the sun shadow's caster draws spheres.
- **Which style draws which set** (`ColourCues.SetOf`, exhaustive and throwing like `ShadingOf`; its doc gives each reason in full). A set goes where its kind of figure reads as part of the material, and away from the special kinds that style's levels hang:

  | Style | Set | Why |
  |---|---|---|
  | Wool | 1 figures | the reported style: its tight pairs (black/brown/silver, white/yellow, red/orange) differ in the KIND of figure only in this set, which is what survives at 1920x1080; knitted colourwork |
  | Porcelain | 1 figures | painted china carries every kind of motif; the Spectrum's hue families get different kinds; not the lines, whose navy seam is the clear glass's (the Mirage hangs it) |
  | Plasma | 1 figures | the Nebula hangs all thirteen colours and the plasma is value-weak (black, white and silver all burn pale arcs) |
  | Vinyl | 2 emblems | the Big Top's ball printed like a deck of cards; what unauthored maps draw, and the set that moved the colours least |
  | Metal | 2 emblems | die-struck insignia; a band would run along the brush, the metal's rolling cue |
  | Marble | 3 lines | banded stone, the calmest set for the Reveal's quiet material |
  | Ice | 3 lines | strata in glacier ice; an emblem on six cube faces is the frozen ball |
  | Gem | 3 lines | banded agate across the facets; the emblems' hexagons, diamonds and triangles read as more facets |
  | Bubble | 4 spots | polka-dot balloons, for the first chapter |
  | Lava | none | the one style without a figure: neither carrier tried (the seams' heat scale, then an ash crust inside the spots) read at play distance; the glowing seams own the ball, and the HUD's discs draw none for it either |
- **How each style carries it.** A **dye** where the colour is a body: the vinyl's paint job (the figure **replaces** the gores and the polar discs, so a vinyl ball with a figure is no longer a beach ball; its welds and gloss stay), the wool's yarn, the marble's inlay (its veined stone scaled by the ground's share), the bubble's film (printed in the dye, both walls; the rim glow keeps the plain hue), the ice (frozen in, under the cracks and the frost), the gem's stone (under the facets; `SaturateTint` is linear in a scale) and the porcelain's glaze (painted under it; the **meander gives way**, bead and all, as the gores do on the vinyl, because it ran straight through the emblems and over the bands). A **scale** where the colour is a factor in something else: the metal's reflectance (enamel: the floored F0 scaled, a darker or a brighter alloy), the plasma's discharge (the arcs dimmer or brighter inside the figure) and the lava's seams and heat halo (a cooler or hotter patch of the flow); their shell, crust and glow take the same scale. ⚠ **The lava draws no figure** (`ColourCues.SetOf` is `NONE` for it): its seams are thin and its crust near black, so the scale left the spots invisible, and lifting the crust inside them to an ash of the ball's tint (`LavaAshDark` against `LavaCrustDark`) still did not read at play distance (laptop captures, 2026-10-13: Meander identical with the cue on and off). Its program keeps both carriers for `colourcue=motif:N`.
- ⚠ **The scales are their own uniform** (`ColourCueInkScales`, `ColourCues.InkScales`): every ink but black's is the plain colour scaled, so its scale is its factor, but black's figure ink is a grey ADDED to a near-black, about 24 times it: taken as a luminance ratio it made black's arcs and seams 24 times as bright inside the figure and clipped its reflectance to a full mirror. On the three scaled styles black has something to scale (the gunmetal floor, the white arcs, the seams), so it balances there like any darker ink and its scales average to 1.
- **Off is the shipped code, by construction.** Every style's body (`PatternShade`, `BubbleShade`, `MarbleShade`, `WoolShade`, `MetalShade`, `IceShade`, `GemShade`, `PlasmaShade`, `LavaShade`, `PorcelainShade`) takes its switch as a literal, the figures' file (`BallColourCue.fxh`) declares no uniform (`ColourCueTests` parses its top-level statements, and its own cases show the check firing on a non-static `const`, an `extern`, a `matrix`, an `int2` and an indented declaration), and the uniforms sit in `BallColourCueTechniques.fxh`, last. Compiled offline with `d3dcompiler_47`, all 45 shipped entry points come out instruction for instruction as before the cue, so `colourcue=none` draws exactly the old look. `InstancedModelRenderer` throws at load if the effect lacks a style's program (`ColourCueTechniqueNames`): with the cue on by default, a missing one would draw that style in colour alone and nothing would fail.
- **The Potato path paints the figure through one program for every style** (#828, `PotatoBallCueBands`/`Spots`/`Checks`/`Emblems` in `PotatoModel.fx`, one program per kind of figure with the kind a compile-time literal, since one program carrying every kind's branch was sized by V3D for the dearest): `BallColourCue.fxh`'s field functions verbatim, read from the same four figure uniforms, the inks folded on the CPU into the crust and the glow in each ink and lerped per pixel by the coverage (the styles' bodies are one there, so a dye and a scale are the same thing); the HUD's strip paints the discs' figures on the Pi too. ⚠ A figure changed in `BallColourCue.fxh` is changed in `PotatoModel.fx`'s copy in the same commit, as in `ColourCueField.cs`.
- **A figure is one of four kinds**, each a signed field on the unit sphere in **object space** (so it turns with its ball and every ball of a colour carries the same one), read in one of seven frames: **bands** round the frame's Y (how many, how wide, wavy, hollow or dashed), **spots** at the vertices of a regular solid (4, 6, 8, 12 or 20; solid, rings, or two sizes), **checks**, and **emblems**, one printed symbol on each face of the frame's cube like a card's pips (star, heart, spade, club, diamond, square, ring, triangle, moon, drop, flower, chevron, saltire, hexagon). The packing is tabulated beside `ColourCuePaint` in `BallColourCue.fxh`. `ColourCueField` is the same field ported line for line to C#, for the HUD's discs and the tests: **a figure changed in the shader is changed there in the same commit**.
- **The four sets** (`ColourCues.Sets`, in the palette's order red, green, blue, white, cyan, magenta, yellow, black, orange, brown, silver, navy, olive):
  1. *figures*, mixed kinds so that the tight pairs differ in the kind of figure: one wavy band, chevrons, diamonds, checks, crosses, hexagons, stars, small squares, six rings, twelve dots, three stripes, moons, triangles;
  2. *emblems*: hearts, clubs, hexagons, flowers, crosses, spades, stars, rings, chevrons, diamonds, moons, drops, triangles;
  3. *lines*: one broad band, a dashed band, two wavy bands, one wavy band, a double line, a dotted line, four broad bands, one thin line, a wavy line, two bands, three bands, a seam, seven pinstripes;
  4. *spots*: eight dots, thirty-two tiny dots, six rings and eight dots, twenty small dots, four big and four small, eight rings, six big and eight small, six small rings, four big spots, twelve dots, four big rings, six dots, twelve rings.

  The sets are not disjoint: eight of the first set's figures are emblems and seven of them are kinds the second set also draws (its stars, crosses and triangles on the same colours, within a size), the first and the fourth give brown the same twelve dots, and the first set's silver stripes are the third set's silver bands in another frame.
- **The inks keep the ball's average colour** (`ColourCues.Inks`): the figure is a darker or a paler shade of the ball's own colour and the ground the plain colour scaled the other way, by the share of the ball the figure covers, so the two average to the plain colour and a ball whose figure is too small to see shows its plain colour. A figure painted in a darker ink on the plain colour instead darkens a light ball's average towards the dark colours. The darker ink is the plain colour times `DARK_INK` (0.28) on a ground brightened to pay for it, at most `GROUND_MAX` (1.5); brown, silver and navy take a paler ink, their colour times a gain, on a ground darkened to pay for it, at least `GROUND_MIN` (0.25). ⚠ **A pale ink is the colour scaled, never a grey added**: the ground is the plain colour scaled, so nothing pays for a grey's hue, and the average keeps its luminance and loses its saturation (0.05 of grey leaves the navy 10-12 dE greyer, towards the black and the brown). `ColourCueTests` checks the balance channel by channel, that every set gives all thirteen colours a figure and no two the same one, that the figure's ink stands off its ground (luminance ratio at least 2.5), that no family of bands covers its frame's poles and that every frame is a rotation; `ColourCueStyleTests` that every style has a set and every set a style, that each style's inks and scales keep the average, that no style draws the figure kind its levels' special kinds would be taken for, and that every style's shading has a program the effect declares.
- ⚠ **The share is what the play camera sees, not the sphere's** (`Figure.C.W`). A hanging ball stays upright and is seen from the side and from below, so a band round the upright axis faces the camera across its middle and shows 15-18 % more than its share of the sphere (the broad band 0.374 against 0.320), while spots and emblems show their share within 0.01. Balanced on the sphere's share, a band darkens its ball (the lines set's red by 4.7 dE). The share is projected area seen from every side at 0-30 degrees below the ball; `ColourCueStyleTests` measures every figure's through `ColourCueField` on 60,000 even points and holds the table to within 0.006 (it agrees to 0.0023), so a figure changed without its share fails there.
- ⚠ **Black alone cannot be balanced** by a dye: nothing is darker than its ground, so its average can only rise, and its figure is a dim grey held to an average under L* 15 on a ground at `GROUND_MIN`. It reads up close; at play distance black is the colour whose figure is faint.
- ⚠ **A ball is largely its own emission**, wholly under a dark dome: a glow in the plain colour over a darker figure washed it out (the wool lost every figure under dome 13). So the figure glows in its ink on every style; the inks average to the plain colour, so the ball as a whole radiates what it did.
- **What no figure may look like** (the list heads `BallColourCue.fxh`): a special kind (the bomb's two slanted glowing seams, a "+" face on; the stone's broken outline and cracks; the frozen ball's six faces; the heavy casting's patches and mould flash; the gravity well's spiral and its bullseye; the zap's forked bolts; the clear glass's cast seam; the acid's glowing lower half; the infectious ball's one lumpy crust; the buckshot's clump of loose pellets; the wildcard's two colours swirled together; the dead weight's dither) or the vinyl's gores, which are the rotation cue. So no figure spirals, zigzags or runs along meridians, no ring sits inside another, no family of bands covers its frame's poles (a cap inside a ring is the bullseye again: an even count of bands takes phase 1), no two bands cross round the ball (there is no grid), no X is pale on a dark colour, no figure sits on one side of the ball (each covers it evenly, round an axis or at a solid's vertices), none is in a second colour or has an outline of its own, none glows brighter than its ball, and none is a thin dark crack. The nearest calls: the smallest spots (the spots set's 9-degree dots) are two or three pixels on a 23-pixel ball at 1920x1080 and still read as dots of the ball's darker shade, where the dead weight shows what is behind it; the lines set's navy seam follows the clear glass's two-wave curve on an opaque ball (no style that hangs clear glass draws the lines); its black thin line is the nearest thing to the heavy ball's mould flash and, one and dim and nearly level, to one of the mine's two glowing seams (the lava, which hangs the mine, draws spots). Frames 0-4 are turned well off object Y, where the gores converge, and off the horizontal; frame 5, which carries the lines set's bands and the figures set's red band, is tipped only 10 degrees, so those bands stand nearly level; frame 6 is object space and carries only the brown's dots.
- ⚠ **An emblem's field jumps where two faces of its cube meet** (each face has its own projection), and a soft edge whose width is taken over the pixel quad spans the jump: a faint line of ink, up to a third of the figure's, along parts of the cube's edges (the chevron, drop, triangle, star, spade, club and diamond leave one). No emblem reaches its face's edge, so a quad that straddles two faces draws the ground (`ColourCueMotif`; the HUD's bake does the same per texel).
- **Too small to draw**: below about seven target pixels of radius a figure only aliases, so it eases out on the pixel footprint (whole up to 0.2, gone by 0.4) to its coverage share of its ink, which is the plain colour on average. A ball in play is 0.07-0.15 at the desktop's and the laptop's resolutions, so this is the overview's far balls.
- **What the whole-disc colour does** on the three styles measured so far (`Thirteen_Colors`, meadow, domes 1 and 13, `nopost nooverc weather=clear ssaa=2`, two shots each, `palette.ps1 -Whole`, CIEDE2000; all 78 pairs, every set on each of the vinyl, the wool and the marble). This is the colour a ball shows once its figure is too small to resolve, and the inks hold it only in linear light: the tonemap does not average a brightened ground and a dark figure back to the plain colour, so a colour with a large dark figure darkens on screen by up to 3.6-4.5 dE (the figures' white checks, the lines' red band, the spots' orange) and some pairs come closer. On the wool and the marble no pair under 12 dE comes more than 1.3 dE closer in any set: the tightest pair anywhere is black/brown on the wool under dome 13, 4.5 with no figure and 5.5 (figures, the wool's set), 4.6 (emblems), 5.5 (lines, the marble's) and 5.5 (spots); red/orange, the next, reads 5.3-6.8 against 6.1. Pairs further apart move more: the figures and the lines pull a few 4-5 dE closer (white/silver 21.9 to 16.9 and 17.5 on the wool under dome 1, the lines' red/brown 25.0 to 19.9), and across all six conditions the figures pull 20 pair-conditions closer by more than 2 dE, the emblems 3, the lines 50 and the spots 7. The navy keeps its hue in every set, so black/navy reads 16.6, 16.0, 13.0 and 17.4 against 14.5 with no figure. On the vinyl every set separates the tight pairs (black/brown 6.4 to 11.3-12.4), because the figure replaces the white gores, which pulled every colour towards white. Two capture runs of one look give a colour within 0.1 dE of itself as a rule and up to 1.8 dE apart at worst, so a move under about 2 dE is not a finding. The other seven styles have not been measured with a figure.
- **At play distance** (Testbed game camera, `ssaa=2`, `sceneseed=7`, the gun held level with `aim=1.2:0:0,6.5:0:0`; ⚠ unpinned, the gun follows whatever the mouse did, and it stood in front of the cluster in some captures and not in others; Footbridge in the wool and the vinyl, Crane in its marble, Garland, which hangs all thirteen colours, in all three; 3840x1600 and 1920x1080): at 3840x1600 every set's figures read on every colour but black, whose figure is faint by construction. At 1920x1080 a Garland ball is about 23 pixels across, and the kind of a figure survives (a band, spots, checks, an emblem) while its detail does not: a heart, a spade and a club become blots of one size, and the lines' counts and the spots' sizes take a second look. So a set that gives a tight pair two different kinds of figure (the figures set does for six of its seven tight pairs) keeps the pair apart at the laptop's resolution, and a set of one kind leans on detail there.
- **Cost** (`d3dcompiler_47` instruction slots; no frame was timed). With every figure's branch compiled in, each style's motif program is 329-453 slots longer than its shipped one, at 9-17 temporary registers, at most one more than the shipped program's except the plasma's 9 against 6 (shipped / motif: vinyl 742 / 1104, bubble 621 / 1065, marble 672 / 1124, wool 691 / 1138, metal 612 / 1064, ice 1254 / 1698, gem 674 / 1127, plasma 140 / 591, lava 747 / 1196, porcelain 832 / 1161). The branches are on per-draw uniforms and cannot diverge, so a draw runs one figure's path; compiled with each of its own set's thirteen figures as literals, that path is +19 to +68 slots on the bubble, marble, wool, metal, ice, gem, plasma and lava (the plasma's +22 to +67 on a 140-slot program is the largest share, up to half), and below the shipped program on the vinyl (23 to 53 slots, the gores' colour work gone) and the porcelain (52 to 97, the meander gone); the emblems' seam test is 8-9 of those slots. The one timing there is is of four figures whose paths ran +24 to +61 on the wool: +0.02 to +0.03 ms on a 9.7 ms frame (RX 6900 XT, 3840x1600 `ssaa=2`, `Bell` and a copy of it with every plain ball carrying a figure, one form per process, paired repeats within 0.01 ms). For a style's measured cost run `colourcue=none` against the default in two processes, as the benchmark skill does.

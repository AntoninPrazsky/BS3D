# Ball shading styles

Part of [Rendering](../rendering.md).

A map chooses what its balls are made of (#258). A style decides the shading and nothing else: lattice, physics, match rule and score are about `BallType`.

## The style system

- **`BallStyle`** is what a level file names (`"balls": "bubble"`, absent = vinyl; "Levels and serialization" in `docs/formats-and-tools.md`); **`Prazsky.Core.Render.BallShading`** is which compiled program draws. `BallRenderSet.ShadingOf` maps between them; `InstancedModelRenderer` indexes a technique table (`BallTechniqueNames`, checked against the enum at load, so a shading without a technique throws).
- ⚠ **`ShadingOf` is exhaustive and throws** (#307): it once ended in `_ => Vinyl`, a new style never added to it **drew as a beach ball**, nothing failed, and two rounds of tuning went into a shader that was never selected. (Symptom: changing a style's figures changes nothing on screen.) `BallStyles.IsTransparent(_style)`, not `== Bubble`, decides the two-wall draw; `ApplyStyle` has one case per style.
- `BallRenderSet.Style` is the one switch and **every caller states it**. Game `balls=<style>` overrides every level; the editor cycles on **L** and `F4` writes it into the level; the Testbed cycles on L and takes `balls=<name>` (#318), opening a level in its own material.
- **The campaign hangs a material per chapter** (`LevelGen`'s `BALLS_*` carry the reasoning): Meadow bubble, Gallery wool, Coil marble, Tower ice, Reveal vinyl, Quarry metal, Nebula plasma, Eruption lava, Spectrum porcelain, Arcade gem, Mirage porcelain again (chosen against the two ball *kinds* there). Metal needs something to mirror; plasma a dark dome. Vinyl is what everything unauthored draws.

## What every ball technique owes (header in `InstancedModel.fx`)

A technique that drops a line of this looks right in a still and is wrong in play: **(1)** the dissolve clip on **both** signs of `Dissolve`, over `DissolvePixelSize` cells; **(2)** the heartbeat with its `dot(worldPosition, PulseDirection) / PulseWavelength` phase (a wave, not a strobe); **(3)** the ripple in **both** meanings (the sign picks landing wave or ceiling alarm: implement only the positive and that style silently loses the alarm); **(4)** `SurfaceOcclusion`; **(5)** `ApplySeaSubmerge` then `ApplyKillPlaneFade`; **(6)** a cue **in object space** that the ball is rolling (a world- or view-space figure, e.g. a mirror, draws a spinning ball as a still one).

## Lessons for any style

- **A figure built from COLOUR competes with the shading and can lose; one built from the NORMAL is shading and cannot** (marble's veins vanished on six of thirteen tints, wool's strands read on all). Photograph a colour-space figure against the full palette.
- **Cost is dominated by what a style does not do** (plasma never calls `ShadePixel`): estimate against the vinyl's whole lighting model. Against the same vinyl control (cavern, 959 balls, ssaa 2): bubble **+8-10 %** (a doubled pass; the only dearer one), lava -0.5 %, wool -0.9 %, porcelain -1.2 %, gem -2.0 %, marble -2.5 %, metal -4.9 %, plasma -7.4 %; **ice about +19 %** since the #337 fracture (2.04 to 2.44 ms at ssaa 2, `Full.json` 1000 balls, 6900 XT). Run drift is 0.3-2.6 %: trust ratios.
- **Regular line families read as the wrong material** (three `SeamLine` families: quilted leather; three great circles: wire cage): warp the coordinate or use Voronoi. A band limit on a thin line must start late (against its width), on a wave it can start at once; a strand or brush finer than a few pixels at play distance is present and invisible.

## The styles

- **Vinyl (`Beach`)**: [Ball rendering](ball-rendering.md); the baseline.
- **Bubble** (`InstancedModelBubble`, its own technique: a runtime branch costs the union of both register allocations): a dyed film, `DielectricF0` rising to a rim mirror, **thin-film interference computed** (phase `thickness / cos(theta)`), draining down under the *world* normal, marbled in object space (rolling cue). A **tinted edge glow** beside the Fresnel mirror (under space there is no sky to mirror: flat circles). **A dye must not depend on the backdrop**: transmitted light takes the backdrop as brightness (a red film over a blue sky passed nothing), and `BUBBLE_BODY_OPACITY` is 0.90 (0.26 left 64 % of a bright sky behind each ball) at no cost: re-check before lowering. **A shell fades by how much of the pile is in front** (`BubbleScreenFade`, per ball, short of 1 so holes still read); emission is occluded, squared (films sum into a pastel wash). `BallRenderSet.Draw` owns the states: far wall first (opposite cull, `DepthRead`), near wall **writing depth** (the aim beam, muzzle halo, ceiling glass and laser net composite against it); balls cannot be sorted.
- **Marble** (`InstancedModelMarble`): cut stone with **no relief or seams** (hence cheaper than vinyl), a polish (`MarbleBroadHighlight` 0.3, `MarbleEnvironment` 1.6, one tight lobe `MarbleGloss` 190 off the key only: three pinpoints read as three suns), warped object-space veins. **A figure competes with the shading, not the body colour**: a fixed white vein makes a white ball, and a tint carried towards white vanished on cyan/red/magenta/green/yellow/orange. What works is a second mineral desaturating along the one axis shading does not use (`MarbleVeinPale`, `MarbleVeinFloor`, `MARBLE_VEIN_CONTRAST` 0.6; above it pale types run together) plus more veins and **a dark margin beside each pale core** that takes back the value (#335).
- **Wool** (`InstancedModelWool`, the only soft one): three winding axes blended by a soft maximum (`WoolWindSelect`: too low is a crosshatched net, one axis a spool), a wrapped diffuse added only as the difference from Lambert, a fuzz halo in the **ball's own colour** (a wool ball that mirrors the dome is plastic), almost no specular.
- **Metal** (`InstancedModelMetal`): the tint is **F0** with **no diffuse term**; environment as brightness, not colour (red metal under blue sky is black); Fresnel brings the real sky back at the rim; `MetalF0Floor` 0.16 keeps Type8 a gunmetal. `MetalSky` is `SkyRadiance` with **the horizon edge and a sun disc put back** (a polished surface shows its environment; local to this style, since sharpening `SkyRadiance` would draw a horizon across everything else). **The brush is the rolling cue**: four collinear octaves along `MetalBrushA` (near-parallel waves of two frequencies beat into bumps), `MetalBrushB` perpendicular only for depth variation. Loosest palette of any style.
- **Ice** (`InstancedModelIce`): **opaque** (frosted is scattering, not clear; a transparent one costs the two-pass machinery). `ICE_TRANSLUCENCY` 1.1 is the read: backlit it glows in its own colour (invisible from the sun's side). A **Voronoi crack net** (`VoronoiEdgeCell3`; width wandering per stretch around `ICE_CRACK_WIDTH`; each plate's own value from the cell id, darkening *and* lightening, `ICE_PLATE_CONTRAST`), four-wave frost grain. ⚠ The crack test uses a raw sine, not `ReliefOctave` (which fades amplitude: the whole ball reads as one crack), with a late-starting limit.
- **Gem** (`InstancedModelGem`): facets **shaded, never built** (#271: a polygonal outline is a defect in a cut). ⚠ **A pixel shader here cannot rotate a vector from object to world space** (no tangents, no rotation in that stage), so "snap the normal to the nearest facet" cannot be written: the facet must be a **height field** (distance to its plane) whose screen-space gradient `PerturbNormalFromHeight` takes. This rules out a family of obvious constructions. `GemBodyFloor` keeps Types 8/10/12/13 off one near-black.
- **Plasma** (`InstancedModelPlasma`): dark globe, filaments crawling from a bright core; its read is motion (judge by two frames five seconds apart). A **domain warp** (`PlasmaWave`), heartbeat riding on the filaments (`PLASMA_EMISSION` 0), colour the tint normalised to its peak (Type8 draws **white** arcs). Never calls `ShadePixel`. Hue-strong, value-weak; needs a dark dome.
- **Lava** (`InstancedModelLava`): black basalt plates, molten seams breathing on the beat routed *into* the seams (`LAVA_EMISSION` 0); keeps `SurfaceRelief`. ⚠ **Seams must wander** (the plasma's domain warp, weaker, unanimated): three sine fields cut great circles, a wire cage. **Heat halo (#338):** colour comes from crust within a plate-width of a seam, not from lightening the crust (throws the style away) or widening the seam (a coloured ball with black spots); the halo is the **max** of the three seam fields (a sum saturates), shaped by a power (a plateau is a neon cage), and its width is a *multiple* of the seam's (`LavaHeatWidth` 1.55 with seam 0.46: widening the seam alone floods the plates). **#395: judge the seam cores, not the disc** (`palette.ps1 -Cores`): the carry to near-white was uncapped, so eight of thirteen cores were neutral; `LavaCoreCarry` 0.30, `LavaCoreLift` 1.4, `LavaHuePower` 1.7 (⚠ not `SaturateTint`, whose first step `primary/peak` this style already had), `LavaValuePower` 1.0 raised core saturation 0.283 to 0.510. **A palette here has as many colours as hue families** (brown arrives as dark orange; black, silver, white as a pale net; brightness is what a hanging cluster loses): the Eruption block is inked red and yellow hot, black, cyan and navy cold, a sixth only where needed, never more than six a level (`Tools/LevelGen/Designs/Block08_Eruption.cs`, `ERUPTION_*`): tightest pair 25.2 dE against 6.1.
- **Porcelain** (`InstancedModelPorcelain`): glaze over a **white clay body** (it had been tint-straight: "painted plastic", #339); thin coat shows clay, a craze is glaze parted to stained clay, the clay transmits (`PORCELAIN_TRANSLUCENCY`, a third of the ice's). The highlight uses the **smooth** normal (a broken one says chipped). **The ornament names the style**: a **Hilbert meander in white enamel** round the equator, evaluated per pixel from its own cell (no texture, no UVs; it tiles and closes), a colour step plus a raised bead, antialiased off the footprint (not `fwidth`: the distance jumps and `atan2` would leave a bad column). Traps: a craze band-limited on its wavelength was white sparkle (it is a close-range figure); a pure white band lifts every ball to white (black/brown 9.3 to 6.0), so a fifth of the glaze shows through (7.1); crack tone follows the glaze's luminance and **crosses over**.

## The colour cue (#737)

A second channel beside the hue: every colour carries a painted **figure** of its own, so two colours that are close under one material (the wool's black, brown and silver, its beige white and its yellow) also differ in shape. The owner chose a painted figure over a carved one (a carve needed the finest sphere mesh to hold its outline) and is picking one of four **figure sets**; until he has, nothing a player can reach draws any of them.

- **The switch is off everywhere a player can reach.** `colourcue=<none|motif|motif:N>` in the Testbed (also an `alt=` dial) and the Game sets `BallRenderSet.ColourCue`, the set's number (`ColourCues.NONE`, 0, by default). Setting it works out every colour's `ColourCuePaint` once (`ColourCues.Paint`: the figure, the frame it is read in and its two inks); `DrawPlane` states the colour's paint per bucket like its tint and puts the empty paint back afterwards, and the renderer then picks `InstancedModel<Pattern|Wool|Marble>Motif` and sets its six uniforms. Other styles and the special kinds draw as shipped. The loaded rounds and the ball in flight carry the figure (they are `DrawPlane` too); the HUD queue's discs do not, and the sun shadow's caster draws spheres.
- **Default off is the shipped code, by construction.** The three bodies (`PatternShade`, `WoolShade`, `MarbleShade`) take their switch as a literal, the figures' file (`BallColourCue.fxh`) declares no uniform (`ColourCueTests` parses its top-level statements, and its own cases show the check firing on a non-static `const`, an `extern`, a `matrix`, an `int2` and an indented declaration), and the six uniforms sit in `BallColourCueTechniques.fxh`, last. Compiled offline with `d3dcompiler_47`, all 45 existing entry points come out instruction for instruction as on `main`.
- **A figure is one of four kinds**, each a signed field on the unit sphere in **object space** (so it turns with its ball and every ball of a colour carries the same one), read in one of seven frames: **bands** round the frame's Y (how many, how wide, wavy, hollow or dashed), **spots** at the vertices of a regular solid (4, 6, 8, 12 or 20; solid, rings, or two sizes), **checks**, and **emblems**, one printed symbol on each face of the frame's cube like a card's pips (star, heart, spade, club, diamond, square, ring, triangle, moon, drop, flower, chevron, saltire, hexagon). The packing is tabulated beside `ColourCuePaint` in `BallColourCue.fxh`.
- **The four sets** (`ColourCues.Sets`, in the palette's order red, green, blue, white, cyan, magenta, yellow, black, orange, brown, silver, navy, olive):
  1. *figures*: one wavy band, chevrons, diamonds, checks, crosses, hexagons, stars, small squares, six rings, twelve dots, three stripes, moons, triangles (the prototype's four, the silver's stripes, the brown's dots, the white's checks and the yellow's stars, on the colours the owner saw them on);
  2. *emblems*: hearts, clubs, hexagons, flowers, crosses, spades, stars, rings, chevrons, diamonds, moons, drops, triangles;
  3. *lines*: one broad band, a dashed band, two wavy bands, one wavy band, a double line, a dotted line, four broad bands, one thin line, a wavy line, two bands, three bands, a seam, seven pinstripes;
  4. *spots*: eight dots, thirty-two tiny dots, six rings and eight dots, twenty small dots, four big and four small, eight rings, six big and eight small, six small rings, four big spots, twelve dots, four big rings, six dots, twelve rings.
- **The inks keep the ball's average colour** (`ColourCues.Inks`): the figure is a darker or a paler shade of the ball's own colour and the ground the plain colour scaled the other way, by the share of the ball the figure covers, so where the figure is too small to see the ball still shows its plain colour and no two colours move closer. A figure painted in a darker ink on the plain colour darkens a light ball's average towards the dark colours (white/silver fell from 18-25 to 8-12 dE that way). The darker ink is the plain colour times `DARK_INK` (0.28) on a ground brightened to pay for it, at most `GROUND_MAX` (1.5); black, brown, silver and navy take a paler ink (their colour times a gain plus a grey) on a ground darkened to pay for it, at least `GROUND_MIN` (0.25). The marble scales its veined stone by the ground's share. `ColourCueTests` checks the balance, that every set gives all thirteen colours a figure and no two the same one, that the figure's ink stands off its ground (luminance ratio at least 2.5) and that every frame is a rotation.
- ⚠ **The share is what the play camera sees, not the sphere's** (`Figure.C.W`). A hanging ball stays upright and is seen from the side and from below, so a band round the upright axis faces the camera across its middle and shows 15-18 % more than its share of the sphere (the broad band 0.374 against 0.320), while spots and emblems show their share within 0.01. Balanced on the sphere's share, a band darkens its ball (the lines set's red by 4.7 dE). Each share is measured with a line-for-line port of the shader's field on 400,000 even points, weighted by projected area seen from every side at 0-30 degrees below the ball: a figure changed without measuring it again leaves its ball's average off by the difference.
- ⚠ **Black alone cannot be balanced**: nothing is darker than its ground, so its average can only rise, and its figure is a dim grey held to an average under L* 15 on a ground at `GROUND_MIN`. It reads up close; at play distance black is the colour whose figure is faint.
- ⚠ **A ball is largely its own emission**, wholly under a dark dome: a glow in the plain colour over a darker figure washed it out (the wool lost every figure under dome 13). So the figure glows in its ink on all three styles; the inks average to the plain colour, so the ball as a whole radiates what it did. On the vinyl the figure **replaces** the gores and the polar discs: the paint job is the figure in its two inks, so a vinyl ball with a figure is no longer a beach ball (its welded seams and its gloss stay).
- **What no figure may look like** (the list heads `BallColourCue.fxh`): a special kind (the bomb's two slanted glowing seams, a "+" face on; the stone's broken outline and cracks; the frozen ball's six faces; the heavy casting's patches; the gravity well's spiral and its bullseye; the zap's forked bolts; the clear glass's cast seam) or the vinyl's gores, which are the rotation cue. So no figure spirals, zigzags or runs along meridians, no ring sits inside another, no two bands cross round the ball (there is no grid), no X is pale on a dark colour, none glows brighter than its ball, and none is a thin dark crack. The frames are turned off object Y, where the gores converge, and off the horizontal, the bomb's latitude.
- **Too small to draw**: below about seven target pixels of radius a figure only aliases, so it eases out on the pixel footprint (whole up to 0.2, gone by 0.4) to its coverage share of its ink, which is the plain colour on average. A ball in play is 0.07-0.15 at the desktop's and the laptop's resolutions, so this is the overview's far balls.
- **What the whole-disc colour does** (`Thirteen_Colors`, meadow, domes 1 and 13, `nopost nooverc weather=clear ssaa=2`, two shots each, `palette.ps1 -Whole`, CIEDE2000; all 78 pairs). This is the colour a ball shows once its figure is too small to resolve. On the wool and the marble no set moves a tight pair closer by more than 1 dE: the tightest pair anywhere is black/brown on the wool under dome 13, 4.5 with no figure and 5.5 (figures), 4.4 (emblems), 5.4 (lines) and 5.5 (spots); red/orange, the next, reads 5.3-6.8 against 6.1. What moves is the tonemap's doing, which does not average a brightened ground and a dark figure back to the plain colour: a colour with a large dark figure darkens by up to 3-4 dE (the figures' white checks 3.4, the lines' red band 4.3, the spots' orange 3.6), and the lines' pale seam leaves navy greyer and darker (up to 2.5 dE), which takes black/navy from 14.5 to 10.8 under dome 13. The emblems move least: no pair closer by more than 2 dE on the wool or the marble. On the vinyl every set separates the tight pairs (black/brown 6.4 to 11.0-12.2), because the figure replaces the white gores, which pulled every colour towards white.
- **At play distance** (Testbed game camera, `ssaa=2`, `sceneseed=7`; Footbridge in the wool and the vinyl, Crane in its marble, Garland, which hangs all thirteen colours, in all three; 3840x1600 and 1920x1080): at 3840x1600 every set's figures read on every colour but black, whose figure is faint by construction. At 1920x1080 a Garland ball is about 23 pixels across, and the kind of a figure survives (a band, spots, checks, an emblem) while its detail does not: a heart, a spade and a club become blots of one size, and the lines' counts and the spots' sizes take a second look. So a set that gives a tight pair two different kinds of figure (the figures set does for six of its seven tight pairs) keeps the pair apart at the laptop's resolution, and a set of one kind leans on detail there.
- **Cost** (`d3dcompiler_47` instruction slots; no frame was timed): with every figure's branch compiled in, the three motif programs are 1088 (vinyl, against `PatternPS`'s 742), 1123 (wool, 691) and 1110 (marble, 672) slots at 13-14 temporary registers, at most one more than the shipped program's (the prototype's needed 14-16). The branches are on per-draw uniforms and cannot diverge, so a draw runs one figure's path; compiled with a figure's numbers as literals, that path is +20 to +57 slots on the wool, +26 to +63 on the marble and 29 to 66 slots below `PatternPS` on the vinyl, whose figure replaces the gores' colour work. The prototype's four figures, compiled the same way, ran +24 to +61 on the wool and measured +0.02 to +0.03 ms on a 9.7 ms frame (RX 6900 XT, 3840x1600 `ssaa=2`, `Bell` and a copy of it with every plain ball carrying a figure, one form per process, paired repeats within 0.01 ms). For a set's measured cost run one set per process, as the benchmark skill does.
- **Not built yet**: the other seven styles (metal, ice, gem, plasma, lava, porcelain, bubble) draw no figure, the HUD queue's discs carry none, and the Potato path has no programs for it (#828).

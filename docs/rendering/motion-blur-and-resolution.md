# Motion blur, resolution and aspect

Part of [Rendering](../rendering.md).

## Motion blur (`Prazsky.Core.Render.MotionBlur` + Game, #402)

What moved while the shutter was open is smeared along its path: the barrel as it is swung and aimed, the shot in flight, released balls falling, and the whole frame under the recoil's kick and a blast's rumble. A per-pixel velocity target and McGuire's reconstruction filter (I3D 2012) over the HDR scene; `MotionBlur.fx` in `Prazsky.Shaders`. **Only the Game** enables it (`PostProcessPipeline.EnableMotionBlur`, a session opens the velocity pass); the Testbed, editor and front end resolve as before.

- **The blur must be SEEN** (owner, 2026-09-25): a physically correct one-frame blur is nearly invisible at 144 Hz. So **the shutter is a length of time** (`MotionBlur.SHUTTER_SECONDS`, a twentieth; a thirtieth left the muzzle soft, not smeared) and every smear is the distance covered over it, the same picture at 60, 144 or 500 Hz. Camera, barrel and carriage keep a pose record (`ShutterHistory`, 64-sample ring); balls use their body's velocity times the shutter (`ClusterCollector.Collect`'s `shutterSeconds`), scaled by the simulation's time scale and zero while not stepping.
- **The camera's motion counts for a quarter** (`GameplayScreen.CAMERA_SHARE`, scaled by the overview lens's `M22` over the current): at full share a 118 deg/s swing smeared the whole frame and every recoil kick defocused it. ⚠ **The gun is measured against the camera's WHOLE motion** (`AgainstCamera`, #611): the camera follows the gun, so against a quarter the barrel smeared by motion it does not have on screen (walking the gun with A/D). Its shutter pose is `worldThen x VP_then x VP_then_share^-1` (projective, carried through `w`); leaned in, `world x VP_now x VP_then_share^-1`.
- **Frame flow:** the session collects balls with `BallRenderSet.RecordMotion` on (a per-frame opt-in `Draw` closes: every ball once, pose now and a shutter ago). After the scene and **before the resolve**, `BS3DGame.FinishSceneDraw` runs the velocity pass: `MotionBlur.BeginVelocity` (back-buffer-size `HalfVector4`: half-velocity, view depth, coverage, own depth), `CannonRig.DrawMotion`, `BallRenderSet.DrawMotion` on the coarsest sphere (the one moment a target can be bound without a second multisample resolve). `PostProcessPipeline.Resolve` runs the reconstruction first and reads its output **everywhere it read the scene target**.
- **The cluster is drawn only on a frame where some ball moved** (`BallRenderSet.MOTION_THRESHOLD`, a fiftieth of a ball over the shutter); everything undrawn is *background*, reprojected at **one depth**. ⚠ **That depth is the cluster's *distance* from the lens, not its view depth** (#559): the chapter intro flies shots looking *away* from the arena, the view depth fell to or below zero, `BeginVelocity` clamped it to 0.1 and the background became a wall a tenth of a unit away (every star streaked radially, the Moon scene's Earth a capsule). ⚠ A test `Settings.json` needs `"format": "bs3d-settings", "version": 1` beside `"motionBlur": false`, or `GameSettings.Load` rejects it and the blur stays on. When one ball moves, the still ones go in too (they hide a ball falling behind the lattice).
- **Reconstruction:** tile max velocity (`MAX_REACH_FRACTION` 3 % of the height), 4-32 jittered samples (one per two pixels of length; one per three read as speckle). **Not covered:** the island, drain, ceiling and scenery are never in the pass (a ball behind the island's rim smears a little rim), the wheels' roll, the landing ghost.
- **Gated twice:** the Settings row **Motion blur** (default on) and `QualityPreset.MotionBlur`; `BS3DGame.MotionBlurActive` is both. `Low` gives it up. **Cost, in one process** (`mbflip=4` flips it every four seconds and splits `[fps]` by state; separate runs drifted 2x on a shared desktop): about a millisecond at 3840x1600 on Paroxysm; not measured on the APU.
- **Driving it in the Game** (`Game/ScriptedPlay.cs`): `sweep=<from>:<to>[:<deg>[:<period>]]` (sine traverse, +-30 deg over 1.6 s by default), `rmb=<from>:<to>`, `fire=<t,...>`, `mbflip=<seconds>`, on the wall clock `shot=` and `detonate=` count.
- **Shot stretch (the fallback, #402):** `BallDrawFrame.AddOriented`'s `stretch` applies the world-space outer product `I + k n n^T` after the ball's own turn (a local-frame scale would swing the smear with the pattern), **only on a frame that is not motion-blurred** (`ClusterCollector` drops it when handed a shutter) and **only for a shot in flight** (a cluster raining ellipsoids reads as the frame rate collapsing); it grows above `STRETCH_FLOOR_SPEED` and caps at `STRETCH_CEILING`. ⚠ From the play camera the shot flies **away**, so any velocity stretch is foreshortened to nothing there.

## Resolution and aspect (Testbed)

The Game's player-chosen resolution is "The resolution" in `docs/game-shell.md` (`PostProcessPipeline.RenderSize`). The targets are 3840x2160 down to 3840x1600 (**16:9 to 21:9**); the Testbed's windowed default is 1600x900.

- **`width=<n>` / `height=<n>`** set the windowed back buffer (#141; nothing in fullscreen). The screenshot skill's `CopyFromScreen` clips a capture wider than the monitor.
- `CreatePerspectiveFieldOfView` takes the **vertical** FOV (Hor+): vertical is the binding constraint at every targeted aspect; `GameCameraFit.Solve` still checks both axes (a tall resized window flips which binds). `InfoRenderer` scales by `Viewport.Height / 2160`.
- **Supersampling is the dominant frame cost at 4K** (desktop): `Full.json` at 3840x1600 uncapped, `ssaa=2` ~118 FPS, `ssaa=1` ~362 (8.5 vs 2.8 ms). That is a statement about *this desktop*: on an integrated GPU (Ryzen 7 2700U / Vega 10) `ssaa=2` to `1` went ~8 to ~22 FPS while tripling the balls (1000 to 3000) moved ~8 to ~6-7; the fixed scene cost dominates there, which is why the game measures instead of assuming ("Adaptive quality" in `docs/game-shell.md`; #62-#64).

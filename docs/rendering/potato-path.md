# The Potato path (GamePi, #789)

Part of [Rendering](../rendering.md).

GamePi (Raspberry Pi 5, `docs/build-and-release.md`) draws through its own path: none of this area's effects exists there (DesktopGL's MojoShader stops at Shader Model 3.0) and the V3D could not afford them. The host half is `Game/BS3DGame.Potato.cs`, branched into from `BeginSceneDraw`, `FinishSceneDraw`, `DrawTranslucentsBehindGlass`, `CompositeForegroundLast` and `BeginOverlayLayer`; the nine effects are in `GamePi/Shaders`.

## Shape

- **Forward, into the 8-bit back buffer**, or with Resolution below native (#801) into an 8-bit colour+depth target scaled up by `PotatoUpscale.fx`'s one bilinear tap before the menus and HUD draw at native. ⚠ Its depth is **discarded before it is left** (`Platform/TargetDiscard`: `glInvalidateFramebuffer`) or the tiler writes it to memory: the scale-up cost ~2.9 ms with it, ~0.9-1.3 without.
- No `PostProcessPipeline` (no HDR, bloom, defocus, motion blur, grain, aberration), no `SceneRenderer` (the island stands under the dome's gradient), no `Sky.fx`, no shadows. **A scene's ground and static scatter where the scene has them on this path** (#829, `PotatoBackdrop`): the terrain's mirror (`TerrainMirror`) as a low-poly grid with the arena's hole, the scatter's buckets with the desktop's Low tier's detail left out, every piece an `InstancedModelRenderer` on the Potato effect lit by the sky rig with the island, drawn before it. The savanna first: 386k vertices a frame, +0.7 ms at native and at 720 on Giraffe; then the desert (its dunes alone, 25k) and the meadow (its oaks and fence through `MeadowScatter`, 467k): Basket 15.60/10.54 ms against 14.08/9.75 without, Pennant 14.90/10.48 against 13.10/9.35, the figures of #828 included in the first figure of each pair. Then the relief scenes, 96-cell grids: the mountains as two grids over one field, snow and rock by `Mountain.fx`'s rule per triangle (Belfry 18.84/12.46 against 17.88/12.09); the Moon with `MoonPlanting`'s boulders, the weathered ones as blobs over 400 vertices (Colossus 17.38/12.13 against 16.35/11.27); the volcano with `VolcanoLava`'s rivers as emissive ribbons (`PotatoStripMesh`) and the crater's lake, on the lamps' own seed (Vent 16.00/10.97 against 15.21/10.65). ⚠ Three corrections learnt on the third step: a config's colours are linear and the renderer's material sRGB (the first grounds were dark and, under the dome, mauve); an instance's `Custom` is the Potato effect's occluder and openness, and a planting writes its own figures there with w = 0, which the effect read as buried (the savanna's trees were a quarter lit, the Moon's boulders black); and the backdrop is matte (`SpecularAmbientStrength` 0, a specular too small to glint) since Schlick's rise mirrors the whole dome at a grazing angle whatever the reflectance - the far rim's white band, the lava's pale sheen. Then the two cities: `City`'s towers and cornices on the host's config (its seeds rolled), one unit box each in the facade's colour, no windows, the near 250 units only (a tower beyond the near ranks shows only its top), culled to the frustum a frame, over a plane of asphalt at the street level: Girandole 18.67/12.97 against 18.21/12.64, Cabinet 18.80/12.74 against 17.68/12.03. ⚠ Measure base and new in one chain from a baseline build of main: a lone Cabinet base of 11.5 ms (87 FPS, no session line) sent the first neon figure to +8 ms and three experiments (lamps off, 100 units, nothing) before the base was re-run at 17.7. Then the cavern (a shell, `PotatoShellMesh`: the cylinder and its ceiling in the rock's colour seen from inside, the river as a plane with a little of its glow, eight crystal clusters as leaning boxes radiating their colour at `CrystalCenter`'s places; Grotto 17.97/11.90 against 16.90/11.59 - the shell is a full frame of fill) and the Grid (the floor with the hole and a share of the traces' light, the solids as boxes on the config's band and seed; Cantor 15.45/10.73 against 14.98/10.45). ⚠ Both, and the Moon, stand under the dome's lilac and its rig where the desktop replaces the sky with a void or a cave and lights with the scene's own rig (`TryGetLightRig`, which the Potato host has no renderer to ask): the next step was theirs: **a scene that replaces the sky stands in its void here** (`PotatoSceneRig`, #829): the frame cleared to the colour its backdrop's pass has behind everything (the void, the cavern's fog, the big top's haze) and no dome, and `SkyLightRig` takes the scene's own rig and sun from the configs through its `SceneRig`/`SceneSun` lookups when it has no scene renderer to ask - the Moon under a black sky and its 16-degree sun, the cavern dim and cool, the Grid's solids teal on dark, the dream violet, the big top warm. No cost: a draw fewer. Then the big top, the desktop's quadrics as shells seen from inside (`PotatoShellMesh` generalised to a frustum with a segment filter): the canvas as two cones over one, red panels and cream, closed by the crown; the curtained wall; the seats' rake; the curb; the sawdust floor with the hole; the king poles as boxes. BigTop 14.57/10.26 against 13.90/9.69. Then the aurora's stand (the owner's „chybí stromy“): the desktop's second `ForestScatter` planting on its own seed, every spruce a cone on a stem in its variant's proportions (`ForestScatterRenderer.CONIFER_VARIANTS`, the one copy of the six; `PotatoBackdrop.SpruceProxies`), over the snow of `TerrainMirror.Forest` on a 96-cell grid: 157k vertices a frame in three draws (850 stems of 48, 850 cones of 72). Twelve of the fourteen shipped scenes have a backdrop on this path, and the three that stand in their void have **a sky of their own** (`PotatoSkyFigure`, `PotatoSky.fx`'s `PotatoSkySpace`/`Aurora`/`Dream` and `PotatoSkyStars`): a 48 by 96 sphere round the camera whose vertex shader computes the Milky Way's band and nebula, the aurora's folded, pulsing curtains or the dream's bent-sine marble and breathing orbs, interpolated as the gradient is, and the stars as a batch of 900 to 1,400 small screen-aligned quads added over it; the look's uniforms come from the scene's config (`PotatoSceneRig.SkyLookOf`). Analemma 13.64/9.97 against 13.40/9.33, Anchor 14.17/9.85 against 13.52/9.50, Anvil 14.14/10.08 against 13.60/9.66. ⚠ **Per pixel the same three figures cost 12 ms at 1080p** (13.4 to 25.9 on Analemma): a hash lattice of stars, the curve and a few sines on every pixel of the frame. The dome's rule holds for anything smooth: compute it at the vertex. A point is a quad. ⚠ Two prices learnt there: a plant mesh as the desktop plants it is thousands of vertices (a trunk with its branches 5,000 to 9,000, a termite mound 50,000: 1.2 million a frame), so a mesh over 1,200 vertices is drawn as an ellipsoid of its bounding sphere, shaped by the bucket (a wood tall and thin, a crown flat and wide), and a single wide flat bucket is a ground patch left to the grid (as a blob it stood round the camera: a black frame); and **the backdrop's pixels are a third of the frame, so it is lit at the vertex** (`PotatoVertexLit`: `Shade` from the vertex shader as the fins take it, display-encoded, the pixel one move) - through `PotatoLit` per pixel the same ground cost 3 ms at 1080p, and the blobs, bigger on screen than the plants they stand for, more. Every effect ends in `PotatoOutput.fxh`'s `ToDisplay` (exposure, ACES fit, sRGB), so colours stay linear to the last line; an RGBA16F target plus resolve cost 9.0 ms against 4.5 drawn straight (#785).
- **`PotatoModel.fx`** keeps `InstancedModel.fx`'s lighting model and nothing more: `PotatoLit`, `PotatoTextured` (island stone, no relief), `PotatoBall` (every `BallShading` as one lit coloured ball with heartbeat and ripple), and **`PotatoBallCueBands`/`Spots`/`Checks`/`Emblems` for a ball whose colour cue paint is not empty** (#737, #828: `BallColourCue.fxh`'s figures verbatim in a program of their own, the inks folded on the CPU into the crust and glow of each ink; `docs/rendering/ball-styles.md`). `InstancedModelRenderer` recognises it by technique and draws through `DrawPotato`; tintless kinds get stand-in colours, and **each special kind gets a look of its own from the uniforms alone** (#863, `PotatoKindLookFor`: the bomb a dark casing beating red, the zap a slate one flickering pale blue, the acid a dark body glowing green, the clear glass translucent through `PotatoLit`'s alpha, the frozen ball its colour in a pale glass-smooth shell, the sick ball an olive matte crust with a spore-green glow, the gravity well dark and glossy, the heavy ball matte iron with its colour worn in) - a colour, a glow, a gloss, an alpha, never a pixel of shader on every ball - and **three kinds get their desktop shape as a mesh** (`PotatoKindMesh`, `PotatoKindGeometry`: `StoneVS`'s carve and `FrozenVS`'s rounded cube ported figure for figure onto the sphere's own topology per LOD and drawn in place of it, the mine's eight studs as a frustum mesh of their own drawn after the casing on the same instance buffer; no rim ring for a replaced sphere). ⚠ A stud is not one vertex across on the play sphere (24 x 12), so raising the sphere's vertices as `BombVS` does would raise nothing: the studs are a mesh; and **three kinds get their desktop figure from a program of their own** (`PotatoBallKind`, `KindFigure`/`KindGlow`/`KindAxis`: the acid's lower half full of glowing liquid under a foam line, the sick ball's ragged crust over the third of the ball that faces the player, the gravity well's dark eye where the ball faces the eye with the whirl's ring round it) - `BallRadiance` with the figure painted on before `ToDisplay`, chosen by `DrawPotato` for those three shadings only, so the ordinary ball's program is untouched instruction for instruction; and **each of the ten materials gets its gloss** (#828 stage 1, the same table: the metal's reflectance floor and brushed softness, the glazes of porcelain, marble, gem and bubble as roughnesses from their desktop gloss powers, the wool matte, the ice glassy, the plasma glowing on a strength of its own since its desktop emission is nought) - a colour is never changed by its style; translucent light (`EmissiveTint`) is kept apart so the alarm flash and the cup's glints are not capped at the glass's alpha.
- ⚠ **One indexed uniform array per GL shader** ("GamePi" in `docs/build-and-release.md`).
- **No anti-aliasing filter at 720** (`Fxaa` +3.3-3.8 ms, 4x MSAA +1.8 ms: neither held the refresh; edges are drawn instead, below). `upscale=soft` (cubic B-spline) is **off by default: +1.5-1.8 ms** (V3D full-screen passes cost ~0.5-0.6 ms per texture tap at 1080p).

## The Windows build runs it too (#808)

With `potato` or Potato on the Quality row (next start): `Prazsky.Shaders` compiles `GamePi/Shaders` again for DirectX 11 into `Content/Potato/Shaders`, a Potato run's content manager (`Game/PotatoContent.cs`) looks under `Content/Potato` first (six of the nine share a desktop effect's name), and the run is held at the tier (`Platform/QualityLock`). `PotatoProfile.fxh` holds what the two compilers need apart:
- **Profile names** (DirectX 11 takes nothing below SM 4, MojoShader nothing above 3: techniques compile `POTATO_VS`/`POTATO_PS`) and **`VPOS`** (SM4 has none).
- ⚠ **A pixel shader's input must begin with the position under DirectX, and nothing says so when it does not**: stages pair by input order, so every input read its neighbour's value (all six ports compiled and drew; the confetti fell black). `POTATO_PIXEL_HEAD` is that member under DirectX, nothing under OpenGL.
- `PotatoShaderSourceTests` hold these in the sources' text; `build.yml` compiles DirectX, `gamepi-shaders.yml` OpenGL.

## Cost levers (Pi 5, 1920x1080, `nocap logfps`, mean of the last six one-second readings of a 26 s run)

- **The balls were the frame** (Girandole, 1204 balls: 30.8 ms; without their draw 12.3; `ballflat` draws them unlit to isolate shading). `BallRenderSet.LodBias` 2: 25.0. Upload **nearest first** (`OrderForPotato`) and draw with **no `clip()`**: a shader that can discard defeats early depth rejection (26.8 ms with the clip, **15.5 ms** without). Since #794 only the aim ghost is a size (`GhostScale`); dithered balls go in a second run of the instance buffer through `PotatoBallDither` (the technique with `clip()`), so only they pay. ⚠ A VNC client costs 3-5 ms: compare only runs in the same state.
- **A transparent material has one wall (#797):** `DrawShell`/`DrawHollow` skip the far wall when `InstancedModelRenderer.Potato` (Pennant, a Bubble level, 22.2 to 18.7 ms, 2026-10-05).
- **Scene lamps at the vertex (#795):** the volcano and neon city came out near black. `PotatoModel.fx` takes the desktop's `SceneLight*` uniforms; `BeginPotatoSceneDraw` lights the neon ring and the volcano (`VolcanoLava`). ⚠ **Per pixel is too dear** (Cabinet 19.9 to 34.1 ms): `PotatoVS` runs them unrolled with constant indices (a loop indexing three arrays by its counter is the dynamic indexing GL forbids). Lamps needing a backdrop (campfires, big top, planetshine, earthshine, lightning) push none. Lava gets `BallCrustTint`/`BallCrustDark` and a `BallGlow` (`LavaGlow x 0.3`, matched to the desktop's Caldera luminance); it glows evenly, not through cracks (the owner's verdict).
- **Compute per-draw constants on the CPU** (#804): MonoGame compiles with **no preshader**, so uniform-only expressions run per pixel. `DrawPotato` multiplies out sRGB decodes, hemisphere, reflectance, roughness and the crust/emission/glow/ripple colours; the heartbeat's `exp`s go to the vertex. Ball 391 to 250 instructions; native Girandole 16.56 to 13.58 ms. **`V3D_DEBUG=shaderdb` is the instrument** (no run-to-run noise).

## Edges are drawn where they are (#804)

A full-screen filter guesses edges from colour and pays for every pixel; Potato has only **geometric edges**, so they are drawn (discontinuity edge overdraw).
- **Rim for every ball** (`PotatoBallRim`, `BallRimMesh`, `rims=<pixels>`): after all balls, `RimPass` draws each as an instanced strip on its exact silhouette circle, blended, depth-tested, **not depth-written**, alpha 1 on the outline falling to 0 a pixel outside, measured per pixel (round at any LOD). **No depth bias** (the ring hides behind its own ball); dithered balls and the shot's stretch get none. ⚠ Shade it at the normal **half a pixel inside the limb for both rows** (the grazing normal peaks Fresnel into an outline). +0.3 to +1.2 ms.
- **Fins for other outlines** (`EdgeFinMesh`, `PotatoFinLit`/`PotatoFinTextured`, `fins=<pixels>`; `POTATO_FIN_PIXELS` 1, `fins=0` off, `finsall`/`finsless=` for taking it apart): island, drain, plate, barrel, collar, carriage, wheels (**not the rollers**: dozens of instances would each be handed every fin). A collapsed quad per candidate edge that `PotatoFinVS` opens a pixel outward where it is an outline or a sharp convex **crease**. Rules (`EdgeFinTests`): **no fin on a concave edge** (hidden by the surface) except a **concave crease where shading breaks**, with the fin on the face that faces the eye more and a **decal depth bias** (`FinRasterizer`); a crease is a **shading break** (vertex normals > 30 degrees apart), not a sharp meeting; an open rim opens only where its face is drawn; no edge a wall runs on past (`MeshBuilder`). Only meshes built inside `EdgeFins.Wanted()` get fins (all: 142 000 edges, 177 ms of load); `SelectLive` picks the frame's edges on the CPU and fins are shaded at the vertex. **About +0.66 to +0.78 ms on the Pi at native; on by default** (heaviest level 15.2 ms against 16.1; Resolution Auto steps to 720 if a Pi does not hold it).
- ⚠ **`RasterizerState.DepthBias` is in the depth buffer's [0, 1] and the framework multiplies by 2^24 for GL**: -2 put every fin in front of everything.
- ⚠ **The muzzle's collar is lathed inside out** (its profile runs up the lathe's axis; normals and front faces inward); only its rims get fins. Fix filed as #812, unmerged (it pales the round's colour, tuned in #478).
- **The drain's seams are the band's own to draw:** the gold band goes under the stone and the pit sheath, so no mesh edge marks where the eye sees it end. Each band vertex carries its height off the surface and `PotatoBand` fades it over its last pixel (`PotatoBandUnderPit` in pit scenes only). ⚠ **In pit scenes the glass goes down before the band** (its half-covered pixels stood in the depth buffer and failed the glass: dark specks); the band writes depth (else the near glass wall overdrew the lip); no rim fins on it (`EdgeFins.Wanted(rims: false)`).

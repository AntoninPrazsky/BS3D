# The weather: clouds, sun and sky

Part of [Rendering](../rendering.md).

Procedural clouds drift, shade the sky, throw shadows and flatten the light when they cover the sun. They live on **a flat plane at a finite altitude**, not as a dome texture (which sits at infinity and casts no derivable shadow, so what you see and what shadows would be two drifting fields). One field, three ray-plane intersections: the sky shader with the view ray, the scene shader with the sun ray, the CPU with the sun ray from the arena.

## The field and who owns it

- Defined **once** in `BS3DLibs/Prazsky.Shaders/Content/Shaders/Clouds.fxh`. The **weather** layer is coarse and mirrored in `Prazsky.Core.Render.CloudField` (the only part that exists twice; its hash uses `frac`/`dot`/multiply and **no `sin`**, because a sine hash is where C# and HLSL part company); the **detail** layer is fine, sky-shader only, and nothing the shadow or light rig reads touches it.
- `CloudField.ApplyTo(effect)` hands the shared parameters to both shaders. A **weather** (shape plus character: `DetailStrength`, `Opacity`, two billow figures, `CharacterStrength`, underside) is pushed **per frame**; `ApplyStaticParameters` pushes once what no sky disagrees about (absorptions, silver lining, horizon fade, sun disc size); `ApplyDome` (each dome/scene switch) pushes dome-dependent colours and remembers the dome's tint for the underside. The forest's haze reads the deck too (`Forest.fx`'s `ForestSkyBehind`, #1121: the opacity and the underside through `ApplyTo`, the horizon fade as `CloudField.HORIZON_FADE` from `ForestBackdrop`), so the sky behind its far trees is the one drawn, lid and all ([the twilight grade](../scenes/forest.md#the-twilight-grade-1121)).
- **Six authored skies** (`clear`, `scattered`, `mixed`, `broken`, `overcast`, `storm`; `WeatherLooks`, #221): a word, not eleven floats. **`Scattered` is the original weather to the last digit.** **`Mixed` is Broken's clouds with as much open sky** (#1172): every figure Broken's but the coverage bias (0.10 against 0.30), so a fade between them is a change of cover alone; it leaves the sun's disc more than half seen from nearly half the deck, where Broken leaves it a twentieth (`SeaCloudCoverTests`). Its enum value is written last so the five older ones keep their numbers. `SceneConfig.Weather` (base class) is the place's usual sky; `Level.Weather` overrides; an unrecognised word falls back to the scene's own. Defaults: desert `clear`, volcano `storm`, city `overcast`, sea `mixed`, mountains `broken`, forest `overcast`, meadow/savanna/outback/beach `scattered`, sky-replacing scenes `clear`. Testbed `weather=<name>` compares them.
- **A change of sky fades over 8 s** (`CloudField.WEATHER_FADE_SECONDS`; 2.5 s read as a dissolve). `SkyLightRig.StepOvercast` follows the deck's cover with its own 2.5 s lag (`OVERCAST_RESPONSE_SECONDS`) so the light arrives a beat behind the cloud; ⚠ that constant is not the fade's dial and the Game never steps it ("The clouds are the Testbed's weather, minus its ambient half" in `docs/game-session.md`).
- **Winds are slow** (#809): preset `Wind` halved, Storm 0.4, the second octave 1.25x the wind in `Clouds.fxh` and its mirror `CloudField.Weather` alike (volcano, `campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,90,160 fov=60 sceneseed=5`: 107 to 24.7 px/s).
- **A light on the ground can light the deck** (#509): `SceneRenderer.TryGetGroundGlow` (the volcano's crater) goes to `CloudField.SetGroundGlow`/`ClearGroundGlow` **every frame** so no scene inherits the last. **Black is an exact no-op, not a branch** (a uniform branch in a program that shades every pixel of sky keeps its registers either way).

## The sun

- **The disc** is a dot product and `smoothstep` in `Sky.fx`, added *before* the deck composites, so the same field occludes it. Radius `SunDiscAngularRadius` **1.6 degrees** (the true sun's quarter degree flickers in the glare pass's sparse grid). Colour: the rig's `sunRadiance` without the horizon lerp the clouds' lit side gets.
- **Each sky states where its sun stands** (`SkyDome.SUNS`, elevation and azimuth per dome, read by `SkyLightRig.SetSky`). Elevation carries the mood, read off the palette (dome 1 at 48 degrees, 11 at 55, 16 and 7 at 4). ⚠ **The azimuth covers the whole circle, not the half behind the opening view**: the gun's orbit is free 360 degrees, so every dome is seen front-, side- and back-lit anyway; fencing threw away the variety. An azimuth decides the world direction of shadows and glints and the opening frame (orbit starts at `+Z`).
- **Elevation 35 / azimuth 40 means "unchanged"** (`-DefaultLighting.Light0Direction`), kept by the sky-replacing scenes (`SkyLightRig.DOMELESS_SUN_DIRECTION`) **except the Moon and the Big Top**, which state their own sun (`SceneRenderer.TryGetSunDirection`: the Moon's is 16 degrees, #508; the Big Top's is its spot rig's key): a hidden sky byte must not swing the Moon's Earth phases or the space planet's terminator.
- **The map editor has no disc and no weather** (building `Sky.fx` there costs 6.4 ms of 24.9); `SkyDome` takes an optional `Effect`, null draws the plain gradient, and its cloud uniforms stay zero so `CloudSunlight` returns 1.

## The dome's gradient

The captured palette banded (sixteen rings, ~ten colours in pairs: a Mach band across every sky). `SkyDome` collapses same-colour rings, interpolates a 256-entry table **boxed twice** and draws a generated **64 x 48** mesh; `ZenithColor`/`HorizonColor` still average the capture's 92 entries, so the light rig is unchanged. ⚠ **The capture's vertex order is the welding order, not top to bottom**: read as a ladder it paints the sky its own ground colour.

## Shading the deck

- **Shading needs unclamped thickness, opacity the clamped** (`CloudThickness` runs past 1). **ACES eats cloud contrast**: the underside must be well below the lit top (0.18 vs 1.7). Detail strength is read against `CoverageGain`.
- **Cloud shadows** plug into `ShadePixel`'s `keyShadow`, coarse layer only. ⚠ **The key is dimmed per pixel by the shader, the ambient on the CPU, never both** (it counts the cloud twice). **Ambient reads an area** (`CoverAround`, five taps): one tap lurches at every cloud edge.
- **Cloud colour follows the dome**: the lit side starts at `SkyLightRig.SunRadianceTinted` and goes *further* towards the horizon colour (`CloudField.SunTintStrength`) than the scene's key (a daylight deck over a dusk dome read as pasted on, #50); the underside takes the zenith, harder. Test per dome with a launch per `sky=<n>`.
- **Shaded off its own slope** (`CloudNoiseD`, `CloudThicknessD`, `CloudFormStrength`, `CloudShapeStrength`). ⚠ **The derivative stops at the fourth detail octave while the value runs to seven** (a gradient scales with frequency: summed to the end the finest grain owns the slope and the form fizzes). ~1.8x the field's ALU (the one A/B ran on a degraded session: re-measure before quoting).

## The clouds as volumes on High and Ultra (#989)

The owner: "On high/ultra we need dramatically better clouds that are spatial, dynamic, massive, real, believable ... The clouds must be dense!" On High and Ultra (`SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`) every heap of cumulus near the lens is a volume of its own (`CloudVolumes`, `CloudVolume.fx`). The deck stays: it is what the shadows and the light rig read, and Low, Medium and the Potato path draw only it. References: `989-zimage`, `989-klein` (a cumulus field, a towering cumulus, stratocumulus, a storm front, the view from a hilltop, dusk, billows close up).

- **Where a heap stands is the deck's own weather.** The heaps sit at most one a cell on a lattice in the weather's drifting frame (`CloudField.Wind` x `Time`, the first octave's drift), a cell half the weather's feature size (`CELL_SHARE` / `Scale`), jittered. A heap stands only where `CloudField.Cover` is at least `COVER_MIN` and fades in over `COVER_FADE`; as the second octave evolves the field, a heap grows, thins and is gone. The heaps stand on the deck's cover but do not fill it: the lanes between them are cover the deck still shades the ground with (within 1500 units of the lens, a heap stands over only about 30 % of the ground under a deck cover of 0.3 or more, in a scattered sky and a broken one alike). Its flat base sits on the deck's plane. Out to `REACH` (4000), dissolving between `FADE_NEAR` and `FADE_FAR`: **past the camera's far plane (2000)**, since the layer has no depth buffer and the march takes a projection of its own (`CloudVolumes.FarProjection`, out to `LAYER_FAR`). At 1750, inside it, the band just over the skyline had no cloud where every reference has small heaps down to the horizon. The layer's far plane may stand anywhere past `FADE_FAR`, whatever the size of a heap's box: the shader dissolves a heap by where its march starts, which is never nearer the lens than the box face a pixel is drawn for, so a face the plane clips would have shown nothing (`CloudVolumeTests`).
- **A field, not a grid of buns** (#1145; the owner: "similarly sized little buns laid out on a grid"). References: `1145-zimage`, `1145-klein` (the distribution: a field from the ground and from a hilltop, one tower among small heaps, cloud streets, a line along a front, dusk). Each cell's heap draws its radius across from a spread (`SIZE_SMALL` to `SIZE_BIG` of the cell, skewed to the small end: many small heaps and fragments, a few big ones); a thin patch draws only small ones (`THIN_SPREAD`, `SKEW_THIN`), a dense one from `SIZE_DENSE` up and towards the big end (`SKEW_DENSE`), so a broken sky is big heaps with lanes between them. Every heap is drawn out down the wind (`STRETCH_MIN` to `STRETCH_MAX`, its axis within `AXIS_SPREAD` of the way the heaps travel), a small one flat and a big one domed (`ASPECT_FLAT` to `ASPECT_BIG`), and `TOWER_SHARE` of the big ones tower (`ASPECT_TOWER`), narrower and leaning down the wind, the top ahead of the base (`LEAN_TOWER`). ⚠ Down the wind is **minus** `CloudField.Wind`: the deck reads its noise at the world plus the drift, so its cloud travels the other way, and a lean taken from the wind vector leaned every tower upwind (`CloudVolumeTests` holds it). Where a slow field of the weather says the sky is organised (`ORGANISED_SCALE`, `ORGANISED_FROM` to `ORGANISED_FULL`) the heaps line up in **streets** along the wind, `STREET_SPACING` cells apart, bent, a heap between rows shrunk to `STREET_GAP` of its draw. Elsewhere a patch gathers its heaps in **clumps** with open sky between them, the more the denser it is: a second slow field (`CLUMP_SCALE`, `CLUMP_FROM` to `CLUMP_FULL`) says where a clump stands, a heap in one draws its size as the densest patch does and grows to `CLUMP_CREST`, one out of one shrinks to `CLUMP_GAP` and is squeezed out. ⚠ One heap a jittered cell, every cell kept, spaced a broken sky as evenly as a grid; the clumps are what open it. Then **the room**: each heap is no bigger than its neighbours (the cells `ROOM_CELLS` round) leave it, two outlines overlapping by at most `ROOM_OVERLAP` of the distance between them and the room shared out by the square of each one's reach, so the bigger keeps most of it: a big heap stands among small ones squeezed round it and a pixel marches about one heap, two where two meet; a heap squeezed under `SQUEEZED_TO` of the cell fades out. Every term is continuous in the cover, the streets and the clumps stand still in the weather's frame, and a neighbour fading in makes room as it fades in, so nothing pops. Within 1500 units of the lens a broken sky stands heaps over about 28 % of the ground, with about 11 % of it more than 0.6 of a cell from any heap, a mixed one (the sea's, #1172) over about 19 %, and a scattered one over about 15 %.
- **A heap**, in its own measure (`HeapLocal`: radii along its axis, across it and up, its stretch and lean taken out, `CloudVolumes.Heap.Form`): a flat base, widest a third of the way up (`CLOUD_WAIST`, the base `CLOUD_BASE_WIDTH` of that), rounding in to the top in a cap whose distance is iq's estimate for an ellipsoid; one unit is one radius every way, so the billows push a flat heap's top as far as its flank. Billowed by a baked 3D noise in the world (`ProxyVolumes.GradientNoiseTexels`, the fire volume's helper, #886) the size of `CLOUD_BILLOW` of the heap's size (the mean of its radius and height) on a heap of `BILLOW_RADIUS`, growing slower than the heap (`BILLOW_GROWTH`), so a big heap climbs in turrets and a small one is a lump or two; up the heap (`CLOUD_PUFF_FROM` to `CLOUD_PUFF_TO`) the billows are the noise's magnitude less `CLOUD_PUFF_BIAS`, puffs with creases between them, the cauliflower every reference's top is; and its rim eroded by a finer octave. Its edge goes from clear to solid over `CLOUD_EDGE` world units, the same for every heap. Every pixel of its box marches the part of the view ray inside the heap's **margin** (`CLOUD_MARGIN` radii past the outline: the cap's ellipsoid grown by it, centred on the waist so it holds the base too, above the base plane, the box round it), 10 jittered steps, falling to 5 for a heap past 1500 to 3000 units (`CLOUD_STEPS_FAR`: a few pixels tall, nothing ten steps could find). **The light**: the sun through one noisy tap close by and the heap's outline alone further on (its sunward side lit, its belly in its own shade, for no texture read), Beer-Lambert with a powder term, a two-lobe phase (gentle, `0.3` forward); the sky's light from above and the deck's underside colour from below (`CloudSunColor`, `CloudShadowColor`, read back from the sky shader, so the heaps take each dome's light as the deck does).
- ⚠ **The billows' push fades out before the margin's bound** (`CLOUD_PUSH_FADE_FROM` of the way out to it), so whatever the noise does the density is zero on every face the march stops at (#1145). Cut at the march's bound instead (a cylinder 1.12 radii round the axis, inside a box whose sides stood at the outline), a billow rolling out to it was solid there: **a straight vertical edge on a heap's flank**, in the phases a lobe stood at it, and every heap seen near a world axis cut at its waist by the box's sides. The bound is curved everywhere, so a billow the fade does press flat is pressed into a curve, not a wall. `CloudVolumeTests` holds the bound against the baked noise's extreme, for a heap of any height.
- ⚠ **The puffs only near the outline and up the heap**: the bias is gone a third of a radius in, or every crease through the body was a thin clear sheet, and a heap overhead, seen from under its belly, read as marbled smoke. ⚠ **An edge as a share of the radius** (a ninth, before #1145) made a heap three times the size three times as soft. ⚠ **Not much overlap**: the layer composites whole heaps far first by their centres, so where two heaps stand in each other the farther one's flank is laid over the nearer one's; at an overlap of 0.4 the heaps overhead read as columns of smoke.
- ⚠ **A heap's lower part greys** (`sun *= lerp(0.3, 1, smoothstep(0, 0.45, h))`): lit as brightly as its top, the lower side of every farther heap showed as a white seam under the flat dark base of the nearer one (the mountain's dome 8, the sea's).
- ⚠ **The forward lobe at 0.6** (two thirds of the light) peaked ten times the side's, and against a backlit dome every rim lit white.
- **The deck gives way to the heaps** (`Sky.fx`'s `CloudVolumeCut`, `CloudVolumeNear`/`Far`): fully inside `FADE_NEAR`, not at all past `FADE_FAR`. The deck's own horizon fade (0.16 of the climb) has taken it out past about 1230 units anyway, so a scattered sky is heaps on open blue, as every reference is. `CloudVolumes.ApplyToSky` states it every frame before the sky, off wherever no heaps are drawn.
- **So on High and Ultra the sun's disc is the heaps' alone to hide**, and what the lens sees of it is worked out on the CPU each frame (#1172): `CloudVolumes.SunPast` marches the lens's own sun ray through the heaps `Place` stood, as `CloudVolume.fx` marches a pixel's ray (the bound, the steps, the density with the billows of the same baked noise, the fade; each step at its middle where the shader jitters), and `March` hands the share of the disc left seen to the field with the deck's cut (`CloudField.SunPastHeaps`, `SunHeaps`), which `ApplyTo` pushes to any effect declaring `CloudSunPastHeaps`/`CloudSunHeaps` (the sea's glint, "The cloud and the glitter" in `docs/scenes/sea.md`). ⚠ **The march exists twice**: its figures are named as the shader's and `CloudVolumeTests` reads them there, with the literals it copies; change the shader's march and the copy together. Under the sea's mixed sky the heaps leave the disc seen from the play camera and the prologue's climb about a third of the time (`SeaCloudCoverTests`), where the weather layer leaves it more than half seen from nearly half the deck: a low sun's ray runs a long way through the heaps.
- **Which skies stand as heaps** (`CloudVolumes.Strength`, off the weather's coverage bias): Scattered (0.02), Mixed (0.10) and Broken (0.30) fully, Overcast (0.85) and Storm (0.72) not at all (a lid, and the Storm has its own cells, #953), fading between them as a change of sky does, so nothing pops. Clear has no cover to stand on.
- **Marched at the display's size, laid over the deck.** The heaps go into a target the back buffer's size, not the supersampled scene's (a cloud is soft, and four samples a pixel only march one soft edge four times over), cleared to nothing, composited far first, before the scene target is bound (`CloudVolumes.March`), then laid over the deck with it (`CloudComposite`, `CloudVolumes.Composite`): behind the opaque scene at the far plane, as the dome is ("The sky is drawn behind the opaque scene" in `docs/scenes/backdrop-architecture.md`), so a heap is covered by whatever stands in front of it, exactly as the deck is. ⚠ Marched into the scene target after the ground and depth-tested, a broken sky cost +3.2 ms: what is depth-tested now is only the composite's one texture read.
- `CloudVolumeTests` holds the placement without a device: every heap stands on cover the deck has, inside the reach, far first (seen to fail: a clear sky places none); the heaps ride the deck's drift; a lid places none; a sky is a spread of sizes with flat heaps and towers (seen to fail on the placement before #1145, whose smallest tenth and biggest tenth stood about 71 and 105 radii in a scattered sky, a ratio of about 1.5); a heap leans the way it travels (seen to fail with the lean along the wind vector); a broken sky leaves open sky between its clumps (seen to fail without the clumps: about 1 % of the ground near the lens more than 0.6 of a cell from any heap); two whole heaps overlap no more than the room allows (seen to fail without the room); half a second on, nothing has popped (seen to fail without the cover's fade-in; it caught a big heap shrinking at once when its neighbour's cover crossed `COVER_MIN`); the broken sky keeps inside the instance buffer; the layer's far plane stands past `FADE_FAR`.
- **Cost**: the heaps and the deck's cut against the whole deck with none, in one process (desktop Testbed Release, 3840 x 1600 at `ssaa=2`, which is High's, `nopost`, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=0`; a throwaway build with a dial that leaves the heaps out and the deck uncut, as the frame before #989 drew it, alternated in settled windows, 62 to 67 paired cycles, the sign held in every one). At the play camera (`campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,-2,-60`, no map) **+0.37 ms** under the meadow's scattered sky (5.05 to 5.43) and **+0.93 ms** under the mountain's own (dome 8, Broken: its ten levels are the broken skies that play; 5.47 to 6.39). At the game-mode camera (`at=3:F10`) with the level loaded: **+1.37** on `Belfry` (5.71 to 7.08), **+0.53** on `Pennant`, the meadow (4.86 to 5.39), and +0.53 on `Heart`, the savanna (6.32 to 6.85), which is what "Finer meshes" in [What the frame costs](../game-shell/quality-measurements.md) reads off the lever's pair under the two skies (+1.4, +0.53, +0.53). So the gap between the meadow's +0.37 and +0.53 is the camera and the level, not the way of measuring. ⚠ The first cut, 24 steps and two noisy sun taps into the scene target, cost +3.4 to +3.6 ms scattered; the cylinder, 10 steps, the outline-only far tap and the display-size layer each took a share. **The field of #1145** (the spread, the room, the stretched heaps and their margin, without the clumps, which stand fewer heaps: about 600 against 700 in a broken sky) against the grid of buns those figures priced, in the Game at `quality=ultra`, 3840 x 1500, `sceneseed=0`, `fpscap=1000`, two Release builds alternated level by level in two rounds (the second reversed), the lower of each build's two run medians: **+0.27 ms** on `Belfry` (9.20 to 9.47), **+0.34** on `Pennant` (7.30 to 7.64), **+0.33** on `Heart` (7.33 to 7.65), the sign held in both rounds. No `alt=` dial takes the heaps alone (`finemeshes` is the whole High lever), so builds are what was alternated. ⚠ **That frame is GPU-bound and hides the main thread's share**: placing the field (`CloudVolumes.Place`, every frame the heaps are drawn) costs the desktop's CPU 0.25 to 0.35 ms under a scattered sky and 0.5 to 0.6 ms under a broken one (Release, 300 calls after warm-up, nothing allocated, the spread over four runs on a shared machine), against about 0.1 for the grid of buns; most of it is the weather's noise read four or five times a cell and the room's pairs, of which those too far apart to bind are skipped. ## The sky at display resolution on High and Ultra (#1104)

On the tiers that supersample (High and Ultra, `ssaa` 2), the dome (`Sky.fx`: the gradient, the sun's disc, the deck) is shaded **once a display pixel**, not once a sample: `SkyPass.Prepare` draws it into a target of its own the scene target's size over the factor, before the scene target is bound, and the late draw behind the opaque scene ("The sky is drawn behind the opaque scene" in `docs/scenes/backdrop-architecture.md`) brings it in, depth-read at the far plane, and lays the heaps over it as before. Medium, Low, the Potato preset and a phone draw the dome into the scene target as before (they have one sample a display pixel already).

- **The switch is one line**: `SkyPass.DISPLAY_RESOLUTION` (true; false is the sky at every sample, the look before #1104). The Testbed's `skyres=<display|full>` overrides it for a run and is an `alt=` dial, so the before and the after are one process.
- **Point, not linear.** The factor between the two targets is exactly the supersample factor, so with point sampling each sample of a display pixel reads that pixel's own texel, and the resolve's box over them returns it exactly: one sample a display pixel, which is what Medium and Low show. A linear stretch would mix each sample with the next texel a quarter of the way over, and the box over the four a blur of the sky the resolve would add on top. Where the sky meets an edge (a tree, a tower, the island's rim) the edge keeps every sample it had: only the sky's samples are its pixel's texel.
- **What changes in the picture**: the deck and the sun's disc get one sample a display pixel. The deck's footprint (`Sky.fx`'s `fwidth(hit)`) is a display pixel's, twice a sample's across, so every detail octave fades out where it would at half the resolution, and the deck's finest grain (about 1.5 world units, `Clouds.fxh`) is retired nearer the lens: #104's warning, the reason a lower-resolution deck was once left. The heaps do not change.
- ⚠ **The heaps stay out of the sky's target.** They are marched at the display's size already, one jittered march a pixel (`CloudVolume.fx`), and laid into the sky's target and read one texel a sample their rims showed the jitter's checker at full contrast (up to 160 steps of 255 in the Game's 1920x1080 frame, Zigzag at Ultra), where the linear stretch of their layer over the supersampled target has always smoothed it. Laid over the scene target as before, they cost one texture read a sky sample.
- **It loses what the late draw saves on the dome itself**: the target is shaded whole, before anything stands in front of it, so the dome's program runs on every display pixel, where at every sample behind the opaque scene it runs only on the samples the sky shows through. The late draw is then only the target's read. Where the towers hide most of the sky the second is the cheaper, so **the two cities keep the dome at every sample** (`SkyPass.DisplayResolutionPays`, with the Scenery row on): at the pin below the neon city read 8.74 ms behind the scene at every sample against 9.30 at display resolution, the city 6.61 against 6.65; in the Game at Ultra (the pin of the Game's figures below) the neon city's Trophy 6.96 against 7.55, the city's Girandole 8.75 against 8.86 (10.10 and 11.39 before both steps). The Testbed's `skyres=` named on the line or in a variant is obeyed in the cities too.
- ⚠ **In a view with little or no sky the target is the whole price**, and the sky at every sample behind the scene is the cheaper: a Testbed lens straight down onto the island (`campos=6,40,4 camtarget=0,-9,0`, the pin below, one process a scene cycling `skyres=full` and `skyres=display`, every window settled, the sign held in every cycle) read 0.76 to 0.79 ms dearer at display resolution (forest 9.82 against 10.59, meadow 7.78 against 8.57, volcano 6.53 against 7.29), and a high oblique lens that keeps only a strip of sky along the top (`campos=40,18,40 camtarget=0,-2,0 fov=50`) 0.50 to 0.79 dearer (meadow 8.30 against 8.79, forest 11.39 against 12.18). Against the dome drawn first at every sample it is still the cheaper: the forest's look-down 13.18 ms on the build before #1104, 10.59 on this one (one process a figure, before, after, after, before). The Game's play camera and its front ends see sky enough to pay (the figures below). **The choice is made per scene, not per frame**: made from how much sky a lens sees, the deck's finest grain would change under a moving camera as it crossed the line.
- The target follows the scene target (the back buffer, the player's lower Resolution row, a quality step) and is let go in any frame that does not use it (a sky-replacing scene, a tier at `ssaa` 1, a city), about 46 MB at 3840x1500.
- **What it saves** (the pin of "The sky is drawn behind the opaque scene" in `docs/scenes/backdrop-architecture.md`, one scene a process cycling the sky behind the scene at every sample and at display resolution, every window settled, the sign held in 95 to 100 % of the cycles), in ms: forest 9.75 to 8.88, Mars 6.68 to 5.01, volcano 9.05 to 8.00, tropical 7.53 to 5.85, polar 5.92 to 4.14, sea 6.97 to 5.04, savanna 8.55 to 7.25, desert 5.53 to 3.99, mountains 7.94 to 7.14, meadow 7.32 to 6.16, outback 10.13 to 8.59, storm 9.47 to 7.57; with the late draw, 2.41 to 2.57 ms off every one of these scenes' frames. In the Game at Ultra (the same desktop, a 3840x1500 window, one process a figure, both steps against the late draw alone, before, after, after, before): Zigzag (the meadow) 8.77 to 7.64, Organ (the mountains) 8.83 to 8.11, the forest's front end 11.36 to 10.50, the tropical's 9.34 to 8.17; against the build before both steps, 9.94, 10.66, 13.02 and 10.49.

## ⚠ Real vertical extent for the deck was built and rejected

The flat deck has no silhouette with height and streaks towards the horizon from underneath. **The flat deck wins on looks, not only cost**, over a marched slab; what High and Ultra add instead is a volume per heap (above).
- *Displacing the underside*: fixed-point iteration diverges towards the horizon (marbled); offset limiting is nearly invisible; a linear search smears (the displacement needed exceeds the field's features). *Lowering the deck / stronger billow shading*: a wash / speckle.
- *A marched slab* (four slices; was `QualityPreset.CloudSlices`, removed) delivered layered undersides and was **judged worse by eye** (blurry, hallucinated). Its traps: the body cannot run negative over clear sky, so ungated detail paints cloud on every clear pixel; the path lean `1/climb` unbounded is a navy wall and `min` creases (use a softened reciprocal); sampling where the ray first meets cloud quantises onto the slices (use a density-weighted centre of mass); a belly ramp narrower than the slice spacing gives coverage staircases, and slices `depth / climb` apart at the skyline give a moire only fading depth with the climb removes.
- **Cost to weigh any future attempt against** (Game front end, reference APU, meadow, ssaa 2, the dome shaded at every sample of the supersampled target, which no tier draws any more: High and Ultra shade it once a display pixel, [above](#the-sky-at-display-resolution-on-high-and-ultra-1104), so re-measure before weighing one): the flat deck's cloud pass is **6.4 ms of a 24.9 ms frame** (40.1 vs 54.0 FPS with `SkyPS` returning the bare gradient; 7.3 ms on the sea); marched **40.1 to 26.3 FPS**, six slices no cheaper than four (a fixed penalty, probably register pressure). A tier could only switch it off.

## Cheaper sky

- **The deck's own program: measured, not worth it.** Testbed, sea, dome 13, `nocap`, fixed camera: the deck's shading costs 1.56 to 0.75 ms; approximating the shadow's second `CloudCover` **and** cutting `CloudDetailD` from seven octaves to five together still measure **1.56 ms**: the cost is the field evaluation itself (as for the forest floor and the dream, `docs/scenes.md`). ⚠ **A fixed camera is not optional** (an orbiting-camera sweep once showed two reductions together worse than either alone).
- **A lower-resolution deck: taken.** It is what High and Ultra draw since #1104 ([above](#the-sky-at-display-resolution-on-high-and-ultra-1104)); below them there is nothing to take, the scene target having one sample a display pixel already.

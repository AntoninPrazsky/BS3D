# The aurora

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The nineteenth `SceneKind` (#205), rebuilt from the ground up in #963 after the owner's verdict that it "looks like a bug" and like "a concept for a GameCube or 90s PSX game": a moonlit snowfield in a boreal clearing under a great folded aurora. Made of `AuroraBackdrop`, `Aurora.fx`, `AuroraSceneConfig` (groups `Terrain`, `Snowfield`, `Sky`, `Curtains`, `Mist`, `FarStand`, `Snow`, `Stars`, `Lighting`, `Potato`), `AuroraMoundMap`, `AuroraCollars`, `AuroraStandMesh` and `SceneFog.fxh`. Launch: `scene=aurora` (`sceneseed=` pins the planting); the Silhouettes chapter plays its ten levels here (Fish to Anchor).

**It is in both families, after the Moon:** `IsSolidTerrainScene` (real ground, footprint cut out, pit shaft, no dive) and `ReplacesSky` (no dome or cloud deck, its own sky and rig through `TryGetLightRig`); see "The sky-replacing family" in [Space](space.md). Its rig moves (`SceneCatalog.AnimatesLightRig`). It has a terrain mirror (`mirrorcheck`).

## What the references settled (#963)

Rendered with both models (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\963-zimage` and `963-klein`, prompts `prompts-963.json`): the clearing from a low camera, the curtains, a trunk's foot, a wind-cut snowfield, layered hills, a log in snow, and img2img over the play and drop cameras. They agree on:

- **The value structure:** the moonlit snow is the brightest surface and its shade is blue; the spruces are dark and carry snow; the sky is a deep blue with a paler glow along the horizon the treeline stands against.
- **The aurora is a few distinct curtains**, each a folded sheet with a sharp, bright green lower edge, rays straight up it and red or magenta at the top, with dark starry sky between them.
- **A low Moon** throws long tree shadows across the snow; **mist** lies low at the forest's foot; the hills recede in layers, each paler, their skylines jagged with tree tips.
- **The snow has relief** and rises against every trunk (Z-Image drew a flare, klein a well; the flare buries the foot, so the flare), a log lies half buried, boulders are humps.

## The snowfield

- **The field** is the forest's clearing and hills (`ForestGround.fxh`, the one copy) plus the snow's own geometry: the wind's **drifts** (`SnowDrift`: gradient noise stretched along the wind, warped, in patches), the **bank** against the island's drum (`SnowBank`, deeper on the lee side; it stands in for the island's berm planting here), and the **lee tails** downwind of the trunks (the mound map, below). `TerrainMirror.AuroraBase` mirrors the first three term for term (the noise is `ShaderMath.Noise`); `TerrainMirror.Aurora` adds the mound map. `mirrorcheck`: max 0.000069; seen to fail with the drifts 5 % high (0.0149, 95 samples over) and the mound map 5 % high (0.0160, 319 over).
- **Two grids and no `clip()`**, MountainFine's lesson ("High and Ultra's range" in [The mountains](mountains.md)): a fine grid round the camera (481 over 360, 0.75 a cell, near first) carries the drifts as geometry; the coarse grid (260 over 1800, its own: the last ridges stand on it out to 900) runs out to the hills and sinks four units wherever the fine one draws. Both programs shade a point alike (normals of the base field per vertex, the mounds' slope and everything finer per pixel), so where the two overlap at the seam it does not matter which wins. The island's hole is cut in the vertex shader (every vertex inside it to one point under the clearing, the cone painted black down the throat); ⚠ never onto the circle: three corners on it are a triangle across the hole.
- **The mound map** (`AuroraMoundMap`, 1024² half floats over ±512, baked once from the planting): a lee tail downwind of every trunk and snag (`LeeTail`) and the crowns' shade on the snow (`CanopyShade`, the fourth channel: the dark round a tree's foot that seats it). Kernels add and then saturate softly (`CAP`), so two trees share one tail. A texel holds the height **and its exact slope**: a slope taken from neighbouring heights is flat per texel and faceted the drifts. The mirror reads the very half floats the texture holds, bilinear by the shader's own four point taps (a hardware filter weights in 8-bit steps).
- **The collars** (`AuroraCollars`, `Aurora.fx`'s `AuroraCollar`): the drift against the foot of every trunk, snag, boulder and stump, and along every log, is a mesh of its own, one polar grid instanced about a thousand times. It starts inside the wood at its full rise (`TrunkDrift`, a rock's or stump's share of its height, a log's `LogDrift` over its own axis) and falls to the snow over its reach (`TrunkDriftReach`), further downwind (`CollarLee`), with a level tangent; it stands on the drawn snow (`SnowHeight` per vertex) and is shaded by the snow's own program, so its rim melts into the ground and only the rise against the wood shows. A log's collar runs round it as a stadium, moved halfway to the log's bow and widened by the other half (`DeadwoodMesh.Bend`). ⚠ **The drift was the mound map's first**, at a unit a texel under a grid of 0.75: a rise over less than a unit came out at four fifths of its height smeared over two units, and every foot read as a cylinder stuck into a plane. ⚠ A log's straight core left a log bowed a metre sideways lying on its own drift.
- **The planting is the backdrop's** (`ForestScatterRenderer.Plant` on `AuroraBase`, `SceneRenderer.AuroraPlanting`), and every host's aurora `ForestScatterRenderer` draws it (its `planting` argument), so the tails, the shade and the collars lie round the trees that are drawn. The planting sinks a trunk 0.15 and more under the base and the collar rises over it, so no foot stands proud. `AuroraSnowfieldTests` holds it at the bark's edge, where the eye sees the join (the snow stands over six tenths of `TrunkDrift` up the bark, over the foot, and is level with the ground by its reach), and that no log is buried whole or lies on the snow; each seen to fail.
- **What it costs to build:** the planting, the map and the collars are made on the thread that builds the renderer, at every start of every executable (the renderer builds every backdrop eagerly): on the desktop the planting 11 to 17 ms and the map 50 to 60 ms (its rows packed in parallel), the collars under 3.
- **The snow's light:** the Moon (a soft terminator, its shadow map), the curtains' light per frame (below), the sky's hemisphere, the crowns' shade, `DeepTint` (the blue of shaded snow, light come back out of the lit pack), patches of wind crust, the sastrugi per pixel (ridged noise across the wind, each octave faded where the pixel stops resolving it), and **glitter**: one grain in so many of a lattice has a facet of its own, so a glint lights only from where the eye is and twinkles as the camera moves, a dot about a pixel and a half across at any distance. `Low` (`SceneDetail` 0) draws `AuroraSnowFineReduced`/`AuroraSnowCoarseReduced`/`AuroraCollarReduced`: one sastrugi octave, no grain, no glitter.
- **The grid's edge** fades into the mist's colour from 80 % of the coarse grid's half extent, as the horizon does, so the ground never ends in a line.

## The wood

- **Pure conifer** (`ConiferFraction` 1): a shrunken broadleaf read as "little mushrooms with a brown leg and a green cap" (#462). **Boreal spires**: the count of whorls says "boreal" far more than proportions (`ConiferTiers`, `ConiferTierSpread`, `ConiferRaggedness`; a regular stack reads as a pagoda). **Every new dial defaults to what the forest was built with and consumes the seed as before**, so the daytime wood does not move. A wall two to four times the height of the gap (#647), the stand on bare stems (`ConiferStandTrunk`).
- **Dead wood**: `SnagMesh` and `DeadwoodMesh` (snags and logs, scattered **after** the four original kinds: all draw from one rng stream, and anything earlier re-plants the daytime forest). ⚠ Thin pale snags read as bamboo: thick and dark cool grey. A snag's trunk is one `AddSweep` from the foot through the root flare, the kink and the break to the splinter's tip, mitred at each bend and closed at both ends (`SnagMesh.Build`, held by `SeamAuditClosureTests`); four tubes laid end to end opened a wedge at every bend (#974).
- **Snow on the wood** through the triplanar dust term: `CrownSnow` on the lathe crowns, `BoughFrost` (0.7 here, the forest's 0.25) on High and Ultra's boughs over their lumps of snow (`TreeMesh.FineSnow`), and `TopSnow` on the boulders, stumps, snags and logs (#963; 0 in every other wood). ⚠ On a log the dust is a strip along its top, not the pillow of snow the references draw.
- **It casts into the Moon's map**: the Game's and the Testbed's shadow casters draw the aurora's wood; the map editor hands no casters, so its preview has no tree shadows.
- **The far stand** (`AuroraFarStandConfig`, `AuroraStandMesh`): 9 000 spires of four tiers (about 170 triangles) from where the hills rise (200) to 600, in the gaps between the planted wood's groves and never inside one, and 16 000 of two tiers (about 90) filling the square the coarse grid draws round the play camera out to 880, with glades, each mesh wound clockwise from outside and shut (`Tools/WindingCheck` and its `--openness` gate judge it), one instanced draw a ring (`InstanceBinding`), so every ridge's skyline is tree tips. A spire beyond the coarse grid's reach goes to a point in the vertex shader (it would stand on nothing). It casts no shadow and is in no planting: ⚠ a lens laid through it flies through a spire. ⚠ A ring that stopped at 600, or a circle inside the grid's square, left the farthest ridges bare.

### The spruces on High and Ultra (#733)

The owner's playtest note: the conifers "could pass for Low, maybe Medium, but not High or Ultra". Large on a 4K screen, the lathe of smooth skirts reads as lampshades on a pole. High and Ultra draw a crown of **separate boughs** instead. The switch is `SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`, the one #758 made for the acacias (the Testbed's `finemeshes=0`). Low, Medium and the Potato path keep the lathe. The daytime forest's spruces are the same mesh and change with it.

- **One tree, two crowns.** `TreeMesh` rolls its whorls once (`SpruceWhorlPlan`: each skirt's edge, the span, the lathe's wobble), in the order it always has. It builds the lathe and the boughs from that plan; the boughs use a random stream of their own (`SpruceCrownGeometry`). Both crowns have the same height and the same width at every height, and both lean the way the wobble does. No bough reaches past the lathe's widest skirt plus its wobble (`SpruceWhorlPlan.Reach`). A host that keeps the camera out of a tree by the lathe's figures therefore keeps it out of the boughs.
- **What a bough is**, from the references (#733's set, both models). A spruce's outline is its bough tips, each at its own length and turned up. A fringe of branchlets hangs under each bough, snow lies in lumps, and the crown is dark between the boughs. Each bough here is an arm with a diamond cross-section. A ridge runs over the arm, two side edges sit a little below it, and a keel hangs under it, deeper low in the crown. The top and the underside keep their own normals, so a plate is lit on top and dark beneath. Whorls of five to seven arms, with smaller arms between them, turn by the golden angle. High arms climb, and low arms hang and curl up harder. A relief a quarter of a unit long (`NewBoughRenderer`) breaks each plate's faces into a grain.
- **The snow is geometry** (`TreeMesh.FineSnow`, drawn in `CrownSnowColor`): a pillow on the plate of half the arms. ⚠ The dust term whitens a face by how squarely it faces up, and on a wood of flat plates at full strength it turned every bough snow-pale; the boughs take `BoughFrost` of it.
- **The interior is a mesh of its own** (`TreeMesh.FineShade`): a narrow lathe round the stem, drawn at `SPRUCE_SHADE` of the needles' colour and after the boughs. Without it a gap between the arms opens onto the far side of the tree, lit as brightly as the near side, and the crown reads as a wire frame. It starts an eighth of the crown under the tip, and no ring is pushed in by the wobble past half its radius (`SHADE_WOBBLE_ROOM`): the aurora's wobble is as large as the interior's thin top and turned it inside out.
- **One stem from the trunk into the leader** (#960, `TreeMesh.FineStem`, `SpruceCrownGeometry.StemRadius`). The owner, of both woods on High: the tree tips levitate. ⚠ Without the stem the axis under the leader carries no wood for 1.25 to 2 units of the mesh in the Forest's authored spruce, 5 to 6 in the aurora's and 2.75 to 3.5 in its spire, and the leader and the top whorls hang over the gap. The stem is a lathe of the trunk's bark: the trunk's top radius at the crown's foot, tapering straight to three quarters of the leader's radius at the leader's foot (`STEM_IN_LEADER`), closing inside the leader, drawn at `SPRUCE_SHADE` of the bark's colour. ⚠ At full colour the Forest's orange bark reads as a pole through the crown. An arm roots no further out than half the stem's radius (`ROOT_IN_STEM`) and its root ring is no wider than the stem. It casts into the sun's map with the crown. Low and Medium draw no stem: their lathe crown is closed. It cost +0.02 to +0.03 ms on Fish.
- **Two details, by distance** (`SpruceDetail`). A spruce within `FINE_NEAR_RADIUS` (110) of the arena's axis wears `Near`: five-ring arms and a whorl every 1.15 units. Every other spruce wears `Far`: wider two-ring arms every 1.9 units, over a fatter interior. With its snow, a near crown is 2 600 to 5 500 triangles and a far one 470 to 1 230, against the lathe's 250 to 760. The split is made once at planting, so a tree never changes crown as the camera moves. ⚠ With the near crown's thin interior the far crown read as a wood of masts.
- **What it costs**: +0.32 ms at the play camera on Fish and +0.42 from inside the wood (Testbed, 3840 x 1600, `ssaa=2`, `alt=finemeshes=0;finemeshes=1`, the sign held in 97 to 100 % of 27 to 34 paired cycles). ⚠ **The cost is triangles, not pixels**: the difference was the same at `ssaa=1`. The first build, every spruce at the near crown's detail, cost +0.92 ms, which is why the far crown exists.
- `SpruceCrownTests` holds it without a device: no bough outside the lathe's reach, the far crown at most two fifths of the near one, every triangle facing its normals, and wood on the axis from the crown's foot to the leader's at least half as thick as the leader's foot. Both checks fail on every variant without the stem.

## The sky

- **The night:** a gradient from a deep blue zenith (never black: the references' night is blue, and a black one reads as a hole) to a paler horizon, and a glow along the horizon strongest under the curtains (`HorizonGlow`, `HorizonGlowWidth`). The stars are the shared lattice (`Stars.fxh`, [The star lattice](space-stars.md)), thinned towards the horizon and round the Moon. **The Moon**: a disc 1.1 degrees across (a real one is a dot at a game's field of view), over the glare threshold so it blooms, its limb darkened and its maria a gradient noise, in a halo of lit air.
- **The curtains are a few arcs** (`AuroraCurtainsConfig.Arcs`): each a vertical sheet hanging along a line across the sky, the line snaking in a long bend (`BendDepth`, `BendFrequency`) with smaller folds along it (`FoldDepth`, `FoldFrequency`), its lower edge at an altitude and its height above it. A view ray meets each sheet where its line across the sky crosses the arc's mean line, one step off it by the hang there: the altitude at the meeting says where in the curtain the eye looks. A sharp lower edge (`EdgeSoftness`), the body falling away up the curtain to a red top, a thin magenta fringe at the very edge, rays at three widths running straight up the sheet (along it only, faded where they would be finer than the pixel), lit in stretches along its length (`LitFrequency`), brighter where the sheet turns edge-on (the slant), and dimmer low over the horizon through more air. The main arc hangs over the play camera's treeline; a fainter one overhead and one over the other half of the sky are there for the lenses that look up or turn round.
  - ⚠ **The look the owner rejected:** a band over 62 % of the sky filled with soft 3D noise ramping green to violet with elevation, so everything above forty degrees was a magenta mottle (the intro looked 44 degrees up into it for its whole shot).
  - ⚠ **One step, never an iterated fixed point.** Iterated through a deep fold the solve jumps between roots from pixel to pixel and drew the lower edge as a saw.
  - ⚠ **A curtain lit along its whole length is a fence** of even stripes across the sky; `LitFrequency` breaks it into curtains.
  - ⚠ **Under a bright sharp edge the dark sky reads as a range of mountains** cut out against the curtain; a faint glow under each edge (the curtain's light scattered in the air below it) and the horizon glow take that away.
  - ⚠ **Never an unwrapped `atan2` azimuth**: it jumps on one vertical line in the world (the original seam). The arcs are built on the view direction's components.
- **Motion:** the folds re-form in place (`FoldSpeed`, slow; a fast drift turns the sky as a carousel, #462), the rays shimmer along the curtains (`RaySpeed`), and each arc breathes on its own phase (`PulseSpeed` 0.11, `PulseDepth` 0.4), so the light wanders from curtain to curtain. ⚠ **A still cannot judge motion**: the owner's eye is the test.
- **Draw order:** the fine grid, the coarse grid, the far stand, then the sky quad depth-read at the far plane (the Moon's measured order; every pixel the ground owns is rejected before the sky runs).

## Light

- **The Moon is the key** (`TryGetSunDirection`, `MoonElevationDegrees` 17 at `MoonAzimuthDegrees` 290: to the left of the play camera and a little behind it, so the shadows cross the clearing in front of the gun). The scene has a shadow map (`ShadowConfig` strength 0.88 over 300 units; 4096 at High, 8192 at Ultra, none at Low); the snow and the far stand receive through `Aurora.fx`, the wood, the island and the gun through the shared instanced effect.
- **The curtains' light on the snow** is computed per frame from where the arcs hang: the brightness-weighted mean of their centres' directions, in their green, at `GroundLight` of their brightness. ⚠ At 0.11 the snow went cyan.
- **The rig** (`AuroraLightingConfig`): the key's tint is the Moon's colour over the default key's warm white (`KeyTint`); the ground ambient is the moonlit snow's bounce; the back light, from above and behind, is the curtains' green, its brightness following the arcs' total in 24 steps between 0.7 and 1.3 of `BackTint` (a host re-lights only on a step, `SkyLightRig.StepSceneLight`, so nothing strobes); the sky ambient leans `GlowTint` towards the green. The wood's pigments shift by `AuroraGlowColor`, the Moon's white a fifth of the way to the green, constant.
- **The mist** (`AuroraMistConfig`, `SceneFog.fxh`): height fog dense at the snow (`BaseY` -15) thinning by e every 2.5 units up, in banks blown downwind, from 20 units out, with a thin haze at every height for the hills. Its optical depth along the ray is the exponential's integral in closed form, no march. `Aurora.fx` applies it to the snow, the stand and the horizon, and the renderer pushes it every frame into the shared instanced effect in `DrawShadowMaps` (`SceneFogDensity` 0 in every other scene), where the triplanar programs apply it to the wood. ⚠ The mist's colour must be lighter than the trees and darker than the lit snow, or it vanishes against both.
- **The island** is lake ice darker and greyer than the glacier (`ArenaIsland.LookFor`): the old pale ice outshone the moonlit snow round it.
- **The falling snow:** its own `Snow.fx` clone and `SnowConfig`, gentle. ⚠ Near the lens a defocused flake against the night is a big pale disc (the old intro read as dust on the lens): the flakes fade out from ten units and the lens's blur is small (`NearFade`, `Aperture`).
- **Ambience:** an explicit `case SceneKind.Aurora` in `ProceduralAmbience.BakeScene`.
- **Intro:** `AuroraIntroShots` (the wood, the moon, the curtains); see "The volcano (#530) and the aurora (#531)" in [Chapter intro prologues](../game-feedback/chapter-intro-prologues.md).

## The Potato path

The Raspberry Pi's renderer keeps its own stand-in (#829, `PotatoBackdrop.Aurora`: cones on stems over a snow-coloured grid on `TerrainMirror.Forest`). `AuroraSceneConfig.Potato` holds the sky, rig and void it shipped with, and the island keeps its paler ice there (`ArenaIsland.LookFor`'s Potato case), so the desktop's rebuild does not reach it.

## Costs

Desktop (RX 6900 XT), the Game on Fish, 3840 x 1581 windowed, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=3 seed=1`, 40 s runs, the median of the `[fps]` lines from 20 s (after the intro), the before and after builds alternated, the minimum of the run medians: **`High` 9.11 ms, `Ultra` 9.46**, against 9.73 and 9.69 for the scene it replaced, with the Moon's shadow map (4096 at `High`, 8192 at `Ultra`), the collars and the far stand that scene did not have. Not broken down by pass; what it drops is the old terrain's `clip()` (no early depth test) and its sky's four octaves of 3D noise a pixel, against the analytic arcs. ⚠ Something else on the card can hold a run at 10 to 12 ms for stretches (one `High` run of each build did): compare the minima, and measure with the card idle; a local LLM on it reads 10 to 17 ms.

**Risks:** the far stand (25 000 instances, about 3 million triangles), the collars (a thousand, 280 000) and the two terrain grids (some 300 000 vertices) are drawn whole at every tier, with no culling and no tier's scaling; `Medium` now builds a shadow map (2048) it did not have in this scene. The same Game runs at `Medium`: 3.96 to **4.40 ms**; at `Low`, 4.08 to **3.39**. Not measured on the APU or the laptop.

**Open:** the motion and the look are the owner's eye; the far stand's spires are crude seen close (no lens is laid among them); the logs' snow on top is a strip of the dust term, not a pillow of geometry; the map editor's preview draws no tree shadows.

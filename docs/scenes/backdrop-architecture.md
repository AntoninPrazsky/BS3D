# Backdrops and the scene registry

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

How the twenty-one scenes are organised: `SceneRenderer` is a host that dispatches to one `Backdrop` class per scene over shared services, and `SceneCatalog` is the one table of facts about each `SceneKind` (#580). Read before adding a scene or touching what several scenes share. Scene settings are **shared between the game and the map editor**, so a map looks in the editor as it will play.

## What a scene is

- Every scene except the two cities is a `Backdrop` in `Render/Scenes/`, owning its effects, meshes, buffers and tuning constants. Each shader lights itself from a per-frame `SceneFrame` (camera, sun direction, sky palette in linear radiance, the sun's tinted radiance, wall-clock time, optional cloud-apply hook); the shaders are the one compiled copy `Prazsky.Shaders` puts beside every executable (#618). The Testbed passes `_clouds.ApplyTo` as the cloud hook; the editor passes null (full sun, no cloud shadow).
- **Draw slots:** `DrawEnvironment` (terrain, water, scatter, birds; two halves, `DrawEnvironmentOpaque` and `DrawEnvironmentBlended`, [below](#the-islands-depth-goes-in-before-the-environment-1098)), `DrawGrounded` (ground-level things the ceiling glass must bend: the savanna's fires and the meadow's brook and pond water (#892), [Shared effects](shared-effects.md)), `DrawOverlays` (foreground weather last: snow, spray, ash). Hosts call `SceneRenderer.DrawShadowMaps` before binding the scene target ("Sun shadows" in `docs/rendering.md`).
- **Sky-replacing scenes** (`ReplacesSky`: space, dream, cavern, Moon, aurora, Grid, big top) draw no grid, cloud hook or dome, each stating its own light rig (`TryGetLightRig`). The Moon, aurora and Grid write real ground first and read the sky's depth against it; space and the dream are one full-screen pass drawn depth-off over nothing; the cavern is meshes that close round the lens ([The cavern](cavern.md)).
- **The two cities are host-owned** ([The city](city.md)): `_backdrops` is null for them.
- **The cycle.** Every `SceneKind` is on the Testbed's NumPad2 and the editor's V cycle: `SceneCatalog.NextScene` runs off the enum, so a new scene is reachable the moment it is declared. ⚠ A fixed prefix (`CycleLength` of 7) went stale: half the shipped levels were authored past it and the editor could not return such a level to its own backdrop.

## `SceneCatalog`

`Render/SceneCatalog.cs` holds per kind: display name, parse key (what a level file writes and `scene=` takes), aliases (`ice`, `tron`, `bigtop`) and the classifications `ReplacesSky`, `IsSolidTerrainScene` (`OpenBelow` is its negation) and `AnimatesLightRig`. It replaced five parallel tables (one positional array that mislabelled every scene after a reordered member).

- **The type refuses to initialise unless every enum member has exactly one row at its own index and no two spellings collide**, so an executable with a missing row does not start; `Tests/BS3D.Tests/SceneCatalogTests.cs` feeds the check each malformed table.
- It has no GPU type, so `SceneNameJsonConverter` reads and writes scene names off it alone: **the level format does not depend on the renderer.** `SceneRenderer`'s statics forward to it.

## `Backdrop` and the services

- **`Backdrop`** (internal) is one scene's share: config, effects, push, `Draw`, and hooks for what only it can answer (`DrawBlended`, `FollowsIslandDepth`, `DrawDepth`, `DrawOverlays`, `DrawGrounded`, `DrawsAtDisplayResolution`, `TryGetLightRig`, `TryGetSunDirection`, `TryGetViewpoint`, `TryGetSceneEvent`, `TryGetGroundGlow`, `ShadowReceivers`, `TryShadowFit`, `HasShadowCasters`/`DrawShadowCasters`, `BeforeShadowMaps` (a target of its own drawn before any of the frame's is bound: the outback's baked shadows, #967), `TryGetTerrainProbe`, `OnDetailChanged`, `Dispose`). Every default is the answer for a scene that has none.
- **`BackdropServices`** carries what several scenes share: device, full-screen quad, supersample factor, seed offset, `TerrainHoleRadius`, the terrain grids ([Terrain grids and mirrors](terrain-grids-and-mirrors.md)), billboard builders, and the `Snowfall`, `FarField` and `BirdFlock` services. **`TerrainPass`** is the shared terrain skeleton (one effect, grid, extent, time uniform and ten per-frame parameters resolved once, one `Draw(frame, holeRadius)`).
- **The renderer dispatches and falls back only to the defaults.** It holds the frame types, the backdrop array, the services, the sun's shadow map and the ceiling glass's, the display-resolution target for the dream and cavern, `ApplyKillPlaneFade` (the hosts' physics rule, no scene's) and the scene-specific public queries (`StormFlash`, `GridSolids`, `VolcanoLightPosition`, `SavannaCampfirePosition`, `SavannaCampfireLightPosition`, `TropicalPalms`...) as one-line forwarders, because the backdrops are internal. The volcano's flows, vents and lamps are their own class (`VolcanoLava`, #795) so the Raspberry Pi's Potato path, which builds no backdrop (its `PotatoBackdrop` is ground and scatter with no lamps), solves the same lamps.

## The island's depth goes in before the environment (#1098)

A frame of the Game (`BeginSceneDraw`) and of the Testbed is: **the island's depth with no colour** (`ArenaIsland.DrawIslandDepth`), the environment's opaque half (the backdrop's first half or the city, the host's wood), the sky ([below](#the-sky-is-drawn-behind-the-opaque-scene-1104)), the environment's blended half, then the island itself where it always was. The environment's pixel programs are the dearest in the frame, the GPU turns away a pixel behind what earlier draws wrote before its program runs (the desktop's does, for these draws; how much of that a GPU does before a program that can discard is its own), and the island stands in front of the ground wherever a lens looks down on it: the ground behind it is never shaded. The map editor draws no island and calls `DrawEnvironment`, both halves at once.

- **No pixel moves.** A sample of the environment behind the island was always painted over by the island's colour, whatever it was (ground, a tree, steam, a flame, a pass drawn with the depth test off), and is now turned away unshaded, or drawn and painted over as before. A sample in front of the island is drawn as before. Where the two hold one depth (the line where the drum stands in the ground) both pass `LessEqual` and the island, drawn last, wins as it did. So it holds from any lens, and for what a backdrop blends without writing depth as well (the volcano's fountains and steam, Mars's moons, the sea and the lagoon, the storm, the desert's creatures, the Grid's light walls). Shown on the tonemapped frame, two frames of one process with the clock stood still ("Checking a scene change against captures" below).
- ⚠ **The depth is `DrawIsland` itself under a blend state that writes no colour**, one call made twice, so the two depths are one number. The shadow pass's cheaper depth draw is another vertex program, and a depth one step nearer than the colour's would cut holes in the stone.
- **`Backdrop.FollowsIslandDepth`** (`SceneRenderer.EnvironmentFollowsIslandDepth`) is what a host asks. True by default and for the two cities. The island is opaque in every scene, the sea's included: its stone's triplanar program takes no submerge fade (`MainPS`, which the drain's parts draw through, and the balls' do), so the water it stands in front of, which writes no depth, was always painted over. Space and the big top are one pass with the depth test off, which the depth does not turn away, and still measured cheaper with it laid. **False in two scenes**:

  | Scene | Why its environment is drawn first, as it always was |
  |---|---|
  | cavern | its crystals blend **and write depth**: one standing in front of the island shows the hall behind it, the part behind the island included |
  | dream | one pass with the depth test off, and laying the depth measured dearer, not cheaper: 5.59 and 5.63 ms without it, 5.64 and 5.65 pinned on with `envorder=island` (the measurement below, one pin a process) |

- ⚠ **Only on the desktop, and never into a multisampled target**, whatever the scene. `GraphicsSupport.IslandDepthFirst` is false on Android: a phone's GPU is a tiler, where the island's second draw costs its vertex work and binning again and hidden ground may already be rejected tile by tile, and no phone run has shown the pass pays there. `EnvironmentFollowsIslandDepth` takes the scene target: on Windows High and Ultra (supersampled) lay the depth, as does the Potato preset drawn by the desktop path (no samples); Medium (four samples), Low (two) and the Testbed at `ssaa=1` (unless `msaa=0`) draw the environment first as before and save nothing. Under multisampling two frames of one order are not the same picture to the bit, so "no pixel moves" cannot be shown, and the depth laid first was measured to make the island's top and outline differ by more, mostly by one step of 255.
- ⚠ **What would break a scene that answers true**: the island drawn with an alpha under one (none of its draws is), or an environment draw that lets what is behind it show through (a blend, an alpha fade) **and writes depth** where it can stand between a lens and the island. Symptom: that draw's soft edge shows the sky's colour where it showed the ground behind the island. A backdrop that gains such a draw answers false.
- **Only the island's depth, not the gun's and the cluster's**, though they hide ground too. From under the plate the ceiling's glass bends a copy of the frame taken **before** they are drawn (#541, [Glass](../rendering/glass.md)), which has to hold the environment behind them: their depth laid first would leave holes in it. And a ball can itself be blended (the clear glass kind, a transparent style, the fade over the kill plane).
- **Two halves and a gap.** `Backdrop.Draw` is the environment up to its first draw that writes no depth, `Backdrop.DrawBlended` the rest in the scene's own order (`SceneRenderer.DrawEnvironmentOpaque` and `DrawEnvironmentBlended`; `DrawEnvironment` is both). The Game and the Testbed draw their wood between the two, and the sky after it, in the gap ([below](#the-sky-is-drawn-behind-the-opaque-scene-1104)): under everything blended, over nothing that wrote depth. ⚠ Several second halves draw under the scene's baseline states without stating them (alpha blend, depth test and write, back-face culling: the sea's water, the lagoon), so whatever is drawn in the gap puts those three back first.
- **What it saves, at High and Ultra** (desktop, RX 6900 XT; Testbed Release, 3840x1500 at `ssaa=2`, the game-mode camera, `Maps/Full.json` with its 1000 balls, `sceneseed=0`, `fpscap=1000`; one scene a process, the build before against this one in the order before, after, after, before, each run the median of its 32 one-second windows, the two runs of a build averaged; every run after was cheaper than every run before; the island's second draw is inside the figures), in ms: storm 11.77 to 9.48, Mars 14.12 to 11.87, tropical 10.54 to 8.74, outback 12.07 to 10.79, forest 15.88 to 14.66, savanna 12.21 to 11.29, neon city 12.46 to 11.71, the Moon 6.84 to 6.11, sea 7.96 to 7.30, volcano 13.42 to 12.85, space 8.92 to 8.70, big top 12.84 to 12.64.
- ⚠ **The Game saves what the island covers from its own lens, which in play is little.** The Testbed's game-mode camera looks down on more of the island than the Game's play camera, which stands low behind the gun and sees the island as a strip along the bottom of the frame; the front end's orbit and a level's opening tour look down on it. Measured in the Game at Ultra (the same desktop, a 3840x1500 window, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=0`, one process a figure, before, after, after, before): a level in play in the meadow (Zigzag) 11.32 and 11.35 ms to 11.08 and 11.09, cheaper in every one-second window; the front end over Mars, whose lens orbits above the island, 13.78 and 13.34 to 13.19 and 13.14, window against window 0.46 to 0.77 ms cheaper at the median (in 23 to 31 of 39).
- **`envorder=<auto|first|island>`** (Testbed, an `alt=` dial too): `auto` asks the scene and the target as the Game does, `first` is the frame without the depth, `island` lays it in every scene and under multisampling too, whatever the answer. The `[fps]` line says `envorder island` or `envorder first` for what the window's last frame did. ⚠ **A run that isolates the arena** (`arena=` not `all`, or `capprobe=`, on the line or in any `alt=` variant) **reads `auto` as `first`** in every window: the depth is the members drawn, so `arena=all,-cap` under it would also give back the ground the cap hides and price the cap as a saving. ⚠ A per-scene cost is read **one process a figure** (the build before against this one, or one pin a process), never from an `alt=` list of scenes, which carries part of one scene's window into the next (#1129).

## The sky is drawn behind the opaque scene (#1104)

Under a dome (every scene but the seven that replace the sky, and every scene with the Game's Scenery row off) the Game's `BeginSceneDraw` and the Testbed draw the dome (`Sky.fx`: the gradient, the sun's disc, the cloud deck) and the heaps of High and Ultra over it in **the gap between the environment's two halves** (`SkyPass`), not first. `Sky.fx` is among the dearest pixel programs in the frame, and drawn first with the depth test off it shaded every pixel above the horizon that the ground, the wood, the towers and the island then painted over.

- **At the far plane.** Depth-read, with the viewport's depth range closed onto 1 (`Viewport.MinDepth` and `MaxDepth` both 1): the dome's mesh and the heaps' quad land at depth 1 whatever their own, pass the cleared depth (`LessEqual`) and fail everything opaque, so the GPU turns those pixels away before the program runs. The vertex program and the clipping are untouched, so where the sky is drawn it is the same sky to the bit. The Moon's and the aurora's own skies are drawn the same way, a quad at `z = w` ([The Moon](moon.md)).
- **The heaps are marched before the scene target is bound** (`CloudVolumes.March`, into their display-size layer) and laid over the deck with it (`Composite`): they no longer leave the scene target mid-frame. The sky's per-frame uniforms (the weather, the camera, the deck's cut) are stated before the bind for it.
- **On High and Ultra what the gap brings in is the sky's own target**, the dome shaded once a display pixel before the scene target is bound, read one texel a sample, and the heaps laid over it as below them ("The sky at display resolution on High and Ultra" in [The weather](../rendering/weather.md)). Below them the dome is drawn there itself.
- **No pixel moves.** A pixel is sky or something opaque that wrote its depth. Before, the sky was painted first and the opaque thing over it; now the opaque thing is painted over the clear colour and the sky is turned away there. Where nothing opaque stands the sky passes and is painted as before. What the environment blends over the sky without writing depth (the sea and the lagoon, the storm, Mars's moons, the volcano's fountains, plume and steam, the desert's creatures) is its second half, drawn after it. The island, the gun, the cluster and the glass are drawn after the sky as they always were. Every scene's first half, the two cities, the forest's wood and fireflies and the aurora's wood are opaque and write depth (the volcano's flank and the wood draw under the alpha blend with an alpha of 1).
- ⚠ **What would break it**: a draw before the gap that lets what is behind it show through (a blend, an alpha fade, a colour pass that leaves a pixel its depth pass wrote) or that writes no depth where the sky is behind it. Symptom: that draw's soft edge shows the clear colour where it showed the sky, or the draw vanishes in front of the sky. Such a draw belongs in the backdrop's second half.
- ⚠ **`SkyPass` states the blend, the depth and the culling it draws the dome with**, in the Game and the Testbed alike. The Testbed did not state the blend before #1104 and drew its dome under whatever the sun's shadow pass left: its caster blend, `BlendFunction.Min` (`SceneRenderer.ShadowCasterBlend`), so in a shadowed scene each channel of the sky was the lesser of the sky's and the clear colour's (the meadow's dome 1 a teal with the horizon's blue in place of the zenith's: 555,283 pixels of a 1600x900 frame at most 64 steps apart; with `shadow=0` the two builds' skies are one picture). The Game always stated the opaque blend. So a Testbed picture or figure of a shadowed scene under a dome from before #1104 is not the Game's sky, and its sky was a read-modify-write blend.
- ⚠ **Not later than the gap**: from under the plate the ceiling's glass bends a copy of the frame taken before the gun and the cluster are drawn (#541, [Glass](../rendering/glass.md)), and it has to hold the sky.
- ⚠ **Only on the desktop, and never into a multisampled target** (`SkyPass.BehindScene`, which takes the target): a phone (`GraphicsSupport.SkyBehindScene`) and Medium and Low on Windows (four and two samples) draw the dome first, as before. A phone until a run there shows the order pays on a tiler. Under multisampling the two orders are not one picture: the late order moved 137 to 10,904 pixels of a 1600x900 frame (at most 15 to 51 steps of 255) along the silhouettes the environment holds against the sky (the forest's trees, the mountains' ridges, the city's towers, the volcano's flank, the meadow's trees; the sea, with none, 0 to 36), where two frames of one order differ in 55 to 193 (`ssaa=1`, the Testbed's eight samples, `skyorder=late` pinned, the play, low and upward cameras).
- **`skyorder=<auto|late|first>`** (Testbed, an `alt=` dial too): `auto`, the default, asks `SkyPass.BehindScene` as the Game does; `late` pins the sky behind the opaque scene (under multisampling too), `first` over the clear, depth off, the frame before #1104. The `[fps]` line says `skyorder late` or `skyorder first` for what the window's last frame did. `alt=skyorder=first;skyorder=late` with the clock stood still is how the two are shown to be one picture ([below](#checking-a-scene-change-against-captures)).

## Traps, for whoever takes the next scene

- ⚠ **A reduced technique is picked only when `SceneDetail` changes (`OnDetailChanged`), not at load**, and the renderer starts at detail 1: until a host lowers it, the dream draws its effect's *default* (first-declared) technique and the cavern its full program. Scenes that assign a technique every frame (Mars, meadow, forest, savanna) are handed the starting tier and select at load.
- ⚠ **`SupersampleFactor` and `TerrainHoleRadius` are written by the host after construction**, so they live in `BackdropServices`; a backdrop reads them at draw time and **never copies them in its constructor** (every scene would draw at factor 1).
- ⚠ **A backdrop takes its grid from the cache and never disposes it**, and frees only what it built. An effect loaded from the content manager is the manager's; a *clone* (the mountain's and aurora's `Snow.fx`, the sea's and lagoon's `Sea.fx`) is its owner's and is disposed. Clones share compiled shaders and let a scene push its config once instead of re-pushing a shared effect every frame.
- ⚠ **A service the renderer builds mid-constructor must exist before the first backdrop using it**: `Snowfall` and `Birds` are set where the snow and flock stood, so an earlier scene can use them only at draw time. The flock is sized to the largest of the four scenes that draw it (a desert flock was once silently capped to the savanna's count), so those scenes are built before it.
- **Shared helpers are `internal static` on the renderer, not copies** (`SafeNormal`, `AtBearing`, `DirectionFromElevationAzimuth`, `Hash01`). The far ring's tier is `FarField.SceneDetail`, written only by the renderer's `SceneDetail` setter.
- **The volcano does not draw through `TerrainPass`** (it leaves blend state as found where the pass states opaque and restores alpha-blend). The sea sets depth-read around its pass call and puts `Default` back.
- ⚠ **A shadow caster must set its own map matrix**: the beach's palm caster never set `ShadowViewProjection` on `Palm.fx` and drew with the previous frame's, invisible on a still camera and a frame of lag on a moving one.

## Checking a scene change against captures

- ⚠ **Pixel identity cannot be judged frame against frame**: the Testbed runs a variable time step, so frame *n* of two runs of one build lands at different wall-clock seconds and every animated scene differs (the sea in 117,532 pixels). Judge a new-build capture set against the spread of two old-build sets, re-run a flagged scene on both builds, and for the tightest test compare against the previous commit's build.
- **Two frames of one process are one picture once the scene's clock stands still.** `clock=67108864` (2^26, where a float steps by 8 s, so every frame of a short run reads one clock; the Grid's boards step on the clock, so there `clock=8388608` and both frames inside one second) with `sceneseed=0 nopost nooverc at=0:F5,0:F12` and a `shot=` either side of an `alt=` switch: at `ssaa=2` the pair is identical to the pixel, cluster and gun in frame (`alt=envorder=first;envorder=auto` is how [the island's depth](#the-islands-depth-goes-in-before-the-environment-1098) is checked, `alt=skyorder=first;skyorder=late` [the sky's order](#the-sky-is-drawn-behind-the-opaque-scene-1104)). The pair compares the tonemapped 8-bit frame, so a difference under one step of 255 in the HDR target does not show. ⚠ Not the cavern at `ssaa=2`: its hall goes through a four-sample target of its own, which differs from itself by a few hundred pixels a pair; drawn directly without multisampling (`ssaa=1 msaa=0`) its pairs are identical too. ⚠ Not under multisampling at all. ⚠ Not between two **runs**: the gun's magazine is rolled per launch and the cluster has swayed before F5 lands, so two runs of one build differ in thousands of pixels of the cluster, its shadow and the magazine from the play and the low cameras, and the Grid in a hundred thousand and more (its boards and riders step on the wall clock). What repeats across runs and builds is the Game's `capture=` ("Capture: filming the game" in `docs/game-session/controls-and-testing.md`).
- **The rig:** `sceneseed=0 nopost nooverc weather=clear`, `campos=0,6,60 camtarget=0,-8,0`, `at=0:F5,0:F12`, `shotframe=150`, 1280x720 minimized without focus; all scenes at `ssaa=2`, the sky-replacing ones again at `ssaa=1`, a few at `detail=0`. Extra noise: the neon city's windows switch on a clock; the sea and beach differ by 30-45k pixels between runs of one build. The Game needs `sceneseed=0` too (`BS3D.exe userdata=<scratch> windowed scene=<name> shot=8`), since it rolls a seed each launch ([Shared effects](shared-effects.md)).

# The cavern

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The eleventh `SceneKind` and the third sky-replacing pass ([The sky-replacing family](space.md#the-sky-replacing-family), the shared quad): a bioluminescent crystal cavern over an underground river. Noise-carved rock veined with glowing minerals, god rays falling from unseen gaps in the ceiling, crystal clusters pulsing cyan and magenta on the walls, spores drifting up through the dark, glowworms and stalactites overhead, and a river that carries the clusters' light on its ripple.

`Cavern.fx` + `CavernBackdrop` + `CavernSceneConfig` (named groups `Rock`, `Water`, `Air`, `Crystals`, `Lighting`); techniques `Cavern`, `CavernReduced` and `CavernDepth`; `scene=cavern` on either command line, the Game's scene menu and random pick. The river sits at `LevelY` −34, **below the drain's kill plane**, so a ball that falls through the funnel is culled before it reaches water it can never splash. `OpenBelow` includes it; it is not an `IsSolidTerrainScene`, so no pit shaft. Cost, the display-resolution path and the reduced program: [Shaded at the display's resolution](#shaded-at-the-displays-resolution-155).

The scene is **analytic except where marching is gated**. Each layer has a trick worth keeping.

## The shell

- **The cave is a quadratic and a plane** (an infinite cylinder and a ceiling, hit analytically) shaded as **lit, folded stone**: a 3D fBm's gradient perturbs the shell's normal, and every light works against that perturbed normal: a cool key from the ceiling gaps, and **each crystal as a real point light** (N·L over inverse square), so the rock around a magenta cluster is lit from the cluster's side. A wall whose colour varies while its light does not reads as painted plaster. A ridged crack network presses darkness into crevices; rock within a few units of the river is wet (darker, a waterline stain); exponential distance fog sinks the far side into abyssal blue-purple.
- **The mineral veins are `pow`-sharpened ridge lines of a 3D world-space field with a patch mask.** ⚠ They were the scene's one 2D-parameterised field (`atan2` of the bearing) and it paid twice: the branch cut stood as a full-height seam down the −X wall (mirrored by the river), and on the ceiling the mapping degenerated into radial spokes round a singularity. A 3D field has no seam and no pole, and the mask keeps the ore *threading* (following some fractures, leaving others bare) where the unmasked web wallpapered the cave with even neon.
- **The wall-ceiling seam is coved**: the base normal blends over the top twenty-odd units, because rock coves, it does not mitre. A hard select left a sharp lit line.
- **No per-scene colour grade**: the scene renders linear radiance and tonemaps once (`docs/rendering.md`), so the grade lives in the palette and the light rig (dim and cool, the river's teal in the ground bounce, a faint magenta fill from the walls). The one in-pass touch is a subtle vignette on the backdrop alone.

## The river

- **A flat plane with a ripple in its normal**, hit exactly with no search (#250): a 26 wu swell and two octaves of noise at the 8.8 and 3.1 wu scales, value and slope analytic in one pass (`RiverRipple`), read by the normal, the caustic warp and the crest glow. The amplitude dies between 70 and 170 units of horizontal distance, so the far river is flat and cannot shimmer. `WaveAmplitude` joins `WaveScale`/`WaveSpeed` in the `Water` group.
  - ⚠ **Three sines drew a lattice** (#674): three plane waves of near-equal slope cross into a grid, and the crest glow, `pow(crest, 5)`, lit only where all three peak, drew it as bright patches (`cavern-out` 11 % of probe tiles). One sine alone is stripes and stays; the other two are gradient noise stretched along their crests. After: 0 % (the grazing view 14 → 2 %); +0.09 ms at 3840×1600, `ssaa 2`.
  - ⚠ The gradient is taken at **full strength**: the first flat-plane era read as a "blue patterned floor" because its fake normal was damped to 0.18, not because it was flat.
- **The mirror is a ramp, not the cave.** The reflected ray used to be handed back to the wall-shading function, and that second `ShadeWall` per water pixel was the dearest thing in the pass. The stand-in is `lerp(FogColor, RockColor, saturate(bounced.y·2))` plus `RockColor · CrystalLightAt(hit) · 12` (the clusters are the brightest things in the mirror), read once and spent twice (mirror and depths). The steam lerp after it closes most of the rest of the gap.
  - Fresnel's floor is **high for water** (0.25): a cave river is a black mirror before it is a window. The downward fold of a reflected ray is a **soft max**; ⚠ `abs()` folded the image back on itself along a crease that slid with the water.
  - The crystals glint on the ripple (a Blinn lobe per cluster, inverse square, riding the pulse). What the glints lost with the march is the flank: they come out as smooth diagonal streaks rather than broken sparkle. Photographed and accepted.
- **The caustics are a cellular edge field** (`VoronoiEdge2`, `F2 − F1`, zero exactly on cell borders): a bright web running around dark cells, finer than the ripple.
  - ⚠ A wave-interference field must be **normalised before shaping** (`saturate(0.5 + 0.5·h)` over an amplitude of 1.95 clipped into a cyan checkerboard), and even normalised a sine field's crests are blobs, not a net.
  - ⚠ **On a plane the web read as flooring, and the strength dial was not the answer.** A marched surface squeezes and stretches the web for free; on a flat plane it is a coarse regular lattice. The lookup is **warped by the ripple's own gradient** (`CAUSTIC_WARP`, world units per unit of gradient: half a cell at rest, a whole cell on a flank), free because the gradient is already in registers.
- **The waterline's defences**: **steam over the river** (`MistColor`/`MistDensity`/`MistHeight` in `Water`), an exponential height layer integrated analytically along the view ray (height fog, because a slab has a top and the top is a new hard line); a **shore band** (the last few units of water before the wall show pure mirror, so the depths and the crest glow are gone *before* the contact circle); and the rock's wet stain above the contact. The mist's fBm wisp is keyed to the **ray direction, not the hit point**: ⚠ `bestT` jumps across every crystal silhouette, and a hit-keyed wisp printed a ghostly crystal-shaped cutout into the fog. The mirror gets the same mist integral along the reflected path; spores are added after it.
  - Known limit: at `campos=228,-14,0` (looking down at a near shoreline) the shore band comes out a **pale strip** (the ramp's `RockColor·(1 + 12·pool)` against the wall's wetter value). Only the free camera can stand there (the Game's cameras sit within tens of units of the origin, where fog and steam own the contact); the lever, if ever needed, is the ramp's top end under `shore`.

## The crystals, rays and spores

- **The crystals are a gated march**: per-cluster bounding spheres and a short interval trace; each cluster is three interpenetrating octahedra at different elongations and yaws, standing **on** the wall (0.965 of the radius; at 0.86 they floated and read as balloons). They are shaded off the **SDF's own normal** (four tetrahedral taps): flat facets jump at the arris, and the facet *contrast* is what says "crystal" (a smooth radial term made rounded gems). SDF ambient occlusion (four probes) keeps the clefts deeper than the faces.
- **The clusters light the cave**: an inverse-square sum of all eight pools their colour onto the rock and water, eight multiply-adds in the wall shade, and what makes the crystals belong to the cave.
- **God rays and spores are closest-approach glows** (no geometry, no march): vertical-line distance for shafts (height-enveloped with a `smoothstep`, C1 the whole way: a clamped ramp left a Mach shelf), and the dream's spark gaussians slowed for spores. Both are clamped to the **final** solid, crystals included, and the shafts are added **after** the crystal branch (before it, every beam crossing a cluster was punched out along the crystal's silhouette). A spore's climb wraps on a continuous envelope (born dark at the water, dimmed out under the ceiling); the raw `fmod` teleported each mote at full brightness, a hard pop in a scene whose point is stillness.

## Overhead: glowworms, stalactites, columns

- **Glowworms (#507)** are what a living cave reads as from across the chamber: a constellation of tiny blue-green points on the ceiling, dense in places, bare in others. One hash a pixel on a single-cell lattice **behind a data branch on the cove** (only ceiling pixels pay), in patches off the rock's own body field, breathing out of step. **A point stays about a pixel and a half wide at any distance** (the snow sparkle's rule), because a world-sized dot at the far end of a 240-unit cave is sub-pixel and a field of those crawls.
  - ⚠ **The cove made the cave an ellipse (#528).** The worms were cut off at a horizontal ring round the cylinder, so the constellation's edge was a perfect ellipse. Displacing the vault itself photographed *identical* from below (the ceiling is unlit, so a sagging slab changes only depth); the shape the eye reads is the **outline**. The worms' reach down the wall is decided per bearing by a low, slow 3D field (`reachField`, two octaves at 120 and 60 units): tongues down the wall, bays into the ceiling, a dozen round the chamber. The lattice is 3D cubes (`NoiseHash33`), since an XZ lattice on a vertical wall degenerates into streaks. +0.22 ms at 3840×1600 (5.25 → 5.47).
  - ⚠ **A worm standing in the middle half of its cell was a lattice** (#674, the star field's fault in 3D). Each worm now stands anywhere in its cell and the eight cells that overlap half a cell round the pixel are read, keeping worms within half a cell of the surface along its normal (a slab one cell thick, so the density per area is `chance / cell²` whichever way the surface faces; a quarter-cell slab came out at half density), combined by `max`. +0.8 ms (+4 %) on the laptop at 1600×900. The lattice probe does **not** confirm it (its flagged tile is the river at a grazing horizon); the picture does.
- **Stalactites (#676)**: 12 groups of 5 hanging from the ceiling, each Inigo Quilez's round cone upside down (`HangingCone`, an exact SDF) knobbed by drip rings and lumps (⚠ a smooth cone read as an icicle or a cut-out tooth), lengths 16–58, girths 3–6.5, standing 0.3–0.75 of the cave radius out (clear of the air over the arena). **Traced the crystals' way** (only where the ray crosses a group's bounding sphere, 44, up to the solid already found, before the crystals so a crystal in front wins) and shaded as the ceiling's rock (`ShadeFormation`: dark root, paler calcite tips up to 3.4×, the crystals as point lights, the river's glow on the underside, a wet rim). Fogged like the wall, so at 100–200 units most are dim silhouettes against the glowworms. Cone rolls come off low-discrepancy sequences rather than hashes (the SDF runs at every step and for the normal's four taps).
- **Columns (#676, second step)**: six, a stalactite grown down to the stalagmite under it, the one formation spanning water to ceiling. They stand at 0.46–0.7 of the cave radius on the golden angle, clear of the air over the arena (from the gun's starting bearing none stands behind the cluster; the nearest is 25° off). `ColumnSdf` is a solid of revolution flared into ceiling and river and waisted between (0.42 of its girth, which runs 9–15), swelling on two frequencies with no common ratio and **fluted** with flowstone curtains (cos 5θ off the direction's cosine, no `atan2`). ⚠ One ring frequency read as a corkscrew. Traced after the stalactites and before the crystals: an infinite vertical cylinder per axis as the bound, 24 steps at 0.7 of the distance.
- **The Low tier goes without both** (the reduced program's `fullDetail` gates the loops; shell, crystals, river and worms stay). Costs, the Game at `level=Chest`, 1920×1080, `nocap`, two alternating runs against `main`: stalactites High 2.67 → 2.85 ms (+0.18), Low unchanged (1.52) (a first cut of 16 groups of 6, two hashes a cone and 32 steps cost +0.36 / +0.40); columns High 2.851 → 2.894 (+0.04), Low unchanged.

### Formation depth (#755)

The scene pass is a background and writes no depth (and under supersampling shades a target of its own), so every stalactite and column stood at infinite depth and the arena drew over it: the chapter intro's first shot (`Chamber`, lens 168 units off the axis among the columns) showed the island through a column in front of it. `Backdrop.DrawDepth` runs after the backdrop into the scene target, and the cavern's is the `CavernDepth` technique: the very marches the scene pass shades (`FormationsHit`, `ColumnsHit`, shared, so the depth written is the surface drawn), no further than the river, writing the hit's projected depth and discarding where the ray meets neither, colour writes off.

- It runs only with the full program and only for a lens outside `0.3 × CaveRadius − 30` of the axis (42 at the shipped radius: a group's cones stand up to 15 off its anchor per axis, 21.2 radially, and the nearest real group is 63.7 off the axis), so the play camera and the menu orbit never pay.
- It is **scissored to the arena's projected box** (the island's radius + 4, from under the funnel to y 40). Unscissored it cost +1.37 ms at the intro's first camera (`campos=168,45,0 camtarget=0,-16,0`, 1600×900 ×2: 1.47 → 2.83); scissored, 1.47 → 1.52.

## The look (#507)

Dark brown-grey stone with the cool light *on* it: `RockColor` is a dark warm grey (blue-grey under a flat fill read as plaster), the fill is 0.30 against 1.15 of key. Veins are thin and few (`pow` 9 over a sharper ridge and a raised patch threshold; broad soft bands read as cloud on the wall), with `VeinColor` brightened to compensate. The caustic web is 0.28 and the crystals bright (`Emission` 2.3, `WallLight` 0.8): in the references the water is a dark mirror carrying the lights' reflections, and a bright river under a dim cave is the "patterned floor" failure this scene has had twice. Left alone on purpose: the shell, the ripple, the mirror ramp, the god rays (all cost-driven) and the spore count.

## Shaded at the display's resolution (#155)

**The cavern and the dream are shaded into a target the size of the back buffer and scaled up into the supersampled scene target** (`SceneRenderer.DrawBackdropAtDisplayResolution`, a backdrop opts in with `DrawsAtDisplayResolution`), which is a quarter of the pixels at `ssaa 2`.

- **Why:** both passes are occupancy-bound. At a fixed camera at 1600×900 the cave cost 1.5 ms at `ssaa 1`, 5.2 at 2 and 21.0 at 4: 4.04× for 4× the pixels, with essentially no fixed cost. A cheaper technique could never reach `High`'s bar of 75 FPS on the reference 6900 XT (a 0.715× cut lands 20.8 ms at 14.9), but the pixel count could. At fullscreen 3840×1600 `High` the cave went from 20.8 ms to roughly a quarter of that, and the dream went from 18.6 ms (53.7 FPS) to 5.8 ms (172.5 FPS).
- **What it gives up is the backdrop's supersampling, not its resolution.** The scene still comes out at every pixel the display has, and the balls, the arena and the gun keep every sample they had.
- ⚠ **Measure such a backdrop by the back buffer's size, never with `ssaa`**: `ssaa` no longer reaches it, so an `ssaa` sweep says it costs nothing.
- ⚠ **Space must not take this path**: it sizes its stars in output pixels off `SceneRenderer.SupersampleFactor`, so shading it at display resolution would change how big and bright they come out.

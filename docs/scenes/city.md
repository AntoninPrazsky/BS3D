# The city

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The default of the twenty-one settings: the round stone island ([The island](island.md)) in a clearing between the towers of a procedural city, `Prazsky.Core.Render.City`, shared with the map editor. The neon city is the same layout relit. Both are **host-owned**: no `Backdrop` class ([Backdrops and the registry](backdrop-architecture.md)); each executable draws them through an `InstancedModelRenderer` it sky-lights like the balls (`CityWindowBrightness > 0` selects the city technique, `CityNeon` the neon one).

## Layout and drawing

- **The city stands over the arena** and does not fall away under it: no camera looks down the funnel's throat far enough to see a shaft there (#275). ⚠ `CitySceneConfig.RooflineY` sits well above the play surface; with the roofline below the floor no tower was seen against the sky and the roofs read as holes in a white plain (it was reported as inverted normals).
- **No assets.** One 1x1x1 `BoxMesh` under a different instance matrix per building, one instanced draw fed a frustum-culled near-to-far array ("Drawing the city near to far" in `docs/rendering.md`). Non-uniform scale is safe because the boxes are axis-aligned: `normalize` in the pixel shader recovers the normal without an inverse transpose.
- **Windows are laid out relative to each building**, not on a world grid (which jammed windows into corners): `CityVS` hands the pixel shader the offset from the building's centre and its world size; `CityPS` keeps `WindowMargin` clear at every edge and solves the floor and column count from the size against `WindowPitchX/Y`, nudging the pitch so a whole number fits. `buildingId` comes from the tower's **anchor**, the plot's centre, which every box of the tower carries in its instance's `Style.zw` (#1066): a wing or a leaning tier stands off the centre and would otherwise be a building of another tone. Coverage is band-limited so a far tower is a dim block, not a moire.
- **Specular ambient is not multiplied by albedo**, so at grazing angles (nearly every facade) it washes a skyline in sky colour; the city runs the term at 0.07.

## Window rhythm (#619)

Windows keep their own rhythm off `CityWindowTime` (wall clock: a city's lamps do not stop when the simulation pauses). **Rarely, and only some**: a play view holds thousands of windows and one window's calm rhythm made about 71 change per second ("like a disco"). Now only `WindowRestlessFraction` of windows ever change, they hold for `WindowHoldSeconds` plus up to `WindowHoldVariation`, and the switch lasts `WindowSwitchSeconds` (about 2 changes a second in view). The switch is a **duration**, not a fraction of the interval (a fraction would have stretched into a 20-second crawl); the shader fades over the last `WindowSwitchSeconds / interval` of a slot. The neon buzz runs on `NeonConfig.WindowBuzzFraction` of windows. ⚠ The day city's `WindowBrightness` stays under the glare threshold: a window that glares veils the tower behind it, and a thousand turn the skyline into white haze (the neon city's is deliberately over it).

## Towers: setbacks, facades, cornices (#678)

The audit found every tower was one box. Each step is rolled from data the layout does not share, so **block layout, gaps and heights are the same city as before**.

- **Setbacks.** A tower over `SETBACK_MIN_HEIGHT` rises in two or three tiers (chance `SETBACK_CHANCE`), each 0.66-0.82 of the one below, all reaching the same top; tiers are rolled from the plot's own seed. Each tier is its own box laying out its own windows, and shares the tower's hue and pattern through its anchor. A stack may lean (below).
- **Facade kinds**, rolled once per tower and carried to `CityPS` in `Style.x`: classic punched windows (half), glass curtain wall (18 %), ribbon (16 %), piers (16 %). All of a tower's faces, tiers and cornice share the roll; frames, sills, lit share and distance fade read the kind's `fill` and `pitch`.
- **Cornices** crown every tier of every classic-windowed tower: a slab `CORNICE_OUT` wider, `CORNICE_HEIGHT` tall, its top `CORNICE_OVER_ROOF` over the roof. It is solid over the whole roof, so it raises the roof and rooftop equipment stands on it (`City.RoofRise`). The profile is **painted** (`Style.y` marks the slab; `CityPS` gives a lit crown, pale fascia and dark cove), because the slab faces the wall's way in the wall's stone and vanished into the blank band under the roof.
  - ⚠ **Cornices come after the towers in `City.Buildings`; `City.TowerCount` says where the towers end.** Anything standing on a roof or reading a roofline (`CityRooftops`, the intro's rooftop run) walks the towers alone, or a cornice would be dressed as a roof and carry a second set of dishes.
- ⚠ **Integer inputs use an integer mixer, never `Hash21`** (#674): on integer input it repeats every 50 cells in x and 100 in y, six copies across the city. The facade kind is `City.FacadeRoll` (a murmur finaliser, CPU only: a copy on each side of the CPU/GPU line stays right only while both are kept in step); the other rolls use `BuildingRoll` in `City.fxh` (one salt each) and each window one integer seed (`WindowSeed`).
- **Cost** (the Game, 1920x1080, `nocap`, `level=Bolt`/`Cabinet`): each step at most about +0.1 ms at High; cornices at Low on Cabinet +0.12 ms (1,432 more boxes through the cull and sort).

## Tower plans (#1066)

The owner: every tower was squarish. A plot's footprint was one rectangle (sides within 1.33 : 1), and every setback shrank about the same centre. Each tower now rolls a **plan** off the plot's own seed salted with `PLAN_SALT`. The layout's generator and the tiers' stream are drawn exactly as before, so every block, gap and height is the same city. The plans:

- a plain box (`PLAN_BOX`, a third of the towers);
- a **slab**: one side narrowed to 0.48-0.62 of itself, and slid along it;
- an **L**: a main block across one side of the footprint and a lower wing down the other. The wing stands `WING_INSET` back from the main block's wall and stays at least a floor (`WING_BELOW`) under the main block's first roof: level with it, the two roofs and their cornices lay in one plane over the strip where the wing reaches in;
- a tower on a **podium**: a base over the whole footprint, 10-20 % of the height, with the tower (0.5-0.72 of the footprint) standing off-centre on it;
- **twin** towers on one podium, on footprints at least `TWIN_MIN_SIDE` long. One reaches the top, the other 0.75-0.95 of the way.

A setback stack may also **lean** (`LEAN_SHARE`): each tier slides towards a side or a corner by what its step leaves over `TIER_STEP_MIN`, so the setbacks climb one face. ⚠ A tier narrower than `TIER_MIN_SIDE` is not raised, and the one under it goes to the top instead: a slab's or a podium's tower shrinks twice more as it steps back, and on a small plot it ended 1.4 wide, a needle whose roof the dressing split into cells of negative size.

- ⚠ **Every box stays inside the plot's old footprint and no higher than its old top**, and the tallest box reaches it. The chapter intros' flights, the menu orbit, `OpenRadius` and the streets were laid among the old boxes, so they stay clear by construction. `CityPlanTests` holds this, comparing the plots with a city built without plans (`variedPlans: false`), and pins that city against the one the code before the plans built (a hash of every box), which is what says the generators are drawn as they were.
- ⚠ **No two boxes of one tower share a wall.** Each box lays out its own window grid, so two flush walls would meet in a doubled blank margin with their columns out of line. That is why the wing stands back from the main block and a leaning tier keeps `TIER_STEP_MIN`. The test checks every pair of boxes that stand side by side.
- ⚠ **The anchor rides in `Style.zw`, and the Potato path keeps it.** Its facade program (`PotatoModel.fx`) used to hand the custom vector to its lighting as the occluder, so `PotatoBackdrop` overwrote the city's vectors with the open one (`Opened`), and every box rolled its lamp and tile off its own centre. The program now hands its lighting the open vector `(0, 0, 0, 1)` itself, as `CityPS` does, and the city keeps its vectors. ⚠ A zero vector is not "no occlusion" there: its `w` of 0 reads as buried and darkens every wall.
- Every box is a roof: `CityRooftops` dresses a wing's or a podium's roof as its own, and the rooftop shots pick among the new roofs. `CityRooftopsTests`' layout fingerprint pins the dressing of these roofs.
- Boxes, cornices included (`sceneseed=1066`): the day city 4,447 → 5,612, the neon city 5,884 → 6,832.
- **Cost** (the Game, 1920x1080, `nocap`, `sceneseed=1066`, three rounds each way): Bolt at High 4.45 → 4.41 ms; Cabinet at High 4.41 → 4.26 ms, cheaper, because the slimmer plans leave more sky in view and `CityPS` pays by the pixel; Cabinet at Low 1.93 → 2.01 ms, the extra boxes through the cull, the sort and the shadow pass. **On the reference APU at `Low` nothing measurable** (the Game, 1600x900, `sceneseed=0`, the build before the plans against the one with the review's fixes, `history-sweep.ps1 -ByLevel`, the minimum of each build's runs): Girandole 12.36 → 12.24 ms and Turbine 10.94 → 10.86, each build's two runs within 0.05; Ghost 14.34 → 13.91 over four runs a build, which spread 2-3.5 ms there, so no sign either way.

## The far skyline (#774)

The owner: through the streets the end of the rendering was visible. `City.FarSkyline` stands two rows of slabs a side round the grid, `SKYLINE_GAP` block pitches past its last block: the first row cut into slabs 0.6-1.9 pitches wide with gaps of 1.5-4.5 units, the second a street behind it, shoulder to shoulder, so a sight line through a gap ends on a wall. Heights are shares of the grid's own roofline at its last block (`SKYLINE_MIN_SHARE`-`SKYLINE_MAX_SHARE`, the back row `SKYLINE_BACK_RISE` higher, a tenth of them 1.2x): the skyline continues the city's own and does not stand over the horizon as a wall. Its own seed (`SKYLINE_SEED`), so the grid is untouched.

- **Drawn through `InstancedCityFar`** (`City.fxh`'s `CityFarPS`, `InstancedModelRenderer.CityFar`): the window grid of the tower's kind, its lit share and lamps, the tone per building and the neon's colours and sign bands, and none of the frames, sills, bars, grain or cornice mouldings, which are under a pixel from across the grid. Every window is a settled one. ⚠ Not as `City.Buildings` through `CityPS`: that program is occupancy-bound, and there the slabs cost the chapter intro's street swing (Game, `play level=111`, High, 3840x1581) up to 0.45 ms over main, where the sky had been; through the far program the swing reads as main's within 0.1 ms and play is unchanged.
- **`CityRooftops` draws it**, last, with the city's scene parameters and specular ambient: every host already draws the rooftops after the city in the city's state and enrols them for the sky light, so no executable carries a line for it.
- ⚠ **Not among `City.Buildings`**, so it casts nothing into the sun's map, the culled near-to-far array never sorts it, and nothing that walks the towers (`CityRooftops`' dressing, `CitySwing`, the intro's rooftop run) can mistake a slab for a tower. The Potato path draws none of it: it keeps only the towers within `CITY_REACH` of the arena.

## Rooftop equipment (#436)

`CityRooftops`, meshes in `RooftopMesh`, knobs in `CitySceneConfig.Rooftops`: lattice masts (red and white, aircraft beacon), dishes on posts, 5G poles of three sector panels, air-conditioning units; one instanced draw per mesh, frustum-culled, drawn after the city in its opaque state.

- **Placement follows the skyline** (the play camera looks up, so a roof is a silhouette): a tower above the generator's own roofline takes a mast at 17 %, a lower one 2.5 %. Dishes and 5G poles stop at `EquipmentDistance`, air conditioning at `ClutterDistance` (only a camera looking down sees it). A roof is split into cells so no two pieces overlap; every dish faces the same stretch of sky (+-0.35 rad), like real ones.
- **Placement is a function of the tower**, seeded from the roof's centre, so a rebuild at another radius (a quality step) keeps the equipment. ⚠ The seed is an integer mix, **not `HashCode.Combine`**, which .NET seeds per process (7,880 pieces in one launch, 7,800 in the next).
- The neon city rolls each roof a second time after the day's dressing, at `NeonExtra` minus one times the chances, with a neon ring on every dish (`NeonRingBrightness`).
- **The beacons flash in eight clusters, not together** (#902: "such synchronisation across a whole city is improbable"). `CityRooftops` keeps eight beacon kinds over the one sphere mesh (`BEACON_CLUSTERS`), each its own `InstancedModelRenderer` and so its own `EmissiveTint`, read at that renderer's `Draw`: no shader channel, only seven more instanced draws. A cluster is a short bright flash over the first 0.22 of its own period, on the wall clock, over the glare threshold so each blooms; its period is `BeaconPeriod` times 0.75 to 1.02 (1.2 to 1.63 s), its phase a golden-ratio share of that period, and its red is `(1, g, 0.03)` with `g` from 0.03 (a deep red) to 0.16 (an orange-red), all in the tables beside `BeaconFlash`. Cluster 0 is the one beacon the city had before: 1.6 s, phase 0, `g` 0.08.
  - **A mast's cluster is a hash of its foot's `BEACON_CELL` (60 units, two of the day city's blocks) and the dressing seed** (`BeaconCluster`, an integer mix like `RoofSeed`), so the towers of a neighbourhood share a rhythm. ⚠ **Never from the roof's random stream**: the mast is the first roll a roof takes, and one more `Next` there shifts every later roll on every roof and dresses the whole city anew. `CityRooftopsTests` pins the layout's fingerprint from before the split (29,056 and 38,003 pieces over four rolls of each city; a cluster drawn from the stream gave 28,972) and holds that every beacon is in the cluster its foot hashes to, that every mast has exactly one, and that each cluster carries between 6 and 25 % of a city's.
  - ⚠ **The periods stop at 1.02 times `BeaconPeriod`, not +25 %**, because the chapter intro's rooftop shot (five seconds) is promised at least three flashes wherever the clock stands (`chapter-intro-prologues.md`), true of a period up to 5/3 s; the test counts the peaks in every five-second window. Lengthen `BeaconPeriod` past 1.6 and that line must change.
  - ⚠ **The extras share the roof's cell grid with the day's dressing** and take only the cells it left (#850): on a grid of their own about one neon-city piece in seven stood inside another, a second, shorter mast poking out of the day's mast (both take the middle cell) and masts rising through dishes, 5G poles and air-conditioning units. So a roof carries one mast at most, an extra mast stands only where the day's left the middle cell empty, and the neon city carries about 2.46 pieces per roof, each in a cell of its own. `CityRooftopsTests` holds that no two pieces share a cell, over the real layout (`CityRooftops.ArrangePieces`, no device).
  - **So `NeonExtra` = 1.45 gives the neon city about 1.25 times the day's masts, not 1.45** (twelve rolls: 3,826 masts against the day's 3,057). The shared grid took 10 % of the neon skyline's masts (from 4,241) and 11 % of its 5G poles within `EquipmentDistance` (11,231 to 9,987), the extras that had stood in a taken cell; the dishes retry another edge and held (18,110 to 18,129). The play camera does not show the loss: looking up at the roofline in five directions and in the Game's play view (`sceneseed=3`), the frames before and after differ in single dishes and poles and in no mast. `NeonExtra` = 1.70 would give the masts back (4,202) and the pieces per roof (2.75), at 15 % more dishes.
- **Cost at most 0.06 ms, the eight beacon clusters included** (seven more instanced draws than one beacon renderer: the frame time reads the same to the 0.01 ms the `[fps]` line resolves, Release, `sceneseed=0`, `fov=40`, `campos=0,60,30 camtarget=70.5,53,100.5`, five paired rounds each: neon 3.50 against 3.50 ms and day 2.84 against 2.84 at 3840x1600, neon 1.38 against 1.38 and day 1.04 against 1.04 at 640x360 where the frame is CPU-bound); the pinned layout (`sceneseed=0`) is 10,102 instances in the day city and 13,008 in the neon one (the Testbed's `[city]` line: a mast is three, its two colours and its beacon, a dish two with its ring, and both cities build the neon extras). Not done: guy wires and the second diagonal of a lattice (sub-pixel shimmer at a mast's distance).

## The street under the towers (#399)

`CityStreets`, `CityStreets.fx`, `CitySceneConfig.Streets`. Towers ran down to -420 and ended over nothing; **`CitySceneConfig.BaseY` is -100**, the street level. ⚠ Moving it reshuffles which windows are lit (rows run from the base); the neon hue is keyed off the footprint and does not move.

- **One flat quad drawn last of the city**, after towers and roofs, so any street a tower stands in front of fails the depth test before the shader runs; it fades to its average, then the horizon colour, past the last block.
- **The pattern is the city's own grid**, evaluated per pixel from `City.BlockPitch` and `City.StreetWidth` (a snapshot taken at build, so a changed config cannot draw streets through towers not rebuilt): paved squares, `SidewalkWidth`, curbs of `CornerRadius`, centre and stop lines, zebras, manholes, cars at `CarChance`. **A one-texel-per-block texture (`City.BlockBuilt`)** adds what the grid cannot say (a block without a tower is stone with trees) and **stands in for canyon shadows**: sampled bilinearly and a block out, it takes the sky down to `CanyonSkyView` and the sun to `CanyonSunView`, with `FacadeBounce` putting some back (a street 9 wide between towers 100 tall sees a few percent of the sky).
- ⚠ **No haze inside the city**: it read as a brown veil over asphalt; the ground only fades past the last block.
- **The neon city** replaces the dome's light with `NeonAmbient`: sodium lamps every `LampSpacing` along each curb (as two lattices; overlapping pools made one orange floor), shop spill at tower feet, head and tail lights **kept under the glare threshold** (a glow a few pixels across is what glare catches on some frames and misses on others).
- ⚠ **A dark car is seen by what it reflects (#651).** Lit like asphalt at night, dark paints were black on black and only the lights showed. `CarBody` in `CityStreets.fx` returns `rim`, `ridge` and `glass`, and the night adds `spillColor * CAR_SHEEN * (rim*0.8 + ridge*0.3 + glass*1.2)`. **`CAR_SHEEN` is 0.012; 0.05 made every car glow like a toy.** Behind a `[branch]` on `Neon`; cost unmeasured.
- Shown by the chapter intro's "the canyon" viewpoint, through the drain's glass from above and any free camera; not by the menu or play cameras or the drop cinematic.
- **Cost: none, it got cheaper**: the play view (`campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,6,-60`, 1600x900 at ssaa 2, `nocap`) went 2.27 to 1.96 ms, since the old towers were shaded far below -100 past the island's rim and that area is now street.

# The dream

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The tenth `SceneKind` and the second sky-replacing one ([The sky-replacing family](space.md#the-sky-replacing-family): the full-screen quad, the `ViewRayBasis` ray, `DepthStencilState.None` restored to `Default`, no dome, no weather, black clear and its own light rig all apply, and the two scenes share one quad). Where space is the picture of space, this is the picture of a hallucination: slow marbled colour across the whole sky sphere, hard glassy solids tumbling and melting from form to form, never into each other, soft luminous orbs breathing through the murk and fast sparks whipping between them.

`Dream.fx` + `DreamBackdrop` + `DreamSceneConfig` (named groups `Palette`, `Background`, `Shapes`, `Glows`, `Lighting`: [the named-groups shape](space.md#the-config-shape-named-groups)); `scene=dream` on either command line, the Game's scene menu and random pick. `OpenBelow` includes it (the drop cinematic dives under the island against the flowing sky); it is not an `IsSolidTerrainScene`, so no pit shaft.

## What holds it together

The scene is a set of **contrasts**, each carried by a different element: sharp against blurred (the solids are raymarched with crisp silhouettes and fresnel rims; the orbs are closest-approach gaussians with no edge) and fast against slow (the marbling drifts over minutes while sparks cross the sky in seconds; thin ribbons race through the slow flow, `pow`-sharpened so only their crests survive).

- **Everything is analytic 3D** (fields on the view direction, ray-sphere tests, closest-approach glows), never a 2D chart of the sphere, so there are no pole seams.
- **The marbling is twice-warped fractal noise** (`Noise.fxh`, see "The noise library" in `docs/rendering.md`). ⚠ Plane-wave sines under a sine warp read as the plasma effect of a 1994 demo: summed plane waves keep their planes however many there are. Two fBm warp layers give filaments and mixing; the time rides *inside* the domains, so the fluid evolves rather than scrolls; and the **warp intermediates shade the colour** (where the domain was dragged furthest the fluid "mixed"), which is most of why it reads as a substance.
- **One cosine palette** (`colour(t) = A + B·cos(2π(C·t + D))`) is the scene's whole colour identity: every element indexes the same ramp at its own phase.
- **The solids morph as distance fields**: each melts between a sphere, a rounded box and a torus on its own slow cycle (half sphere↔box, half box↔torus); lerping SDFs makes the change a melt rather than a swap. They carry **SDF ambient occlusion** (four probes up the normal), so the torus's inner ring and the melt seams sit deeper than the open faces.
- **The march is gated per shape**: each solid has an analytic bounding sphere, the ray is tested against those first (`SHAPE_COUNT` quadratics) and only a crossed bound is sphere-traced, inside its own interval. Most pixels march nothing. **The bound is the solid's own radius × `SHAPE_BOUND_IN_RADII` (1.45)**, just past the farthest form (the rounded box's corner, 1.39 radii; a melt is a lerp of two distance fields and never leaves their union). ⚠ One bound for every solid, sized for the biggest (`ShapeSize` × 1.9), is up to 4.6 of a small solid's radii, and every pixel inside it marches empty space.
- **The solids write no depth** (the pass draws behind the scene), so they must roam well outside anything the camera can stand near: the inner ring's bounds come no nearer the axis than `SHAPE_INNER_EDGE` 0.47 of `OrbitRadius` 130, 61 units, against the island's 26, the game's lens 28 to 45 out and the menu orbit's 44 (`DreamIntroShotsTests` holds the bounds past 60, and flies the chapter tour's leg into the pose against them: it reaches 54 from the axis at the farthest). A solid between the lens and the island would draw *behind* it.
- **No two solids ever pass through each other** (#1219), by construction, in two rings. ⚠ The march keeps only the nearest hit and shades it from its own field, so two solids that meet draw a hard crease with no blend and the one behind vanishes inside the other; on independent orbits round one band, each at its own rate, the bounds met for 83 % of the clock and the shapes themselves for about half of it. Now **the odd solids fly in the inner ring and the even ones in the outer** (`ShapeInner`; `ShapeRadius`'s hash makes the odd ones the bigger, radius 12.9 to 22.4 against 9.2 to 16.4). **Across, the rings are apart**: each solid stands at its ring's edge plus its own bound (`ShapeOrbit`), so every bound of a ring touches the same circle, and the outer ring's edge is the inner one's plus the widest bound can be on both sides of a centre (`ShapeSize` × `SHAPE_BOUND_IN_RADII` × 2) plus `SHAPE_RING_GAP` 0.1 × `ShapeSize`: the inner centres stand 80 to 94 from the axis, the outer 142 to 152, the farthest bound 176 out. **Along, each ring turns as one** (`SHAPE_RATE_INNER` 0.029 and `SHAPE_RATE_OUTER` 0.022 radians a second, so the rings drift past each other), its six solids in even places a sixth of a turn apart and `SHAPE_SLOT_JITTER` 0.1 radian × a sine of the index off true, so two of a ring keep their distance for ever. The heights swing on their own, which nothing has to keep apart. `DreamSolidsApartTests` holds it over an hour of the clock at 0.05 s: the nearest two bounds keep 2.3 apart (an inner solid passing an outer one; two of a ring keep 17.9), and wherever two bounds overlapped the shapes' own distance fields would be searched for a point inside both. `DreamSolidsSourceTests` holds the shader's ring, orbit and placement to the host's copy, which is the one the test can run.
- **The light rig is coloured** (`DreamLightingConfig`: violet sky ambient over teal ground bounce, a rose key against a cyan fill), so the island, gun and balls sit *in* the hallucination.

## The glare policy

The background stays well under `GLARE_THRESHOLD` (`Background.Brightness` 0.30; at 0.32 it washed out everything hung on it). The **orbs may go over** (smooth areas hundreds of pixels wide bloom steadily). The **sparks stay at the threshold's edge** and read as fast through their three-sample trails, because a small point over the threshold is sampled stochastically by the glare's sparse grid and flickers.

## The look: dark, with colour gathered into plumes (#506)

References (glass, liquid chrome, a lava lamp's plume, orbs in a dark room, ink in water) agree that the picture is mostly **dark** with colour gathered into plumes and filaments. No layer, loop or march was added; the contrast and the shading were rewritten in place.

- ⚠ **A cosine palette whose three channels sit a third of a cycle apart is a hue wheel at constant lightness** (the mean is `A` per channel whatever `t` is), so rotating the hue cannot darken it and lowering `Brightness` only greys the whole frame. The contrast must be a **density over the palette**: the warp intermediates say where the fluid gathered, `saturate(length(r) · 2.4 − 0.18)` squared, multiplying the broad colour. `SwirlScale` 2.1 (2.6 drew a fine even mottle).
- ⚠ **Set the ribbon threshold from the field's real distribution.** A three-octave `RidgedFbm3` is not centred (each octave is `(1−|n|)²`): it runs about 0.63 typical against a 0.875 maximum. A threshold of 0.30 turned the layer into a continuous pale-green wash with holes, which read as mould. The shipped 0.58 with the gain that maps the rest of the band onto 0..1 leaves the typical pixel dark and lights only the crests; gain 1.4, three quarters of the ribbon multiplied by the density so a strand is lit where the ink is. The cores stay under the glare threshold: the canvas must not bloom.
- **The solids are glass** (#506): the sky seen through the body and stained by **Beer absorption over how face-on the surface is** (the only thickness measure without a second march: a ray meeting the body square crosses the most glass), `exp(−facing · Absorption · (1 − own))`, a narrow fresnel rim, and the marbled sky mirrored off the surface with a superlinear response `m · (0.5 + 4m)` so a bright ribbon reads as a glint. ⚠ A flat tint of the transmission came back as flat as the paint it replaced, because the sky behind a solid barely changes across its twenty degrees: the gradient has to come from thickness. `Shapes.Absorption` (2.4) is the dial to reach for first if they go flat. The transmission and glints are free (`color` at that point is the ray's sky already gathered, so an orb drifting behind a solid shines through it), and the emission floor fell from 0.55 to 0.05 because a body that shows the sky through it cannot vanish into it.

## The solids' size and count (#1126)

The owner: the solids were too small and too few, and from the game's lens the plasma filled most of the frame. **Twelve solids** (`SHAPE_COUNT`; the host's `SceneRenderer.DREAM_SOLID_COUNT` and the Potato path's `PotatoBackdrop.DREAM_SOLIDS` are the same number, and `DreamSolidsSourceTests` holds the shader's count, orbit and bound to the host's), **`Shapes.Size` 22.4**, each solid 0.4 to 1 of it by a hash of its index, so a radius of 9 to 22.4.

- **What a game frame holds** (ray-marched off the shader's own fields on an 80-ray-wide grid, from Solitaire's play lens `campos=0,-4.4,29.65 camtarget=0,1.37,2.19`, fov 39.4, every 5 s over twenty minutes of the clock, the rings of #1219): the solids cover **14.3 %** of a 16:9 frame and 14.3 % of a 2.4:1 one, and 3.3 solids show in a 16:9 frame (3.9 at 2.4:1), three or more in 92 % of 16:9 frames and in every 2.4:1 one. The independent orbits before the rings covered 13.8 % with 3.4 in the frame, measured the same way. The island, the cluster and the plate cover some of those pixels; the figures count them all.
- **The chapter intro's shots are laid against the host's copy** (`SceneRenderer.DreamSolidCenter`, static and pure, `DreamBackdrop.SolidCenter`): `DreamIntroShotsTests` builds the prologue on 40 rolls at 98 starts of the clock and flies every shot at the second it plays; every reel builds all three shots and every lens keeps 9.9 units off a bound (the builder keeps `SOLID_MARGIN` 10 at the points it checks and the test holds 9, so a lens nothing checked, such as the sky shot's fixed fallback, would show). The tour's leg into the pose after the reel, flown through `ChapterIntro` from the game's poses 29, 37 and 45 out on twelve bearings, never enters a bound (9.5 off at the nearest).
- **The Potato path** draws as many low-poly bodies, at `Size` × 2 breathing 0.55 to 1.45, **in two rings of its own that never let two bodies meet** (#1219, `PotatoBackdrop.DreamSolidWorld`): the odd bodies (octahedra and bipyramids, no box) at `DREAM_INNER_ORBIT` 1.12 × `OrbitRadius`, 146 out, the even ones (the boxes and the gems) 237 out, past the inner ring by the most a body of each can reach (a sphere-type body's longest half-axis, 32.5, and a box's corner, 56.3, at the fullest breath) plus 0.1 × `Size`; the rings turn opposite ways, 0.024 and 0.018 radians a second, six bodies each a sixth of a turn apart. `PotatoBackdropTests` holds every body's reach past 60 from the axis and every pair apart over an hour of the clock at 0.05 s. The nearest body reaches 113 from the axis, past the chapter tour's rim shot, which this path opens on (it has no prologue): 1.9 to 2.4 times the stand-off out, up to 108 at the widest stand-off of 45. ⚠ That is little to spare: the same test flies the tour through `ChapterIntro` from the game's poses 29, 37 and 45 out on twelve bearings and the lens keeps 9.3 off a body's reach, but at `DREAM_INNER_ORBIT` 1.03 it flies into one.

## The island (#779)

The owner: the island did not fit the scene and spoilt it. It was the stone outcrop tinted pastel. The references (`779-zimage`, `779-klein`) draw a soft pearl disc of swirled pastel marble with a thin glowing ring and a rim that melts into drips, so the island now is that, in the scene's own palette.

- **The shape is `IslandShape.Melt`** (`IslandMesh`): a puffy rolled rim with no arris below the floor's, a soft wall swelling a little under it, the edge wandering like the floe. **The drips are a radial field** (`MeltDrips`, `LatheMesh`'s `radialField`): each is the roll's face carried straight down the side, narrowing, swelling to a bulb and closing in a round tip; the run is cut in rings 0.16 apart at four times the stone's facets. Fat drops, not long runs: ⚠ a long drip that barely narrowed read as a bar stood on the wall, and ⚠ moving whole rings down at each drip (the first cut) notched every lobe of the wall into jagged teeth. Nothing above the roll's equator moves and no drip reaches past the rim or under the platform (`MeltIslandTests`).
- **The material is its own technique** (`InstancedModelTriplanarDream`, `IslandDream.fxh`, selected by `InstancedModelRenderer.DreamSurface`): pastel bands of `DreamSceneConfig.Palette` running round the drain, twisted, warped by 2D noise on the plan, with paler veins; a thin film's rainbow from the same ramp, strongest at a grazing angle; a ring of light inlaid 0.45 inside the floor's arris; no height field (polished, and the relief was most of the stone cap's cost). The drum takes less marble, more film and **a glow of its own**: ⚠ half the drum faces down into the scene's dark teal bounce and drew near black without it. **The recess under the roll is shaded by its radius** (side faces only): a drip's face is parallel to the wall and the hemisphere light shades every vertical face alike, so the drops read only once the wall behind them is darker.
- **Cost**: 0.1 ms cheaper than the stone (Testbed 3840x1600, ssaa 2, `nopost`, the minimum of two run medians: play pose `campos=0,-2,34 camtarget=0,-9,0` 5.97 against 6.07, side `campos=8,-9,44 camtarget=0,-11,0` 5.25 against 5.33). ⚠ The swirl on 3D noise drew the same top and cost +0.45 ms in the play pose.
- **The Potato path** draws the shape in the old pastel tint and without its drips, at the stone's facets (`IslandMesh`'s `reduced`): it has no marble technique, and the drips are four times the facets and a dense run of rings.

## Cost

- **Occupancy-bound (#103).** On the desktop GPU (front end, 1600×900, dome 13, `nocap`, `High` at 4.40 ms) dropping `Background()` from the reflection (4.42), sparks 14 → 4 (4.47) or each trail 3 → 1 (4.43) buys nothing; **an octave off both warp layers (4.08) pays alone**, being the only reduction evaluated on every pixel; all four together reach 3.15. Do not carry an attribution across machine classes ("Shaded at the display's resolution" in [The cavern](cavern.md#shaded-at-the-displays-resolution-155)).
- **The reduced program** (`DreamReduced`) takes all four cuts: the reflection mirrors a flat `DeepColor`, sparks drop to 4 with one trail sample, both warp layers lose an octave. With supersampling pinned, `High` 4.39 ms against reduced 2.84; on the real `Medium` 1.21 → 0.81 ms. `High` is untouched.
- **It is shaded at the back buffer's size and scaled up** (#155; `DrawsAtDisplayResolution`), which took fullscreen `High` on the 6900 XT from 18.6 ms (53.7 FPS) to 5.8 ms (172.5 FPS). Mechanism and what it gives up: "Shaded at the display's resolution" in [The cavern](cavern.md#shaded-at-the-displays-resolution-155).
- The #506 colour pass was free (desktop, fixed camera, 3840×1600, `nopost nooverc`, `fpscap=400`, medians of 12): 5.95 / 5.95 ms before against 5.93 / 5.96, as an occupancy-bound pass predicts (all the new work is ALU, almost all of it inside the branch only a hit solid takes).
- **Twelve bigger solids cost 0.2 ms** (#1126; Game Release, Solitaire, 3840×1500, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=0`, the median of seconds 8 to 37, the two builds back to back in alternating order, three rounds; the window's solids covered 6.4 % of the frame before and 11.3 % after): `Ultra` 5.65–6.00 ms against 5.84–6.22, the paired differences +0.04, +0.37 and +0.19; `High` 5.66–5.96 against 5.86–6.05, +0.21, +0.09, +0.20; `Medium` (the reduced program, which keeps all twelve) 5.05 against 5.26 in both of two rounds. The per-solid bound is why it is small (computed off the shader's formulas at Solitaire's play lens above, 16:9, over twenty minutes of the clock; not measured apart): the bigger, more numerous solids put fewer pixels inside a bound than the old ones did. Summed over the solids, the bounds in front of the lens, where the march runs, cover 29 % of a game frame against 33 %; counted as the gate counts them (`disc > 0` on the line, so a bound behind the lens takes the branch too, for one step of the march), 48 % against 56 %. What is new is mostly the solids' own pixels, each reflecting a second `Background()`. **The dream at `Ultra` is about 6 ms at 3840×1500, some 7 ms under 75 FPS's 13.33 ms.**
- **The rings (#1219) cost nothing over the clock; in any one window they cost what the solids there cover.** ⚠ A window is one stretch of the clock, and the rings stand the solids elsewhere than the old orbits did, so a single window measures where they happen to be. The Game at #1126's pin (Game Release, Solitaire, 3840×1500, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=0`, the median of seconds 8 to 37, the builds back to back in alternating order, three rounds) read `High` 5.94–5.96 ms against 6.18–6.20 and `Ultra` 5.96–6.00 against 6.16–6.19, +0.2 ms, in the stretch 8 to 40 s after launch, where the rings put 18 % of the frame under a solid against the old orbits' 11 %. The Testbed at Solitaire's play lens (3840×1500, `ssaa=2`, the same pin and median) with `clock=` 0, 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1000 read the paired differences +0.23, −0.10, −0.01, −0.19, −0.08 and +0.08 ms, each with the sign of that window's coverage difference, and 5.06 against 5.05 ms averaged over the six. Over twenty minutes (computed off the shader's formulas, a 3840×1500 frame) the rings cover 14.3 % against 13.6 %, the bounds in front of the lens 29 % against 29 %, and the gate takes 53 % against 48 %, the extra being bounds behind the lens, which take one step of the march. `ShapeCenter`'s ring, place and orbit fold to constants in the unrolled bound loop; the dynamic calls (the normal and the occlusion of the one solid hit) add a select and a sine.

# The forest

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

A temperate woodland glade: a mossy, needle-strewn clearing whose hills dress in a dark conifer canopy, with procedural trees, rocks and stumps standing on it. `scene=forest`.

## What it is made of

- **Terrain**: `ForestBackdrop` / `Forest.fx` (`ForestSceneConfig`), the meadow's displaced-grid machinery. `SceneCatalog.IsSolidTerrainScene` lists it (the island's footprint is cut out, a dark pit shaft backs the drain's glass); one shared list, because two hand-kept copies once both omitted it.
- **The wood is shared**: `ForestScatter` decides where each piece stands, `ForestScatterRenderer` draws it, in all three executables. **The planting is the backdrop's** (`ForestBackdrop.Planting`, `SceneRenderer.ForestPlanting`, #962), as the aurora's is: every host hands it to its renderer (`planting:`), so what the backdrop dresses the wood with on High and Ultra hangs on the trees that are drawn. **The caller keeps the scene gate and the placement**: `Draw` touches no GPU state and draws whenever called, so each host wraps it in `if (scene == SceneKind.Forest)` and draws after the terrain, before the island. It passes `SceneRenderer.FineMeshes` to `Draw` and `DrawShadow`, which pick the spruces' crown and the planting a tier draws (`ForestScatter.ConifersAt` and the rest).
- **Trees**: a **spruce** of tiers of branch whorls (each a drooping `LatheMesh` skirt with a shadowed tuck: the sawtooth says spruce, a smooth cone says toy), a broadleaf crown of overlapping lobes, rocks and stumps; snags and logs (#462) on High and Ultra only. **On High and Ultra the spruce's crown is separate boughs** over a dark interior, laid out by the lathe's own whorls (#733), all growing out of one shaded bark stem that runs from the trunk's top into the leader (#960), and the boughs wear needles drawn on their plates (#751). It casts into the sun's map as drawn. See "The spruces on High and Ultra" in [The aurora](aurora.md); this wood plants no crown snow, so it draws no lumps. High and Ultra's wood is a section of its own below.
- **Each kind is built at several proportions AND its own structural seed**, because the eye counts silhouettes and a uniform scale or yaw does not change one. `ForestScatter` splits instances between variants (`ModelInstance[][]`, one instanced draw each, two per tree); foliage tints are `ForestScatterRenderer.CONIFER_TINTS`/`BROADLEAF_TINTS` (a table shorter than its variant array throws). ⚠ Two lathes sharing an edge must share `irregularityPhase`, or the seam opens.
  - Variety is in the **mesh, not a per-instance stretch**: normals go through the world matrix with no inverse transpose, so a squashed tree would shade as the shape it was authored at. A **rotation** is safe.
- **Planting** is the savanna's clumped generator through `TerrainMirror.Forest`. ⚠ `MathHelper.SmoothStep` is not GLSL `smoothstep` (value-interpolate, not clamp-then-hermite): it once scattered the first forest six thousand units into the sky.
- **`ScatterSpacing`** keeps plants apart (one rule with the savanna: packing fraction 0.72, a retry budget, a signed clearance test; nothing is dropped for want of room). ⚠ Changing it **re-rolls a scatter at an unchanged seed**.
- **The wood takes the dome's mood by its pigment** (#108): its red and blue were near zero, so a coloured light had nothing to multiply. `ApplySkyTint` moves each tint `SKY_TINT_STRENGTH` towards the dome's **hue only**, re-encoded only on a dome switch.

## Traps from the first build (black eggs on the ground)

- A crown spans `[trunkHeight, trunkHeight + crownHeight]`, not `+-h` about the base. Lathe profiles run top, outside, underside (what `LatheMesh` documents): bottom-up they were inside out, and a shallow inside-out dome even reads *lit*. A span whose first ring is on the axis got a `+Y` normal for the whole span: take the tangent from the ring that is off the axis.
- Config colours are linear radiance, the material-diffuse uniform is sRGB: encode once at the boundary (`ColorSpace.LinearToSrgb`); a tinted draw multiplies luminance by 1.25, so meshes are built at `SCATTER_MATERIAL_DIFFUSE`. A procedural-mesh renderer defaults to white specular (wet obsidian): pass matte `BasicEffectParams`, a **zero** specular meaning no override, so matte is a dim non-zero colour.

## The terrain

- **Two programs over one body**: `ForestFloor(input, detail)` is called with a literal from `ForestPS` and `ForestReducedPS` (MGFX cannot parse a uniform argument in a `compile` statement). `SceneRenderer.SceneDetail` selects; **Low drops the needle strands and the tree shadows**. ⚠ **`SceneDetail` must re-select the technique on write**: as a plain auto-property the reduced program shipped and **never drew at any tier** while looking correct (caught by making it flat red for one run).
- ⚠ **The floor's cost is occupancy, not work**: removing any one piece saved nothing, pairs saved a lot, and a `[branch]` on a uniform measured 2.72 against 2.70 ms (the register footprint is fixed at compile time). Compile a second program.
- **The base normal is per pixel** (three `TerrainHeight` taps; an interpolated normal leaves a Mach band at every cell edge). **Hills** are five domain-warped sine octaves summing to 1 (so `HillHeight` keeps its meaning).
- **The floor is litter, not lawn** (#281): band-limited noise relief (`Fbm2BandLimited`, `NeedleRelief`; summed plane waves were the lines and waves); rusty needle litter (`LitterColor`, `LitterColorDark`) with bare `EarthColor`, and on it moss (`ForestColor`/`ForestColorDark`, `MossCoverage`), bilberry (`UndergrowthColor`), dry grass (`DryGrassColor`) and needle strands near the lens. **Three fields draw all of it** (dampness, litter mottle, a fine one): a field per layer cost +0.64 to +1.04 ms.
- **The canopy is colour** (`TreelineColor`, its own ramp) on Low and Medium; High and Ultra stand the far stand on it (below). **Haze is two-stage** (`ForestHaze`, shared by the floor and the far stand): into a murk first, then towards the sky drawn behind the point, the cloud deck included (`ForestSkyBehind`), `FOREST_SKY_REACH` of the way by `HorizonHazeDistance`, where the far fade (`FarFadeToSky`) starts and carries it the rest, so the grid's edge never shows. ⚠ **Never towards `HorizonColor`**: see [the twilight grade](#the-twilight-grade-1121).
- ⚠ **`CloudNoise` clusters around zero** (sigma 0.18): `* 0.5 + 0.5` spans 0.35-0.65 and ACES flattens it to one tone. Spread by about 1.6 and saturate before using it as a mottle, anywhere.
- **`ForestShadow` plants a *virtual* tree per hash-grid cell** (the shader cannot know where the real ones stand); density rides `canopyRamp`, zero in the clearing. ⚠ **It drew squares until #648**: a cylinder crown tested only in the cell the ray step landed in (cut flat at the cell's edge), height compared with world y, `CloudHash22` (-1..1) used as 0..1. Now a 3x3 block of cells round where the sun ray reaches an average crown's height, each crown a **sphere** shaded by its chord length, its size from the hash's fractional bits (equal crowns are polka dots). To isolate such an artefact: `scene=forest sceneseed=0 campos=0,30,60 camtarget=0,-14,0 fov=60`, `shadow=0`, then `ForestShadow` returning 1.0. The checkered rectangle on the island's top under a high sun is the ceiling glass's caustic shadow (#553), by design.

## High and Ultra's wood (#962)

The owner, of the wood on High and Ultra: the trees are "strange and unrealistic and too few", a "weak, sloppy video game". The references (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\962-zimage` and `962-klein`, prompts `prompts-962.json`: a clearing's edge, an interior, the undergrowth, wooded hills, the canopy from below, a snag and a log, and img2img over the play camera and one inside the wood) agree: a wall of crowded trees of every age round the clearing, two to four times as tall as the gap; ridges jagged with tree tips to the horizon, spruce stands and lighter broadleaf stands in patches; ferns and fallen wood at the feet; broadleaf crowns of separate leaf clusters with the sky between. Everything here is `SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`; **Low and Medium draw exactly the wood they drew** (the same arrays, the same meshes, the same draws).

- **More trees, of every age** (`ForestTreeConfig.FineCount`, `SaplingCount`, `FineConiferFraction`, `FineMinRadius`/`FineMaxRadius`, `FineClusters`): 400 more grown trees from 54 to 290 out, gathered round 60 cluster centres (three times the base wood's, so they close the wall rather than piling into its groves), and 160 saplings at 0.16 to 0.42 of a tree's size, four in five of both spruces (the references' wall is mostly spruce, and a broadleaf in leaf is the dearest tree on the screen); 40 more boulders, 15 more stumps, 16 snags and 26 logs (`FineCount` on each kind). **Planted after everything else from the same stream** (`ForestScatter.FineConifers` and the rest: each variant's base instances first, the extras after), so nothing a lower tier draws moves, and the aurora, whose config asks for none, gets its own arrays back.
- **The broadleaves in leaf** (`ForestDressing`, `MeadowTreeMesh.ForestInLeaf`, #751's broadleaf half): the very tree Low and Medium draw with solid clumps, grown with the meadow's twigs and broad-leaf cards (#697, `Acacia.fx`'s `BroadLeafMask`) instead; the grown wood (#889) and the twigs within `ForestScatterRenderer.FINE_NEAR_RADIUS` of the arena, the plain wood past it. The bark is a beech grey taken warm (`ForestDressing.BARK`): ⚠ the plant material lights a trunk with the mean of the dome's zenith and horizon colours and a sun tinted by the horizon, at full strength, and under dome 15 the rig's horizon is the teal skirt: by that sum a neutral grey trunk is shown teal (hue 184 in the lid's half sun, 196 in shade) beside the spruces' brown trunks. - **Ferns** (`FernClump`): clumps of 9 to 14 arching fronds (`LeafMask`, a pinnate frond) in 80 patches over the floor, one or two at the feet of half the trees out to 150, and 14 clumps of 4 to 8 on the island's bank against the drum (#983, standing on `IslandBerm.SurfaceY`, the bank's own surface), casting nothing. ⚠ Wound clockwise seen from the face their normal is on, like every card: the plant material turns a card's normal by the face it sees, and wound the other way a frond seen from above shaded as though it faced the ground (`WindingCheck` reports it).
- **The far stand** (`ForestFarStand`, `ForestFarStandConfig`, `Forest.fx`'s `ForestStand`): the hills past the planted wood, from 270 to 560 out, on a jittered grid (6.5 units inside 420, 8 past it), open where a broad field says glade, never in a cell the planted wood stands in, in spruce and broadleaf patches a grove or two across (`BroadleafShare` 0.4). Spires are `AuroraStandMesh` (a broad one of five tiers and a narrow one of six, three each past 420); broadleaves `ForestCrownStandMesh` (a lobed dome on a stem, two rolls); eight instanced draws after the floor, lit and hazed as the floor under them (`ForestHaze`), casting nothing. It stops at 560, past the haze distance, where the haze has done all it does before the far fade; the ridges past it are the hills' own silhouettes. ⚠ Its colours are the canopy colour's (`SpruceColor` near `TreelineColor`): at twice that the stand read as lit plastic over the dark floor. Its broadleaves are the planted ones' olive, `ForestTreeConfig.BROADLEAF_LIGHTER` lighter than its spruces.

## Drawn by what each pass sees (#1099)

The owner, of the forest on the desktop: the conifers have "very little detail", and the scene is "almost night, yet expensive" (#1120, #1122). What he saw: on Low and Medium the lathe spruce; on High and Ultra, in play, the near crowns of the trees round the clearing, sparse wide plates on a long stem; in the chapter intro's *grove*, where the lens flies 100 to 200 out, the far crown a dozen units from the lens, a few diamond plates on a pole.

- **Every pass sends only what its frustum meets.** The hosts' wood (`ForestScatterRenderer`) tests each instance against the camera's frustum on the screen and against the sun's map's box in the shadow pass, by its variant's bounds (`ForestPasses`, device-free, into per-variant arrays made at build: no frame allocates). ⚠ **The map's box, never the lens's frustum**: at the forest's 11-degree sun a crown's shadow runs a hundred units, and a tree well off the screen shades the island in front of the lens. The dressing's buckets and the far stand are static buffers laid out by cell (`InstanceCells`: rings `RING_WIDTH` wide by 32 sectors round the axis, each cell's box turned to its sector) and draw the runs of cells a frustum meets. ⚠ **Rings, not sectors alone**: every sector reaches in to the axis and the play camera looks across it, so by sector alone the play camera met 77 % of a wood planted from 34 to 300 out, by cell under half (`ForestCullingTests`); and a box square to the world round a diagonal sector is as wide as the sector is long. The cut is exact: the hosts' cull left a capture of the play camera as it was but for the run-to-run noise, and `ForestCullingTests` holds that every instance a frustum meets is in a run or a pass, nothing else is, and every vertex of the dressing lies inside the bounds it is culled by (a fern clump's measured from its vertices, at the seeds a launch rolls).
- **The near crown stands round the arena and round the lens** (#1120, #1163, `WearsNearCrown`): within `FINE_NEAR_RADIUS` (110) of the axis, or, where the camera sees the tree, within `NEAR_LENS_RADIUS` (90) of the lens, frame by frame. So a chapter intro's shot among the far trees has near crowns round its lens all the way along it, and a tree swaps its crown as the lens passes 90 units from it. `DrawShadow` takes the camera, and the sun's pass (`ForestPasses.SunPass`, before the camera's) gives a tree the camera sees the crown it is drawn in and one it does not see the crown of the arena's reach alone: ⚠ by the lens alone, the map held trees behind the lens within 90 of it and outside the arena's reach, and their shadows changed crown as the gun turned, now and then across the picture's edge. The play camera, within about 50 of the axis and looking in, sees no tree within 90 of it outside the arena's reach, so the player's aim and a gun walked round the island swap none in either pass (`ForestCullingTests`). The broadleaves in leaf keep the static split (`ForestDressing`). Under the Game's `maxdetail` (#1178, `RenderDetail.Maximum`) every tree is in the arena's reach, so every spruce wears the near crown in both passes, and every broadleaf is dressed as a near one: a filmed lens meets no swap. The forest's own far stand (`ForestFarStand`, above) is drawn whole at every distance: the swap of spires for spruces near the lens is the aurora's ([`StandSpruces`](aurora.md#the-wood)).
- **The near crown is denser** (#1120, `SpruceDetail.Near`): the references (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\1120-zimage`, `1120-klein`, `1120-flux3`, prompts `prompts-1120.json`: a wall of spruce at dusk, one spruce, inside the wood, and img2img over the play camera) draw a spruce as a dense cone of many narrow layered sprays, dark between them, its outline hundreds of tips. A whorl every unit of six or seven arms and one to three smaller ones between, each arm three rings and 0.115 of its run wide, over an interior at 0.28 of the outline; it was a whorl every 1.15 units of five to seven arms and one or two between, five rings and 0.13 wide, over 0.2. 91 to 179 arms a crown where there were 79 to 129, in 1 700 to 2 900 triangles where there were 2 600 to 3 800: three rings an arm pay for the arms. ⚠ **Its cost is the crown's pixels, not its triangles**: a fuller crown covers more of the screen in the needles' depth pass and the shading after it. Half again as many arms as before (a whorl every 0.95 of six to eight and two or three between) cost about 0.3 ms of the Game's frame at Ultra in play in fewer triangles than the old crown, more than the culling saves; this density is what the saving pays for.
- **The camera's passes draw a variant's trees nearest the lens first** (the needles' depth pass and the shading after it, `ForestPasses.Culled.SortFrom`): a near tree's depth is written before the trees it hides, so their pixels are turned away unshaded. 0.03 ms at the Testbed's pins. The sun's map, depth alone, takes the planting's order.
- **What each layer costs, and what the darkness hides** (#1122). Testbed, `scene=forest sky=15 sceneseed=0`, 3840 x 1500 `ssaa=2`, `nopost`, no map, the play camera (`campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,-2,-60`), `alt=` one layer off a variant (`forest=all/-<layer>`, the dial is `SceneRenderer.ForestLayers`, which the Testbed hands the hosts' renderer too), 16 cycles, every sign held, the map at High's 4096 (frame 8.38 ms): the spruces **1.57 ms** (1.14 at `ssaa=1`: three quarters of it is triangles), the broadleaves' leaves **0.89** and their wood 0.41, the far stand 0.19, the casting of the wood and the leaves 0.44 (0.11 the leaves'), the ferns 0.05, the twigs 0.03, the boulders and dead wood 0.01. ⚠ **The needles save 0.14 ms**: off, the near crowns are shaded plate over plate in one pass, where the needles' depth pass lets each pixel be shaded once. So the fine detail the twilight hides (the needles, the twigs, the ferns) costs a tenth of a millisecond between them, and nothing here is cut by darkness or by distance: the cost is the boughs and the leaf cards, the wood's whole structure. Untried: a cheaper path for the leaf cards where the light is low, and the far crown or the lathe past a distance from the lens. At Ultra's 8192 map the shadow costs 1.86 ms (the casting 1.10); 4096 saves 0.68 but softens the shadows the wood throws across the island in front of the lens, which every capture showed.
- **What it comes to**: the cull and the sort save more than the denser near crown costs. The Game at `quality=ultra`, 3840 x 1500, `fpscap=1000 sceneseed=0`, one process a figure, the builds alternated: Heart moved into the forest (dome 15) in the build's output and played from `level=11 seed=7 skiptakeover=1.5`, **10.15 to 10.06 ms** over three pairs; the menu's orbit over the forest (`scene=forest preview=1`) 10.50 to 10.43 over two. The Testbed at the pins above with Ultra's 8192 map: the play camera 9.13 to 9.01, across the wood (`campos=35,-8,45 camtarget=70,-14,110`) 11.63 to 11.52. ⚠ **The intro's *grove* pays for its near crowns**: at its lens (`campos=-137,-4.4,40 camtarget=-176,7.85,29.6`) 10.50 to 10.70 ms, the near crown round the lens where the far crown stood.

## The twilight grade (#1121)

The owner, of the forest: the far trees "completely azure", a strange contrast with the purple sky, and the broadleaf crowns "unnaturally lighter". The references (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\1121-zimage`, `1121-klein` and `1121-flux3`, prompts `prompts-1121.json`: wooded ridges at late twilight, a spruce edge with birches in leaf, a ridge of spruce tips with ridges behind, and img2img over the play camera) agree: the near spruces near black-green; each ridge behind paler and slate blue-violet, the last still darker than the sky behind it (a ridge's linear colour about the sky's times 0.13, 0.29, 0.31, a quarter of its luminance); a birch's or a beech's crown olive and yellower than a spruce, plainly lighter (1.6 to 10 times its luminance, more where the spruces go to silhouettes), never daylight's leaf green; nothing teal anywhere.

- ⚠ **`HorizonColor` is not the colour of the horizon.** It is the average of the bottom fifth of the dome's captured sphere (`SkyDome.ApplyPalette`), nearly all of it below the skyline, and under the forest's dome 15 it is a teal (linear 0.055, 0.282, 0.315) that no part of the sky drawn over the ridges has (`FarSky` at direction.y 0 is a pale blue-grey, 0.134, 0.145, 0.182). Hazed towards it, the far spires come out azure and brighter than the sky beside them. The desert's haze and the Potato path's met the same trap.
- **The sky stage is `ForestSkyBehind`: `FarSkyAt` along the point's own direction**, the colour `FarFadeToSky` ends at, with the cloud deck closed over it (below), so a far tree takes the hue of the sky behind it. **The murk is `ForestBackdrop.Murk`**: the sky drawn at direction.y 0, greyed to `MurkSaturation` (0.5) of its colour and multiplied by `MurkShade` (0.20, 0.30, 0.40), a cooler, darker shade (under dome 15 about 0.028, 0.044, 0.066, under a third of the sky's luminance). It is a uniform worked out a frame (`ForestMurk`), and the plant material's haze too.
- ⚠ **`FarSky` is the dome without the cloud deck.** `Sky.fx` closes the deck over the dome from nothing at the skyline to full cover `CloudHorizonFade` up (`CloudField.HORIZON_FADE`, 0.16), and over the ridges the play camera looks at, a few degrees up, the sky drawn is mostly the overcast lid, under half as bright as `FarSky`. Hazed towards `FarSky` alone, the farthest spires come out lighter than the sky round them, pale cut-outs on the ridges. So `ForestSkyBehind` closes the deck over it as the sky shader does, at the deck's mean: its thickness the bias times the gain (the weather field averages zero), its colour the shadowed underside (`CloudShadowColor`; the sun the sky shader lets through a thick deck is left out, so a ridge under the lid stays a shade darker than the lid). `CloudOpacity` and `CloudShadowColor` come with the rest of the deck (`CloudField.ApplyTo`); with no weather (the map editor) the gain is 0 and there is no lid.
- **`FOREST_SKY_REACH` (0.45) stops short of the sky on purpose**: by the haze distance a tree has gone under half the way to it, and the last ridges stand darker than the sky behind them, as every reference draws them. In the play camera the far spires sit at about half the sky's luminance beside them (median 0.47, nine in ten under 0.75).
- **The hosts' trees take no haze in this scene** (the instanced effect's mist is the aurora's alone, `SceneRenderer.PushInstancedFog`). The plant material hazes to the murk, the dark first stage, so a hazed broadleaf in leaf stays near the unhazed spruce beside it.
- **A broadleaf is olive, `ForestTreeConfig.BROADLEAF_LIGHTER` (1.35) times a conifer's luminance**, near (`FoliageColor` against `ConiferColor`) and on the far hills (`BroadleafColor` against `SpruceColor`) alike. With the leaves in the weather the crowns in the play camera are 0.3 to 0.9 of the sky's luminance and 2 to 5 times the spruces' near them; a pure leaf green in full sun reads as lit by a sun the rest of the wood does not have, lighter than the sky.
- **The aurora shares none of it.** It draws the far stand's spire mesh through `Aurora.fx`, hazed into its own mist (`SceneFog.fxh`), and its sky's lowest band is drawn in the mist's colour, so its stand and its sky agree by construction; its config plants no broadleaves.
- **Cost**: the murk out of the pixel program and the deck into it leave each of them about as long, the registers unchanged (`d3dcompiler_47`'s instruction slots, Shader Model 5.0, against the forest before #1121): `ForestPS` 2115 to 2122 (13 temps), `ForestReducedPS` 1644 to 1651 (13), `StandPS` 197 to 204 (8). The plant pass under the deck adds one evaluation of the cloud field (`CloudSunlight`) a pixel of the dressing.

## The sky and the fireflies (#961)

- **The forest brings its own dome, 15** (`SkyDome.SceneDefault`, so the Game's scene picker and the Testbed's `scene=forest` enter under it unless `sky=` says otherwise; the Game's Sky setting does not override it, as for every scene that states a dome). It is the late twilight (sun 11 degrees, so the canopy still throws its shadows), drawn for this wood (#1165) from references of real dusk over spruce forest (`C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\1165-zimage`, `1165-klein` and `1165-flux3`, prompts `prompts-1165.json`: an overcast dusk from a clearing, a clear twilight over wooded hills, a spruce treeline against the evening sky, a grey evening over a mixed wood, none of them naming a colour). They agree: an overcast lid is a cool, nearly neutral grey (saturation 0.1 to 0.3, hue 210 to 235 in most, a faint lavender grey in one model's) over a paler strip at the horizon, and none of them draws a saturated violet. So the dome is a pale, nearly neutral strip at the skyline under a blue-grey sky and a deep slate zenith, which the lid takes its tint from (`CloudField.ApplyDome`); the hills go slate blue and the broadleaf crowns olive. In the Game (Heart moved into the forest through `levelfile=`, `sceneseed=0`, a still of the capture mode from a fixed camera over the hills, `campos=0,45,20 camtarget=0,25,-300`, 1920 x 1080, High) the sky just over the far ridges is shown about (86, 96, 107), hue 212, and the lid about (48, 56, 71), hue 219. Its bottom fifth is the painted dome's teal skirt, vertex for vertex, below the ground where only the light rig's `HorizonColor` sees it, so the sun's tint and the ground's bounce are the ones the wood was graded under ([the twilight grade](#the-twilight-grade-1121)); and its zenith, the light from above, keeps the painted one's brightness (linear luminance 0.044 against 0.043). The palette is a formula over each captured vertex's height, written out in `SkyDome.Data.cs`; `ForestSkyTests` holds the skyline blue-grey and quiet, brightest at the strip, and the rig's horizon unchanged. ⚠ **Not violet**: the painted dome it replaced drew a violet skyline (shown at hue 268 over the ridges, saturation 0.53) under an indigo-violet zenith that tinted the lid, and the owner: "the sky at the horizon is very purple ... in reality the sky would not be so very purple". Before #961 the forest kept whatever dome was up, usually the Game's 13, a flat saturated teal (57 % of the upper sky bright and saturated at the play camera).
- ⚠ **The weather decides how bright the sky is, not the dome.** Under the Broken deck the upper sky's mean (the top third of the frame, Testbed `scene=forest sceneseed=0 nopost clock=600`, the play camera `campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,-2,-60`, 1920 x 1080) is 149 under dome 13 and 115 under dome 15: the deck's lit side takes the sun's light and is the brightest thing over a dark wood whichever dome it hangs under. So the forest is **Overcast** (`ForestSceneConfig`): a lid with the pale skyline strip under it, mean 54 and nothing bright and saturated, the hills' conifer green showing, and no cloud shadows sweeping the floor. `weather=broken` on the Testbed brings the old deck back to compare.
- **The fireflies are a swarm of twelve black bodies that show only as the light they give** (`ForestFireflies`, `ForestFireflyConfig`; the three hosts draw them whenever the scene is the forest). Each is rolled its own period (2.4 to 4.5 s), phase and slow wander (1.6 units sideways, 0.4 up and down, one circuit in 16 to 42 s) from the scene's seed, hovers 1.2 above the ground 32 to 72 units from the origin, glows for 0.4 of its period (one to two seconds) on a **squared sine**, and is carried to a brightness of 10 over the glare threshold, which is what makes the halo: the bloom's energy goes with how far over the threshold the body is, and a body of a few pixels has no halo of its own.
- ⚠ **The body must stay black.** A lit body (the first build's green sphere with an ambient term) shows as a hard olive disc the moment the sine leaves zero and again as the glow ends: the owner's "something flickers on the ground and I cannot tell what" was that, measured (a six-pixel hard dot appearing, swelling to white in a third of a second and vanishing, once in three seconds on the far floor). The ambient is zero, the diffuse black and the specular dim but **non-zero** (zero means "no override", which is the renderer's white specular). A glow under `MIN_SWELL` is not drawn.
- **The floor itself is steady.** With the camera pinned the floor's block means drift slowly and monotonically (the cloud shadows' sweep, measured under the old Broken deck: up to 8 levels over 23 s, 3 under `weather=clear`; the overcast lid has none to sweep), and a 0.03-unit camera nudge changes about 1 % of the floor's pixels by more than 4 levels (edges, parallax), so it does not shimmer under motion. A report of a flicker here is the fireflies unless a capture of the floor says otherwise.
- **Photographing one**: the fireflies come from `ForestScatterRenderer.DEFAULT_SEED`, so the same `scene=forest sceneseed=0` always plants the same ones, and `campos=-32.8,-6,35 camtarget=-32.8,-13.3,-31` has one in the middle of the frame. ⚠ Consecutive `shotframe=` shots at 1920 x 1080 are about 0.48 s apart on the scene clock (the readback is on the render thread), so a glow of one to two seconds shows in two to four frames.
- **Cost**: about 4.5 microseconds of CPU a lit firefly (about five lit at a time: 20 to 30 microseconds a frame, Testbed, 1920 x 1080), one draw each; nothing is drawn for a firefly in its dark part.

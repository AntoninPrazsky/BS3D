# The island: shape, material and berm

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The round stone island the gun stands on, in every scene. `Prazsky.Core.Render.IslandMesh` builds it, `ArenaIsland` assembles it; the Testbed and Game draw it, the map editor does not. Its shading and cost: [The island's surface and its cost](island-surface-and-cost.md); per-family dressing: [Dressing](island-dressing.md); the drain at its centre: [The drain funnel](drain.md).

## Structure and the rules that hold it

- **`ArenaIsland.TOP_Y` is the plane of the stone top's outer arris.** The walkable top falls `DISH_DEPTH` from it to the drain so a landed ball rolls in; the drain's rim, gold bead and the pit's mouth share the lower plane `TOP_Y - DISH_DEPTH`, and the collision floor runs between by construction. The top runs from `FUNNEL_TOP_RADIUS` out to `RADIUS`; the platform drops `EDGE_HEIGHT`.
- ⚠ **The shaping is all downward.** The physics floor mirrors the walkable top, so anything raised above it is a lip balls pass through. Every moulding (chamfer, coping wash, bullnose, drip, string course, plinth) is cut *below* the top face.
- **An edge reads through the hemisphere ambient, not a shadow**: an upward chamfer takes the sky (bright ring), the overhang's underside the ground bounce (dark ring). That pair is most of what makes a platform rather than an extruded circle.
- ⚠ **The plinth flares rather than tapers**, because its foot covers the hole the solid-terrain scenes cut in the ground (`TerrainHoleRadius`); the island floats half a unit over it, and a narrower foot opens a slot at grazing angles where the sky shows as a pale ring.
- ⚠ **The physics ring stops at `FLOOR_RADIUS`** (`IslandMesh.FloorRadius`, the dish's arris), not `RADIUS`: carried to the platform's widest point it would hold a ball on air over the coping.
- **Two meshes, two materials**, built from one polyline so they cannot drift: the stone cap and the concrete drum (cast: no joints, coarser relief, barely any reflection). `LatheMesh` is a surface of revolution with per-point creases and an optional radial irregularity that is a function of angle and *world height*, so two lathes sharing an edge are displaced identically; an optional radial field of the same two (the dream's drips) and a height field (the Moon's craters) follow the same rule. Triangles wind clockwise from outside, so ordinary back-face culling applies and shows a winding mistake.

## Material per scene (#404)

`ArenaIsland.LookFor(scene)` gives the cap's and drum's albedo, polish (`SpecularAmbientStrength`) and slab size, read off each scene's ground (sandstone, basalt, ice, coral limestone, marble, polished concrete, machined plates...). The meadow keeps the authored stone (`DEFAULT_LOOK`). **Material only**; geometry follows the shape.

- The colour rides the renderer's per-draw diffuse tint, which reduces the authored material to luminance times 1.25, so `TintFor` divides that out and the wanted albedo lands exactly.
- ⚠ **The look is resolved per draw from the scene `DrawIsland` is handed**, never pushed on a scene switch: callers set the scene in several places (startup argument, level load, scene page) and one that forgot to push would stand in the wrong scene.
- The glass is untinted everywhere ([The drain funnel](drain.md)). The change costs nothing measurable.

## Shape per scene (#533)

`ArenaIsland.ShapeFor(scene)` picks one of nine `IslandShape`s; `IslandMesh` builds a cap-and-drum pair for each.

- **Basalt** (volcano): two hard tiers, the drum's joint grid cut as columns (`ReliefFor`). **Ice** (polar, aurora): a lip turning under into a hollow, edge wandering, no joints. **Coral** (beach, sea): soft nose, pitted drum, no joints. **Machined** (space, grid): flat to a 45-degree chamfer, true cylinder, panel lines, no wander. **Plinth** (cities, big top): hard arris, straight side, expansion joints on top. **Monolith** (outback): a leaning sweep down to a splayed foot, flutes bent by `DrumWarp` so no two run parallel (a runnel is not a tile). **Pad** (Moon): a thin slab with a spoil bank spreading past the rim, craters as geometry ([Dressing](island-dressing.md)). **Melt** (dream): a puffy rolled rim pouring down in drops over a soft wall, in its own material ("The island" in [The dream](dream.md)). **Stone** (the rest): the authored stone.

**What a shape may not move:** the arris at `IslandMesh.FloorRadius` stays a true circle at y = 0 (or the drawn floor reaches past the collision and a ball sinks), nothing stands above y = 0 at the rim, the bore's lip (where the gold bead closes) never wobbles, and the foot stays within a fifth of a unit of the rim underneath, or outside it (the pad's bank), to cover the terrain hole; the melting island alone rounds under well inside it, because the dream floats and has no hole to cover. `RADIUS`, `TOP_Y` and `FLOOR_RADIUS` never change, so `GameCameraFit`, the gun's walk, the drain and the plate read what they always read. A wandering rim wanders on cap and drum alike (`LatheMesh`'s seam rule).

**Swapping is free**: all pairs are built at load and `InstancedModelRenderer.SetMesh` re-points the two renderers, leaving material, relief and the renderers' places in every host's sky-lit list untouched (the volcano, with the most joints, measured +0.08 and -0.06 ms: the floor).

## No paving, and the desert's drift (#550)

- **The stone island is an outcrop** (`STONE_RELIEF`): no joint grid on top, a coarser deeper relief instead. The slab coursing came from a castle shader long gone and was never a design (the owner: nothing about it needed keeping). Only the plinth's expansion joints and the machined disc's panel lines are designed and stay (`IslandLook.Slab` is read by those two shapes).
- ⚠ **Joint grids never wander.** A grid bent by noise was ruled out ("wavy paving whatever it is called"); `SlabWarp` stays only for runnels on a drum. A crater is geometry, not texture.
- **The desert's drift is a mesh** (`SandDriftMesh`, own renderer, desert only, sky-lit): a bank against the face the wind (`DesertSceneConfig.Wind`, reversed) arrives from, `DRIFT_HEIGHT` up it, `DRIFT_REACH` out onto the sand, over `DRIFT_HALF_ARC` either side. Its inner edge (`DRIFT_INNER_RADIUS`) is inside the drum so no seam opens whatever the drum's wobble does; its toe is sunk a third of a unit. ⚠ **It receives the sun's map and does not cast**: a low sheet under a grazing sun shadowed itself as a checker of acne.

## The island sits in the ground: `IslandBerm` (#608, #646)

The drum used to stop on the grass with a hard line ("it does not sink into the ground"). `IslandBerm` builds a bank round the foot as geometry in world space off the scene's ground mirror: its inner ring rides up the drum (hidden inside it), its outer edge dips a quarter unit under the terrain so there is no gap. **Two meshes** (the plant material takes one colour a draw): an earthen band against the drum and a cover (turf, snow, dust, sand) over the rest.

- **Scenes:** meadow, savanna, forest, mountains, Mars, outback, tropical beach, and the Moon's dust drift (#983, "The drift against the drum" in [The Moon](moon.md)). The storm and dream float; the desert has its drift, the polar sheet its ice in ice, and the aurora its snow bank, which is the terrain's own (`SnowBank`, #963: the snow rises about a unit up the drum, deeper on the lee side).
- **The forest's bank is the forest floor** (#983): one `IslandBerm` mesh of all the rings, drawn inside the terrain pass (`TerrainPass.Draw`'s `beforeGrid`) through `Forest.fx`'s `ForestBank`, which works out every layer of the floor (litter, earth, moss cushions, bilberry, needles) from the same world point as the terrain's, with the bank's normal for its slope. Where its outer edge dips under the ground the two are the same picture, so the bank reads as the ground rising to the stone. In two flat plant colours it read as a mat laid round the island ("a smooth transition ... is missing"). The planting's bank buckets still cast; only its stones are drawn as plants (`IslandBermPlanting.BANK_BUCKETS`). Ferns stand on it against the drum ([The forest](forest.md)) and moss climbs the drum from it ([The island's dressing](island-dressing.md)).
- **The bank's cost** (#983) is its pixels shaded by the ground's whole program. The ground under it would be shaded too, so the terrain turns away every pixel inside `IslandBerm.CoverRadius` (`BankCover.fxh`'s `UnderBank`, 2.4 times the bank's wander out from the drum, inside its fourth ring). `BankCoverTests` holds the bank over the ground everywhere inside that line, and fails when the line is 3.6 out. Measured on the desktop (RX 6900 XT), Testbed `Maps/Full.json`, `campos=0,-6,40 camtarget=0,-6,0`, 1600x900 `ssaa=4 nopost fpscap=400`, the minimum of two run medians against the build before #983 (2513465d): the forest +0.05 ms (+0.23 without the cover), the Moon +0.51 (+0.82), Mars +0.57 (+1.16). The rest is the bank's outer band, where it goes under the ground and both are shaded. The ceiling's rigging and the drain's throat cost +0.02 ms (the desert, with no bank).
- **Mars and the outback wear their bank as their plain too** (#983): `Mars.fx`'s `MarsBank`/`MarsBankReduced` (`MarsGroundAt`) and `Outback.fx`'s `OutbackBank` (`ShadeOutback`'s `onBank`), drawn in the same `beforeGrid` (the outback's after its formations' discs). Both blend to the plain's normal where the bank lies thin (its height over the field, times 1.6), the Moon's rule, and Mars buries its small craters and fresh rims with it; neither pales its bank (on Mars a paler bank read as a tan ring, #646). ⚠ **The outback never states its plain's technique**: it draws with the effect's first, so a technique added to `Outback.fx` goes after `technique Outback`, or the grid draws with the new one and the run throws on its vertex declaration.
- **Drawn through the scene's scatter** (`MeadowScatter`, `SavannaScatter`) so it casts and receives the sun's map. Every other planted scene (forest, Mars, mountains, outback, tropical beach) owns an `IslandBermPlanting` drawn and cast through `PlantPass` (the forest's bank drawn as its floor since #983, above, and only its stones as plants) (the meadow's plant draw, shared by eight backdrops).
- ⚠ **`PlantPass` once left `BlendState.Opaque` set**; as the environment's last draw in those scenes, every translucent surface after it (the gun's window pane, the drain's glass) drew solid until #667.
- Things must stand *on* the bank: `IslandBerm.SurfaceY` answers its height, and scatters take the larger of it and the ground. References put grass against the stone and stones past the foot (`STONE_RADIUS_MIN`..`STONE_RADIUS_MAX`).
- ⚠ **Judge the bank's colour against the scene's own ground in the issue's frame**: moss read as a bright ring on the forest floor, and Mars's pale pigment as a tan ring.
- **The cap and the body of a shape share their edge rings, so they share their facet count** (#974): the lunar pad's cap is cut four times finer for its craters (`capSegments`), and its body takes the same cut; at 512 against 128 facets the shared rings met in T-junctions a rasteriser rounds into hairline cracks. `WindingCheck --openness` joins the two (`Cap+Body`) and finds any ring that does not close.

# Mars

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The sixteenth `SceneKind` (#277): rust-red cratered ground scattered with dark stone, under a dusty butterscotch sky with two small moons. Made of `MarsBackdrop`, `Mars.fx` and `MarsSceneConfig` (groups Terrain, Surface, Air, Moons). Launch: `scene=mars` (the front end: `scene=mars preview=One`).

## Family and reuse

- **Solid terrain, not a sky replacer.** Mars keeps a thin atmosphere (unlike the Moon, see "The Moon (Game)" in `docs/scenes.md`), so it is a solid-terrain scene under a real dome: island footprint cut out, pit shaft behind the drain's glass, no dive. `Weather` is `Clear`.
- **Not a red Moon.** The crater field (`CraterLayer`, `TurnCrater`, `CraterField`, `MareBase`) is lifted from the Moon, being generic height-field math, but how many craters it cuts is a plain's ([How many craters](#how-many-craters-1167)); the Moon's highland belt and curvature are not lifted, since they only close a horizon with no air. Mars closes it with a two-stage haze fade to `HorizonColor`, the one the outback had until #967.
- **Dome 19** (`MARS_DOME`) is data, generated from five colour stops by vertex height: **brighter near the horizon, dimmer at the zenith** (dust).

## Stones, moons, materials

- **The stones are geometry** (#775, `MarsBackdrop`'s THE STONES): the volcano's broken block (`VolcanoPlanting.BlockMesh`, four shapes) planted as **dark basalt** in three sizes by a power law (`ROCKS_SMALL`/`MEDIUM`/`LARGE`), between `ROCKS_FROM` and the clearing's edge, where the plain is `LevelY` exactly (there is no CPU copy of the craters to stand them on further out), and `TRACK_CLEAR` off the rover's ruts as drawn. They cast into the sun's map. Low and Medium draw the faceted block, High and Ultra the fractured rock off the same instances (`FracturedRockSet`, #777, as the volcano and the Moon do). Rock is the one thing on the plain that is not red, and that contrast is what makes litter read as rock.
- **The height field's stone lattice** (the outback's `RockLayer` ported a second time: `RockSpacing`/`RockChance`/`RockHeight`, a rotated pebble tier `PebbleSpacing`, `ribDepth` 0) still shapes the ground, but **coloured as the plain's own dust**, not basalt. ⚠ As basalt, its stones were low dark mounds the grid gave no silhouette, and from any distance they read as dark ovals, holes in the ground: the owner's "do not look like the real photographs". **The mound's mask fades in as the mound rises** (#1167: a fifth at 1.1 radii, two fifths at the talus apron's top, whole from 0.7 to 0.8 radii in), so the dimmer rust, the rock's rough face and the ruts' fade over it blend into the plain. ⚠ A mask whole from 1.1 radii out drew every mound as a flat disc with a hard rim, discs over about a fifth of the plain past the clearing: once the craters were thinned they read as craters themselves, and they cut the rover's ruts at their edge.
- **Gravel** (#775): a jittered dark point in about a fifth of the 1.3-unit cells (jittered no further than keeps the largest inside its own cell, the only one evaluated), sized by a power law, faded out by the footprint before it is a pixel and not evaluated past that (`[branch]` on the footprint, coherent across the frame). The full program's only.
- **`MarsMoons`, not the Moon's Earth.** `MoonSky` replaces the whole sky; Mars keeps its dome, so `MarsMoons` shares the full-screen quad and `ViewRayBasis` (`SkyRay.Basis`), is **depth-read** against what `MarsTerrain` wrote and **alpha-blended**. Each moon is a `MoonBody` (#744): the Earth's ray-sphere test run on a ray scaled into an ellipsoid, the long axis on the line of sight as both moons are tidally locked to Mars, so the outline is a tilted potato (`PHOBOS_AXES`, `DEIMOS_AXES`); Stickney, three octaves of small craters and slow lumps tilt the normal analytically (no gradient op under the `[branch]`). Lit by Lommel-Seeliger mixed with Lambert, so the sunlit side stays bright to its limb, and by Marsshine (`MarsshineColor`) on the side the camera sees. Composited through the air: `Transmission` of the moon's own light, and `Airlight` of the dome still in front of it (premultiplied `BlendState.AlphaBlend`). ⚠ Drawn without the air, a dark moon on a daytime dome is a black hole cut in it, and was: the first Phobos had a reflectance of 0.095, its shadowed half near 0.005, far under the dome behind it. Neither the size nor the reflectance is true (true ones are sub-pixel and near-black): see `MarsMoonsConfig`.
- **The moons' pass is scissored to each moon's box** (#1138, `MarsBackdrop`'s THE MOONS' BOX). `SkyRay.PixelsWithin` runs `SkyRay.Basis` backwards: the cone `MoonDisc` tests (`MarsBackdrop.MoonCone`: `MOON_CONE_SCALE` of the moon's angular radius, one figure in `Mars.fx` and the backdrop, plus the largest footprint a pixel can have, `SkyRay.MaxPixelAngle`), cut to the frustum's four sides, becomes a box of the target's pixels. The quad, still the whole frame's, is drawn once a box (once over both where the two share a pixel, so none is blended twice: `MoonDraws`) and not at all while neither moon's cone comes within a pixel or so of the frame, wherever the lens is turned. Every pixel the boxes leave out is one the program writes an alpha of 0 to, which the premultiplied blend leaves as it was, and the quad's corners, so every interpolant and derivative, are the full screen's. **Without multisampling** (High and Ultra, `ssaa=2`) **the picture is the full-frame pass's to the bit.** ⚠ With multisampling it is not quite: the full-frame pass changed a few pixels on a silhouette far from either moon, and the boxes leave them as the scene drew them (`ssaa=1`'s eight samples, 1280 x 720, both moons in the frame: the same four pixels on the ceiling plate's edge in every run, by up to 10 of 255, each equal to the frame without the moons; Medium draws four samples, Low two). `MarsMoonBoxTests` holds the boxes against the shader's own rays and footprints, worked out pixel by pixel, and a moon off the frame to no box round a whole turn of the Game's lens; `mars=all/-moonbounds` draws the full-screen quad again, for a pair of frames or a price in one process.
- ⚠ **Over the whole frame, the moons' pass cost what covering the frame cost, not what its program did.** It cost 0.13 ms at every pin #1102 took, `ssaa=1`'s eight samples a pixel and `ssaa=2`'s four times the pixels alike; why the cost does not follow the pixel count has not been found. Taken apart (throwaway variants in one process, at the game camera, `ssaa=2` with `Maps/Full.json` and `ssaa=1` empty): the two bodies' arithmetic 0.02 and 0.01 (a program writing a constant 0 under the same blend instead), the writes 0.02 and 0 (a `clip` wherever nothing is covered), the blend's read 0.01 (opaque writes, `ssaa=2`); the same quad scissored to one pixel saved all of it, so the draw call and the state changes cost nothing measurable. So a light pass that draws something small in the sky belongs in a box of its own.
- **Mesas** (`MesaField`; `MesaHeight`, `MesaInnerRadius`, `MesaThreshold`): thresholded low-frequency noise gives flat tops with cliffs; strata (`StrataColorPale`/`StrataColorDark`, `StrataFrequency`) band by world height. `MesaHeight` is low: tall mesas read as Australia.
- **Dark sand drifts** (`SandColor`, `SandCoverage`) carry ripples square to the wind (without them a drift reads as a cloud shadow); **pale slabs** (`SlabColor`, `SlabCoverage`) are cracked along noise zero-crossings. ⚠ Coarse terms must be gradient noise: hashes held constant over a square step at every edge (the "tiles").
- **One muted tone across the plain** (#775, the references `775-zimage`, `775-klein`): the broad rust patches are gentle (`RustColor`/`RustColorPale` close together), a drift is only a little darker than the rust and soft-edged, and drifts and slabs thin out up a crater's wall (`level` from 0.78) instead of stopping on it. ⚠ Contrasty patches with hard edges were the owner's "blotches" and "torn" areas. A second octave in the drift field and a gravel layer evaluated at every distance cost about 0.3 ms together, and the soft single octave with the branched gravel looks the same.

## ⚠ The haze decided what the mesas looked like

`lerp(color, HorizonColor, haze^4)` with `haze = dist / HorizonHazeDistance` fades terrain to the dome's raw pinkish horizon, not rust. At 460 the mesa ring (300-440 units out) was up to 84 % dome colour, so no retuning of strata could show. `HorizonHazeDistance` is 700 (`MarsAirConfig`).

- **Brightening albedo was the wrong lever** (`BoulderColorBright`, `RockRelief`, `SandColor`): every small crater and pebble became a pale dot.
- **Height-blending `relief` and the coarse grain** did not cure the mesa cliffs' vertical streak, which is open (untested cause: the far-field ring's vertex spacing, `FarField.fxh`).

## How many craters (#1167)

The owner: "far too many craters, they cover absolutely everything; Mars does not have so many". **A Martian plain is a few big craters on open regolith, with the small ones scattered**, so the field's chance of a crater per cell is Mars's own (`CRATER_CHANCE_0` to `_2`, `SMALL_CRATER_CHANCE` in `Mars.fx`):

| Octave (cell) | Rim radius | Chance | One in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 129 units | 15 to 27 | 0.86 | 140 x 140 units |
| 49 | 6 to 10 | 0.20 | 110 x 110 |
| 18.6 | 2.2 to 3.9 | 0.14 | 50 x 50 |
| 7.2, the pixel's (`SmallCraterLayer`) | 0.9 to 1.5 | 0.10 | 23 x 23 |

- **An octave's share of the plain is its chance times pi times `CraterLayer`'s mean squared radius, 0.0232 of a cell.** The four octaves' bowls cover **9.5 %** of the plain, their rims (to 1.6 radii) 24 %; the three smaller octaves 3.2 % of it, and the big ones (the 129-unit octave and [the three lattices](#big-craters)) 12.0 %. `MarsCraterCoverageTests` reads the octaves out of `Mars.fx` and measures each share on the shader's own rolls (`ShaderMath.Hash22`): the bowls under a tenth, the big craters over the smaller octaves, and one call site an octave.
- ⚠ **The Moon's chances (0.86, 0.82, 0.70, 0.64) are a highland's**: under them every octave covers about the same share of the ground, and the four laid bowls over 22 % of the plain and rims over 56 %, the owner's "everything". The big octave keeps the Moon's 0.86, since #638 asked for more big craters.
- **The clearing flattens the three `CraterField` octaves, rims included**: `MarsHeight` multiplies their ejecta by the same ramp as their height. ⚠ Without it each crater's pale rim was painted flat on the clearing round the island, a crater drawn on the ground rather than cut into it. The pixel octave is the one the clearing keeps: its relief is the normal's, at every distance its footprint allows.

## Big craters

The field's biggest bowls were about 2.6 units deep, a dark ellipse from the ground, so big craters are their own lattices (`BigCraters`): a crater is a hole, not a texture.

- Three sparse single-cell lattices (turned so cells never line up), at most one crater a cell. **Depth and rim are shares of the crater's own radius** (`BIG_CRATER_DEPTH`, `BIG_CRATER_RIM`, with an ejecta blanket). None stands whose blanket reaches within `BIG_CRATER_CLEARING` of the arena (left out whole, never cut).
- **Dark inside**, which is what makes them read from a lens ten units up. The terrain casts nothing into the sun's map, so each crater computes closed-form light: the sky its walls hide (off the ambient) and whether the rim hides the sun (one quadratic). The nearest lattice is the densest, since a low lens sees into a bowl only within about ten times its height.
- **Evaluated once a pixel.** The profile is radial, so `BigCraterSample` evaluates all three lattices once and adds the closed-form gradient to the normal taps' slope. Summed into `MarsHeight` it cost +0.65 ms at High, +0.38 at Low.
- **CPU copy:** `OffworldGround.MarsBigCraters` (via `ShaderMath.Hash22`); `MarsCeiling` adds its positive part exactly, not at a bound (which would lift every intro lens by the biggest rims' height, the tracks shot's included); the intro's mesa is `MarsMesa` against `MesaHeight`. There is **no `TerrainMirror` of `MarsHeight`** (nothing is planted), so Mars is not in `mirrorcheck`.

## The rover's tracks (#745, #1115)

The owner: a rover's tracks as a small easter egg; then (#1115) "the intro animation does not deal with the rover tracks". The references (`745-klein`, `745-zimage`) run a twin chevron track across the plain to the dusty horizon, its ends lost under drifted dust. The Moon's mechanism ([The Moon](moon.md), #746: `RoverTracks.fxh`, `RoverTracks.cs`), pushed once at load.

- **One copy of the traverse**: `MarsTracks.TRAVERSE` (its controls) and `TRAVERSE_PATH` (the shader's 24 points), which the shader's constants, the clearing's stones and the chapter intro all read. It comes in from the mesas to the north-west down a straight arm, swings round the arena's west side in one even arc (radius 102 to 103, its nearest 79 from the axis, inside the clearing) and runs off south-west down a second arm towards the far side's mesas.
- ⚠ **The 24 points go evenly over the control SPANS, not the length** (the Moon's lesson, #1114), so the arc is nine short spans and each arm two long ones; every bend between two of the shader's straight segments is under 10 degrees (9.3 measured, `MarsIntroShotsTests` holds 10). The first traverse had a control at each corner of its swing, met at up to 18.2 degrees there, and drew a polygon round the arena.
- `MarsGroundFields` presses the ruts into the normal and darkens the ground the wheels turned over (`MarsGroundBaked` lays them over the bake) (`TRACK_DARKEN`, the darker ground under the dust skin every rover photograph shows), never over a rock or the island's bank. **The full program's only**: the reduced one keeps its budget, so Low draws no ruts (`SceneRenderer.MarsDrawsTracks`) and its chapter intro opens on the plain instead.
- **The stones keep off the ruts as drawn** (`TRACK_CLEAR` and their own size off `TRAVERSE_PATH`), tested after every roll a stone takes, so a change to the traverse takes away or gives back the stones near it and leaves the rest of the field where it lay.
- No level plays on Mars, so the ruts are for the menu's orbit, the drop and the chapter tour, whose prologue opens on a run low down them towards the arena ([the scene shots](../game-feedback/chapter-intro-scene-shots.md)).
- **Cost**: a pixel pays the segments of the runs of four whose padded box holds it; the six boxes cover 19 960 square units, the long diagonal of the south-west arm's last run alone 11 260, far out on the plain.

## The bake (#1100)

The ground does not change, and its full program (`MarsTerrain`, 4203 instructions compiled for DirectX) works it out for every pixel of every frame: a pass bound by the width of its program (`BestPractices.md` §7). On High and Ultra its static fields are worked out once into maps, and the ground near the arena reads them.

- **What is baked** (`MarsBackdrop.BeforeShadowMaps`, `Mars.fx`'s `MarsBake`, three targets in one pass, mipmapped): the albedo (the square root of linear in 8 bits, so the darks do not band); the slope less the mesas', in half floats, with the big craters' sun and sky; the stone mounds' mask, the slabs and the grain's share. `MarsGroundFields` is the one body of the exact ground, the bake and the ground that reads it (`MarsGroundBaked`), so the bake cannot drift from what it stands in for. It is baked again when the sun moves, and its maps are given back on a lower tier and whenever the renderer turns from Mars to another scene or the host draws no scenery (`Backdrop.Leave`), so each showing of Mars on High or Ultra starts with a frame that bakes.
- **What stays the pixel's** (`MarsTerrainBaked`, 1465 instructions, the mesas' taps only inside their ring and the cracks only on a slab): the light (the clouds' shadow, the sun's map), the haze, and whatever is a few texels wide or less: the fine relief, the small craters' relief, the rover's ruts and their darkening, the fine grain, the gravel, the slabs' cracks (one or two texels wide near the arena), and the mesas' slope and strata.
- ⚠ **The bake's band limit is the texel's, which near the lens is several pixels'.** A term faded by the footprint as a pixel fades it reaches the lens with less contrast than the exact ground gives it there: faded so, the drifts' ripples 40 to 60 out would keep a half to three quarters of theirs. A texel holds a term whole until it nears what the texel can carry, so the ripples and the coarse grain are baked with a knee (`MARS_BAKE_KNEE`): their fade ends where a pixel's does and is whole up to half that, which is every texel within about 55 of the arena for the ripples and 85 for the grain. The stone mounds' face keeps the pixel's fade, its finest octave at a half to two thirds of its contrast 30 to 50 out.
- ⚠ **A feature a few texels wide cannot come out of the map.** A normal taken off a few texels drew a small crater's rim as a smudge of blocks, softened the ruts, turned a crater's bowl near the lens into a dark hollow and combed the mesas' cliffs and bands (a cliff stands on a few texels of a map laid flat). What the map still holds of the relief (the stone mounds' face) is tilted off `ddx_fine`: a 2 x 2 quad's step held one tilt over every two texels.
- **Polar round the arena**: the bearing across (`BAKE_WIDTH` 4096), the log of the radius down (`BAKE_HEIGHT` 2048, from `BAKE_MIN_RADIUS` 5 to `BAKE_MAX_RADIUS` 1000, offset by `BAKE_RADIUS_OFFSET` 20), so a texel grows with the distance as a pixel's footprint does: 0.09 out by 0.05 across at 30 from the arena, 0.22 by 0.15 at 100, 0.58 by 0.46 at 300. 128 MiB, 171 with the mips. The lens that sees Mars stands near the arena, so one bake serves every frame.
- **Where it is drawn**: wherever the lens stands within `BAKE_CAMERA_REACH` (70) of the arena, which holds the front end's orbit and the play camera in any window at least about 0.73 as wide as it is tall (the wide leg stands 68.7 out at 3:4). ⚠ In a narrower window (the Game's is resizable) the wide leg stands past the reach, and the lens turns from one ground to the other as it crosses 70 on every fly-in and retreat; at 9:16 the tour's last leg starts past it too. Every shot of the chapter intro's prologue stands outside it the whole way (107.5 the nearest, `MarsBakeReachTests`), so no shot turns from one ground to the other in flight, and a lens 2.5 units over the ruts is never drawn the coarser texels the bake has out there. From any lens inside the reach the bake holds the plain out to where the haze has taken it whole (`HorizonHazeDistance`). Past the reach, on Medium, on Low (`MarsTerrainReduced`) and on OpenGL ES the exact ground is drawn. The far ring is drawn with the ground's program: inside the reach that is `MarsTerrainBaked`, which has no reduced twin and is cheaper than `MarsTerrainReduced` (it reads the maps where that takes the height field's taps); past `BAKE_MAX_RADIUS` it reads the bake's last row stretched out, where the haze from any lens inside the reach has long taken the plain.
- **Cost** (desktop, one process a figure, the builds alternated, the median of each run's `[fps]` lines; the bound is a throwaway ground that reads constants for every static field and keeps the light and the haze, the most any bake could save). Measured on the programs before #1101's shadow gathers, which take 44 instructions off both, and before the slabs' cracks went to the pixel, which adds 77 to the baked one, run only on a slab: main's exact program of 4242 instructions against a baked one of 1432. The Game's front end on Mars at Ultra, 3840 x 1500 windowed (`scene=mars preview=1 quality=ultra sceneseed=0 seed=7`, four runs each): **7.31 ms exact, 4.75 baked**, 4.50 the bound. The Testbed at the game-mode camera, 3840 x 1500, `sceneseed=0` (two runs each): `Maps/Full.json` at `ssaa` 2 4.86 against 4.16 (bound 3.94), the empty field 3.57 against 2.88 (2.66). At `ssaa` 1 with eight samples and the fine meshes on, a Testbed combination no tier ships (Medium draws at 1x with four samples and without the fine meshes, so without the bake): `Maps/Full.json` 6.26 against 3.88 (3.14), the empty field 5.47 against 3.08 (2.33). **The bake is High's and Ultra's alone**, the tiers that supersample at 2x, and on DirectX alone: Low, Medium and the phone draw the exact ground. The pass itself runs on the first frame of each showing of Mars on High or Ultra, and when the sun moves.
- **Not on OpenGL ES**: it is compiled under `!OPENGL`. GL would need three colour targets at once (GLES 3.0 has them), a half-float colour target with its mips (`EXT_color_buffer_half_float`, or the slope packed into 8 bits), explicit gradients for the bearing's seam (`tex2Dgrad`, which GLSL ES 1.00 has only through `EXT_shader_texture_lod`), and it has no fine derivative. No 3D texture is involved. A phone would also want a smaller bake: 2048 x 1024 is 43 MiB with the mips.

## Tiers and costs

- **`MarsTerrainReduced`**: `MarsTerrain(input, detail)` behind two techniques by `SceneDetail`, so the Game's `Low` takes it; it drops sand, slabs, the strata wobble and the coarse grain, keeps the mesas (geometry must light the same on every tier), and runs on a coarser grid (`MARS_GRID_N_REDUCED`, #540). Not taken: dropping the pebble lattice (-1.12 ms) emptied the plain, and a crater octave (-0.25 ms) lost the small craters.
- **Costs** (reference APU, Testbed, 1600x900 ssaa 2): Mars 31.2 ms against the outback's 31.7 (same pin, dome 13), the same band. The mesa ring +0.39 ms from the play level, +0.85 from above (`campos=0,25,20 camtarget=0,-13,120`). Big craters at the pixel: about +0.1 ms (Game front end, 1920x1080). The stones and the gravel (#775, desktop Testbed 3840x1600 ssaa 2, paired): the planted stones about +0.04 ms; the whole change 15.89/15.93 against 15.86/15.93 ms from the play pose (`campos=0,-2,34 camtarget=0,-9,0`) and 15.51/15.52 against 15.37/15.41 from the ground (`campos=0,-9,60 camtarget=0,-11,200`). High and Ultra's fractured rock over the faceted block, casters included (`Maps/Full.json`, `sceneseed=0`, `alt=finemeshes=0;finemeshes=1`, which on Mars, under its clear sky, then swapped the rocks and nothing else that costs (it now turns the bake too); two runs of 32 to 33 paired cycles a camera): **+0.04 to +0.06 ms** from the play pose (8.70 to 8.75, the sign held in every cycle) and nothing measurable from the ground (+0.02 and −0.01, the sign a coin toss).

## What each layer costs (#1102)

The Testbed's `mars=` dial takes the scene apart (`MarsLayer`, "The dials" in [Capturing and measuring](../testbed/capture-and-measurement.md)): `alt=mars=all;mars=all/-ground;…`, every variant naming `mars=` and `shadow=`. Desktop (RX 6900 XT), Testbed Release, `scene=mars sceneseed=0 fpscap=1000 width=3840 height=1500`, the game-mode camera (`at=3:F10`), the scene's own 4096 map (Ultra's is 8192), post on. One process a column, the twelve variants in settled windows (#1129), each one's difference from `mars=all` taken within its cycle; the median of 20 cycles, the sign held in at least 85 % of them for every figure of 0.05 ms or more. `ssaa=1` is the Testbed's eight samples, the sky first and the terrain not laid depth first; `ssaa=2` is High and Ultra's frame: no samples, the terrain's depth first, the sky behind the opaque scene at display resolution, so a layer there is priced net of the sky it hides. - **The Game's frame at Ultra over Mars's front end** (Game Release, `scene=mars preview=1 quality=ultra fpscap=1000 sceneseed=0`, a 3840x1500 window, a scratch profile, four runs, the median of the `[fps]` lines of seconds 9 to 47 of each): **4.66 to 4.93 ms**, the dearest second 6.2. #1103's goal is under 13.33. Four runs of the build that drew the moons over the whole frame, alternated with these, read 4.71 to 4.88: the 0.13 ms the boxes save is inside the spread of the Game's runs, which have no `alt=`.
- **What is left of Mars on High and Ultra is 0.6 ms of a 4.0 ms frame**: the ground and its far ring 0.4 of it. The rest of the frame is the sky, the island, the gun and the cluster, as in any other scene under a dome.
- **The bake is worth 0.7 ms at `ssaa=2` and 2.4 at `ssaa=1`**, where the terrain is not laid depth first and the exact program shades every overdrawn pixel (the figures [The bake](#the-bake-1100) gives). No tier ships that column: Medium draws the exact ground at 1x without the fine meshes.
- **The moons cost nothing measurable where they are not in the frame**, the game camera's case: the boxed pass draws nothing there. Over the whole frame (`-moonbounds`) they cost 0.13 ms in every column, whatever the pixel count ("Over the whole frame, the moons' pass cost what covering the frame cost" above). **With both moons in the frame** (the free camera `campos=0,0,30 camtarget=-46.622,79.109,-9.599 fov=72`, the same window, `mars=all;mars=all/-moons;mars=all/-moonbounds` in one process, 32 and 33 cycles) the boxed pass costs 0.00 ms at `ssaa=1` and 0.01 at `ssaa=2`, the full-screen one 0.14 and 0.16.
- The clearing's stones cost 0.05 ms at `ssaa=1` and 0.01 on High and Ultra's path. The bank's stones and everything the scene casts into the sun's map cost nothing measurable in any column; the map's 0.15 to 0.44 ms is the island's, the gun's and the balls' casting.

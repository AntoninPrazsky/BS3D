# The meadow

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The Windows XP *Bliss* hill: vivid green rolling to a crisp horizon, a flat flowering clearing where the arena stands, rolling up into hills. `SceneKind.Meadow`, `scene=meadow`, sixth in the NumPad2 cycle.

## What it is made of

- **`MeadowBackdrop`**, **`Meadow.fx`**, `MeadowSceneConfig`: the displaced grid (shared `AcquireGridMesh`, [Terrain grids](terrain-grids-and-mirrors.md)) with smooth rolling sines, normal by finite differences. The grid draws the **field** (`GroundHeight`; its CPU copy `TerrainMirror.MeadowGround`), and a patch of its own draws the brook's swale and the pond's crater cut into it (below). `TerrainMirror.Meadow` is the cut ground, the CPU copy everything planted is placed by (`MeadowNatural` is the field without the pond's levelling).
- **Planting** (`MeadowScatter`, #609): old trees (below), shrubs (stems and leaf clumps on High and Ultra, below), hedgerows, post-and-rail fences (`FENCE_SCALE`, twice a field fence's size, #698), hay bales at the fence's scale (`BALE_SCALE`, #864: a bale stands about as tall as a post, 3.2 across, planted after the fences and clear of every span, the path and each other, off a generator of its own), boulders and `DetailOnly` tufts, nothing nearer than `INNER` but the island's bank and what the path and brook arrive at. Drawn as the savanna's planting is (`ScatterBucket`s through `Acacia.fx`). ⚠ Colours are set against the vivid grass: at the savanna's canopy colour the first hedges photographed as black lines.
- **What a camera is told of the planting** (`MeadowScatter.Figures`, a `MeadowFigures` through `SceneRenderer.MeadowFigures`, #893): the old trees (the knoll's first), the bench, every bale, and the low solids (shrubs and hedgerows, boulders, the fence's spans as capsules at the rails' height, the bales and the bench again), each as a `PlantFigure` at its instance. The planting needs a device, so a test builds figures of its own from `BenchWorld`, `BenchFigure`, `BaleBounds` and the constants (`MeadowIntroShotsTests`). ⚠ **A tree's crown sphere is its mesh's bounding sphere and reaches below the ground** (the oak's: a centre 13.6 up, a radius of 20), so a lens near the ground is inside it anywhere under the tree: low shots hold a tree by its stem (`Root` to `Crown`, `Stem`) alone.
- **The path** (`MeadowPath`): `Meadow.fx`'s `PathLateral` and `MeadowPath.Lateral` are **the same arithmetic**; the planting keeps `PATH_CLEARANCE` off it. It **arrives at a knoll** (`PathEnd`, `KnollRadius`, `KnollHeight`: a bump in `TerrainHeight` and its mirror) with the biggest oak and a bench: a path leads somewhere. **The bench is at the fence's metre** (`BENCH_SCALE = FENCE_SCALE`, #891: 3.8 long, seat top 1.0, back 1.96, against a post's 2.7), scaled in the mesh and its bounding sphere like the bale, and stands `BENCH_OUT` (6.6) from the trunk's axis so that its nearest corner is past the trodden patch's edge (`TREE_BARE` + its 0.4 fade, `Meadow.fx`) and the oak's longest root (`MeadowScatter.BenchStand`; `MeadowBenchTests` measure both on the real mesh). ⚠ At one unit to a metre it was shorter than the oak's trunk is thick, and the owner could hardly find it.
- **The brook** (`BrookBearing`, `BrookWidth`, `BrookMeander`; `BrookLateral` in the shader and `MeadowPath`) lies in **a wide shallow swale cut into the field** from its spring on the hillside (`MeadowCarve.BROOK_CARVE_END`, 190 from the arena, short of the crest at 206) to the pond; nothing of it is painted on the field (below). ⚠ The first water mirrored the horizon's pale colour at grazing angles and read as a tan path: the reflection leans to the zenith and is cooled. It runs **into a pond** (`PondCentre`, `PondRadius`, `PondOutline`), a crater with water in it.
  - **The ground under the pond is levelled** (`POND_LEVEL_FROM`, `POND_LEVEL_FADE`; `PondLevel` computed on the CPU off `MeadowNatural`, pushed as a uniform) so still water lies level, and the crater is cut into the level; `TerrainMirror.MeadowGround` carries both terms. The pond **mirrors the clouds** (the reflected ray crosses the cloud plane, `CloudCover` in `Clouds.fxh`). ⚠ Use the *flat* reflection's ray, not the rippled one: a low lens crosses the plane (about 200 up) thousands of units out, and ripple slope moved that point as far again: speckle.
- **The path's edge noise runs only near its line**, behind a `[branch]` on the lateral distance: `GradientNoise2` never leaves +-1, so past the widest edge plus `PATH_RAGGED` every term is exactly zero (the painted brook's had its own, gone with it, #979). They had run on every pixel (-0.50 ms Low, -0.46 Medium, -1.02 High on the APU).

## The brook's groove and the pond's crater (#892)

The owner: the brook and the pond "have no real geometric depth - the brook should be a groove cut into the ground and the pond a kind of irregular crater - only inside them is then the water surface". The references for the brook are in `C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\892b-klein` and `892b-zimage` (a meadow brook from a low angle, from above and across a bank: a grassy hollow two or three times the water's width, the grass rolling over a rounded lip down to a low step of earth and a pale gravel margin, the gravel wide on the inside of a bend), and for the pond in `892-zimage` (reeds and lilies on a sunk bank).

- **The brook lies in a swale** (`MeadowCarve.BrookSwale`, the CPU copy of `Meadow.fx`'s, term for term; `MeadowCarve` is the whole cut's copy of `CarvedHeight`). From its middle out:
  - the bed `BROOK_BED` (0.55) under level water, shelving up to the water's edge over its last `BROOK_SHALLOWS` (0.8) (`BrookBed`);
  - a gravel margin `BROOK_MARGIN_RISE` (0.1) over the water and 0.1 to 0.9 wide (`BrookMargin`), wide on one side where the other's is narrow and swapping over tens of units, as a gravel bar does on the inside of a bend;
  - a low earth step at its back, `BROOK_STEP` (0.22) over `BROOK_STEP_RUN` (0.3);
  - a grassy bank from the step's top to the field (`BrookBank`: 1.8 to 3.0 wide on level ground, each side on its own wobble). It is a hermite blend to the field's **own** height, so it meets the field with no crease on any hill. It is at least `BROOK_BANK_PER_RISE` (2) times as wide as it climbs, up to `BROOK_BANK_MAX` (4.4), so the uphill bank of a cross slope widens instead of steepening;
  - a low lip on the bank's top, up to `BROOK_LEVEE` (0.12) high and gone over long stretches (`BrookLevee`).

  On the default meadow the grassy bank climbs 0.47 at the median (25 degrees), 0.75 at the 90th percentile and 1.1 at its steepest, an uphill bank at its widest. The swale, where it is cut 0.05 or more, is 2.6 to 3.4 times the water's width (median 3.0) and reaches at most 7.45 to the side. `MeadowCarveTests.TheBrooksBankIsASwaleNotAWall` holds the slopes and the spread. ⚠ The first cut was a groove with earth walls four in one, 1.25 high over 0.3, and the owner read it as "as if an excavator dug a hole there; a natural brook looks a little different".
- **The brook's water lies `BROOK_DROP` (1.25) under the lowest** of the field on its centreline and `BROOK_REFERENCE` either side, so a cross slope never tips it over the lower bank. Its width wavers (`BrookWaterHalf`: 1.24 to 2.14 either side). **The pond's crater** (`PondCrater`) is a shelf and a deeper middle under the water, a short steep earth step at the waterline, a grassy bank 1.2 to 4.8 wide (`PondBankWidth`) and a raised lip that comes and goes (`PondLip`). Its water lies `POND_DROP`, the brook's drop, under the levelled ground, so the two waters meet at one level at the mouth. The lower of the field and the crater, and of the field and the brook's bed, is **rounded** (`CarveMin`, `CARVE_ROUND`). ⚠ A hard `min` put a crease at the lip, which the patch's triangles drew as a zigzag.
- **The camera grid cannot carry it.** A bank two or three units wide and a step a third of a unit across fall between vertices 5.5 apart (the [icesheet's](polar.md) lesson), and a grid that follows the camera would swim them. So the grid displaces the uncut field (`GroundHeight`) and **clips a hole** where the cut is: `POND_HOLE_RADIUS` round the pond, `BROOK_HOLE_HALF` (9.3) either side of the brook to `BROOK_HOLE_END`, in the far ring too. **A patch fixed to the world draws the cut** (`MeadowPatchGeometry.Ground`, the `MeadowCarved` programs). It is one grid in the brook's own coordinates (distance out by lateral, `MeadowPath.BrookPoint`), so its columns, 0.2 apart (the pond's earth step needs them), follow the banks wherever the brook wanders. Its rows are 0.25 apart over the pond, whose crater runs every way, and 0.75 along the brook, whose swale changes over units along it. Built once, in whole blocks of 8 by 8 quads (`LOD_BLOCK`): every block with a vertex within `POND_PATCH_RADIUS` or `BROOK_PATCH_HALF` (9.9), 46,097 vertices since the brook ends at its spring (#979; 93,161 when it ran to 470).
  - The patch reaches past the hole on every side. **Past the zone the cut can reach** (`BROOK_CARVE_HALF`, `POND_CARVE_RADIUS`) its vertex shader eases it onto the grid's own triangles (`CoarseGridHeight`) and the grid's normal. That works because the grid's vertices stand on one world lattice whatever the camera does (`CoarseGridPhase`). The ease is needed: the grid's flat cells stand up to 0.19 off the true field. **Past the hole's edge it lies a hair under the grid** (`PATCH_SINK`, more with distance), so the early depth test throws its pixels away there. Level with the grid, that band (up to a block wide) was shaded twice: +0.4 ms at the brook's lens.
  - **It coarsens with distance** (CDLOD in the brook's coordinates): each vertex carries its place at its own spacing and at two, four and eight times it (`GroundMesh.Rows`, `Sides`), and the vertex shader slides it towards the coarser places from `LOD_ROWS_FROM` (14, the rows) and `LOD_COLUMNS_FROM` (22, the columns), one level each time the distance doubles. A slid vertex lands on another, so the triangles between them close to nothing, and since each vertex slides by its own finest place alone, the mesh never opens. Rows slide towards the arena and columns towards the centreline, so a block's edge never moves and the hole stays covered at every level. It took 0.19 ms off the intro's brook shot and 0.05 to 0.08 off play at High.
  - **Its program never discards** (the island and far-ring clips are the grid's alone), and its chunks of four block rows are drawn **nearest the lens first**: a program that can discard loses the early depth test, and it cost +0.58 ms at the brook's lens.
  - **A chunk outside the lens' frustum is not drawn** (`GroundMesh.ChunkBounds`, a box round the chunk's vertices at every level they slide to, with `BOUNDS_SIDEWAYS`, `_BELOW` and `_ABOVE` to spare; `MeadowCarveTests.EveryChunkHoldsItsVerticesInItsBoxAtEveryLevel` holds every vertex of every chunk and the bed in its box). The vertex work (then 93,161 vertices, `MeadowCarvedVS` 2,445 instruction slots) was paid wherever the lens looked.
  - **On the tiers that supersample (High, Ultra) the depth goes first for the chunks within `DEPTH_FIRST_RANGE` (45) of the lens** (`MeadowCarvedDepth`: the same vertex program and a pixel program that writes nothing, then the field's program on `DepthRead`; the farther chunks are drawn as before, writing their own depth, which the planting after them needs). Every pixel of the cut near the lens is then shaded once whichever way the lens looks along the brook: within a chunk the rows run outward, so for a lens beyond them the triangles come far to near, and over the pond's crater every wall hides the one behind it. Beyond the range a chunk is a few pixels and the second pass of vertex work costs more than it saves. At Low and Medium (frames a third of High's, multisampled) the second rasterisation cost more than it saved (Medium +0.04 to +0.09 ms with it on), so they do not take it.
  - **The bed has a program of its own** (`MeadowBed`): a block whose every vertex lies `BED_UNDER` (0.12) under its water is drawn last with the bed and its light alone. The brook's bed is sand with round pebbles in it (`BrookPebbles`, #979, in both programs), the pond's silt. ⚠ Voronoi cells shaded one stone each, flat or domed by `F2 - F1`, read as crazy paving: the pebbles are discs round the sites (`F1`), and not every cell has one. The water hides most of it, and the field's program worked out flowers, clumps and blades there only to throw them away.
- **The water is a surface of its own** (`MeadowPatchGeometry.Water`, `MeadowWater`): a strip down the brook (`WATER_HALF`, 2.5 either side) and a disc over the pond, flat across, reaching under the margin and the pond's step so the depth test cuts its edge.
  - It is drawn in `DrawGrounded`: after the planting (a reed's foot is under it), before the ceiling's copy, blended premultiplied, reading the depth without writing it. The bed shows through by the water's depth off the cut's own profile, over the view's steepness: the brook runs clear (`BROOK_MURK` 0.6 a unit, into a green body, `BROOK_BODY`), the pond as murky and dark as all the water was (`POND_MURK` 1.8), the mouth's current between them (#979).
  - **The brook flows into the pond as one water** (#970; the owner saw "a sharp straight line in the water" where they met, and a join that does not blend reads as a fault). The two meshes still meet at the shore, each clipping the other's side, so the mouth is never covered twice; but nothing the water is shaded by depends on which of them drew a pixel (`MeadowCarve.WaterShading` is the CPU copy):
    - the depth is the deeper of the brook's bed and the crater wherever both are, as the ground's own `CarveMin` is: the brook's channel runs on under the pond's shallows to about 4.7 units in, and the pond's water over it covers the bed as the brook's does;
    - the brook's current (`MouthCurrent`) slackens over its last `MOUTH_UP` (3) units and carries `MOUTH_INTO` (7) into the pond in a tongue whole across `MOUTH_CURRENT_HALF` (the brook mesh's own half-width, so the brook is all current wherever it draws), fanning out `MOUTH_SPREAD` (0.5) a unit in and fading over `MOUTH_SOFT` (2) at its sides; the running ripples ease into the pond's calm across it, and the lily pads keep out of it;
    - the swale's shade on the sun (`GrooveSun`) lifts over the brook's last `MOUTH_UP` units, on the water and on the ground alike (`MouthGrooveSun`), and is gone at the shore, where the swale's bank has opened into the crater's;
    - `BrookMouth`, the distance at which the centreline crosses the shore, is worked out on the CPU and pushed as a uniform. Away from the mouth every input is exactly the one that water's own terms give: the extra terms sit behind branches on the distance from the arena, and each is exactly nothing at its branch's edge.
  - ⚠ Shading each water by its own mesh's terms (its depth off its own profile, its own ripple slope, its own sun) puts a seam on the shore: the cover falls from about 0.97 to the Fresnel's 0.23 at a 17 degree view and the ripple slope from 0.18 to 0.018, and it reads as a ruled line from every lens that sees the mouth.
  - The ripples, the clouds in it and the lily pads are `WaterReflection`'s and `LilyPad`'s. **The running ripples flow down the brook's own course** (#979): they scroll in its coordinates (distance out, lateral), so they follow every bend and never shear however long the scene runs; until then they ran radially, across the water wherever the course bent. Two layers: a slow one, and a fast one drawn out along the flow, with streaks of foam (`FOAM`), that takes over where the water falls steeply (`BROOK_FAST_FROM` 0.08 to `BROOK_FAST_AT` 0.45 of height a unit, the fall the water's vertex shader works out over two units).
  - Over the swale's last stretch (`BROOK_CARVE_FADE` to `_END`, 168 to 190) the water narrows to two fifths of its width (`BrookWaterHalf`) and fades out as the cut shallows into the field: its spring. The reeds and stones stop at the first.
- **The water falls all the way from its spring to the pond** (#979; the owner: "the water does not flow downhill"). The level follows the ground under the course (`BROOK_DROP` under the lowest of three samples across it), so it can only fall towards the pond if that ground does, and the course is chosen so it does: the pond lies at the foot of the valley the brook comes down (`MeadowPath.BROOK_START` 1.18 of the clearing's radius, 112 out) and the spring short of the crest. `MeadowCarveTests.TheBrookFallsAllTheWayToThePond` holds it. ⚠ At `BROOK_START` 0.82 the pond lay up the slope from the valley's foot, 1.46 over it, so the brook's last thirty units ran uphill into it; and past the crest the brook, painted on to 470 and the horizon, climbed 16 units to the next valley and on over the hills. The brook's planting draws from the meadow's shared stream, so moving it re-rolled what is planted after it (the pond's shore, the island's bank stones, the tufts).
- **How the cut is shaded**: the swale's bank is grass, and its upper part flowers as the field does (only the wet ground within about 0.9 over the water goes without; the pond's whole crater does). The brook's shore (`shore` in `MeadowField`, from `Carve.w`) is earth on the step and a mottled pale gravel on the margin (`GRAVEL`, `GRAVEL_DARK`) up to a ragged line. Anything steep enough, the pond's step above all, is bare earth in layers (`CUT_EARTH`), the bed under the water silt, and the ground just over the waterline dark and wet. That work sits behind a branch on only the pixels it reaches: the grassy banks take none of it. Ambient is occluded by depth (`BROOK_OCCLUSION`, 0.3 in the swale against the bowl's 0.25). A low sun across the swale is blocked by the top of the bank on its own side (`GrooveSun`, per vertex, the top taken where the bank meets level ground): the far bank is lit, the foot of the near one and the water under it are in shade.
- **What stands on it**: the planting stands on the cut (`TerrainMirror.Meadow`). The brook's stones lie on its gravel margin (`STONE_PAST_WATER` plus a share of `BrookMargin`), its reeds at the water's edge from the shallows to the step's top (`REED_FROM_WATER` to `MeadowCarve.BrookShore`), and the pond's reeds in its shallows and on its step. Tufts keep off the water, the margin and the step (`MeadowCarve.OnBrookBank`, by the water's wavering width, not the mean one) and off any of the bank steeper than `TUFT_SLOPE` (0.65). A reed's or a tuft's foot on the swale is sunk by the slope under it (`REED_BASE`, `TUFT_BASE`), or its downhill side hangs in the air. Where to put a tuft is decided on the cut ground on both paths, so the Potato path plants at the same places. A tree, a shrub, a hedge, a boulder or a fence post at the swale's edge is sunk by how much deeper the swale is cut under its base than under its centre (`seat`; `SHRUB_SEAT`, `POST_SEAT`), and not moved: a site refused for it would have drawn every later one anew and moved half the meadow's planting. The chapter intro's runs ride over the **uncut** field (`MeadowGround`), and its two low orbits round the bales and the bench (#893) over the cut one, kept off the water and its banks. The brook shot flies `BROOK_ABOVE` (1.8) over the field, 3 over the water, down the brook's steep stretch (134 to 166 out), and looks at the pond's water down in its crater (`MeadowCarve.PondWaterLevel`).
- **Every tier the meadow is drawn on carves**, Low through Ultra (Low on the reduced program, `MeadowCarvedReduced`). The planting stands on the cut, so a tier that painted the brook would leave its bank stones floating a unit over the paint and its reeds' feet in the air. The patch's own cost is pixels and so follows the tier's resolution, but the play frame's cost is not mostly the patch (below), and Low and Medium are measured on the desktop only, not on the APU. **The Potato path does not carve**: its 15-unit cells would pull a whole cell into a dent wherever a vertex fell into the swale. Its grid and its planting stand on the uncut field (`MeadowGround`), and it paints no brook or pond; its planting is placed where the desktop's is.
- ⚠ **The brook's angle is taken in its centreline's own frame** (`BrookLateral`, in the shader and `MeadowPath`): `atan2` of the point turned by the bearing and the wander, an angle within hundredths of zero near the brook. Taken as `atan2` of the point less the bearing, the GPU's approximation stood up to two thousandths of a unit of lateral off the CPU's 280 units out. The groove's first walls steepened that into a hundredth of height, `mirrorcheck`'s tolerance; on the swale its worst is 0.0007.
- `MeadowCarveTests` hold the rest: the cut ends inside the zone the shader cuts in, the patch covers the whole hole at every level it coarsens to, a vertex slides onto another vertex and a block's corners stay, no bank stands under the water and the water's edge is under the margin, the two waters meet at one level and are one surface through the mouth (`TheWaterIsOneSurfaceThroughTheMouth`: no input of the water's shading steps between neighbours 0.05 apart within 10 of the mouth), every triangle is wound clockwise from above, and every cut and hole constant of the shader is the mirror's.

## The props are closed solids (#943)

The owner, in a close-up of the hay rolls: "a hole along their edges". An end made as a separate disc a hair under the tube's ring leaves a slit round it, open to the hollow, and under back-face culling the sky shows through. `Tools/WindingCheck`'s winding judgement says which way a face points, not whether the solid is shut; `WindingCheck --openness` (#974) and `MeadowMeshClosureTests` count the open edges.

- **A closed prop's ends are made of the tube's own ring.** A separate `AddCap` lays its own polygon, and the smallest disagreement in radius or phase opens a sliver; `AddSweep(capStart/capEnd)` and `BaleGeometry` copy the ring's vertices, so every edge belongs to exactly two triangles. `MeadowMeshClosureTests` count them (`MeshOpenness.OpenEdges`: positions welded, normals and UVs ignored) over every prop the planting builds without a device, and each check has its old construction beside it, seen to find the holes.
- **The hay bale** (`BaleGeometry`, 256 triangles: the tube of `SIDES` 32 and two dished ends; 18 sides showed their corners on an end close to a lens, #971) has **a dish and a polar UV on each end** for the wound-straw shader: the end recedes `DISH` (0.06 of the radius) to its centre along a parabola, in `DISH_STEPS` rings, its normals off that parabola's slope; its UV is `(CAP_U + angle round the axis as a share of a turn, distance from the axis as a share of the radius)`, u from 2 to 3, v from 0 (core) to 1 (rim), where the side's u runs 0 to 1 and its v 0 to 1 along: `uv.x >= 1` is an end. The core's point is one vertex per sector, so u follows the sector. It costs the Potato path 768 vertices a bale (a mesh over 1,200 is a blob there), 14 a scene.
- **The straw** (#971, `Acacia.fx`'s `StrawStrength`, the bales' bucket at 1 with no bark): an end shows the windrow rolled up from a loose, darker core (`STRAW_CORE`) in `STRAW_LAYERS` turns of a spiral, each turn a strip bulging between two seams that wander and come and go, the straws laid along the turns, loose ends catching the light at the rim; the side shows straws running round it and `STRAW_TWINES` bands of sisal twine. On top it is weathered, at the foot damp. ⚠ The end's v is linear across each sector of the polygon, which overstates the radius along a chord: the shader divides that out (`STRAW_SIDES` must be `BaleGeometry.SIDES`), or the spiral is a polygon. ⚠ A strip's tone runs along the strip (its layer plus its turn), not by layer index, which steps by one across u's seam and drew a radial line. Cost: none measurable in the intro's bales shot at High (3840x1581, within 0.05 ms).
- **The fence's rails are capped.** A rail's end ring reaches past the post it ends at, and at the last span of a run no post stands at all: the rail ended in an open pipe. The post's foot is the one open end (buried 0.3 of `FENCE_SCALE` under the ground).
- **Closed, found by the check:** the bench (seven `AddBox`es), the shrubs' lobed masses (`FoliageMesh.Generate`, a sphere with poles), the oaks' crowns and fine wood, and the fine shrubs' stems and branches (#890, both ends from their own rings; their twigs are open only at their base rings, as a tree's). **Open on purpose or buried:** the coarse wood's root rings start inside the trunk (the test holds them under one trunk radius from the axis); a twig's base ring sits at its branch's end (a twig's tip comes to a point, where a ring a quarter of the base's size was open); the grass tufts, reeds and leaf cards are two-sided ribbons. The boulders are a lathe whose buffers cannot be read back: its profile runs from a pole to a pole and shares its rings, so it is shut by construction, and the close-up captures of #943 show no gap. ⚠ **A new prop that ends in a tube ends it with the tube's own ring**, and `MeadowMeshClosureTests` is where its openness is stated.

## The old trees

`MeadowTreeMesh`: oaks, limes and a willow, **grown from the crown inwards**: the dome first, then wood to it (limbs from the fork, boughs from each limb's knee, a branch into every clump), so no clump floats. Trunk and thick boughs are **swept, not stacked** (`TubeGeometry.AddSweep`: stacked end rings met in two planes, a sleeve over the wood). `MeadowTreeMesh.Build` makes the whole tree as lists with no device (`MeadowTreeGeometry`), which the mesh uploads and `MeadowForkTests` reads.

### Two woods (#889)

The owner's playtest note: the trunks and the branches "do not connect up", in the flybys and in play. Both faults were in the photographs.

- **Every tube is closed, on every tier.** A bough leaves its limb's knee thinner than the limb ends and at an angle, so an open end was a hole under back-face culling: the sky showed through every knee. `AddSweep` closes a tube from its own rings (`capStart`, `capEnd`; a separate `AddCap` lays its polygon turned against the ring and leaves slivers open). On Low and Medium only those holes change: against main, 168 to 255 pixels at 1600x900 move by more than the run-to-run noise (2 codes), every cluster at a knee or a root tip. The forest's broadleaf (`ForForest`) shares this wood.
- **`MeadowTreeMesh.Wood` is the coarse wood**, for Low, Medium and the Potato path: the limbs and the leader start on the trunk's axis at the fork, tubes through a cylinder. **`FineWood` is High and Ultra's** (`SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`; the Testbed's `finemeshes=0`), two buckets on the same instances (`ScatterBucket.FineMeshes`). `PlantPass` takes the tier's one on screen and into the sun's map, or a tree casts twice. The Potato path draws a column for the coarse one (its census line is unchanged).
- **The fine wood is the acacia's grown fork** ([The fork](savanna-trees.md#the-fork-grown-for-high-and-ultra-758)) on the meadow's bent, leaning trunk. The `Crotch` swells from `CROTCH_HEIGHT` fork radii under the fork to `CROTCH_SWELL` and takes the shape of the limbs' root circles; a dome (`CROTCH_DOME`) closes it, dipping between limbs (`SADDLE_DIP`). It is fed only by the coarse wood's rolls, so the coarse wood, crowns and leaves are unchanged, and every branch ends where the coarse one does, where its twigs (#697) start.
- **A limb comes out through the trunk's side, it does not leave along it.** Rooted inside the crotch `LIMB_ROOT_DEPTH` fork radii under the fork, it leans `LIMB_SPLAY` out of the surface. ⚠ Leaving along the surface, as the acacia's boughs do, it fell behind the swell, which is fastest at the fork. The rim then stood round it as a ledge, or flush with it and fighting for the depth: a jagged line at the fork from every side. `NoRimStandsRoundALimb` holds the rim at least a hundredth of the trunk inside every limb.
- **Normals are blended by distance, not by ring.** Where a limb crosses the trunk, or a bough or branch its parent, the piece takes the parent's normal at the surface and fades to its own over `LIMB_BLEND` of its radius. This is only done where the two face the same way (`BLEND_FACING`): in the crotch between a piece and its parent they face each other, and the parent's normal shaded the piece from behind. The vertices deep inside a parent keep their own normals.
- **Bends are limited.** An offshoot leaves and arrives no more than `OFFSHOOT_TURN` off its chord, and a stub (`OFFSHOOT_STUB` radii) runs straight. The trunk's ring at the bend is square to the bend's mean, as a sweep's is. Otherwise the rings fold through each other, which `Tools/WindingCheck` reports as wound backwards.
- **Size and cost.** A fine wood is 8 200 to 9 600 triangles against the coarse 3 300 to 4 150. The plugs add 460 to 530 a tree to the coarse one. The fine wood **costs nothing measurable** in the Testbed (reference desktop, `alt=finemeshes=0;finemeshes=1` in one process, 3840 x 1600, `ssaa=2`, `sceneseed=0`, `nopost`, 44 paired cycles). At the play camera on Pennant the difference is −0.03 ms at High and −0.04 at Ultra (`shadowmap=8192`), on frames of 11.2 and 10.2 ms. At the intro's path shot, with the knoll oak a third of the frame, it is −0.06 and −0.05 (8.4 and 7.7 ms). The sign held in 59 to 91 % of cycles, and all four differences are negative, which a wood with 2.3 times the triangles cannot give: the pairs carry an order bias of about 0.05 ms (the second window of a cycle runs a little faster), and the intro runs had another process's exe on the GPU. So read the Testbed pairs as "within 0.05 ms", not as a saving. In the Game on Pennant (3840 x 1581 windowed, `fpscap=1000`, `sceneseed=0 seed=1`, main and the branch alternated twice, the minimum of the run medians after the intro), High goes 11.35 to 11.40 ms and Ultra 11.54 to 11.60, in both repetitions: **about +0.05 ms is the fine wood's own cost**. The plugs are not in it, because High and Ultra never draw the coarse wood. **Low and Medium pay the plugs**: about 500 triangles more a tree kind (3 348 to 3 807, 3 616 to 4 149 and so on, +14 to +18 %), not measured on the laptop or the APU. Potato is unaffected (its column replaces the wood).

- **A twig's base starts inside its branch** (#974): the sweep's first point is two twig radii back along the branch from its end (`AddSpray`'s `backAlong`, the unit direction back along the branch from its end), so the ring that is open is wholly inside the wood whichever way the twig leaves. A ring on the end disc itself, tilted to it, would stand half proud of it. The cards hang off the unmoved curve. The willow's fine wood keeps two such rings and the shrubs two to four, rows of `WindingCheck --openness`'s table with the reason.
- **Crowns are sprays with no ball inside** (#697): each clump has `TWIGS_MIN`..`TWIGS_MAX` twigs from its branch end over its shell (the detail-only `MeadowTreeMesh.Twigs`) and leaf cards hung along their outer part (`LEAVES_FROM`), **each card's stem on its twig**. `Acacia.fx`'s `BroadLeafMask` cuts sprigs of broad leaves. The Low tier keeps solid lumps, the only place a ball is drawn.
- ⚠ **`BroadLeafMask` drew a solid rectangle at every twig's tip**: `step(p.x, 0.74)` is zero past the stem's end, not negative, and `clip()` keeps a zero; it is -1 now (the acacia's `LeafMask` had the same fault).
- ⚠ **A sprig lies along its clump's shell** (`SPRIG_FLATTEN`) **and its front faces out whatever the jitter did** (`face . shell >= 0`): `Acacia.fx` shades a card seen from behind by its flipped normal, and sprigs pointing straight out made a third of the sunny side shade as if facing in.
- **The leaves cast the crown's shade** (the caster clips the card mask: dappled). ⚠ **Nothing solid inside a crown may cast**: anything inside a caster is in shadow from every direction and reads as a dark ball. The Low tier's lumps cast because they are what is drawn; at tiers that do not draw them they must not, or every clump shades its own sunny side.

## The shrubs (#890)

The owner's playtest note: the shrubs were "green loaves - on High and Ultra these should be more detailed shrubs"; the rows of them in the distance may stay as they are. The references are in `C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\890-klein`, `890-zimage`, `890b-klein` and `890b-zimage` (a free-standing hawthorn or hazel at eye level and from a low hill, the base where its stems meet the grass): a rounded but lobed mass of separate leaf clusters, the sky between them at the rim, the inside and the underside in shade, and under it a vase of thin pale stems out of one spot in the grass, the grass grown up round them, the rim's leaves hanging down nearly to it.

- **A shrub is a `FoliageMesh` in `FoliageStyle.Scrub` (the loaf) on every tier and in every hedgerow.** Three variants, 55 free-standing (90 to 460 units out) and about 240 in five hedgerows.
- **On High and Ultra a free-standing shrub is a `MeadowShrubMesh`** (`SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`; the Testbed's `finemeshes=0` draws the loaf): five to seven stems, 13 to 17 clumps, each clump the old trees' spray (`MeadowTreeMesh.AddSpray`, broad-leaf cards on twigs, `LeafStrength` 2) at `SHRUB_COVER` and `SHRUB_CARD` (denser, and smaller leaves against the clump, than a tree's) on three to five twigs. **Grown from the crown inwards**: the clumps are laid over a dome first (`DOME_CENTRE`, `DOME_HALF`, `DOME_LOWEST`, `DOME_WIDTH`, a few broad lobes round the bearing, one or two lifted on top), each stem is grown to the clump it leads to, and every other clump takes a branch from the nearest point of a stem under it, so no clump floats and no stem ends in the air. A clump set inside the dome (`INNER_SHARE`) or under its middle (`UNDERSIDE`) takes the inside's layer code, the darkest ambient: the shrub is dark under its skirt as the references are. The leaves cast (dappled, the caster clips the card); the wood does not. Every stem and branch is closed at both ends from its own rings, the foot buried as it is ("The props are closed solids" above).
- **The base**: the stems leave the ground within `ROOT_SPREAD` of the axis and reach `FOOT_DEPTH` under the pivot, which is sunk `SHRUB_SINK` (0.3) under the ground at the shrub's centre, so the base is one rooted spot whatever the slope and the scale (0.8 to 1.4). - **The draws** are `MeadowScatter.SHRUB_DRAWS`: every shrub's loaf where `FineMeshes` is off, in the one bucket a variant and the one place in the list it always had, so Low, Medium and the Potato path draw what they drew; at High and Ultra the hedgerows' loaves (a bucket of their own on the same instances), the fine shrubs' wood, their leaves and the foot grass. `ScatterBucket.DrawnAt` is the one rule `PlantPass` and `MeadowShrubTests` read.

## Flowers and the grass

- ⚠ **Roll on the cell's integer seed** (`HashCell`, `HashSalt`, `HashBits` in `Noise.fxh`), never `Hash21(cell + k)`: on whole-number cells that hash is periodic (`frac(p * 123.34)` is `frac(p * 0.34)`) and repeated the small flowers every 40 units, the big ones every 110 and every lily pad (#674).
- Flowers drift in `FLOWER_PATCH`es, each mostly one of five kinds, mostly small (`FLOWER_SMALLEST`..`FLOWER_LARGEST`).
- **A rosette has a shape of its own** (#127): petal domes and a boss eye summed into the height field the grass relief feeds `PerturbNormalFromHeight`, the grass's share faded out under it (#283). ⚠ **The weight is the smooth `petalProfile`, NOT `flowerMask`**: the mask is antialiased over a pixel and a half, and in a *differentiated* field that puts a pixel-wide step in the derivative and lights a hard ring round every rosette.
- **The grass has a material of its own** (#281; it read as green stone), each term a `MeadowSceneConfig` dial: clumps, tips and hollows (read off the relief that tilts the normal), blades seen from the side, dry patches, the velvet `GrassSheenStrength` (0.55 made the far hills pastel) and the glow `GrassTranslucency`. ⚠ **The clump seam is the zero set of TWO noises** (#674): one gradient noise is exactly zero at every integer lattice point, so its crossings tie a lattice of dark spots (what the lattice probe's `meadow-down` flagged).
- **`MeadowReduced`** (the Game's Low tier via `SceneRenderer.SceneDetail`) drops the clump seams and blade strokes; check it draws with `detail=0`.

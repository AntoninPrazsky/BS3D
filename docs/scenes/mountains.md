# The mountains

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

A snow basin ringed by tall snow-capped peaks that rise with distance into alpine haze. `SceneKind.Mountain`, `scene=mountain`; all ten mountain levels name dome 8. Its frame cost is in [The mountain's frame cost](mountain-cost.md).

## What it is made of

- **`MountainBackdrop`** with **`Mountain.fx`** (`MountainSceneConfig`); the snow is `Snow.fx` through `Snowfall` (below).
- **Terrain**: the desert's machinery with another field: the shared `AcquireGridMesh` grid (`MOUNTAIN_GRID_N`, [Terrain grids](terrain-grids-and-mirrors.md); High and Ultra's is twice as fine, see below) displaced by `RidgedFbm2` (five octaves, 130-unit first spacing), low round the origin and rising with distance (`ClearingRadius`/`ClearingTransition`) so the basin stays below the platform glass. The base normal is three finite-difference taps per vertex; a band-limited `RockRelief` roughens it per pixel; the range dissolves over `HorizonHazeDistance`.
- ⚠ **Any terrain grid over 255 a side needs 32-bit indices** (`TerrainGridCache` builds them): 16-bit wraps past 65,536 vertices, and the tall peaks stretched the wrong triangles into regular dark bands across the whole sky, which read as a glare artefact and cost a long hunt down the wrong path.
- **Massing** (#86). ⚠ **Never plane-wave sines**: summed `1 - |sin|` waves keep their planes and have no high frequencies, so peaks were smooth cones. `RidgedFbm2` cannot go in raw (a white blanket): a 0.24 floor taken out and the rest **cubed** restores the height distribution `Height` and the snowline were tuned against. **Massifs**: one octave at `MASSIF_SPACING` scales height (`MASSIF_LOW`..`MASSIF_HIGH`, mirrored in `TerrainMirror`), or a ridged field is a saw of equal crests.

## Rock, snow and the light

- **Snow and rock are two materials with a crisp edge**: the facing band `RockSlope`..`SnowSlope` and the altitude snowline are each read through a second narrow smoothstep (`SNOW_EDGE`); read linearly, every mid-steep face was a lilac blend. The rock is near black (`RockColor`, `RockColorLight`); **the basin floor is snow**, and **so is every gentle valley floor below the snowline** (#959, the owner: snow is missing "during the fly-throughs in the lowlands"): below it the same sharp band, moved up by `LowlandSnowSlope` (0.15), decides, so the floors are white and the foot of every flank stays rock; a lower snowline would have whitened the flanks too. The Potato path's per-triangle rule takes the same band (`PotatoBackdrop.Mountain`). Cost: within 0.06 ms at High, 3840x1581, on level 31's intro and play.
- ⚠ **Up-facing surfaces take 75 % zenith and 25 % horizon ambient** (`0.25 + 0.5 * (n.y * 0.5 + 0.5)`), not the zenith alone: dome 8's saturated violet zenith turned the snow floor blue-violet.
- **Steep faces are fluted** (`FluteSnow`: two gradient noises stretched down world Y shift the snow's threshold). ⚠ **Not by combing the rock relief down the fall line**: a combed domain is rotated about the world origin, so at every grid facet the coordinate jumps (mesh-following chevrons). ⚠ **Per vertex, not per pixel**: per pixel cost 0.9 ms of a 10.0 ms frame, still 0.3 behind a data branch (the branch keeps the registers).
- **Alpenglow**: at a low sun only the heights take the light (the sun's share climbs from `AlpenglowLow` to `AlpenglowHigh`); not a cast shadow.
- **Rock relief**: four octaves of `Fbm2Combed` with a grain (`ROCK_GRAIN`, `ROCK_STRETCH`); the old four plane-wave sines laid a plaid over the snow. The config frequency is an *angular* one and goes in as `f / 2pi`; amplitude is bought back by the shader gain `ROCK_FBM_GAIN`, not `RockReliefStrength` (hand-built levels pin it), doubled when the relief turned out to have been applied twice since #208 (#504).
- **Snow's sparkle** (`SnowSparkle`, gated on the snow mask). ⚠ **The lattice cell says only where a glint is; its size comes from the footprint**: a world-fixed cell is 30 pixels on the basin floor and lit white parallelograms across the ground nearest the player (#278). The drift relief was **cut at every tier** (#296: 1.08 ms of 10.99, indistinguishable photographed; partial cuts bought proportionally less on an occupancy-bound pass). **`MountainReduced`** (`detail=0`, Low) drops the sparkle and shades the rock on three octaves: 0.18 ms at `ssaa` 1, 0.58 at 2.

## Low's range on the reference APU (#1035)

The range was the one chapter over `Low`'s 16.1 ms budget (Spyglass 16.95 ms in #1026's table; `Low`'s far plane stays, by the owner's ruling). Taken apart with the Testbed's `mountain=` dial at `Low`'s settings (one process, `campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,6,-60`, 35 cycles): of a 13.27 ms frame the range is **5.48 ms**, the snowfall 0.31, the far ring 0.15 and the bank's planting nothing. Two cuts:

- **The grid is drawn only where the lens looks** ("The terrain grids and the billboards" in [Terrain grids](terrain-grids-and-mirrors.md)): Spyglass −0.63 ms.
- **`MountainReduced` takes the rock relief at one octave** (three before; the authored program's four and the fine program's stay). In one process: two octaves −0.47 ms, one −0.96, none −1.61. The far ring past the grid draws the reduced program on every tier, where the relief is band-limited away; High's captures differ only by the flakes.

Both together, in the Game beside the build before them: Spyglass **17.32 → 15.44 ms** and Belfry 18.80 → 16.85 at `Low`, Belfry −0.63 at `Medium`.

## High and Ultra's range (#781)

**`SceneRenderer.FineMeshes` (High, Ultra) draws a third program, `MountainFine`, on a grid twice as fine** (`FINE_GRID_N` 720, 1.67 units a cell, 518 400 vertices; its own `TerrainPass`, built on the first fine draw and kept, so switching tiers rebuilds nothing). Low and Medium draw `Mountain` and `MountainReduced` on the 360 grid exactly as before: `finemeshes=0` matches the previous build at four pinned cameras but for the falling snow.

- **What limited the detail was the projection first, then the octaves.** Measured at four pinned Testbed cameras (3840x1600, `ssaa` 2) as Laplacian-band contrast over the rock, against img2img references over the same frames. The relief, the rock's patches and its grain were sampled on world XZ, so on a face steeper than 45 degrees every feature was stretched down the fall line by 1/normal.y. The peaks' horizontal gradients outweighed the vertical 1.4–1.6 to 1 at 2–16 px, where klein's references measure 0.75–1.0. Below that, detail stopped at the finest relief octave (1.3 units): the band contrast at 2 px was a tenth to a fifth of the 32 px band's, where klein's references keep a third to a half. `MountainFine` keeps 0.5 to 0.95 of it, four to seven times the old fine-band contrast, with the gradients 0.75–1.0 to 1 as in the references. The mesh was the third limit: the field's finest octave is 8 units, so every crest was a straight edge between vertices.
- ⚠ **Z-Image at img2img strength 0.5 keeps the game's own smoothness** (its fine bands measured only 1.5–2 times the game's). Compare frequency content against klein's references, which redraw the rock.
- **The geometry.** A sixth ridged octave, **centred** on the ridge's mean (`RIDGE_MEAN` 0.75), so it breaks crests without lifting the range: a ridged octave is never negative, and carried on uncentred it raised every crest by 1.7 units on average and 7.3 at most. Two octaves of creased crags over the upper flanks (`Crags`, `CRAG_SHARE` times the field squared, `CRAG_MEAN` the mean of `|n|`). Second flutes at half the spacing. Neither is in `TerrainMirror`: the probe's drawn height is the fine field, and `mirrorcheck` reports how far it stands off the mirror (see below). The chapter intro's lenses keep 10–14 units of clearance over the mirror.
- **The rock in three dimensions.** `RockRelief3` is six octaves of 3D gradient noise, from the authored scale down to 0.33 units, the finest three creased (`-|n|`, joints rather than dimples), squashed across the beds. Its footprint is `length(fwidth(worldPosition))`, the pixel's true size; the XZ one is a fraction of it on a cliff. `StrataLedges` steps the face back at the top of each bed into a narrow up-facing tread. The snow decision reads the tilted normal, so the snow lies on the ledges and in the creases without a term of its own. `BedTone`, `Laminae` (fine light and dark layers inside a bed), patches and grain are 3D as well. The beds' warped coordinate, the ledges' run and the patches are coarse enough for the fine grid, so the vertex shader evaluates them (`BedField`) and the pixel shader reads them interpolated.
- ⚠ **A ledge on every bed is a zebra.** With a 20-degree dip and every bed ledged alike, the range came out banded like contour lines. The beds dip about 40 degrees (`STRATA_UP`) and wander (`STRATA_WARP`, two octaves). Each bed rolls its own ledge depth (squared, so most are slight) and width off its index, and a ledge shows over about a third of its bed (`run`). The step is zero at both ends of a bed, so a bed's own figures change at a boundary without a seam.
- **Faded by the footprint like everything else**: a ledge as its tread nears two pixels, a lamina near three, the relief's octaves as their period nears the pixel.
- ⚠ **The program never calls `clip()`, and that is why it costs less than the authored one.** A pixel shader that may discard loses the depth test before it, so the range was shaded under every ridge in front of it: the fine program measured +3.05 ms with the clip and −4.12 ms without, beside the authored one in the same process. So the island's hole is cut in the vertex shader (`InHole`: every vertex inside `IslandHoleRadius` goes to one point 400 units under the basin, and the cone that makes is painted black below `HOLE_WALL_DARK`, the only place it shows being down the drain's throat), the far ring keeps its clip in its own technique (`MountainFineReduced`, the authored ring), and the grid's quads run near first (`TerrainGridCache`, `nearFirst`). ⚠ **Not onto the hole's circle**: three corners on a circle are a triangle across it, and that cut laid snow across the drain. The authored and reduced programs still clip, so Low and Medium pay that overdraw.
- **What it costs: less than the authored program it replaces.** Desktop (RX 6900 XT), 3840x1600, `ssaa` 2. Testbed `alt=finemeshes=0;finemeshes=1`, 41 paired cycles, the sign held in 98–100 %: Belfry's play camera −3.26 ms at High (14.15 → 10.88), −3.58 at Ultra (`shadowmap=8192`, 15.44 → 12.38); the pinned near face −3.83, the peak −2.52. The Game on Belfry, the cleaner of two alternated rounds: High 14.73 → 11.31 ms, Ultra 14.67 → 11.32. The pixel shader is where it would cost: the six 3D relief octaves measured about 1 ms each while the program still clipped, and moving the beds' coarse fields to the vertex shader took 4.6 ms off it.
- **`mirrorcheck`**: the mirror still passes (max 0.00075); the drawn ground stands off it by 4.88 at most and 0.45 on average, nearly all of it below (on a numpy copy of the field, at most 3.4 units above), well inside the intro's clearances.

## The sky (#661)

Dome 8 is **generated** (colour by each vertex's height, stops in `SkyDome.Data.cs`) after a painted violet dusk made the whole scene violet: the rig reads the dome's top quarter as sky ambient and its bottom fifth as the sun's tint. ⚠ **That bottom fifth is the colour of the light on every lit face** (`KeyTint = lerp(1, HorizonLinear, SKY_TINT_STRENGTH)`): a peach there turned the range to sandstone, so it is a near-white rose.

## The snowfall (#654)

`Snow.fx` is a static buffer of billboard flakes animated in the vertex shader (fall, wind, sway, `frac` wrap in a box that follows the camera: it never pops). Drawn last, alpha-blended, depth-read, no depth write. Each scene (the mountain; the aurora with a gentler config and part of the buffer) draws through **its own clone** of `Snow.fx`; `Snowfall.Prepare` returns it with parameters cached. Dials: `MountainSceneConfig.Snow` (`SnowConfig`).

- ⚠ **`BlendState.AlphaBlend` is premultiplied.** The shader returned a straight colour, so a flake added its whole colour and no feather or opacity softened it: the "crisp crystal icons". It premultiplies now; the sea's spray and the storm's clouds share the shape.
- **A flake is what a lens makes of it**: nearer than `Focus` it is blurred by up to `Aperture`, **its alpha falling with the area it spreads over**; nearer than a quarter of `NearFade` it is dropped; it streaks over `Shutter` and is never under one pixel radius (0.6 px shimmered). The silhouette is a six-armed tumbling crystal (`cos(6 * theta)` by the double-angle identity: no `atan2` seam).
- **Colour sits just under `GLARE_THRESHOLD`**, darker than a bright sky. ⚠ Dim the colour, not the opacity, if flakes ever glow. `FlakeCount` 9000, drawn twice (near box, and `FarLayerScale` times larger as the distant veil).
- **The terrain takes a snowfall veil** (`SnowVisibility`: `1 - exp(-(d - 60) / SnowVisibility)`); haze and veil go to the colour **the dome is drawn in behind the pixel** (`FarSkyAt`, #551), not `HorizonColor`, which painted the range pink.
- Cost +0.06 ms (Game, `level=Belfry`, 1920x1080, `quality=high`, `nocap`); the play pin (`campos=0,2,45 camtarget=0,-4,0`, dome 8, `nopost nooverc`) is about 10.0 ms at 23 Mpix.

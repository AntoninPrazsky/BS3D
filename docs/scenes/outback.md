# The outback

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

## What it is

The thirteenth `SceneKind` (#112, the horizon rebuilt in #967): red monoliths on a wide near-flat spinifex plain, buttressed loaves (Uluru) and clusters of domes (Kata Tjuta), the island in a clearing among them, under a deep blue sky of its own.

- Code: `Outback.fx`, `OutbackBackdrop`, `OutbackRockDiscs`, `OutbackSceneConfig` (groups Terrain, Surface, Air, Birds; the shape is explained in [Space](space.md)), the CPU mirror `TerrainMirror.Outback`. The plain's camera grid is `OUTBACK_GRID_N` 400 over `OUTBACK_EXTENT` 1000; over 255 vertices a side, so the grid cache's 32-bit index buffer is load-bearing.
- Launch: `scene=outback`. No shipped level names it; players meet it as a front-end start and in the Extras scene picker.
- `IsSolidTerrainScene`. **It brings its own dome, 21** (`SkyDome.SceneDefault`): a deep blue zenith paling to a warm white skyline over a gold sun at 21 degrees (azimuth -125, to the left of the opening view, so the formations' shadows fall across the plain towards it). The bottom fifth, the light rig's `HorizonColor` and so the sun's tint, is dome 14's gold a shade warmer. ⚠ Every one of the other twenty drew the outback garish (teal, lilac, magenta or olive over red ground) or flat. ⚠ **Judge its colour in the Game**: the Testbed draws this dome teal-green over a yellow skyline (it does the same to domes 1 and 14), so a Testbed capture says nothing about the outback's colours, only its shapes. It shares the [flock of birds](birds.md).
- ⚠ `ProceduralAmbience.BakeScene`'s `default:` arm is a deliberately near-silent neutral, so a new scene with no bed is audibly missing (the arm once played cave drips on the Moon and vacuum here).
- The Potato path has no outback backdrop: the Pi draws only the island.

## One height field, two kinds of mesh

`OutbackHeight` is the land everywhere (the plain's swell, the monoliths, the boulder outcrops) and `TerrainMirror.Outback` copies it term for term (`mirrorcheck`). Two kinds of mesh draw it:

- **The plain**: the camera grid and the [far ring](shadows-and-far-field.md), technique `Outback`. They carry the swell alone (`PlainHeight`) and clip every pixel inside a formation's **footprint**, an ellipse along its axis (`InsideFootprint`: four hashes and no noise). So the plain, most of every frame, pays for neither rock lattice.
- **The rock**: `OutbackRockDiscs`, one polar disc mesh instanced over every footprint (`TerrainMirror.OutbackFootprints`), technique `OutbackRock`, displaced by the whole field. Four instanced draws: monoliths within 650 of the arena on a fine disc (160 x 80) on High and Ultra (`SceneRenderer.FineMeshes`) and on the coarse one below, farther ones on the coarse one (96 x 40), boulders within 450 on 48 x 16 and farther on 24 x 8, every footprint within 1450 (`SEARCH_RADIUS`). The split by distance is made once at build, so a formation never changes mesh as the camera moves. **Only the footprints in the view are drawn**: each frame they are tested against the camera's frustum and copied into a dynamic instance buffer, because a disc's vertex shader runs the whole height field and most footprints stand behind or beside the lens. A boulder's disc clips where it overlaps a monolith's footprint, so no point is drawn twice (`OutbackRockCuts`, the rock's one cut). How the four draws are drawn on High and Ultra is [below](#how-the-formations-are-drawn-1137).
- ⚠ **Why the rock left the grid.** A near-vertical wall needs several vertices across its fall: the grid's 2.5-unit cells held a wall no narrower than 0.30 of a formation's radius (narrower drew horizontal ripples, one per row of the grid), and past the camera grid (some 500 units from the lens) the far ring's 432 cells round the arena are about fifteen pixels each at 1080p, so a far formation came out as a flat-topped trapezoid with 45-degree sides, the "hats" of #967.
- **No crack and no line where they meet.** A footprint holds its formation apron included, so the land at its edge is the plain on both meshes. Each disc instance is the ellipse scaled by 1/cos(pi/segments) (its rim's chords circumscribe it) plus 0.5 units, and the grid stops on the exact ellipse: the two overlap by a sliver of flat plain and no ray can pass between them.
- The disc is a height-field patch drawn two-sided like the grids, not a solid, so `WindingCheck --openness` does not list it.

## How the formations are drawn (#1137)

The rock's program is the dearest in the scene: `ShadeOutback` with `rocky` takes the base normal off three taps of the whole `OutbackHeight` field (formations, buttresses and gullies), then the varnish, the bounce and the light. It may discard (the boulders' cut), so it writes its depth only after it has run and nothing inside its own draw is rejected early; and the discs are drawn two-sided, so a loaf's far wall and whatever one formation hides of another were shaded before what hides them.

- **On High and Ultra each pixel of a monolith is shaded once** (`OutbackRockDiscs.Draw`). The monoliths' depth is laid first with `OutbackRockDepth` (their vertex program and no colour, `TerrainGridCache.DEPTH_ONLY`), then the four draws are shaded in their order, the monoliths on that depth with `DepthStencilState.DepthRead`: a hidden pixel fails the test before the program runs. It is the grids' pattern (#1097, [Terrain grids](terrain-grids-and-mirrors.md)) with one difference: **the twin makes no cut.** The rock's one cut (`OutbackRockCuts`) is a boulder's and never fires on a monolith (`Outcrop` 0), and the twin is laid for the monoliths alone, so the laid depth is the only judge of what is drawn there and the depth pass keeps the early test inside its own draw (a twin that cut cost 0.10 ms more at the pin below). ⚠ So a cut added to the rock must spare the monoliths, or High and Ultra get a hole where the depth is laid and nothing is shaded; `TerrainDepthTwinSourceTests` holds the rock's program to its one `clip()`, that clip to the boulders' flag (`Outcrop`, which each instance takes from its group), the twin to none and the depth pass to the monoliths' groups.
- **The boulders are one pass on every tier.** Their cut fires, and laid by a twin it would be computed by two programs compiled apart, the risk #1130 holds open for the grids. Below High, and on OpenGL ES (no twin is compiled there), every formation is the one pass it always was. `outback=all/-rockdepth` draws High's formations in that one pass (`OutbackLayer.RockDepth`).
- **The picture is unchanged, bit for bit.** A pixel keeps the last fragment drawn at the nearest depth (`LessEqual`) and the groups are shaded in their order either way, so laying the depth first moves nothing; `OutbackRockPS` compiles to the instructions it had on both profiles. `alt=outback=all;outback=all/-rockdepth` draws both ways in one process, so a pair of captures from one camera compares them; two launches never match at the game-mode camera, whose magazine is rolled each launch.
- ⚠ **The cut stays in the one program.** The monoliths drawn with a program of their own without it, so that their draw would keep early rejection below High too, compiled `ShadeOutback` to other instructions and moved pixels: up to 153 of a 1600x900 frame by 1/255 at `ssaa=2`, 82 by up to 10/255 at `ssaa=1`. It would have bought 0.46 ms at `ssaa=1`'s eight samples (the formations 1.78 to 1.32 at the pin below) and 0.06 on top of the depth laid first, so `OutbackRockPS` keeps the instructions it had.
- ⚠ **The discs' back faces are not culled.** From any lens above the ground a back face lies behind a front one, but at a far formation's crown the two land on one depth value (24 bits, the near plane at 0.01) and `LessEqual` keeps whichever was drawn last: culled, 293 to 752 pixels of each 1600x900 view moved, by up to 75/255.
- **What it saves, 2.1 ms of High and Ultra's frame at #1102's pin** (desktop, RX 6900 XT, Testbed Release, `scene=outback sceneseed=0 fpscap=1000 width=3840 height=1500`, the game-mode camera `at=3:F10`, dome 21, `ssaa=2`, `Maps/Full.json`; one process a figure, `alt=outback=all;outback=all/-rockdepth;outback=all/-formations;outback=none` in settled windows, 28 to 33 cycles, each variant differenced inside its cycle, the sign held in 97 % of the cycles or more): the frame **8.13 → 6.02 and 6.03 ms** (two processes each), the formations 3.26 → 1.17, every layer of the scene 2.05; `outback=all/-rockdepth` reads +2.09, within 0.02 of the build before's frame. The empty field the same: the frame 6.89 → 4.80, the formations 3.26 → 1.17, every layer 4.14 → 2.05. **The Game's front end at Ultra about 1 ms** (Game Release, `scene=outback preview=1 quality=ultra fpscap=1000 sceneseed=0`, a 3840x1500 window, a scratch profile, four runs a build alternated A B B A A B B A): the median of seconds 9 to 47 **8.56 to 8.94 → 7.47 to 7.95 ms**; over seconds 2 to 17, where every run's orbit is at the same place, 8.07 to 8.13 → 7.08 to 7.13, each second 0.64 to 1.26 cheaper. The dearest second 10.0 to 10.9 → 9.6 to 10.8.

## The monoliths

- **Placed on a jittered single-cell lattice** (`RockRoll`/`RollRock`): a formation is held inside its cell by its full reach, so only the pixel's own cell is read. `RockSpacing` 270, `RockChance` 0.70, radius 0.10-0.16 of the cell, elongation to 1.8; a rotated second lattice carries the boulders (`OutcropSpacing` 110). ⚠ **The cell shrank rather than the formations growing** (#171): the margin already sits at 0.44 against the 0.45 clamp, so a larger fraction breaks the single-cell read. #967 moved no formation: the kind is a new roll (`RollF`).
- **The clearing gates the rock per FORMATION**, from its centre (`ClearingRadius` 95): per pixel, a radial ramp slices a monolith and draws it half sunk. The plain's swell is not ramped, so no field with a mean meets a radial ramp.
- **Two kinds**, rolled per formation (`ClusterChance` 0.4):
  - **A loaf** (`Bornhardt`): a superellipse crown, `sqrt(1 - t^p)` with p from 2.0 to 3.4, multiplied to nothing over the last `LOAF_WALL` 0.20 of the radius, and a second lobe for a shoulder. ⚠ At squareness 5 a far loaf read as a table.
  - **A cluster**: three to five round domes (radius 0.46-0.62 of the formation's, p 2.0-2.5, wall 0.42) turned round the centre by the golden angle, the first nearest the middle and the tallest; their union is a max, so the cleft between two is a V of their own walls. `ClusterHeight` 1.0. Each dome's rolls are arithmetic on the cell's rolls, not a hash of their own, and the golden angle is a rotation by literal cosine and sine: ⚠ a hash of a folded constant or a GPU `sin` would move a dome off its mirror.
- **The buttresses.** The gully field is gradient noise on the **unit circle** of the formation's frame (no seam or pole, unlike an `atan2` count), `RibCount` 3.2. A gully is where it crosses zero: `(1 - saturate(|n| x 2.4))^2` has a cusp there and rounds onto the face, so each buttress is a rounded column and each gully a sharp V (every reference drew this). They cut the radius by up to `RibDepth` 0.18, fade out towards the crest, and reach the silhouette. ⚠ The outcrops carry no gullies: at 25 across and 5 tall they drew a starfish.
- **The apron** is 0.05 of the height (`APRON_SHARE`), off the unribbed radius: at 0.10 a far loaf read as a hat with a brim.
- The colour selector's weights must land inside 0…1 or the near monolith pins to `RockColorBright`; the surface field folds in **world height**, since an XZ field is constant down a vertical face.

## The formations' shadows

- **Baked once per sun on the GPU** (`OutbackBackdrop.BeforeShadowMaps`, technique `OutbackSunBake`): a `HalfSingle` target over 3072 units round the arena (2048 a side on High and Ultra, 1.5 units a texel; 1024 on Low and Medium), each texel walking 48 steps towards the sun through `OutbackHeight` with a soft edge. It runs in `Backdrop.BeforeShadowMaps`, the first thing `SceneRenderer.DrawShadowMaps` does, before any target of the frame is bound, and again only when the sun moves (a dome change) or the bake's size does (`Services.FineMeshes` turning over with the tier).
- Both meshes read it (`TerrainSunShadow`, 0.9 of the sun) and take the darker of it and the sun's [shadow map](shadows-and-far-field.md), which still holds the island, the gun and the bank near the lens. A sun under `SHADOW_MIN_SUN_HEIGHT` bakes nothing.
- ⚠ **Only the land receives it**: the island, the gun and the cluster read the sun's map alone, which the formations do not cast into. Under dome 21 no formation's shadow reaches the island; a dome with a low sun from another side could lay one up to the island's edge and leave the island lit inside it.

## The surface

- **Desert varnish** is a threshold of `GradientNoise3` over (bearing, height), behind a `[branch]` on `wallGate * rockMask`.
  - ⚠ Streaks run on the formation's **bearing** (a cluster's, on the dome that holds the point): a world-position field breaks into blobs on a leaning wall. A monolith's body always owns the fields it hands back, or a boulder on its wall fans them into a starburst.
  - ⚠ `rib` is high in a gully, and varnish and occlusion take the sign that puts them there.
  - Band-limit off the **view ray** (`dist × fwidth(towardsEye)`), not the surface, whose `fwidth` steps per facet.
  - `VarnishGloss` 0.04 (0.5 painted sunlit streaks white; a glossy plain reads wet), and `VarnishColor` is a warm near-black, because a neutral grey out-reflects a red wall under blue domes.
- **`SoilBounce`** 0.35 (light off `SoilColorPale`, weighted `1 − normal.y`): without it shaded walls go near black under a low sun.
- **The spinifex is cellular**, and a raw `Voronoi2` is a honeycomb. ⚠ Both fixes are needed: warp the domain at about a third of the hummock spacing, and let the same field jitter the threshold. Band-limit the **rim**, not the hummock, and fade to the field's **average cover**, not to bare sand.
- ⚠ Tussocks read as beans until the relief became a low dome `(1 − x²)²` (`SpinifexRelief` 0.06) with a frayed outline and a colour lighter than the sand (darker read as pits).
- **The crust** (#776, `GroundDetail`, on at High and Ultra): the bare earth between the tussocks cracks into plates (`VoronoiEdge2`, `CRACK_SCALE` 2.4 plates a unit, warped by one noise) in patches, and is mottled over a few metres, as the references (`776-klein`, `776-zimage`) draw it. Never on a tussock or on the rock. ⚠ **It is computed only where a plate spans `CRUST_PIXELS` (6) pixels and whole at twice that**: out to a plate in three pixels it cost a low lens 0.7 ms (Testbed 3840x1600, ssaa 2), and past it the network is an even texture. Now +0.55 ms at that lens (`campos=-20,-9,40 camtarget=-35,-12,60`: 11.97 against 11.42), and nothing in the front end's orbit, which sees little near ground.
- ⚠ **The gibber is geometry, not paint** (`OutbackPlanting`): drawn in the ground with a lit top and a cast shadow, a stone with no silhouette still read as a hole in the earth at every low angle.

## What grows on the plain (#749)

The owner: "there is no vegetation at all", only the painted spinifex. `OutbackPlanting` stands on the plain what the references (`749-zimage`, `749-klein`) stand on red earth, through the scene's `PlantPass`, casting into the sun's map: spinifex hummocks (the bulk), silvery saltbush, bleached grey timber lying about, and a few mulga and dead trees (`AcaciaMesh`'s young and dead kinds), two thirds of them at a formation's foot. Nothing stands on a footprint (`TerrainMirror.OutbackFootprints`, the ellipses the plain stops on) or on the island's bank. Its own seed, so nothing else moves.

- **A hummock is a dome of needles** (`OutbackPlanting.SpinifexMesh`): thin triangles from one root out to the dome, each emitted once per face, its normal half the face's and half the dome's, so the hummock shades as a mound and each needle still reads.
- ⚠ **`PlantPass` draws every instance every frame, into the scene and into the sun's map.** At one 180-needle mesh for all of them the first cut cost the plain 2.5 ms. So the hummocks are dense within `SPINIFEX_NEAR_TO` (140) and sparse past it, where the painted spinifex carries the plain; the near band has `NEEDLES` (130) at High and Ultra and the far band's `FAR_NEEDLES` (50) below that; the far band always has 50; every hummock casts as a `CASTER_NEEDLES` (24) stand-in.
- **The saltbush is the sea grape's build** ([The tropical beach](tropical.md), #753): the scrub dome at Low, Medium and on the Potato path, the meadow's shrub with round leaves at High and Ultra.
- ⚠ The timber was bone-white at first, and a log with its stubs read as a toy aeroplane on the red earth: it is weathered grey (`TIMBER`).
- **The gibber** (#776, High and Ultra): 4,600 small faceted stones (the volcano's `BlockMesh`, twenty faces) out to 180 units, dense to 90, iron red-brown and dusted, casting. Planted last, so it moves nothing above it. About 0.3 ms at the low lens.
- **Cost**, the Game's front end as in the table below (one run each, `sceneseed=3`): `High` 11.95 against 11.45 ms, `Medium` 5.11 against 4.83, `Low` 3.83 against 3.79. In the Testbed at 3840x1600, ssaa 2: a low camera among the hummocks (`campos=-20,-9,40 camtarget=-35,-12,60`) 11.14 against 9.97, the plain from above (`campos=40,10,60 camtarget=120,-12,180`) 14.37 against 13.93.

## The air

**An extinction by distance and nothing else** (#967): the light from a surface falls off as `exp(-d / HazeDistance)` (6000) and the air replaces it, the colour the dome is drawn at the skyline (`FarSkyAt(0)`, what a horizontal ray of endless air looks like) leaned towards `HazeTint`'s hue by `DustStrength` 0.3. The plain 300 units out is a twentieth air, and a formation 1000 units out keeps 0.85 of its own colour. **Nothing in the air moves**: the effect has no clock (`OutbackAirTests`). What moves at the skyline is a pass of its own that reads the drawn frame, [the heat shimmer](#the-heat-shimmer-1135).

- ⚠ **What made the "low pale fog" (#1123).** The owner saw a pale fog creeping low along the ground, in the intro and elsewhere. There is no fog object; two things in the air made it:
  - **The heat shimmer**: gradient noise of the *view ray* added to the haze, sliding upwards with the clock. It laid blotches 7 to 11 levels of 255 paler (the wide shot's green channel, 1st percentile) over the whole middle plain and the formations' walls, fixed to the lens's directions rather than to the ground, so a moving lens saw them creep along it. It was removed, not reworked: a real shimmer bends the far horizon line by a pixel or so, which a surface's colour cannot do. [The heat shimmer](#the-heat-shimmer-1135) that took its place reads the drawn frame and touches nothing nearer than the plain's last fade.
  - **The haze**: the air is the HDR sky at the skyline, far brighter than the red ground, so a few hundredths of it veil the plain. At `HazeDistance` 3000 the plain 300 units out was a tenth air: in the wide shot its saturation was 0.34-0.41, against 0.59-0.65 with no air, and the references (`1123-zimage`: the plain from knee height and from a drone, low sun) keep it red to a crisp horizon, paler only in a thin strip there.
- ⚠ **What made the hats.** The two-stage haze before #967 reached a pale murk by `HorizonHazeDistance` 480 whatever stood there, and the far fade then carried each pixel to the sky drawn *behind* it. Measured under dome 13 (`campos=0,-9,40 camtarget=0,-11,400`): far formations (151,137,138) against the plain in front of them (128,59,42), their red gone (saturation 0.09 against 0.67) at twice the plain's brightness, the farthest the lilac of the dome's skyline band.
- **The rock is hazed towards the sky behind it**, the plain towards the skyline, blended by the rock mask (which fades out over the apron, where the two directions are the same within a degree). ⚠ Hazed towards the skyline band, the brightest sky there is, a formation 1200 units out stood paler than the sky round it. ⚠ **One extinction for both**: the plain at 8000 under the rock at 3000 left every far formation paler than the ground at its foot.
- **The last fade**: the plain takes the far field's (`FarFadeToSky`), from `HorizonHazeDistance` 1000 to `FarField.FAR_FADE_END` 1300, which hides its edge; the rock takes its own, `ROCK_FADE_START` 1000 to `ROCK_FADE_END` 1450 towards the sky behind it, inside the distance the discs are laid out to, so a far formation keeps its colour and then dissolves rather than standing up as a ghost. Blended by the rock mask, so a disc's own plain matches the grid's at the footprint's edge.
  - **The outback's pass lifts the far field's 600 cap** (`TerrainPass.FarFadeStartLatest`, [the far field](shadows-and-far-field.md)): the last few hundred units are squeezed into the skyline's pixel or two, and the plain there is still 0.85 its own colour (`1 - exp(-1000/6000)` is 0.15 air), so they show its own edge going into the sky, not a line of another colour. ⚠ The icesheet's teal line was a haze that had made its ground wholly `HorizonColor` by 900: a scene whose haze is strong where the fade would begin, towards a colour that is not the sky's, keeps the cap. (The volcano's land lifts it too: its air is the drawn horizon itself, darker, `VolcanoBackdrop.FarFadeStartLatest`, [the volcano](volcano.md), #1164.) ⚠ Begun at 600, the fade laid a pale band a couple of degrees deep along the skyline of every raised lens (the intro's wide shot, the crane over the crown, the menu's orbit); begun at 1000 it is the thin pale strip of a flat horizon. A low lens (the approach, 2.4 over the ground) sees all of it inside a pixel or two.
- The air's colour is the drawn sky, never the `HorizonColor` uniform: that one is the dome's bottom fifth, the sun's tint, which some domes never draw above the skyline. Without a far sky (the map editor) it falls back to `HorizonColor`.
- `HorizonHazeDistance` is also the plants' haze distance (`PlantPass`, quadratic to 1.5 times it), so a hummock 320 units out is about as hazed as the plain under it.

## The heat shimmer (#1135)

**The far horizon refracted in a thin strip at the skyline**, not a colour on the plain: the plain's edge and the feet of the far formations waver by about a pixel, the pattern rising, and under the skyline a sliver of the sky and of the rock standing on it is mirrored, as hot ground mirrors it (the references, `1123-zimage/heat-shimmer-horizon`, exaggerate it: floating rocks over a sheet of mirrored sky, a crisp plain in front). Code: `HorizonShimmer` (Prazsky.Core), `HorizonShimmer.fx`, the far grab in `Tonemap.fx` (`SceneGrabFar`) and `PostProcessPipeline.GrabSceneFar`.

- **Where: a ring round the lens** at `HorizonHazeDistance` (1000), where the plain's last fade begins, from just under the plain at that radius to `REACH` (1.5 degrees) over the **skyline's axis**, the plain at the fade's end (`FarField.FAR_FADE_END`, 1300). Drawn depth-tested and writing no depth, so everything nearer hides it: the plain inside the radius, every nearer formation, the island (its depth is laid before the environment on High and Ultra), and whatever the host draws after it (the island's colour, the gun, the cluster). From the play camera (6 over the plain) the far plain it may touch is the skyline's last two pixels at 1080 rows; from the intro's wide shot (40 over it) a strip half a degree deep.
- **How: three steps in the scene target's frame**, called by the host after the sky and the environment's second half and before the island's colour (`SceneRenderer.DrawHorizonRefraction`, in the Game's `BeginSceneDraw` and the Testbed's draw):
  1. **Mark**: the ring writes an alpha of 0 where it passes the depth test and nothing else. Every other pixel the scene drew holds 1 (an opaque write is 1 and the scene's premultiplied blend keeps it).
  2. **The far grab** (`GrabSceneFar`): the rows the ring covers, copied through the resolve's own box with every tap weighed by that mark, so the copy's alpha is how much of each output pixel is far and its colour is the far samples' alone. **Scissored** to the ring's projection (`HorizonShimmer.TryRows`, the chords' ends clipped at the near plane and the screen's sides, padded by the largest offset in rows): a strip, not the frame. Nothing of the ring on the screen (a lens looking down at the island), no copy.
  3. **Refract**: the ring again, opaque, reading the copy. Its own colour is its texel's (the texel's centre, so every sample a display pixel resolves is written its own box average and the resolve gives back what it had where nothing moves); the waver reads the copy at the pixel's elevation moved by `AMPLITUDE` times a field, the mirage under the axis at the elevation mirrored about it, and each read is composited over the own colour **by its far share**, so a read that lands on a near pixel brings nothing back. It writes the alpha back to 1.
- ⚠ **A resample beside a near silhouette pulls the near thing's colour into the strip**: a far pixel above a near formation's crown reads a pixel lower and finds the rock. The mark and the weighed copy are what stop it, not the order of the draws: the near plain, the near formations and the island's laid depth are already in the frame when the ring is drawn. `HorizonShimmerTests.TheGateKeepsNearThingsOut` holds the three places it lives.
- ⚠ **The offset is an angle**, never pixels: a direction's elevation moved by radians and projected back through the camera (`AMPLITUDE` 0.07 degrees, 1.8 pixels at 1080 rows through the play camera's 42.9-degree lens and 2.6 at 1600, under half that most of the time; `MIRAGE_DEPTH` 0.08 degrees deep, `MIRAGE_STRENGTH` 0.85 at the axis), so the waver is the same piece of the world at any resolution and a narrower lens shows it larger, as a telephoto does. The field is gradient noise of the heading on a circle (no seam behind the lens) and of the height over the axis, rising 3.5 cells a second with a finer octave at 9; cells of 0.25 degrees across and 0.15 up. **The clock is the scene's** (`SceneFrame.Time`: the Game's wall clock, the Testbed's scene clock, so `clock=` and `clockstill` hold it), wrapped at `CLOCK_WRAP` 600 s. At 0.05 degrees a still pair a quarter of a second apart moved a few hundred pixels of a 1080-row frame and nothing in it read as movement.
- ⚠ **The copy's target is preserved** (`PostProcessPipeline.GrabTarget`, shared with the ceiling glass, #541): binding a discarded target clears the whole of it, several times the strip's cost. Each reader reads only what its own grab wrote. From under the plate the glass's copy is taken after the shimmer and holds it.
- ⚠ **One register of the matrix read for nothing**: the refraction's program projects through `ViewProjection` and reads only its x, y and w rows, and on OpenGL ES MojoShader would drop the z row and put every uniform after it a slot late; `KEEP_READ(clip.z)` (`ShaderTarget.fxh`).
- **Tiers: High and Ultra** (`QualityPreset.HorizonShimmer`). COST_AND_MEDIUM_TBD **Not under OpenGL ES** (`GraphicsSupport.HorizonShimmer`): the copy leaves the scene target mid-scene, which on a phone's tiler stores the whole target and loads it back, unmeasured; a phone starts at Medium and reaches High only from the Quality row. The effect still compiles there. **Not on the Potato path**, which draws no outback backdrop. The Testbed draws it always; `outback=all/-shimmer` takes it out.
- `OutbackAirTests` still hold that no colour noise is back in the plain's haze: the shimmer is not in `Outback.fx`. `HorizonShimmerTests` hold the band at the skyline and its bottom beyond the last fade's start at every lens height, the offset as an angle, the scene clock, the gate, the scissored rows and the tiers.

## What each layer costs (#1102)

The Testbed's `outback=` dial (`OutbackLayer`, "The dials" in [Capturing and measuring](../testbed/capture-and-measurement.md)), at the pin the three layer sweeps of #1102 share: desktop (RX 6900 XT), Testbed Release, `scene=outback sceneseed=0 fpscap=1000 width=3840 height=1500`, the game-mode camera (`at=3:F10`), dome 21, the scene's own 4096 map (Ultra's is 8192), post on. One process a column, the variants in settled windows (#1129), each one's difference from `outback=all` within its cycle; the median of 13 to 24 cycles, the sign held in every cycle for every figure over 0.02 but the re-taken rows below. `ssaa=1` is the Testbed's eight samples with the sky first; `ssaa=2` is High and Ultra's frame, the sky behind the opaque scene at display resolution, so a layer there is priced net of the sky it hides (a formation out shows the sky through its footprint). The empty field and `Maps/Full.json` agree to 0.06 ms a layer (every layer at once to 0.07), and a second process with the variants in the reverse order read the `ssaa=2` Full column again to 0.01 ms a layer. The `ssaa=2` columns' first three rows are taken again since the monoliths' depth is laid first ([above](#how-the-formations-are-drawn-1137), 28 to 29 cycles, the sign held in 97 % of them or more, two processes for the Full column, which agreed to 0.01); the change touches no other layer's draw, and nothing at `ssaa=1`.

# The polar icesheet

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The eighteenth `SceneKind` (#222): a flat white expanse carved into sastrugi by the wind, with a crevassed pressure front at distance, under a low sun. Made of `PolarBackdrop`, `Polar.fx` and `PolarSceneConfig`. Launch: `scene=polar` (alias `ice`). A solid-terrain scene that keeps its dome, built on the desert's machinery a third time: a camera-centred `AcquireGridMesh` grid snapped to its cell (`POLAR_GRID_N` over `POLAR_EXTENT`, a cell of about 2.86 units), displaced in the vertex shader, the normal taken **per pixel** from the height gradient. It has a `TerrainMirror`: run `mirrorcheck` after touching its height.

**White in reflection, cyan in transmission: that opposition is the whole look.** The sky reflects off the glaze as white; light that goes into the ice comes back cyan, deeper the further it travelled. A crevasse is a slot that **glows**.

## ⚠ Rules and traps

- **The shadows carry the picture.** An all-white field flattens into one tone under ACES. So the snow's albedo is not 1 (0.72/0.76/0.82, cool), the shadow is the **sky's** colour (blue), and the sastrugi carry their own trough shading independent of the sun: under a high sun a flat field's `ndotl` is nearly constant, and a trough term of +/-0.12 photographed as white paper (it is +/-0.42). High `DriftAmplitude` and low `AmbientStrength` make carved snow, not a printed pattern.
- **The sastrugi are a SURFACE, not terrain.** At their real scale one is about one grid cell, so the mesh cannot hold it. Geometry carries the broad swells and the front; sastrugi are a normal perturbation sampled in **wind space** and stretched (`DriftStretch`), because an isotropic field reads as gravel.
- **The pressure front is tilted plates in a world-space lattice** (`SlabSize`, `SlabTilt`, lean hashed per plate, about 11 units tall). Smoothed it reads as breaking waves; quantised in **polar** coordinates the cells are wedges meeting along radial seams and read as unnamed columns ("a graphics glitch"). Real rubble ice is plates wider than tall.
- **A front, not a ring**: closed all round it reads as an arena wall. It covers a sector (`RidgeBearingDegrees`, `RidgeSpanDegrees`) and fades over its edges.
- **The cracked ice must be where the level is played.** Gating crevasses on the front's strain put them 300 units out. So crevasses open in **fields across the plain** (`CrevasseField`), and **blue ice** (`BlueIce`, `BlueIceThreshold`) marks scoured glacier, its edge a narrow **rim** (wider photographs as a halo), the sastrugi flattened on it. The island's clearing stays whole (`ClearingRadius`): a crack under the arena would be a hole the player cannot fall into.
- **⚠ Size a crevasse to what the grid holds.** A slot narrower than several cells falls between vertices: `CrevasseSharpness` 8 (a 3.3-unit slot) with `CrevasseDepth` 8 smeared into a V with near-vertical flanks that drew as ice **brighter than the snow**. It is 4 (a 6.7-unit slot) and 3.
- **It reads as a crack** only when narrow, with walls the sun can shade, a dark throat (`CrevasseDarkening`), transmission peaking **on the walls** and the walls' Fresnel held back (a steep face at a grazing angle mirrors the horizon). Wide and shallow it is a welt.
- **Judge it in `BS3D.exe`**, not the Testbed's F10 camera, which stands low enough behind the gun that the island hides the plain.
- **The sparkle** (one hash per fine cell) is faded out well before cells reach pixel size, or it crawls (the desert's grain rule).
- ⚠ **The sastrugi fade on the share of a noise cell a pixel spans, and are gone by sixteen pixels a cell**, eight times nearer than the noise's own Nyquist (half a cell a pixel). Faded on the standard rule the plain beyond the deck, which the play view sees at a grazing angle, was a comb of horizontal stripes (#1168). See [the band limit](#the-sastrugis-band-limit).

## The sastrugi's band limit

The sastrugi are a normal perturbation (`Sastrugi` in `Polar.fx`), two octaves of `1 - |noise|` in a domain stretched along the wind. **Each octave is whole while a pixel spans `SASTRUGI_WHOLE_CELLS` (a 48th) of its noise cell and gone at `SASTRUGI_GONE_CELLS` (a 16th)**, the span taken as `length(fwidth(...))` of that stretched domain, so the ridges last further out down their long axis than across it. The fine octave fades on its own frequency. **Each octave fades towards its own mean** (`SASTRUGI_FINE_MEAN`, `SASTRUGI_RIDGES_MEAN`, sampled off the noise), so the faded plain keeps the carved snow's tone: the tone darkens with the sastrugi, and towards 0.5, which looks like the middle and is about the field's third percentile, the plain beyond the deck came out with its tone factor a ninth under the carved snow's (2 to 4 levels of 8-bit luma in the play view). `PolarSastrugiSourceTests` holds all of it, the means against the noise's C# twin.

- **Why so far in**: the normal is taken from `ddx`/`ddy`, once per 2x2 quad; `1 - |n|` makes every crest a knife edge between a lit face and a shaded one; and under the 13 degree sun N·L swings hard with any tilt, so a crest the fade has only half taken still draws a whole stripe.
- **The symptom of a laxer limit**: the plain the play view looks across, all of it beyond the deck and 6 units under the lens, drawn as fine horizontal stripes with ladders along the blue ice's rim: the owner's "almost a moire". It is the sastrugi's **normal**: with it held flat the stripes go, with the sparkle off nothing changes (from the play lens the sparkle is faded out everywhere the plain shows).
- **The measure**: the mean absolute second difference down the rows of a crop of that plain, in 8-bit Rec. 709 luma (a copy of Smiley with `"scene": "polar"` through `levelfile=`, `sceneseed=0 seed=7`, Ultra, at 14 s, the aim held at rest with `aim=0:33.78:0`). With no sastrugi at all it is 0.95 at 1920x1080 and 0.79 at 3840x1581; on the standard rule, to nothing at half a cell of the world footprint, 22.9 and 26.7; with this limit 0.93 and 0.80, the floor. On Medium (no supersampling) the standard rule's 5.4 becomes 0.97, at the floor (0.99). A laxer limit (a linear fade to nothing at an eighth of a cell) leaves the comb's residue at 3840.
- **What it costs the near snow**: the intro's low lenses over the ice (the ridge truck and the camp, at 7.0 and 10.0 s) keep their near ridges and soften the mid-distance carving. Against the standard rule they differ by 3.5 and 4.0: the mean, over each frame's lower half, of the largest of a pixel's three 8-bit channel differences.
- **Cost**: 14 instructions on the ground's pixel program of about 1,900 (one `fwidth` of the domain and two smoothsteps).

## The dome is the biggest dial

Dome **13** (`POLAR_DOME`): a 13 degree sun over a teal horizon into indigo rakes the sastrugi, makes the front glow cyan and the crevasses slots of light. The rule is **a low sun and a cold horizon**. Dome 11 (a high sun) flattens the field, 16 goes golden-brown, and 17 is white on white: **transmission needs the sun behind the ice**, and a high sun is never behind anything.

## Open, the owner's call

- **Blue-ice brightness.** A scoured patch photographs as a pale cyan lagoon brighter than the snow; references show it slightly darker. Holding back its reflection (as a crevasse wall's is) was tried and made it **brighter** (a lower Fresnel hands back `snow * 0.55 + transmission`). The transmission is deliberate (it answered "the glowing cracked ice is nowhere"), so the lever is `TransmissionStrength` and `BlueIce`'s weight in `thickness`. The measurement is in `Polar.fx`.
- **Cast.** The lavender cast is the dome's (references are gold-and-blue).

## The research camp (#747, #1128)

The owner: "a polar station, a mast with a flag, a tracked vehicle"; then (#1128) "the building/camp should also be bigger and placed so that it is visible even while playing", and the intro should show it. `PolarPlanting` builds the camp the references (`747-zimage`, `747-klein`) draw, as hand-placed landmarks through a `PlantPass` the scene has, casting into the sun's map: three red modules on stilts with snow on their roofs and a railed walkway, an antenna, a mast with a white flag (the Moon's frozen cloth, #746, twice its size), a small wind turbine behind the station's near end, an orange tracked vehicle with a sled of crates parked before the station's near end, red fuel drums and snow drifts banked against them all. Every object stands on its own spot of `TerrainMirror.Polar` (the swells are metres high across the camp), and the stilts run down into the snow.

- **Built at `PolarPlanting.SCALE` (2.5) times its own figures**: a module 16 long with its roof 10 up, the mast 27.5 tall, the vehicle 9 long. ⚠ **The deck hides everything low out there**: the play lens opens 1.1 over the deck (`GameCameraFit`'s lens floor, 4.1 once the overview focuses) just outside the island's rim, so the deck's far edge hides the plain out to about 300 units and, on it, anything lower than about 6 minus a fiftieth of its distance from the lens. At its own figures the modules' roofs (4 up) were under that line wherever the camp could stand in the view. The layout is written in the camp's own units and `PolarPlanting.Seat` scales each object's place and size together, so it is #747's camp, bigger; `PolarPlanting.Landmarks` gives where its modules, ends, mast, flag, vehicle and turbine stand without a device, for the tests and the intro.
- **It stands where the play camera looks over the deck**: `CampBearing` -1.92 and `CampDistance` 150, about 180 from the lens, its middle 17 degrees left of the play axis, before the pressure front's southern half (no plate under it). `CampTurn` 0.96 turns its front 55 degrees off the arena towards the dome's low sun (ahead and right of the play view): square to the arena its front was in shade and the station too wide to stand between the gun and a 4:3 frame's edge. No campaign level plays here, but the pause's Scene list puts any level on the ice, so `PolarCampTests` holds every shipped level in both poses the overview holds, the fit's frame it opens on and the focused one it keeps from 4 s on (`OverviewFocus`, #1006: the lens 3 up and 2.5 in, the field of view 0.92 of the fit's, which pushes everything out towards the frame's edges), its look pulled towards the gun's aim at rest both at the beam's first contact and, where the beam meets nothing, at the cluster's centre: the whole camp in a 16:9 or wider frame, the station, the mast and its flag and the vehicle at 16:10 and 4:3, their tops and middles behind neither the gun at rest (its barrel and wheels as `CannonRig` builds them) nor a ball the level opens with, each module at least half over the deck's line, every module 53 px from floor to roof at 1080 lines and the mast 277, and at 16:9 and wider the mast's top and the flag right of the HUD's cluster profile (at 16:10 and 4:3, which squeeze the frame's width under it, they stand behind its lower end). ⚠ **Its whole layout is set by that frame**: the station seen square on, or the vehicle or the turbine at the station's far end, did not fit between the gun, the cluster and a 4:3 frame's edge once the overview had focused. So the vehicle is parked before the station's near end (off on its own beyond the station it reached the 16:9 frame's edge under the HUD's ball count), and the turbine stands behind that end (at the far end its blades stood behind the lower corner of the widest clusters). In front of the station the vehicle hides a little of the near module's walkway.
- ⚠ **The crevasse fields cover the plain past the clearing with parallel slots**: no spot at the camp's distance came out clear of them. So the ice is cleared round the camp instead: `PolarSceneConfig.CampBearing`, `CampDistance` and `CampClearRadius` (58, over the camp's whole `PolarPlanting.Reach` of 55.8, the drift against the vehicle; the crevasses come back over half as far again), in `Polar.fx`'s `CrevasseField` (`CampClear`) and term for term in `TerrainMirror.PolarCrevasse`. `PolarCampTests` holds no crevasse and no plate within the camp's reach.
- **The sun's map reaches over the mast**: `PolarBackdrop.ShadowAbove` lifts the box's top from the front's plates (11 over `LevelY`) to the mast's height (`PolarPlanting.Tallest`, 27.5); a caster over the box's top is clipped out of the map. The swell the mast stands on is left to the fit's slack (`SceneRenderer.ShadowBoxTop`): the mast's top stands at 14.3, the box's top at 24. `PolarCampTests` holds every landmark's top under it. ⚠ **The taller box spreads the map's texels over more of the ice**: under dome 13's 13-degree sun the light-space window (`SunShadowMap.Fit`, over the box's corners) is 137.8 units tall where the plates alone made it 127.5, so a texel covers 8 % more ground along the sun's direction, the camp's, the island's and the gun's shadows alike; across it nothing changes. The swell added to the top as well would make it 142.2 (11.5 %) for nothing. The bias is in world units (`SunShadowMap.DepthRange`), so the taller box does not move it.
- **The tour films it**: *the camp*, the prologue's last shot ("Polar" in [the other scenes' shots](../game-feedback/chapter-intro-scene-shots.md)); every other lens of the reel keeps off it.
- **Cost**: the same meshes as #747's camp, one instance each, drawn into the scene and the sun's map: no vertices over #747's, only the pixels it covers.

## Costs

- **Against the desert** (Testbed, laptop, ssaa 2, 1600x900, `fpscap=400`): **+16 %** (32.6 against 28.2 ms); crevasse fields and blue ice add about six gradient-noise evaluations a pixel.
- `CrevasseField` once sampled the pressure ridge itself; passing it down bought 1.2 ms. The next lever: the crevasse mask is far lower in frequency than the normal taps, so all three could share one sample.
- Desktop, play pose, 3840x1600 ssaa 2, `nopost nooverc`, `fpscap=400`: about 12.3 ms.

The ambience is a long flat wind with spindrift, not the mountain's gusts. Airborne diamond dust is not built.

# The savanna's campfires

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The savanna lights itself with a **ring of campfires** (`CampfireConfig`): the flame (`Flame.fx`), its sparks, the point light each one carries into the grass and the balls, and the hearth it stands in. The ground they burn on is in [The savanna](savanna.md).

## The ring

- `CampfireConfig.Count` (8 by default) fires evenly spaced round the island. One fire lit a single flank of the stone; eight light it all the way round, and the island's drum takes a warm rim wherever the camera orbits to.
- **The ring is derived from the one config point.** `GroundXZ` is fire zero; the radius and the ring's rotation come out of it, so the fire a single-fire config authored stands where it did. Range, flame size, colour and height above the terrain are shared across the ring: a range or colour per fire would be a config nobody could tune.
- **Eight is the shader's cap, not a taste figure.** Each fire is a real point light and `SceneLights.MaxLights` (8) matches `MAX_SCENE_LIGHTS` in the instancing shader's lighting include. The savanna can spend the whole budget because nothing else in the scene lights.
- ⚠ **Each fire burns on its own clock**, which is what makes the ring read as fire and not as decoration. The light's flicker (`SceneRenderer.CampfireColor`) and the flame's shape (`Flame.fx`'s `FlameSeed`) take the fire's index, which offsets the time **and** stretches every rate (the vertical frequencies too) by a few per cent. A time offset alone leaves eight flames licking one pattern a moment apart, which the eye reads as a single wave travelling round the island as soon as two are in shot.
- Cost: 1.75 to 1.85 ms for one fire against eight (Testbed, dome 13, `nocap`, fixed low camera): +0.10 ms for eight point lights in the grass's per-pixel loop and eight billboard draws.

## The mounds the fires stand on (#887)

Each fire stands on a low mound: the plain is raised under it to one **seat**, `CampfireConfig.SeatBelowPlate` (2.5) under the island's top plate, so y -11.0 for all eight fires whatever the relief under them. The plain at the ring runs y -15.5 to -11.3, so the mounds are 0.3 to 4.5 units high (2.5 on average) and the two fires on the high ground (bearings 1 and 2) stand on next to nothing.

- **Why: the island's rim hid the fire's body, and the ring's radius cannot reveal it.** The play camera stands at y -7.4, 1.1 over the plate, 30.0 to 30.6 out (`[tilt] pos` on every savanna level). A point of a flame is hidden when the sight line to it is under the plate's height where it leaves the disc of the walkable top (the far arris, r 24.7), and that line falls only 0.02 per unit of distance: at a fire 46 to 61 units from the lens it is at y -8.7. The fires stood on ground 3 to 7 under the plate, so the rim hid the lowest 2.5 to 6.7 units of the 13.8-tall billboard, the broad base where the flame's mass is, and left the tips (the owner's note, 2026-10-16).
- **Measured on the unchanged ring** (`CampfireRimClearanceTests`, the sight line over the orbit's 72 bearings, two lens distances and the 16:9 and 21:9 frustums, 936 sightings): the least a fire showed was **0.521 of its height, the mean 0.667**. **Moving the ring out does nothing**: at radius 43.3 the same test reads 0.520 and 0.666 (the sight line falls 0.2 over the ten units and the relief out there is 0.2 lower on average, and the fire shrinks on screen by about a seventh), which is why the owner's own suggestion ("a little farther from the island") was not taken. In frames (Testbed, the Game's play pose pinned with `campos=0,-7.4,29.965 camtarget=0,-1.6,0 fov=42.857`, 1920 x 1080, `nopost`, 16 to 18 frames of the animated flame each with and without the island, `arena=none`): the share of a fire's flame pixels above the arris was **0.49 at the fire 46 units away and 0.35 at the one 56 away**, and the share of the billboard's height 0.70 and 0.61.
- **After the mounds**: least **0.846**, mean **0.849** of the height (the test asserts at least 0.7 and 0.8); in frames the flame pixels above the arris are **0.79 and 0.74**, the height 0.84 and 0.84. Elephant's pose (30.6 out, aim y -1.2) reads the same: 0.49 to 0.79 and 0.35 to 0.74. The rim still takes the lowest 2.2 units, which is the hearth and the base's skirt. A seat nearer the plate shows more and costs a steeper hill (at -9.5 the least is 0.954 and the mounds 6 high).
- **The shape**: flat top of radius 3.2 (`MoundTopScale` 1.4 times `FlameSize`: the hearth's stones are laid out to 3.2 and stand level), then a smoothstep down to the plain at the foot, 10.6 out (`MoundFootScale` 4.6). The steepest slope is 42 degrees at the lowest fire, on average 25. Eight mounds stand 25.5 apart, so their feet never touch, and the inner foot reaches in under the island's drum, where the terrain is cut at its own radius: against the wall at r 26 a mound banks 1.9 units of grass at most. From above they read as eight low knolls with a fire on each; from the play camera they are behind the rim.
- **One height, in three places that must agree.** `TerrainMirror.Savanna` is `SavannaPlain` (the relief, as before) plus `SavannaMound`: the **nearest** fire's `SavannaMoundLift` (how high its mound stands at its centre) times one minus a smoothstep from the flat top to the foot, with `SavannaMoundShape` holding the foot under half the ring's spacing (so a point is in one mound at most and the nearest is its mound; shipped 10.6 against 12.8). `Savanna.fx`'s **vertex shader** adds the same (`HearthMound`, over `PlainHeight`; one pass over the fires finds the nearest), reading the lifts and the shape C# pushes in `ApplyHearthParameters` (`HearthLift`, `MoundTop`, `MoundFoot`; with the mounds off every lift is 0), with the same early-out as the hearth's burn (`HearthNear`/`HearthFar`), and hands the mound's analytic **slope** to the pixel shader as an interpolant, which adds it to the normal's two plain taps. The pixel shader's own pass over the fires stays the hearth's burn alone. ⚠ The first version asked for the mound at each of the pixel shader's three height taps and cost +0.75 ms at the play camera against +0.54 now. `SavannaBackdrop.CampfireRingXZ` is the ring's rule, which both the fire's position and the mirror read. **`mirrorcheck` compares the GPU's height with the mirror's**: savanna 0.000033 (the mounds included, over 65,536 samples, 0 over tolerance). **Seen to fail**: the mirror's mound lifted 10 per cent less gave max |dh| 0.448, 125 samples over tolerance, FAIL. Everything standing on the plain follows the mounds through the mirror: the fire (`CampfirePosition` is the seat plus 0.2), the hearth stones, the scatter, the chapter intro's lenses.
- ⚠ **The light moved with the seat.** The island's light stands at a share of the flame's height over the ground (next section), so with the seat 2.5 under the plate the old 0.65 put it at y -2.0 and the plate took 0.122 of the ring where #885 was judged at 0.079; `LightHeightScale` is 0.45, light at y -4.8, plate 0.077 (`CampfirePlateLightTests`).
- **Potato draws no fires and no mounds**: `PotatoBackdrop.Savanna` sets `MoundFootScale` to 0 before it builds its ground and its scatter from the one height function (`CampfireMoundTests` pins the line and that 0 is the plain's own height to the last bit). Its `[potato] Savanna` census line (ground 24,552 vertices, 36 draws) and its frame below the horizon are unchanged.
- **Check it** from the Game: `BS3D.exe level=Heart seed=1 sceneseed=0 quality=high windowed width=1920 height=1080 nosplash userdata=<dir> shot=22` (the play camera at rest, after the 14 s intro). In the Testbed, the same pose with the island removed is the control: `Testbed.exe Testbed\Maps\Single.json scene=savanna sky=14 sceneseed=0 nopost nooverc weather=clear width=1920 height=1080 ssaa=1 campos=0,-7.4,29.965 camtarget=0,-1.6,0 fov=42.857 at=0.5:F12 shot=4,5` and `... arena=none`; the knolls from the side: `campos=-72,6,-30 camtarget=-30,-12,-4 fov=50`.
- Cost (Testbed, 3840 x 1600 at ssaa 2, `nopost nooverc weather=clear`, `fpscap=1000`, ABBA, 4 runs of 19 s each, the first four `[fps]` windows dropped; desktop, RX 6900 XT): the Game's play pose pinned (`campos=0,-7.4,29.965 camtarget=0,-1.6,0 fov=42.857`, `Single.json`, dome 14) **10.653 ms before, 11.191 after, +0.538** (runs 10.616 to 10.668 against 11.182 to 11.200); an elevated camera over the ring (`campos=0,45,90 camtarget=0,-12,0 fov=55`) **12.053 before, 12.059 after, +0.006**. Taken apart on the first version of the shader (same camera, four builds in rotation): the same shader with the mounds switched off **+0.013** (the code costs nothing; the lifts are what cost), the flames not drawn **-0.18** of the +0.58 (the flame's visible pixels, which the rim used to hide), and the rest, about 0.4, shows from the play camera only and is not explained. ⚠ A hypothesis, untested: the terrain grid is drawn first and the island over it, so a mound (which the sight line to the far side of the ring goes over) hides terrain behind it that is shaded and then overdrawn. See "Costs a change must compare against" in [The savanna](savanna.md).

## The light on the island's plate (#885)

Each fire's light stands at **two heights over one ground point** (`SavannaBackdrop.CampfireRingPoint`, the one copy of the ring's rule, static so a test can call it without a device):

- **The ground point** (`CampfireConfig.HeightAboveTerrain`, 0.2; `SceneRenderer.SavannaCampfirePosition`): the flame's base, the sparks, the hearth, and the grass's own eight-light loop (`DrawSavanna`).
- **The flame's body** (`LightHeightScale`, 0.45 of the flame's own height, so 6.2 over the seat at the defaults, y -4.8; `SceneRenderer.SavannaCampfireLightPosition`): the light `SceneLights.Apply` pushes onto the shared instanced effect, for the island, the gun and the balls.

- **Why it is not one point.** The ring's seat is y -11.0 (the mounds above; on the plain's own relief the fires stood at y -15.5 to -11.3, `CampfirePlateLightTests` measures the gap), 2.5 below the island's top plate at -8.5, which dishes to -9.7 at the funnel. A light below an up-facing surface has N.L at or under zero, so with the light at the ground the plate took **exactly nothing** from the ring (`CampfirePlateLightTests`' control: 0 at every sampled point), and only the coping and the drum, which face outward, took the rim light. The raised lights stand at y -4.8, 3.7 over the plate's top, inside a flame whose top reaches y +2.8 (a light under 2.5 over the seat would be level with the plate and light nothing on it). The grass's light and the flame's base stay on the ground: lifting them would lift the glow off the grass and the fire off the lawn.
- **What the plate receives.** The shader's own diffuse term, `saturate(N.L) * saturate(1 - d / range)^2` summed over the eight fires (colour left out), over the plate from radius 14 to 24.7 with its own normal (the dish leans 6.4 degrees away from every fire): **mean 0.077, least 0.030** (0 everywhere before #885, and 0.079 and 0.015 as it was judged, before the mounds). On the bearing of a fire: 0.03 at the funnel's mouth, 0.07 at radius 19 (about where the gun stands), 0.17 at the arris; at radius 19 all the way round the ring it reads 0.066 to 0.070, an even wash (0.032 to 0.106 over the relief). It rides `CampfireColor` (2.4, 1.0, 0.32 at 0.44 to 1.0, mean 0.72), so the plate round the gun takes a warm tint and the plate beside a fire a pool. Measured in 8-bit pixels (red channel, over 150 to 200), #885 gained +4 to +23 on the plate in the Game (`level=Elephant quality=high`, dome 14, 1600 x 900, `seed=7`) and +94 beside a fire in the Testbed at dusk (dome 15). The mounds then moved the light (fire 0's came 0.9 lower, the rest are level): the same dusk check reads the plate's mean red at 150 against 156 before (-4 per cent) and 181 against 189 in the strip under the flame, 14 frames of the flickering light each.
- ⚠ **The height is a share of the flame, over its own seat**, because a light belongs to its flame. Over the plain's relief the two fires on the high ground (bearings 1 and 2, y -11.3) lit the plate at radius 19 about three times as much (0.106) as the two on the low ground (bearings 4 and 5, -15.4: 0.036); the mounds level that. A taller light flattens the pool under the flame (the #885 captures: 0.85 of the flame, 11 units over the ground, was a flat gold with no pool), and **the light's absolute height is what the plate reads**: 0.65 over the seat is y -2.0 and the plate takes 0.122, 0.45 is y -4.8 and 0.077 (`TheMoundsLeaveThePlateTheLightItWasJudgedAt` holds the mean between 0.065 and 0.095).
- **The balls read as before.** The ring is out of the cluster's reach (a ball at the edge of a 20-wide field is 19 or more units from the nearest fire): from the fire's side under dome 15 the cluster's mean colour moved +2.1, +0.9, +0.2 of 255 and the thirteen-colour row +0.6 (`Thirteen_Colors.json` under the game camera). The mounds then moved the row's mean colour by -0.07, -0.06, -0.06 (the play pose from fire 0's side, dome 15, 11 frames each). The gun's barrel takes +13 red beside a fire. The volcano's rule applies here too ([The volcano](volcano.md): all thirteen colours must read).
- **Potato is unchanged**: it carries no campfire light (`SceneLights` is handed no renderer there).
- **Check it** with `Testbed.exe Testbed\Maps\Single.json scene=savanna sky=15 sceneseed=0 nopost nooverc weather=clear campos=10.1,-2.5,-6.5 camtarget=26,-9,-16.7`: the plate glows under the flame and the coping's edge is lit; before, the plate was flat brown with a dark edge. Dome 15 (dusk) shows it most; dome 14 under the Game's play camera shows the plate round the gun warmer.
- Cost: **none measurable**. Only a position moved: the lights' count (8), the shader and its loop are the same. Desktop (RX 6900 XT), Testbed, 3840 x 1600 at ssaa 2, `nopost nooverc weather=clear`, `fpscap=1000`, 12 runs of about 18 s per camera in ABBA order across the two builds (before and after are two exes), the first four `[fps]` windows of each run dropped: the plate camera above (dome 15, `Single.json`) **11.890 ms before, 11.885 after** (the three blocks +0.020, -0.005, -0.010); the game camera on `Dense20x10x15.json` (`at=0.5:F10`, dome 14) **15.170 before, 15.170 after** (blocks 0.000, -0.030, -0.150). ⚠ A first set of the plate camera read 16.7 to 17.1 ms in two runs of each build: another process on the GPU, not the code; the repeat was clean, so read the spread of identical builds before believing a difference.

## The flame

`Flame.fx` draws **three tongues** (each its own centre and width, on rates stretched by `FlameSeed`, bent by the turbulence more the higher they reach, so where two overlap they read as one splitting or two rejoining) over **two fBm fields scrolling upward**. The turbulence *cuts* the body more with height: solid and broad at the base, separate licking tips and detached shreds above. The colour ramp runs red, orange, yellow-white by heat and is hotter low down, so the core stays at the base. The mass sits in the lower two thirds of the billboard; the reference fire is a pyramid about twice as tall as its base.

- ⚠ **Radiances must sit a stop under what looks right in isolation.** Core 2.6/2.0/0.85 and an orange at 2.0 came out as a white column under a daytime dome: the flame is additive over a bright sky and the tonemap's shoulder takes everything over about 1.5 to white, so the red never showed.
- **Sparks** (`CampfireConfig.SparkCount`, 20 of a shared buffer of 32): a second technique over one buffer of billboards built on the fountain's pattern (`BuildBillboardParticles`), each spark a camera-facing quad on a looping life off the wall clock, born in the base, climbing on a swirl, cooling from yellow-white to red. No particle state anywhere. One draw per fire after its flame.
- **Three billboards per fire** (`SUBFLAME_COUNT` = 3 in `Flame.fx`, `FLAME_SUBFLAME_COUNT` quads in one static buffer in `SavannaBackdrop`). A single camera-facing quad never shows a different face as the camera orbits round it, which a real fire always does, and the intro and the front end both orbit fire 0. The sub-flames sit at fixed world-space offsets from the hearth centre (`SUBFLAME_OFFSET`, `SUBFLAME_SCALE`, `SUBFLAME_SEED_OFFSET`), each its own quad with its own scale and seed; the depth comes from the three offsets parallaxing against each other and the ground. Sub-flame 0 is the single quad exactly (zero offset, full scale, no seed shift), so the tuned front-on view is unchanged. `LavaFountain.fx`'s jets use the same pattern.
- **The quad is wider than the fire, and the heat tapers to nothing before its border** (`FLAME_QUAD_WIDEN` = 1.3 in `Flame.fx`). A billboard is a rectangle, and the tongues sit anywhere in U = -1..1 on their own sines; the broad base of the outer ones reaches past |U| = 1 in most phases (a port of the pixel shader over 400 phases found a pixel above the clip level out to 1.31, and 95 % of the phases stayed under 1.29), so a quad exactly as wide as the fire cut them with a straight vertical edge. The vertex shader multiplies the corner's U by the factor in the world offset and in the UV together, so one U unit keeps its world length and the tongues do not grow; the pixel shader multiplies the heat by `smoothstep(FLAME_QUAD_WIDEN, 1, |U|)` before the clip: exactly 1 out to the old border (the fire inside it is as it was) and 0 at the new one. Every pixel within about 0.025 U of the border (3 px at the fixed camera below) is discarded whatever its heat, so no phase shows an edge. `FlameQuadSourceTests` pins the three places that must agree. ⚠ A larger factor buys area that draws nothing: the fBm runs before the clip, so the margin costs a full pixel shader per pixel.
- ⚠ **Check for a cut with a colour step, not a luminance one**: orange over the savanna's yellow-green grass has almost no luminance difference, and a luminance detector sees only a fraction of it. At the fixed camera below (`sceneseed=1`, `nopost`, 80 frames 0.6 s apart, the overlay hidden with `at=0.5:F12`), a straight run of 12 rows or more (one missing row allowed) of one-pixel steps of 40/255 or more in the largest colour channel, on the main quad's border columns (its lateral axis is the screen's horizontal: columns 685 and 914 of 1600), was present in 35 of the 80 frames with the exactly-as-wide quad and in none with the widened one, at the new border and at the old columns alike. A pixel that steps in half the frames or more (the island's rim, the crosshair) is masked out first.
- **The height is a config value, decided by the game's camera.** `FlameHeightScale` (6) is the billboard's height as a multiple of `FlameSize`, separate from the width. The fires stand 2.5 units under the island's stone (on their mounds; on the plain's relief 3 to 7) and the play camera sits about level with it, so the rim hides the lowest 2 units of a 13.8-tall flame and shows 0.85 of it; a flame at the old 2.4 times (5.5 tall) stood on the relief and cleared the rim by half a unit, showing only its tip. Growing `FlameSize` instead raises the tip by widening the fire into a bonfire. 4.5 is present but slight, 8 becomes a beam standing in the sky. The flame is about 14 units tall; the eye judges it against the island beside it, since the fires are 33 units out.
- Cost (13 units from a fire, flame across the lens: `campos=38,-10,-10 camtarget=28,-8,-18`, dome 14, 1600 x 900 at ssaa 4, `nopost`, `fpscap=400`, desktop): +0.13 ms. The sub-flames' added cost is under the notebook's own noise (no discrete GPU: 105.6 against 101.2 ms at ssaa 4, 30.5 both ways at ssaa 2): a clean number needs the desktop.
- Cost of the widened quad (same camera, desktop, 3840 x 1600 at the default ssaa 2, `nopost`, `nocap`, 12 runs of 22 s in ABBA order, the first four `[fps]` windows of each dropped): **9.78 ms before, 9.85 ms after, +0.07 ms** (the three paired blocks: +0.059, +0.070, +0.074; a run's own spread is under 0.1 ms). That is the extra 30 % of every fire's pixel-shader area, the fBm included, since it runs before the clip.

## The hearth (#282)

A fire with a point light under it and nothing else on the ground reads as fire burning out of a lawn. Each fire stands in a hearth made of two parts that live in different places:

- **The stones**: a ring of `RockMesh` boulders (the forest's boulder, re-proportioned; `CampfireConfig.StoneCount`, 7 per fire) set into the ground, drawn on the **acacia's own instanced path** (same shader, same sun-and-dome lighting as everything planted on this terrain), one draw per fire.
- **The scorched ground**: `Savanna.fx`'s own burn field ashes the grass over the ring and chars it nearly black at the fire's foot, with the grass relief and the wind's combing fading out with it. Everything is sized off `FlameSize`, never in world units, so a hearth belongs to the fire in it.
- **The firelight on the stones is a per-draw additive, not a ninth point light** (`Acacia.fx`'s `AddedLight`, zero for every plant). All stones of a ring stand at one distance from one fire, so the attenuation is a constant: the same quadratic falloff the ground uses, times the stone's own albedo, riding `CampfireColor` at this frame's time. It does not favour the flame-facing side of a stone.
- **The hearth positions are pushed at config time, not per frame**, and the grass's per-pixel loop is bounded by an early-out on the ring's own extent (`HearthNear`/`HearthFar`), measured in C# **from the fires' own positions**. ⚠ Never re-derive the config's ring rule inside the shader: a second copy of a placement rule is how a scene grows a fault nobody can see (#297).
- **From the play camera you will not see it.** The fires are 33 units out and their seat is 2.5 under the island's rim, hidden from where the gun stands (the sight line over the arris is at y -8.7, the seat at -11.0), so a player sees only the flame against the sky. The hearth reads from the front end's orbit, a chapter intro and any elevated vantage.
- Cost (dome 1, `nocap`, `nopost`, 1600 x 900 at ssaa 4, a fixed elevated camera with all eight hearths in frame): +0.14 ms for the rings and the burn term.

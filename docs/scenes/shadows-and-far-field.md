# The sun's shadows and the far field

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

Two things every open-ground scene shares: which scenes throw the sun's cast shadows (#469-#471), and how the land reaches the horizon (#551). A change to either reaches both executables at once, as the shared shaders do; the per-scene sections hold what each scene does.

## The sun's shadows (#469, #470, #471)

**Twelve scenes throw cast shadows**: savanna, meadow, forest, mountains, desert, outback, beach, volcano, Mars, polar ice, the Moon (since #680, for its rocks) and the city. One orthographic map is fitted round the camera each frame; the scene's planting and the host's island, gun and wood are drawn into it; each terrain shader multiplies its **sun** term by what it reads back (the dome's ambient is untouched, which is what a shadow is). The mechanics, bias and cost are "Sun shadows" in `docs/rendering.md`.

- **Who asks.** Eleven scenes ask through `Shadows` on their config (`ShadowConfig`: strength, extent, map size). The city, the one backdrop `SceneRenderer` does not own, hands its own dials and fit over through `SceneRenderer.SetHostShadowScene`. A scene that asks for neither gets none (strength 0 allocates no target and hands every receiver 0).
- **Sea and storm are out on purpose.** The storm draws no ground (`StormClouds.fx` *is* the cloud; the island stands on nothing a shadow could land on), and the sea is water, where a shadow inside the Fresnel, foam and subsurface terms is a look decision of its own. The other sky-replacing scenes have no sun above the horizon and the gate skips them.
- **The map's box is computed from each scene's terrain figures**, not authored beside them, so a re-tuned hill height cannot leave the map covering the wrong slab of air. The mountain and the volcano take half their relief deliberately: their peaks stand outside the map's 260-unit extent and shade only themselves, and stretching the range to reach them would coarsen the bias on the ground at the gun's feet.
- **Cost is the receiving, not the casting**: the meadow (only the island casts) measured the same +0.19 ms as the forest with 380 casters; the city +0.22-0.23 ms with some 1800 towers casting.
- **The city's towers cast and receive; its street does not**: the street shader's occupancy term (#399) already reads a canyon as shadowed at every sun height, and a shadow-map receiver changed a paired capture's facades dramatically and its pavement almost not at all ("The city's shadows" in `docs/rendering.md`).

## The far field (#551)

Every camera used to clip at 500 units and the open-ground backdrops were cut off in front of the player, from **two different faults**: where a scene's haze had not finished by 500 (the volcano's plain and the icesheet, haze 900; the sea, 700; the storm's cloud band) the far plane sliced the ground off with a line; where the haze *had* finished (Mars), the cut was **the camera grid's own edge**, +-500 round the lens, visible because a fully hazed landform is not the sky: the haze ends in the light rig's `HorizonColor`, an average over the dome capture's bottom fifth, which on Mars is yellow under a pink sky. A scene takes three parts:

- **The far plane is 2000** (`BasicCamera3D.DEFAULT_FAR_PLANE_DISTANCE`, which `RecoilCamera` takes too). The near plane sets depth precision (the 24-bit step at distance *z* is about z^2/(near * 2^24)), so the cluster, island and cannon lose a few parts in a hundred thousand.
- **The far ring.** After its camera grid, each open-ground scene (sea, polar, desert, outback, tropical and its lagoon, savanna, mountains, meadow, forest, volcano, Mars) draws its terrain a second time over one shared mesh: a polar ring **centred on the arena**, 200 to 1500 out, 432 cells around, rings spaced geometrically so every cell subtends about the same angle (a camera grid is fine everywhere, but reaching 1500 at its cells would be about nine times the vertices). It runs the scene's own technique and height function with `OriginXZ` zero (the ring's vertices are already world positions), so it is the same land, fixed to the world. `FarRingClip` gives every pixel inside the camera grid back to the grid (the ring takes over 6 units inside the grid's edge so the tessellations never open a crack); the rows the grid covers are not drawn at all (a start offset into the index buffer). It uses the scene's `...Reduced` program where it has one.
- **The fade to the drawn sky.** `FarFadeToSky` (`FarField.fxh`) carries the last stretch to the colour the dome is *drawn* in behind that pixel (`SkyDome.DrawnLinearAt`, handed to the scenes as nine samples over direction.y, `SkyLightRig.FarSky`). It begins where the scene's haze ends (`HorizonHazeDistance`, read back from the effect) or at 600, whichever is nearer, and is complete at `SceneRenderer.FAR_FADE_END` (`FarField.FAR_FADE_END`), inside the ring and the far plane so nothing about where either ends can be seen. ⚠ Begun at 900, the icesheet's last few hundred units, squeezed into a pixel or two at the skyline, kept the haze's teal as a line under a lilac sky; hence 600.

**The storm has no ground and needed the opposite.** Its cloud band reaches 560 across the wind and wraps 780 along it, and the 500 far plane hid both; `StormClouds.fx` thins each puff's opacity to nothing over 380-460 from the lens (`STORM_FAR_THIN_*`), so the horizon stands where it stood and stops being a line.

**Not in it:** the Moon (no air to fade into; its highland crest and curvature hide the grid's edge), the aurora and Grid (their skies are their own passes and their floors fade well inside the grid), space, dream and cavern (no far ground), and the two cities (enclosed by their towers).

⚠ **The reduced tier draws no ring.** Seeing further costs the **new pixels** (a band of mesa that used to be sky, shaded by a full terrain program), not the ring's vertices: on Mars from a low camera over the mesas (`campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,-5,-400`, dome 19, 1920x1080 at ssaa 2, `nopost nooverc nocap`, three alternating pairs, desktop) the ring cost 4.32 to 5.16 ms, a quarter of the vertices 5.36 against 5.45, and skipping the covered rows nothing. At `SceneDetail` 0 the ring is not drawn and the fade completes by the largest circle the camera grid covers round the lens (its half-width less the overlap), beginning at 85 % of that: no edge anywhere, a nearer horizon.

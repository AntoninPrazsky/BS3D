# The star lattice

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

`Stars.fxh` is the one star field that the skies with no atmosphere share: space ([Space](space.md)), the Moon ([The Moon](moon.md)) and the aurora. `StarLayer` is the function all three call, the uniforms in the header (`StarCellScale`, `StarChance`, `StarPeak`, `StarSpread`, `StarFalloff`, the spike pair, `SupersampleFactor`) become each including effect's parameters under the same names, and one C# push routine (`SpaceStarsConfig`) serves all of them. Change it and you change three scenes.

It has three layers (coarse and bright with spikes, middle, fine) on a cube-face chart, `uv = tan(angle)` about each face centre. Brightness is `hash^StarFalloff` with a steep exponent: real star counts climb hard towards the faint end, and a field of evenly bright dots reads as noise. A brighter star is also drawn a little *wider*, because no optics resolve a star as a point.

## The glare rule

- **Everything small stays under `GLARE_THRESHOLD` (0.55 on luminance); the brightest stars carry their diffraction spikes in the shader.** `DrawGlare` binds the supersampled scene target into a half-back-buffer bright pass (4:1 per axis at `ssaa 2`) read with a single bilinear tap, so a one-pixel star over the threshold is sampled stochastically and pops in and out of the glare as the camera turns, which reads as a fault. The bloom pyramid halved the problem; it did not repeal the rule.
- **The one exception is a planet's lit limb** (space's planet), a smooth arc hundreds of pixels long that the sparse grid lands on many times, so it blooms steadily while the disc's body stays under. A small disc is not allowed over (the Moon's Earth, ~30 px).
- **A spike is a `max` with the star's core, never an addition.** Added, the arms cross at the centre and double the peak (~0.97 luminance, nearly twice the threshold). A max also reads as physics: a diffraction spike is the star's own light spread out.
- The threshold is on **luminance**, so a saturated hue can be far brighter than a neutral one before it blooms (a red nebula peaks at 0.78 in red for a luminance of 0.32).
- **In vacuum a star must not twinkle.** Any shimmer is an artifact, never a feature.

## The cell lookup: a margin box is a lattice (#674)

- ⚠ **A star that may land only in the middle `1 − 2·margin` of its cell is a periodic structure whatever the jitter inside the box.** `Tools/LatticeProbe` on the space scene (1600×900, `sceneseed=3`, four vantages away from the arena) flagged 22–67 % of scored tiles over the noise null at a period of 8.83 px on the axes, the same size on screen whichever way the lens pointed (the grid lives in direction space). The moon (10–12 %) and the aurora (5–8 %) showed the same 8.83 px. #88 and #370 had only made the lattice smaller.
- **What `StarLayer` does now.** The star is jittered over the **whole** cell and `StarLayer` looks up the cells its light can come from (those overlapping `quickReach` either side of the pixel: up to a 2 × 2 block) and combines them with **`max`**. A max and not a sum for the spikes' reason: overlapping stars now occur (a real sky clumps), and two bright ones within a pixel or two must not add past the threshold. Cells off the cube face are skipped: that is the next face's sky, drawn by its own lookup, and drawing it here too would double the stars in a band along all twelve seams.
- What is left of the margin is the **cube face's own edge** (a cell on a face edge remaps into the box the margin leaves, so the star's light is not cut at the seam) and its cap, **half a cell**, which is what a 2 × 2 lookup can promise.
- ⚠ **The spike arms keep the old cap** (`armMargin`, 0.34 of a cell). Letting it rise to 0.5 gave visibly more and longer crosses, a change of look nobody asked for.
- After, same vantages: space 0 / 2 / 2 / 0 % (was 67 / 63 / 60 / 22), moon 2 / 0 / 0, aurora 0–2 %, at noise level; what the probe still flags is terrain. Density, colour, peak and spikes are unchanged. Two limits left: an interior star is clipped at half a cell as a hard circle (a few 8-bit codes at a large jacobian; a smooth fade near `quickReach` would remove it), and faint star centres stay about 2.5 px clear of each face seam, the only linear structure left.
- ⚠ **It costs frame time, more than the arithmetic suggests.** Laptop APU, Testbed, 1600×900, the space scene looking up, alternating builds: 22.2 → 26.6 ms (+4.4) at `ssaa 2`, 7.56 → 8.98 (+1.4) at 1×, the Low tier's setting. A cheap reject before a star's expensive half (its second hash, log, exp, sqrt run only inside `quickReach`), looking up only the cells the reach can touch, and a constant `STAR_MAX_REACH_RADII` in place of a `log` and `sqrt` per pixel per layer got it there from a first cut of +7.3. A real loop instead of the unrolled pair was slower. It is paid on the three scenes with stars, a fifth of the laptop's High frame and little at Low; gating the lookup to High is one uniform (an owner decision, not taken).

## Cell scales, chances and density

- **`CellScale` must be an integer** (the four properties round on set, #149). The cube face's edge has to land **on** a cell wall, or a star centred just past the seam is drawn up to it and stops: a step of up to `peak · magnitude` along all twelve cube edges. A property grid or a level saved with a detuned value cannot break it.
- **The fine layer's cell scale is what cured "graph paper" at a face centre** (#370). At a face centre the chart-to-screen map is locally a similarity, so a lattice of cell-centred stars projects to a perfect rectangular grid; at a corner `uv = tan θ` shears it and nothing assembles. Found by autocorrelating the row luminance of a face-centre capture (`scene=space arena=none nopost ssaa=2`, 1600×900, `campos=0,10,30 camtarget=0,60,10`, detrended against a 31 px moving average): rings at lag 5–6, 11–12 and 17–18, a ~5.9 px fundamental against the fine layer's ~6 px cell. `FaintCellScale` 110 → 70 made the cells ~10 px and the fundamental vanish. `FaintChance` stays at 0.40: raising it to hold the old count would put the face-centre fill near 1, the corner-knot artifact and the surest way to make a grid legible.
- ⚠ **That cure is resolution-dependent, because the disease is.** At 1366×768/72° the same cells are ~8.7 px and the ring is halved, not removed; coarsening further thins the sky. Re-check small screens if they report it.
- **Per-star cutoff and per-star spikes.** A spiked star needs `StarSpikeLength · 2.5` core radii against a plain star's ~3, and only ~4 % of the coarse layer is over `StarSpikeThreshold`, so spikes are decided per star, not per layer. The plain cutoff is solved per star from its own peak (`exp(−r²) · peak · magnitude = STAR_CUT`, a third of an 8-bit code) at about 2.2 radii typically; `STAR_MAX_REACH_RADII` is the constant bound for the lookup.
- **Density carries the solid angle of its own cell** (the "cube showing" bug of #87). The chart is uniform in area and the sky is not: `uv = tan θ` puts the solid-angle element at `dA · J^−1.5`, so a corner cell covers a fifth of the sky a face-centre cell does, and density ran 5.196× at the eight corners and 2.83× along the edge middles. What the eye assembled out of eight knots and twelve bands was the cube. ⚠ **It is a smooth gradient with no discontinuity anywhere, so three passes that hunted for a step (#87, #88, #148) could not find it.**
  - The existence roll is `chance · STAR_DENSITY_GAIN · rsqrt(J³)`, evaluated at the cell **centre**, not the pixel (one answer per cell, or a star is cut in half along an invisible contour). `STAR_DENSITY_GAIN` = 4 / (4π/6), the chart area of a face over its solid angle: weighting alone would leave 52.4 % of the stars.
  - Measured (`scene=moon nopost arena=none`, 200×200 patch at frame centre): corner `(1,1,1)` against face centre `(0,1,0)` 4013 against 1244 lit pixels (3.23×) before, 2276 against 2008 (1.13×) after.
  - **`SpaceStarsConfig.MaxChance` (0.523) is arithmetic, not taste**: a face centre rolls against 1.91 × chance, so anything above 1/1.91 saturates there while the corners still thin out. All three chances clamp on set. A denser sky wants a finer `CellScale`.

## Sizing, footprint and shape

- **A core is sized in OUTPUT pixels** (`SceneRenderer.SupersampleFactor`, which each executable sets; the Game's follows the quality tier). In texels the same star is four times dimmer at 2× than at 1×. `Spread` ships at exactly the shader's anti-aliasing floor (0.62 texels), which makes the two factors measurably the same (mean radiance 8.37 at 1× against 7.05 at 2×, a 19 % difference where the wrong answer is 4×); at 0.45 the floor took over at 1× and drew stars 38 % wider.
- **The pixel footprint is measured on the DIRECTION, never the chart.** `duv/dangle = 1 + uv²` depends only on `|uv|`, so it is continuous across all twelve seams (`CubeJacobian`). ⚠ `fwidth()` of the chart coordinate jumps by a whole cell at each seam and rings the sky with lines of wrongly sized stars. The footprint is the **Frobenius norm** of the screen-to-direction Jacobian, not `fwidth`'s L1 sum, so it is isotropic in the camera's bearing and the core does not swell and shrink as the lens turns (#150).
- **Stars are round in the sky, not in the chart** (#87). `uv = tan(angle)` turns the view direction by `cos²θ` radially but `cos θ` tangentially, so a circle in the chart is an ellipse on the sky (1.41:1 mid-edge, 1.73:1 at a corner). `StarLayer` measures the corrected distance as the closed form `dot(offset, offset)·J − dot(offset, chart.xy)²` (no normalise, no divide, no branch; it reduces to the plain form at a face centre). Median axis ratio at 1366×768/72°: 1.22 / 1.51 / 1.54 (centre / edge / corner) before, 1.28 / 1.28 / 1.35 after. Spike arms are round too, with a **per-axis** margin (`sqrt(jacobian − chart.xy²)` per axis). `Galaxies` needs the corrected *vector* (it builds its own ellipse) and pays one `rsqrt`.

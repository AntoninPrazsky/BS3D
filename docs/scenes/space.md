# Space

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

Space is the ninth `SceneKind`: the island floats in deep space under a long-exposure astrophoto sky (a dense starfield, the Milky Way with dust lanes, three emission nebulae, distant galaxies, one banded planet) and inside a marched volume. It is deliberately the *picture* of space, the thing the eye recognises as space, not what an eye in orbit would see (mostly black). The star field is shared with the Moon and the aurora: [The star lattice](space-stars.md).

It is `Space.fx` on the shared `SceneRenderer` (`SpaceBackdrop`), configured by `SpaceSceneConfig`. `scene=space` works on any command line; it is on the Game's scene menu and in the random pick, and the Testbed's NumPad2 and the editor's V reach it (#380).

## The sky-replacing family

Space was the first backdrop to replace the **sky** rather than the ground. The dream, the cavern, the Moon (the one of them that keeps a ground), the aurora, the Grid and the big top followed; `SceneCatalog.ReplacesSky` is the flag, and it decides every item below. These scenes draw no dome, no cloud deck and no weather, clear to black, suppress the cloud shadow on the instanced effect and take their light rig through `SceneRenderer.TryGetLightRig`.

- **One full-screen pass, not a displaced grid.** A four-corner quad already in normalised device coordinates; the view ray is rebuilt per pixel from a `ViewRayBasis` uniform (`SkyRay.Basis`: the lens's right and up axes over the projection's slopes, and forward).
  - ⚠ **Never `Matrix.Invert(View * Projection)` carried to the far plane** (#635). In single precision across the 0.05 : 2000 depth range it made every sky object tremble under a moving camera: twelve 0.03° turns that should each move a star 0.41 px moved it 0.10 to 0.67 px; on the basis, 0.35 to 0.42. All sky-replacing scenes use the basis.
  - It covers the **whole** frame, so the clear colour is black and the wide-aspect corner band the other scenes clear the horizon for cannot happen. There is no `OriginXZ`, no `IslandHoleRadius` (nothing is drawn under the island) and no cloud hook.
- **Depth.** The pass draws under `DepthStencilState.None`, writing no depth, so the island, the cluster and the gun cover it. ⚠ `SpaceBackdrop.Draw` must restore `DepthStencilState.Default` on the way out: left at `None`, the island would not occlude the cluster and the frame would draw in submission order.
- **No weather, and the scene must say so.** `InstancedModel.fx` calls `CloudSunlight` unconditionally and its parameters persist on the shared effect between frames, so a coverage gain left over from the previous scene would keep shadowing the cluster with a deck that is not in the frame. `CloudField.SuppressOn` pushes a gain of zero (what `CloudSunlight` reads as "full sun") through the cached parameter slots `ApplyTo` uses.
- **No dome is forced**, unlike the sea and savanna. The dome is neither drawn nor read, so changing the player's dome would be a side effect with no visible effect.
- **`OpenBelow` includes space and it is not an `IsSolidTerrainScene`**: the drop cinematic dives under the island and watches the balls leave the drain against the starfield, and there is no pit shaft, so the drain looks straight onto the stars.

## The marched volume

Everything above `StarNestVolume` in `Space.fx` is a function of the view **direction** alone; the camera's position enters the shader only to build the ray. A sky made only of that is a painted dome: move the camera and not one pixel changes. So one layer is **marched from the camera's own position**, and structure one step out crosses the frame faster than structure eight steps out. That parallax is the scene's depth (two frames along the same direction from positions 45 units apart differ by a mean |ΔRGB| of 39.8; before the volume, exactly 0).

- **The field is Pablo Roman Andrioli's Star Nest** (MIT): space folded into mirrored cells by a triangle wave, then an iterated map `p = abs(p)/dot(p,p) − FORMULA` whose per-iteration *movement* is summed (an inside-out distance field: dark tracery inside a glowing web). Colour comes from `(s, s², s⁴)` along the ray, so the near web is cool and the far one warm.
- ⚠ **The fold is spelled with `frac`, not the original's GLSL `abs(T − mod(p, 2T))`** (#147). HLSL `fmod` truncates toward zero where GLSL `mod` floors, so the straight port stopped mirroring below zero and jumped by the full 2T period at every `p = −2T·k`. The drift walks `p.z` through those planes (the first crossing enters the marched span between 483 and 772 s at the shipped ~33-unit camera) and the eight march circles stacked into one hard, frame-crossing brightness step. Reproduce with `SpaceTime` pinned to 661 s. The `frac` fold is also marginally the cheaper one (123 against 124 instruction slots).
- **Four changes make it belong to this scene.** It is entered at the camera; it is rotated into a basis of its own (`VOLUME_BASIS`), or the fold's cell walls read as a grid squared up with the island; the world is scaled down on the way in (`Volume.Scale`, the parallax dial; the cells are 0.85 units wide in the field's space against an island 26 across, so raw coordinates would fly the eye through thousands of cells and boil); and it carries an **extinction** (the original needs none, drawing nothing behind itself) taken from the emission's own luminance, as a reciprocal so it cannot go negative. Without it a bright web that lets every star shine through its knots reads as a decal.
- **`VOLUME_HAZE` decides whether the volume is an addition or a takeover.** The original's `v += fade` lifts every pixel (the series sums to about 3.7) and would take the void from the 3–4 display codes this scene is authored around to a uniform grey that swallows the Milky Way and the nebulae. It is kept at a small fraction, and its whole job is the thin stuff *between* the filaments.
- **One iteration may move the point at most `VOLUME_JUMP_CAP`, the first step `VOLUME_JUMP_CAP_EYE`** (#401, the "disco strobe"). The inversion divides by `dot(p, p)`, so an iterate near the origin is thrown out; summed and cubed, one throw lit a disc of the web far over the glare threshold, a bright point with a bloom halo that came and went within a third of a second because the drift carries a near-singular iterate off the step it was sampled on. It was the volume, **not the star lattice**. The cap sits just above the 99.9th percentile of an ordinary iteration's move, so only the singular tail is clipped.
  - The disc itself stays (every pixel in it runs the same orbit afterwards: it is the fractal's structure); the cap only sets how bright it is.
  - ⚠ Rejected, each measured: a cap on the step's **sum** (flattened the disc's core, left it popping); a flat per-iteration cap of 12 (dimmed the first step's frame-wide glow and took the sky's mean down a tenth); a cap rising as 1/s toward the eye (lifted the second step's cap, so the nearest discs became the brightest).
  - Effect from the pinned camera `campos=0,8,33 camtarget=0,11,0` (1600×900), frames 0.3 s apart: sky pixels changing by more than 40 codes fell from ~4 600–5 300 to ~50–680 and none reaches code 220. Cost +0.03–0.04 ms on a 1.8 ms frame.
- **Cost: the step and iteration counts are constants, not dials** (`VOLUME_STEPS` 8, `VOLUME_ITERATIONS` 10), because they multiply and a dial invites a setting that drops the frame off a cliff. The original's 20 × 17 is a Shadertoy's budget. At `ssaa 2` against the painted sky it replaced (60.4 FPS): 11 × 12 gives 29.5 FPS (+105 % frame time); the shipped 8 × 10 gives 36.5 (+65 %), and the two cannot be told apart side by side. Steps are deliberately not cut further: each is weighted by `VOLUME_DISTANCE_FADING`, so 0.76ⁿ has spent 93 % of its total by the eighth, but the march's depth is what the layer exists for.
- **`Volume.Strength` of 0 skips the march** behind a uniform branch and restores the painted-dome scene bit for bit: the lever for a weak machine or a quality tier. It ships at 0.6.

## The scene's own light

Space states its own rig (`SpaceLightingConfig`, handed over by `TryGetLightRig`, which the sky-replacing scenes do as a group). **All three executables honour it, the map editor included**, because loading a level sets its scene from the level and a space level previewed under the wrong sun is what that editor exists to prevent. The sea's trick of defaulting to a suitable dome fails twice here:

- the key tint is `lerp(white, horizon, SkyLightRig.SKY_TINT_STRENGTH)`, so the darkest dome **also halves the sun** and leaves the scene grey and flat;
- the drain's gold beads are metallic, so their reflectance is almost all hemisphere ambient, and near-black ambient turns the two rings that mark the drain black.

So the ambient is low, cold and **deliberately non-zero**. At zero the balls would survive (emissive) but the island, the gun and the glass would not: their shadow sides reach code 0 and the island reads as a hole cut in the starfield. Measured with the shipping numbers: island top 156–177 codes (the band the city's island has), planet's lit side 199, night side 22, void 3–4. Under the same dome 13 the city's island measures 114; space is the brighter, because its sun is not halved.

**Planetshine is a scene point light, not more ambient** (`SpaceBackdrop.TryGetPlanetshine`, `SceneRenderer.TryGetSpacePlanetshine`). Ambient is directionless, so raising it for a coloured flank flattens everything, and a real light puts a highlight back into the metallic beads. It stands `PlanetshineDistance` out along the planet's direction with a range three times that, so across a 26-unit island the `(1 − d/range)²` falloff varies by a few per cent and reads as directional.

## The layers and what holds them

- **Small things stay under `GLARE_THRESHOLD`; the brightest stars carry their spikes in the shader.** The reason, the max-not-add rule for spikes and the planet's limb exception are in [The star lattice](space-stars.md); the same constraint governs every layer here.
- **The Milky Way's elongation is mild.** A latitude squash of five at a third of the frequency gave long smeared streaks that read as motion blur, at a brightness (0.30) that tonemapped to a 75 %-grey wash burying the stars. It ships at a squash of 1.6 over a cloud frequency of 13 (2.2 over 9 still read airbrushed), with most of the band's weight carried by `Stars.StarBoost`, which is the right way round: a Milky Way *is* stars. The band is dense, narrow and golden at the core with ragged dust lanes cutting along it, as in the references.
- **Dust must dim the stars as well as the glow.** A lane that dims the band but lets the starfield shine through reads as a stripe painted on the sky. The nebulae do the same through a transmittance the pixel shader carries out of them.
- **Domain warping is the biggest lever in the nebulae** (0 = blobs, 1.5 = filaments and cavities), and the density must be spread hard about its own mean (`(body − 0.44) * 2.9`) before use, or ACES flattens a timid mottle into one tone. The nebulae are H-alpha red (`NebulaOne`), a bicolour emission region (`NebulaTwo`: a teal oxygen core, `Color`, inside red hydrogen filaments, `RimColor`) and a blue reflection region (`NebulaThree`), not hot pink. **The filaments sit in a soft glow** (`Glow`, the share of a mottled haze under them, coloured as the core) and take the skirts' colour but at the very middle (#978). ⚠ The spread alone, with no glow, cuts a nebula into hard blobs on black: the plain teal one, alone on its side of the sky and the subject of the chapter intro's "the nebula" shot, read to the owner as "random blue stripes". The references are in `C:\Users\panrd\AI\sd\out\978-zimage` and `978-klein` (bicolour HOO nebulae: a teal core glowing inside red filaments).
- **The planet is solved analytically in the same pass** (a unit sphere at the distance that gives its angular radius; the quadratic gives the normal) and lit by the same `SunDirection` as the island and the balls, which ties it to the frame. Its direction also sets the **phase**: the lit fraction is `(1 − dot(Direction, SunDirection)) / 2` and the dot should land in −0.4…−0.7 (it ships at −0.51, a fat gibbous): a full disc is flat, a thin crescent hides the bands. The terminator is a `smoothstep`, never a hard `N·L`, and the night side keeps `NightAmbient` or the planet reads as a hole. Bands carry contrast and a hard limb with a thin rim (`RimStrength`): a gas giant, not an Earth.
- ⚠ **The storm oval is placed by a direction.** A pair of projections `(dot(n, forward), latitude)` is satisfied by two points, so the oval appeared mirrored and half the placements put the ghost on the face being drawn. Measure a tangential offset from a centre direction with the antipode rejected (`step(0, dot(n, centre))`). The same trap waits for anything else placed on a sphere by two projections.
- **The void must be dark.** Every astrophotograph keeps black sky between its structures (#505). Two figures hold it: `VOLUME_HAZE` and `Volume.Strength`.
- **Galaxies place their smudge by remapping, not clamping.** A clamp piles every out-of-box roll onto the two margin lines (about a fifth of all galaxies on four straight lines per cell).
- **Config values**: angles are authored in degrees and converted where pushed at the shader; the galactic core is orthogonalised against the pole on the way through, so a hand-typed pair need not be exactly perpendicular; directions go through a `SafeNormal` with a fallback, because a zero typed into a property grid would otherwise send a NaN through the whole sky.

## The config shape: named groups

`SpaceSceneConfig` uses **named nested objects, never arrays** (`NebulaOne`/`Two`/`Three`, `Stars`, `MilkyWay`, `Galaxies`, `Planet`, `Lighting`, `Volume`): the shape the map editor's `PropertyGrid` required (`IgnoreCollections = true`, so a list was invisible). The grid went in #522; the shape stays because a named group reads as a config where an index does not. Every other scene that says "the named-groups shape" means this paragraph.

## The island: a station's deck (#954)

The owner: the island "does not feel finished", and he asked for a space base, perhaps held up by simple rocket engines. It was the grid's machined disc with a square grid of seams. The references (`954-zimage`, `954-klein`) agree on a machined disc laid in radial wedge plates round a collar at the drain, a blue light strip running round the rim in a groove, a darker hull stepping in underneath and thruster pods hanging from it.

- **The shape is `IslandShape.Station`** (`IslandMesh`): the machined top and chamfer, the rim's face, the strip's groove (`STATION_GROOVE_DEPTH`, `STATION_GROOVE_TOP`/`BOTTOM`), a second band and three steps in to a flat ring at `THRUSTER_MOUNT_Y`. The Grid keeps the plain machined disc.
- **The material is its own technique** (`InstancedModelTriplanarStation`, `IslandStation.fxh`, selected by `InstancedModelRenderer.StationSurface`): polar seams cut as grooves (the normal leans into them), the collar uncut, then 16 wedges and 24 staggered (`STATION_RINGS`, `STATION_SPOKES`), plates of slightly different tones with a darker panel here and there, a round hatch on one plate in five, yellow and black hazard arcs inside the floor's arris (`STATION_HAZARD`), the seams' faint running light (`JointGlow`, #537), and the strip on the groove's face at a radiance that blooms, with its spill on the groove's floor and ceiling. The island's detail texture gives the metal its grain.
- **The thrusters** are five pods on the mount ring (`ArenaIsland.THRUSTER_*`): a lathe shell in brushed steel, sky-lit like the hull, and a core inside the bell lit by its own emissive blue, steady (#401). ⚠ Wide and squat on purpose: the drop cinematic keeps its lens 1.5 under the drum (`STONE_CLEARANCE`), and a pod as long as the references' would stand in that band; the foot stops about 0.2 above it. WindingCheck joins shell and core (`Shell+Core`).
- **Cost**: cheaper than the machined disc it replaces, which drew the four-octave relief (Testbed 3840x1600, ssaa 2, `nopost`, two pairs: play pose `campos=0,-2,34 camtarget=0,-9,0` 8.08 against 8.72 ms, from below `campos=18,-19,40 camtarget=0,-12,0` 8.46 against 8.75).
- **The Potato path** draws the shape and the pods (their cores emissive there too) in the plain colour: it has no station technique.

## Cost

A full-screen procedural pass, so the expensive parts sit behind **uniform-coherent `[branch]`es** (a nebula's angular falloff, the Milky Way's band mask, the planet's disc), and the pixel footprint is taken **once at the top of the pixel shader**, before any of them: a gradient operation inside a divergent branch is undefined, and that ordering is the only reason those branches are safe. Frame cost by tier: "The quality tier" in `docs/game-shell.md`.

- **Do not shade it at a lower resolution.** Space sizes its stars in *output* pixels off `SceneRenderer.SupersampleFactor`, so the display-resolution path the dream and cavern use ("Shaded at the display's resolution" in [The cavern](cavern.md#shaded-at-the-displays-resolution-155)) would change how big and bright the stars come out.
- Measured against `main` for the #505 colour pass (desktop, Testbed, 1920×1080 at `ssaa 2`, `fpscap=400`, `arena=none`, the pinned repro vantage): 2.74 → 2.64 ms, nothing added.

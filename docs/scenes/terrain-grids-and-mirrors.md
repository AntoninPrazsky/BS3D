# Terrain grids, billboards and the CPU terrain mirrors

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

What every terrain scene shares underneath its own look: the camera-centred grids (#589), the billboard index buffers (#589) and the C# copies of each terrain's height field that everything standing on the ground is placed by (#590).

## The terrain grids and the billboards (#589)

- **Every terrain scene's camera-centred grid comes out of one reference-counted cache, `TerrainGridCache`**, keyed on (vertices a side, extent) and reached through `AcquireGridMesh` on `BackdropServices`. A grid carries nothing scene-specific (each shader moves it by its own `OriginXZ` and lifts it in the vertex shader). Three pairs exist: 360 over 1000 (desert, tropical, Mars), 360 over 1200 (mountains, volcano, Moon) and 220 over 1200 (meadow, forest, aurora); fourteen grids and 56.1 MB of buffers became eight and 34.0 MB.
- **The cache owns every buffer: a scene gives its grid back (`ReleaseGridMesh`) and never disposes it.** That matters for the two grids that change: the volcano's and Mars's step down to 256 a side under the reduced detail program (#540), and each step releases the full grid without taking it from scenes still drawing it (the cache holds ten grids at the reduced tier). Grids are built eagerly, so a scene switch builds nothing.
- ⚠ **Every billboard index buffer comes out of one builder, `BuildQuadIndexBuffer`, which refuses a count past `MAX_BILLBOARD_QUADS` (16,383).** Past 16,384 quads a 16-bit index wraps and later quads silently draw the first ones' vertices (five copies of that loop existed; only the volcano's counts were clamped, `MAX_BILLBOARD_PARTICLES` stays its dial). Snow and spray go through `BuildBillboardParticles` with their own seeds; the flame keeps its own vertices and takes the builder's `mirrored` order, because its quads stand on their base.
- **The tropical beach's dressing is uploaded once**: each palm, rock, scrub, tuft and driftwood variant holds a static instance buffer (`StaticInstances`, the savanna's `ScatterBucket` pattern) instead of copying matrices into a shared dynamic buffer every draw. It measured no gain, so it is one pattern instead of two, not a performance change (BestPractices §9). The savanna's hearth stones keep the dynamic path.

## The terrain mirrors (#590)

**Everything that stands on a terrain is placed by a C# copy of the field the terrain shader displaces its grid by**: the scatters' trees and rocks, the savanna's fires, the volcano's vents and lamps, and the chapter intro's lenses. All of them are `TerrainMirror`'s static, config-taking methods: `Desert`, `Mountain`, `Outback`, `Polar`, `Savanna`, `Tropical`, `Meadow`, `Forest` (the aurora's ground too, on `AuroraSceneConfig.Terrain`), `Volcano` and `Moon` (#680), on `ShaderMath`'s one copy of the helpers. The renderer keeps only the live-config wrappers a host asks through (`SavannaGroundHeight`, `VolcanoGroundHeight`, `PolarGroundHeight`). The savanna's and forest's ramps keep their hand-spelled clamp-then-hermite rather than `ShaderMath.SmoothStep`, which divides by `(R + T) - R` (not `T` to the last bit).

**`mirrorcheck` compares them with the GPU** (the Testbed switch, "Checking the terrain mirrors" in `docs/testbed.md`). Each terrain shader gains a `HeightProbe` technique by defining its height function and including `HeightProbe.fxh` last; the Testbed draws one quad through it into a 256x256 `Vector4` target covering +-512 round the arena, each pixel evaluating the shader's own height at the world XZ it hands back, so no grid, camera or vertex interpolation stands between the two numbers. Tolerance 0.01 world units. No frame of any executable selects the technique.

Measured max |dh| (desktop RX 6900 XT, 65,536 samples a scene), all passing: savanna 0.000033, meadow 0.000037, forest and aurora 0.000069, beach 0.000634, volcano massing 0.001717, desert 0.000232, mountains 0.000748, outback 0.000293, polar 0.000992, Moon 0.000109. The volcano also prints how far its mirror stands off the ground actually drawn (the scoria the mirror leaves out by design): **1.35 at most, 0.24 on average**, the clearance a path over the cone must allow.

- **Seen to fail (BestPractices section 10).** Before trusting the passes, the forest mirror's second octave was changed from 0.26 to 0.27 and the check run again: forest and aurora failed at 0.290 max, 61,795 of 65,536 samples over tolerance, exit 1.
- ⚠ **The noise hash is ill-conditioned in float, and the GPU's compiler rounds differently from C# in two places.** `NoiseHash22` takes `frac((x + y) * z)` of a product near 20,000, where a float carries the fraction to about 1/500, so a different rounding of the steps moves the hash by whole quanta and a ridged field raised to the third power magnifies it into units (a lens pulled several units off `TerrainMirror.Mountain` could be sixteen units inside the drawn range). Both causes were **measured, not guessed**, and both are now matched on the CPU (#598):
  - the compiler **fuses the hash's `dot` into multiply-adds**: `ShaderMath.Hash22` fuses its dot the same way (`FusedMultiplyAdd(z, x + 33.33, FusedMultiplyAdd(y, z + 33.33, x * (y + 33.33)))`);
  - it **folds literal constant offsets** (`cellId + seed + 23.7` with a literal `seed` becomes `cellId + 37.4`): the outback mirror writes `cellX + (seed + 23.7f)`.
  - The shaders stay as they are; what moved is everything the CPU derives from the noise (the four terrains' mirrors, `CloudField`'s weather mirror, `SavannaTrails`), now on the quanta the GPU draws.
- ⚠ **Still an assumption about the GPU**: this is the desktop's compiler output. It is the same DXBC on every machine (fxc compiles once at build time), so the fusion is in the bytecode and should hold on the laptop, but it was not measured there. A durable fix is a hash well conditioned in float on both sides, which changes the look and wants its own issue and captures.
- **Run `mirrorcheck` on every mirrored scene after touching a terrain shader's height or its mirror.**

# The scenes' observation points

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

Each `SceneKind` names **somewhere in itself worth looking at, and how a camera should stand to frame it**: `SceneRenderer.TryGetViewpoint`, the per-scene question (beside `ReplacesSky`, `IsSolidTerrainScene` and `OpenBelow`) answered in one place rather than in each caller (#289). The one asker is the Game's chapter intro ("The chapter intro" in `docs/game-feedback.md`), whose opening legs used to look across the island's far rim on a rolled bearing whatever the backdrop was. The figures live in each backdrop's `TryGetViewpoint` (and the two host-owned cities' arms in the renderer); this page holds the design and the traps.

## A point plus a recipe

- **Where the interesting thing is** is the scene's own business, so every point is read off the scene's **live config** and never written down twice (the volcano's crater moves with its cone config, the savanna's fire with its own).
- **How far out and how high** a camera stands is the camera's business and must scale with the **level**: a tall cluster is played from further back, so the distance is a **multiple of `GameCameraFit.Solve`'s own stand-off**, never a world distance. The bearing offset is measured from the subject's own bearing: 0 puts the camera between the arena and the subject looking outward (pure scenery, the island out of frame), 180 puts the subject beyond the arena so the island and its cluster stand in front of it.
- **"No landmark" is not "no viewpoint".** A meadow is the same in every direction but still wants a *low, raking* look out over its hills where the mountains want a *far, raised* one at the peaks. Such scenes build their point from the caller's rolled bearing; since #409 the chapter intro places that bearing off the gun's own bearing, so the whole flight is one turn round the arena that lands on the gameplay pose, and a landmark scene's stand is still the landmark's.

## What each scene points at

City: the canyon (down the shaft the towers rise out of). Neon city: the lit roofline at the top of that shaft. Sea: the open water at the horizon, at `SeaSceneConfig.LevelY`. Savanna: the campfire, `SavannaCampfirePosition(0)` (the only subject that is also a light). Desert: the dune skyline. Mountains: the peaks (`LevelY + Height * 0.75`). Meadow: the hills, from down in the grass. Forest: the tree line just outside the clearing, at crown height. Space: the planet (`SpacePlanetConfig.Direction`), behind the arena because a planet with nothing in front has no scale. Dream: the marbled sky at the solids' orbit radius. Cavern: the river at `Water.LevelY` (the light is all below). Moon: the highland belt at `HighlandCrestRadius`. Outback: the monoliths. Tropical: the lagoon out to the far shore's ring, from over the palm tops (at 8 degrees the lens stood inside the grove once the palms grew to 22 units, #555). Volcano: the crater, read off `VolcanoLightPosition(0, ...)`, the scene's own solved vent and not the cone's centre. Mars: Phobos, placed through `DirectionFromElevationAzimuth` exactly as the shader places it. Storm: the cloud deck, which is **below** the arena. Polar: the pressure front at `RidgeRadius`, the only thing with a silhouette to read distance against. Aurora: the aurora itself, high overhead. Grid: the floor, low and close so the lines rake off to a vanishing point.

## Traps

- ⚠ **The city viewpoints are stated and no longer flown (#488).** A viewpoint is a bearing on a circle round the arena, and in a city that circle runs through the towers (the neon roofline's stand came out as a facade at arm's length straight after the prologue's cut). Both cities, and since #559 the sea, desert, outback, mountains, meadow, savanna, forest and tropical beach (and before them the volcano, #530, and aurora, #531), open the intro on their own prologue and fly only the last leg, so their rows are flown by no intro. The rows stay because `TryGetViewpoint` is still the one answer for anything else that asks.
- ⚠ **The meadow's entry was wrong until #447, and the same mistake is available to every scene.** It named the flowers, 70 units out at one unit over the grass. The look-at was inside the clearing (70 against a `ClearingRadius` of 95), so it pointed at flat ground with the hills *behind* it; and the **elevation is measured from the tour's centre**, which is the level's camera target up at the hanging cluster, so six degrees off *that* rides high over a meadow whose ground is fourteen units below the arena plane. The fix: the look-at goes out **onto the rise** (`ClearingRadius + ClearingTransition * 0.55`) and the elevation goes **negative**, which is what puts the lens near the ground. The grass's shading (tips, tufts, wind bands, translucency) reads at a low raking angle and not from above.
- **Two were changed by the photograph, not the argument that chose them.** The cavern looked *up* at the roof (almost black: everything glows from below), and its viewpoint is low over the lake; the dream tilted the lens off the island entirely (wallpaper, not a place).
- **Scenes no level names cannot be photographed in the intro.** An intro opens only on a block's first level, and the shipped level set (`grep '"scene"' Game/Levels/*.json`) names fourteen scenes, ten levels each; the sea, forest, outback, tropical, Mars, storm and polar scenes have viewpoints from their configs that no run of the game shows. Photograph them the moment a level names one.

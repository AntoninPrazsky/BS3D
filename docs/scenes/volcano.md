# The volcano

Part of [The scenes](../scenes.md).

The fifteenth `SceneKind` (#223): the island stands on the flank of an erupting volcano. A black basalt cone rises off to one side with gullies down its flank and thin incandescent rivulets from the rim. `VolcanoSceneConfig.RiverCount` lava rivers run in the gullies and out across the plain, one passing the arena. A lava lake churns in the crater, and the field round the island is glassy black lava with glowing cracks. Fountains spray from the crater and two side vents, an ash column opens into a head the wind takes, the cloud deck over the crater glows, and ash drifts through the frame.

- **Files:** `Volcano.fx`, `LavaFountain.fx`, `Ash.fx`, `VolcanoSceneConfig` (named groups `Fountains`, `Eruption`, `Ash`, `Steam`), `VolcanoBackdrop` (rivers and vents are placed in `VolcanoLava`).
- **Launch:** `scene=volcano`. The campaign's eighth chapter, The Eruption, plays all ten levels here (lava ball style). Its intro opens on a prologue (#530) built by `VolcanoIntroShots` through `SceneRenderer.VolcanoGroundHeight`; see "The volcano (#530) and the aurora (#531)" in [Chapter intro prologues](../game-feedback/chapter-intro-prologues.md).
- **Flags:** `IsSolidTerrainScene` (the island's footprint is cut out of the flank, a dark pit shaft backs the drain's glass); it does not replace the sky. `SkyDome.SceneDefault` gives it dome 9, a dim mauve-and-slate dusk with no bright band. Dome 16 is darker at the zenith, but its cream horizon and its sun disc beside the cone compete with the crater.

## The ground is the light

This is the first scene whose ground lights the cluster. The crater and the flows are real `SceneLights` point lights throwing red-orange up at the undersides of cluster, island and gun, while the dome lights their tops. Slot 0 is the crater, which takes the eruption envelope on top of its base glow. Every other slot is a flow front travelling downhill, swelling in and dying out over its run, so these are the game's first moving lamps; this is why `VolcanoLightPosition` takes a time. Slots 1 and 2 ride the river aimed past the arena, a third of a span apart, so a front is nearly always near the play field. `LightCount`, `LightRange`, `LightStrength` and `DeckGlow` are the dials.

- **The readability constraint is the scene's first rule.** Red and orange balls hang over red-orange lava. `LightStrength` (0.22 at range 130) is set against the savanna's campfire, the only other lamp of this kind, which is (2.4, 1.0, 0.32) at range 32. At 0.55 and range 190 the island's stone turned gold. Check with `Thirteen_Colors.json` hung over the cone from the play camera: all thirteen colours must read, the reds included.
- **⚠ A backdrop behind the cluster counts as much as a light on it.** `DeckGlow` (0.11) is held low because from the Game's play pose the cluster hangs directly in front of the glowing deck over the crater. At 0.22 the Game still put a saturated red-orange sky behind the red and yellow balls, although the Testbed's play camera, aimed lower, did not show it.
- **Two figures that were wrong on the first pass.** The halo (`HaloWidth`, three river widths) is how far a flow visibly heats the ground; at six, with the arena beside the river, the whole foreground glowed. `LavaHot` stays low because ACES takes a saturated colour straight to white-yellow, and a white river is a lit crack rather than molten rock.
- **Haze is `HorizonColor × HazeTint` at `HorizonHazeDistance` 900**, an ash pall rather than the dome's lit horizon. Fading to the dome's cream horizon painted the whole cone the colour of sand.

## Terrain: rivers, summit, mirror

- **The rivers run where the ground drains.** The gullies are a bearing term in the height field, and `SceneRenderer.SnapToGully` solves that same term for its floors and lays each river in one (four contraction passes). Laid on arbitrary bearings the flows crossed the channels obliquely and read as paint on a cone. River 0 is aimed past the arena; if the snap walks it inside the island plus two river widths, it takes the next gully out.
- **⚠ The CPU mirror carried its own gully band for a long time.** `TerrainMirror.Volcano` must carry the shader's gully figures. Until #509 it did not, so it put the ground up to 7 units above the channel on the lower flank, where the flow fronts' lamps run, and nothing had ever compared the two. Run `mirrorcheck` after touching either ([Capturing and measuring](../testbed/capture-and-measurement.md)). The volcano adds a second line for the drawn ground, which includes scoria the mirror leaves out by design.
- **⚠ A cone's summit is a sharp point whatever crater term you subtract.** The flank `ConeHeight · pow(1 − r/ConeRadius, ConeProfile)` is maximal at `r = 0` with a non-zero slope, and a crater term that is also maximal at `r = 0` only steepens the final approach (a nozzle tip). The flank's radius is therefore clamped to `CraterRadius`, so it plateaus at its own rim height inside the crater, and the crater bowl is cut into that plateau. It is mirrored in `TerrainMirror.Volcano`.

## The flows, the flank and the field (#509, #554)

Drawn from 33 references (not in the repository). Every one said the opposite of the first build.

- **A flow is mostly dark.** `FlowRadiance` composes a skin of chilled crust (`CrustColor` lit, plus a dull `CrustGlow` fading down the run), fissures in its bed (`CrackGlow`), an open core, and a thin bright line along each bank. The core is `CORE_AT_VENT` of the half-width under the vent, pinched to `CORE_AT_FRONT` by the front and torn wider in slow patches. Lighting the whole width as a warped honeycomb read as a giraffe's hide.
  - **The fissures stay put and the crust rolls over them (#554).** The fissure network is an anisotropic Voronoi read in the channel's unscrolled coordinate (`ConeR`); the crust rafts are a gradient noise in the scrolled `Along` coordinate, thresholded so `RAFT_COVER` of the channel is crust. A fissure under a raft shows only `RAFT_SHOW` of its glow; one in the lead between rafts glows in full, and the lead shows the melt as a dull glow (`LEAD_GLOW`). ⚠ More `LEAD_GLOW` turns the channel back into the lit width #509 took away. The crater lake's plates are carried, as a lake's crust is.
  - **Re-check in motion, not by eye:** `scene=volcano sceneseed=0 sky=9 nopost campos=-46,0,0 camtarget=-64,-13,-16 shot=6,8`. A line detector over the channel overlapped between the two frames at IoU 0.36 before and 0.82 after.
  - **Cost: none measurable** (15.19 against 15.20 ms beside the flow, 14.03 against 14.07 at the play pin; `VolcanoPS` 2256 instruction slots against 2187).
- **The core's streamlines are stripes, not contours.** The zero contour of a stretched noise closes round every saddle and filled the core with glowing eyes. It is `cos(across · 2.2 + 2.5 · bend)` cubed, the phase bent by a noise stretched along the flow.
- **The flows are narrow under the summit** (0.4 of their width at the rim, full width by 0.55 of the cone's radius). At full width the five flows paved the upper cone, which no reference drew.
- **The flank is black rock with thin glowing threads.** `Rivulets` draws the zero contour of a `GradientNoise3` read on a circle in noise space, so the threads wander and close round the cone with no seam (an `atan2` bearing tears along −X). The summit is oxidised (`ScoriaColor`, strongest at the rim). The crater holds a lava lake of drifting crust plates with incandescent seams, the one place a Voronoi web is the right picture. The halo is kept faint (`river.Halo² × 0.07`) so rock between flows stays black.
- **Cooled lava is glass.** A Fresnel-weighted sheen (`SheenStrength`) sits on young basalt and the flows' crust, not on scoria or the oxidised summit, and on the crests of the ropy relief, because an even film reads as a wet floor. It reflects mostly the zenith: 8 % of the dome's warm `HorizonColor` is kept, since in full it turned the field brown mud. The relief's grain runs across the downhill direction; downhill it drew brushed metal.
- **The field cracks** (`FieldCrackStrength`): the zero contour of a two-octave noise (one octave reads as a glowing wire), a fixed 0.22-unit half-width, antialiased off the noise's own slope.
  - ⚠ **Thresholding a noise near zero against an assumed steepness paints a blob at a saddle.** `fwidth(n)` cannot sit in a branch, so every terrain pixel paid for cracks only a few patches have. The slope comes analytically from `CloudNoiseD` in `Clouds.fxh`, which lets the crack sit behind a data branch worth 0.4 ms of the wide vantage's 23 Mpix frame.
  - The flow's Voronoi and three noise taps are also behind a data branch on the flow mask; they were paid by every terrain pixel before.

## Eruption, fountains, plume, deck glow

- **The eruption is a pure function of the wall clock.** `SceneRenderer.VolcanoEruption(time)` puts one burst in each `EruptionConfig.Period`, hashing where in the period it starts and how big it is off the period index, with a fast attack and a long decay. One figure drives the jets' reach, the plume's density and the crater's light, and it holds no state, so game, Testbed and map editor see the same eruption at the same second.
- **Light first, sound a beat behind.** `SceneEventSounds` rides `VolcanoBurstSchedule` through `TryGetSceneEvent` and speaks `ProceduralAudio.PlayEruption` from the crater after distance over `SPEED_OF_SOUND` (a flat 0.78 s for the 266-unit crater). See [The storm](storm.md).
- **The fountains are ballistic, in the vertex shader.** One static billboard buffer: the first slice is the plume and the rest the jets (`PlumeFraction`), as two index ranges and two techniques, so neither pass touches the other's particles and neither carries a runtime branch. Each blob picks a vent, a lifetime, a launch azimuth and a tilt (sqrt-distributed, so the cone fills evenly) and flies `p = vent + dir·v·t − ½g·t² + wind·drag·t²`. It is thrown white-hot at `LavaHot × 1.35` and its colour is its age, so a jet's top is darker than its root with no extra particles. The terrain's depth hides anything that falls below the surface.
- **The fountains are streaks, with a heart.** A quad's long axis is its velocity's component across the line of sight times `StreakTime`, so a blob crossing the frame draws an arc and one flying at the lens stays round. Round blobs read as confetti. The blaze at the vent is one soft additive quad over the crater (`GlowStrength`, the `Glow` technique), depth-read so the rim hides its lower half from a low camera.
- **The plume is an ash column with a head.** The swirl radius grows with `age + 2.4·age²` and the drift with `t²`, so the head opens and bends downwind while the stem stands (linear growth kept a tube). A puff is shaded as the sphere it stands for: its lower side takes the crater's glow (`PlumeGlow`, falling off as `exp(−4.5·age)`), its upper side what little the night sky gives. The column does not thin between bursts.
- **⚠ `BlendState.AlphaBlend` in MonoGame is premultiplied** (`One, InverseSourceAlpha`), so a shader returning a straight colour has it added at full strength on every layer. The smoke was written that way, and 900 puffs of 0.085 grey came out as a white steam plume brighter than the sky behind it. Plume, ash and steam premultiply (`float4(color * alpha, alpha)`). All the weather does now (`Snow.fx` since #654, `Spray.fx` and `StormClouds.fx` since #675). A look tuned under the fault comes out fainter or dimmer with it fixed.
- **The cloud deck over the crater glows.** The deck is the sky's (`Sky.fx`), so the scene reaches it as a light on the ground: `SceneRenderer.TryGetGroundGlow` hands the crater's position, a colour and a reach to `CloudField.SetGroundGlow`. Both hosts state it every frame (clearing it for every other scene, so none inherits it), and the sky shader adds it to the cloud's underside with a Gaussian fall-off, more in thick cloud than in a wisp. It rides `VolcanoEruption`. It is black elsewhere, which makes the term an exact no-op in the hottest pass in the game rather than a branch. The map editor draws no deck and cannot show it.

## Fumaroles, blocks and spatter cones (#679)

References of an active lava field raise white steam columns from vents scattered across it.

- **Steam:** `SteamConfig.VentCount` vents at rolled bearings `NearestVent`–`FarthestVent` from the arena, none in the crater or on river 0's line, each with its own strength (0.6–1). `LavaFountain.fx`'s `Steam` technique runs over its own billboard buffer (`ParticleCount`, each puff tied to one vent), drawn before the plume, premultiplied, reusing the plume's pixel shader. The column climbs on a saturating rise (a subtracted square sends puffs back down), leans downwind and opens to three or four times its width. `Color` sits under the glare threshold and far above the plume's ash; young puffs are lit from below by `LavaCool × Glow`.
  - ⚠ **A fumarole is tight at the vent and wide at the head** (`Size` 3.6, `Rise` 7.5, `Life` 12). A 2.2-unit puff rising 4.5 a second for 7 s read as cigarette smoke at a hundred units; doubling the size gave floating clouds with no column.
- **Scatter:** `VolcanoPlanting` through the planted scenes' `PlantPass`, casting into the sun's map: 1400 basalt blocks (size roll cubed, 0.5 to 5) and 600 rounder bombs, sunk to the lowest ground under their footprint and kept off the island, the crater, every river's line and every vent's mouth. The three strongest fumaroles stand on spatter cones (`SPATTER_CONES`, a lathed cone with a crater in its top), because a vent that has spat for a while builds a chimney.
  - ⚠ **Blocks from `RockMesh` (a lathe) came out smooth pale mushroom caps.** `VolcanoPlanting.BlockMesh` is an icosahedron with every vertex pushed in or out, squashed, flat-shaded, each face wound by its centroid (`WindingCheck` judges all six shapes).
  - ⚠ **At 0.018 the basalt read black on black.** It is 0.028 dusted to 0.06, and there are enough blocks to be seen. At night they read mostly as silhouettes against the cracks and rivers, as in the references.
- **Cost** (Game, `level=Breach`, 1920×1080, `nocap`, alternating against `main`): steam +0.25 ms High / +0.07 Low (3200 puffs cost +0.43 / +0.12, pure fill, and photographed the same as 1800 at a denser `Strength`); blocks, bombs and cones +0.08 / +0.036.

## Tiers and costs

- **`VolcanoReduced`** is selected by `SceneDetail`, so the Game's `Low` takes it. It gives up the rivulets and the field cracks together (a lone reduction buys nothing on an occupancy-bound pass); the flows, the lake, the sheen and the haze stay on every tier. Since #540 it also drops the combed relief and its derivative normal, an octave of the rock's patches and two of the vertex program's scoria, ends each flow's front on two crossed sines instead of the gradient noise (five taps a pixel in the river loop, 0.95 ms on its own), and runs on a 256-vertex grid (`VOLCANO_GRID_N_REDUCED`, against `VOLCANO_GRID_N` 360, rebuilt when the tier crosses the line). On the APU the Game at `Low` went Caldera 18.31 to 14.49 ms and Paroxysm 17.35 to 13.47 (the #540 table in `docs/game-shell.md`).
- **The layer probe:** `volcano=` (`VolcanoLayer`: `Terrain`, `Plume`, `Jets`, `Glow`, `Ash`, `All`) pins layers, and alternates as `alt=volcano=all;volcano=all/-plume;volcano=all/-ash`. On the APU the plume, jets, blaze and ash cost nothing measurable and the terrain was 5.2 ms of a 14.9 ms frame ("The dials" in [Capturing and measuring](../testbed/capture-and-measurement.md)).
- **Desktop figures to compare against** (RX 6900 XT, Testbed fixed camera, 1600×900 at ssaa 4, dome 9, `nopost nooverc`, `fpscap=400`, 18 s runs, first four readings dropped, alternating builds): wide vantage `campos=90,30,140 camtarget=-45,40,-250` **11.1 ms** (`VolcanoReduced` 10.5), play vantage `campos=0,-4,30 camtarget=0,-8,0` **11.6 ms** (reduced 10.3). It was the dearest terrain scene by about 2.5 ms when built (two `VoronoiEdge2` plus four warp taps over a full-frame terrain) and is now cheaper than it was, since the flows, lake and crust moved behind data branches. **No APU figure is claimed for the full tier.**
- **⚠ A Testbed launched with PowerShell's `-WindowStyle Minimized` is activated and takes the owner's keystrokes.** Launch through `CreateProcess` with `SW_SHOWMINNOACTIVE` instead; it measures the same as a visible window. A run that falls from ~11.7 to ~4.9 ms partway, with nothing in its log, was disturbed; discard and re-run it.

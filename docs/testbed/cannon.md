# The cannon

Part of [The Testbed](../testbed.md).

Built and drawn by `CannonRig`, posed by `Cannon` ([The hardware shared with the Game](shared-hardware.md)); both executables draw the same gun.

## The barrel and the magazine

`CannonMesh` is a profiled, thick-walled steel tube, open at the muzzle, closed at the breech by a dome with a cascabel, with a **slot** along the top (`SLOT_HALF_ANGLE`) whose cut edges have rim faces.

- **The slot's cheeks and the bore share their stations** (#974): a cheek is cut at every Z of the outer profile, so the slotted bore has a station at each of them (collinear, so it shades as one span) and the two meet vertex to vertex; the bore as one span from the muzzle to the window's end met the cheeks in a row of T-junctions, a hairline crack along the slot that the rasteriser's rounding decides. What is left open (the steps of the girdle and the taper, three vertices each) is exact coincidence and is a row of `WindingCheck --openness`'s table.
- **It must stay a tube with a slit**, or it reads as a trough. The balls sit wholly inside the bore (radius 0.5 in `BORE_RADIUS` 0.6), so the slot's width decides how much of the queue shows.
- The magazine is `Magazine.SIZE` real balls added to the shared buckets by `CollectMagazineBalls` at `BorePose.SlotPosition`, `Magazine.SPACING` apart, slot 0 at the muzzle (the spawn point), in the barrel's basis. The Testbed deals `RandomBallType` over all thirteen colours.
- **The freshest round parks in the breech**: the slot stops `CHAMBER_DEPTH` short of the breech face, the dome hides a cavity of that depth, and `BorePose.SlotPosition` clamps the last slot's glide to it. Hood, cavity and park depth are **one constant**, which keeps the handoff seamless.

## The glass over the queue

A thin pane (`CannonGlassMesh`; `GLASS_COLOR`, `GLASS_ALPHA`, `GLASS_SKY_REFLECTION`) fills the slot, with the front of the queue notched out so the round about to fire is never seen through glass.

- **The notch is half an ellipse reaching `GLASS_NOTCH_REACH` (3 ball radii)**, bounded on both sides from the precise-aim lens (the constant's comment has the geometry): shorter hides the front round from that lens, longer uncovers nothing more. It uncovers the second round too.
- ⚠ **So the Game's mark on the next round must not be removed as redundant.** The barrel hides its own muzzle end from the play camera, leaving only the front ball's cap in open air. The Game marks that round with the muzzle collar (`CannonRig.DrawMuzzleCollar`): "The muzzle round's mark: a collar on the gun" in [The magazine strip, the chips and the muzzle's marks](../game-feedback/hud-magazine-and-muzzle.md).
- **The pane is recessed into the steel** (`GLASS_EMBED`, `UNDERSIDE_LIFT`, #708), so nothing is coplanar. ⚠ A pane held off the steel left slits showing the rounds at full colour along the cheeks. To inspect: `campos=` on a circle round the window (centre about (0, -5.6, 20.1) with the gun at rest), `fov=32`, `nopost`.
- **`DrawGlass` is not part of `Draw`**: it is drawn **last of the translucent surfaces**, with the exact matrix the barrel was drawn with, recoil included.
- **Tuning**: only the alpha dims the queue (transmission `1 − alpha`); zeroing the colour and the reflection moved the covered band by half a level of 255, so darken with the alpha and leave those two. Screen brightness goes roughly as `transmittance^0.44` under ACES. The colour arrives premultiplied by the alpha, so lower every channel and keep the saturation in their ratio. The sky reflection is multiplied by the alpha and held far under other surfaces', because at the game camera's grazing angle Fresnel turns it into an opaque pale sheet.
- ⚠ **A pane opaque on only some scenes is a blend-state leak** (#667): `PlantPass.Draw` once left `BlendState.Opaque` set on the meadow, mountains and forest. `PlantPass` now restores its states and `BS3DGame.BeginSceneDraw` restates the translucent baseline. Check the blend state at `DrawGlass` before retuning.

## The carriage

Cheek plates holding the trunnion pins, two axle stubs, a split trail and two omnidirectional wheels (`GunCarriageMesh`, `OmniWheelMesh`/`OmniRollerMesh`).

- ⚠ **Nothing of the frame stands where the tube can reach.** The carriage yaws with the aim, so the tube only elevates and recoils along its axis. The plates hug it (`CHEEK_INNER_X`) only inside the hub (`CannonMesh.NearestSteelWiderThan` less `CHEEK_HUB_CLEARANCE`) and stand `CHEEK_RELIEF_CLEARANCE` past the widest steel elsewhere; the trail sits outboard of that plane; the axle is two stubs because the breech sweeps between the plates. The trail is split because the breech dips between its legs. The pins are the carriage's, not the tube's, because pins riding a recoiling tube would tear along the plates. Check a change by sweeping every elevation and recoil stroke against every part.
- **The gun stands on the stone under it**: `CannonRig.TrunnionHeightAt(radius)` = `ArenaIsland.FloorHeightAt(radius) + AXLE_DROP + WHEEL_RADIUS` on every move, because the island's top is a dish. `CannonRig.StanceGradeAt` pitches the carriage with the ground (`Cannon.CarriageWorld`'s up is the ground's normal, never the aim's), and `GroundHeight` is re-anchored per frame, or the ground darkening saturates under a sunken gun.
- The wheel bodies roll by `Cannon.WheelTravel` (`RollTravel` plus the recoil's shove) and not during an orbit, which drags a carriage rather than rolling it; the rollers spin by `SlideTravel`. ⚠ **Neither walk owns an axis**: the carriage yaws and the walk does not, so `Cannon.AccumulateWalk` resolves each step vectorially into the carriage's drawn axes.
- The roller profile `ρ(t) = R − √(a² + t²)` keeps the wheel round; `OmniWheelMesh.RollerSeats` throws if rollers collide. Rollers are their own instanced draw and fluted **helically**, because a smooth or straight-fluted roller does not show which way it turns.
- **The wheel is two materials** (#1212): machined metal plates, hub and hub face (`WHEEL_COLOR`, lighter than the carriage's iron and short of the barrel's steel) round **dark rubber rollers** (`ROLLER_COLOR`) with a soft sheen of their own (`ROLLER_SPECULAR` at `ROLLER_SPECULAR_POWER`, through `InstancedModelRenderer.SetMaterialSpecular`; at the renderer's default white highlight at 16 a dark albedo reads as glossy plastic). ⚠ **Both halves of the contrast are load-bearing**: the plates lighter than the rubber tell the rollers apart standing still, and rubber dark but not black keeps the flutes shading, which is what shows them turning. At sRGB 0.18 the rollers measured 17 of 255 in daylight and read as black. The step is measured from pixels against a mask capture of the two parts (the Game at High, the gun at rest, the meadow's daylight, side-on and from behind the flank): the rollers 38 to 40 of 255 with their flutes spread over 93 to 96 (10th to 90th percentile), the plates 62 to 66, a step of +22 to +29 (×1.6 to ×1.8 in linear luminance); the neon city's night 49 against 101. Measure it again before retuning either colour; `OmniWheelTests` holds the order (rubber under the plates, the steel and the iron, over a 0.2 grey).
- **The hub face** (`OmniWheelMesh.AddHubFace`, #485, #1212) is lathed onto each plate's outer face: a lip round the plate's edge, a chamfered bolt flange with eight hex nuts standing proud, and a cap that steps up to a shallow dome with the axle's nut on its crown, so the plate reads as a dished disc and the centre as a machined hub. All of it lies inside the plates' radius, so only a roller touches the ground, and its feet are sunk `FOOT_SINK` into the plate so the body is shut (`WindingCheck --openness`). It is the body's mesh and colour: a colour of its own would be a third draw, with its shadow and motion passes, for a part its shape already sets apart. It stands `HUB_FACE_PROUD` past the plate, which `LaserEmitterClearanceTests` models as a second, narrower disc on each wheel (`OmniWheelTests` holds the mesh inside both).
- **The play view does not show the wheels**: with the gun at rest they stand under the frame (measured in the meadow's Amphora at 16:9, 2.4:1 and the phone's 2.2:1). So a change to them is photographed from the menu's pass at the gun (`menugun=pass`), on the website's cannon, or from a pinned camera (the Game's capture mode with `campos=`/`camtarget=`, or the Testbed's).

## The metal's wear on High and Ultra (#1199)

`CannonRig.Worn` draws the barrel, the carriage and the wheels' plates through `InstancedWornMetal` (`InstancedModel/WornMetal.fxh`) instead of the plain material, and the drain's laser emitters take the same with the gun's figures (`LaserEmitters.Worn`). The Game turns it on for High and Ultra (`QualityPreset.MetalWear`, set in `ApplyQuality`); the Testbed draws it unless `wear=0` (also an `alt=` dial). Low, Medium and the Potato path draw the plain material. The rule behind it is #640's: a material that renders perfect reads as CG.

- **Five flaws, in the mesh's own space**, so they ride the barrel as it elevates, a wheel as it rolls and a housing as it turns: a patina's mottle, oxidation (warm, matt patches, nearer on the mesh's underside), grime, rubbed edges, and scratches with a sprinkle of pits. How much of each a material carries is `MetalWear.Amount`: `CannonRig.STEEL_WEAR` (the barrel and the emitters' housings, the most scratched), `IRON_WEAR` (the carriage and the emitters' pedestals and yokes, the most tarnished) and the wheels' own (mostly their machining). Never the rollers (rubber), the collar (its colour is a statement, #425) or the glass.
- **Tarnish and polish go to the profile's corners.** Every worn part but the carriage's plates and the yoke is turned, so its profile's corners are named from the very profile the mesh is turned from (`CannonMesh.WearCorners`, `OmniWheelMesh.WearCorners`, `LaserEmitterMesh.WearCorners`): an inside corner (a ring's foot, the pedestal on the glass) is a join, tarnished and grimed; an outside one (a ring's crest, the muzzle's two lips, the cascabel's knob) is an edge, rubbed bright in places. The shader measures to them along and off the part's axis, at most `MetalWear.MAX_CORNERS` (16) a part, and `MetalWearTests` holds which kind each is. ⚠ A screen-space curvature cannot find them: a crease is two triangles with a normal each, and derivatives never cross a triangle's edge. The bands' width is the part's (`MetalWear.Bands`): the barrel's joins reach 0.07, out onto the tube in front of a ring (the ring's own face is rarely lit), the wheels' and the emitters' `SMALL_JOIN_WIDTH` (0.022), since a band as wide as the barrel's covered a wheel's plate in rings.
- **Scratches** run along the bore on the barrel, round the axle on the wheels' faces (their machining), along the emitters' axis, and every way on the carriage, fewer on the undersides. A scratch is a rough groove, so it shows the light's average round it (the sky and a share of the sun), which reads lighter than the steel. ⚠ Scratches drawn only as a lighter albedo vanished into the sunlit barrel. Every mark fades by the share of a pixel it fills and is never drawn thinner than one, so a gun far off on the menu's orbit loses its scratches rather than crawling.
- ⚠ **In full sun the barrel's lit flank sits in the tonemap's shoulder** and its wear barely shows there: it reads in the shade, on the breech, on the wheels, and from the play camera, which sees the barrel from behind against the sky.
- **Cost** (RX 6900 XT, the Testbed's `alt=wear=0;wear=1`, 43 paired cycles each, another session's game sharing the card): the play camera's view at a 3840x1600-class load (1920x800 at `ssaa=4`) +0.06 ms; a close view with the gun filling about a third of a 23 Mpix frame (1600x900 at `ssaa=4`) +0.81 ms. The pixel program is about 1,280 instruction slots against the plain material's 507 (fxc, `ps_5_0`).
- The website's live cannon draws the plain material ([The website](../website.md)): the wear changes no colour or figure `--export-cannon` writes.

## The pose: trunnions, world up and the recoil

- **`Cannon.Position` is the trunnions**, the point the barrel turns about. The mesh is laid out about `CannonRig.PivotToFrontBall`, and `Cannon.MuzzlePosition` derives the muzzle the magazine and `ShootBall` hang off.
- ⚠ **The trunnions sit `TRUNNION_SETBACK` behind the tube's midpoint** (#287), like a howitzer's, because elevation otherwise drove the cascabel into the stone. The setback lives only in `PivotToFrontBall`, so everything derived from it moves together.
- ⚠ **Not fixed: the recoil stroke.** At the elevation limit `RECOIL_BACK` drives the cascabel about 0.84 under the stone for ~0.24 s. Clearing it needs a taller carriage (another gun) or variable recoil, which would cost the shot's feel at the elevations most played; that is the owner's call.
- ⚠ **The slot's up is world up, never the camera** (`Cannon.BarrelOrientation`): rolling it towards the camera looked wrong on a stand that only elevates and traverses. The low camera does not always see the queue, and that is accepted.
- **A loaded round lies turned in that frame by one constant** (#939, `BorePose.LOADED_TURN`, applied in `BorePose.SlotWorld`): a quarter round the barrel's right, so its own Z stands up out of the slot and its own Y points forward along the bore. The slot shows only a round's top, and untouched that top was its own Y pole, where the colour cue's figures are not: every band runs round its frame's Y (the wool's red wave, the marble's and the ice's whole set), and the owner saw "only a red ball". Turned, the slot shows the side the HUD's queue disc shows (face-on along Z, Y up the screen as the player looks along the bore). The turn reads no camera, aim speed, clock or colour, so a round never turns as the camera moves or a swap or re-colour changes it. **The Cutter keeps the barrel's own frame** (`SlotWorld(..., barrelFrame: true)`): its saw's bearing in the bore is #767's verdict. A fired round still flies from the identity (`AddShotBall`), as it did when the loaded one stood in the barrel's frame, so its figure turns at the muzzle as it always did. `LoadedRoundTurnTests` holds the frame to the barrel's times the constant, and every figure of every set to at least 5 % ink in the cap the slot shows (`CannonRig.SLOT_HALF_ANGLE` round the barrel's up), which the wave had none of untouched.

## The launch smear

`Prazsky.Core.Render.LaunchSmears` (`Shaders/ShotTrail.fx`) sells a launch at `SHOOT_MULTIPLIER` (200 u/s) in both executables.

- **It is anchored at the muzzle**, because the ball attaches in ~0.075 s. It lives `LIFETIME`, fading on `1 − t²`.
- ⚠ **The bright end is the leading (far) end**, because the muzzle end hides behind the barrel. `Add` derives both ends itself, so no call site can invert them.
- Additive, depth-read, boosted over 1 (`BRIGHTNESS`), colour floored at `COLOR_FLOOR` so the near-black ball still leaves a smear.
- ⚠ **Each caller decides when it ages** (the Testbed only while simulating). A screen that stops updating but still draws freezes the smears, so the Game clears them for its result screen.
- **A streak (`AddStreak`, #871)** is the same billboard for one frame: a comet's tail behind something moving now (the Game's Swap, [the Swap](../game-feedback/swap-effect.md)), added from the draw and gone once drawn (`Update` drops any a skipped draw left), at the alpha and half-width it was given and `STREAK_BRIGHTNESS`; the two widths are per smear for it.
- It restores exactly the GPU states it changes, so the translucent baseline the glass after it needs is stated before the scene, never left to the smear.
